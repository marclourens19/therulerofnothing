# Beneath Natharul

The bells had stopped reaching them hours ago.

Water had not.

It followed Seralune through the dark with a voice that changed whenever the passage narrowed. A soft rush around her ankles. A hollow knocking beneath the stone. The occasional fall of a single drop from somewhere too high for Nereth’s flame to find.

**Tik.**

They walked until another came.

**Tik.**

The sound behind them was never the same distance twice.

Seralune looked back.

Green light trembled across the channel they had already crossed. It came from beneath the water rather than above it, thin threads caught between cracks in the floor. Their glow reached the curve of the passage and ended before the place where darkness concealed the palace.

Nothing moved there. No guards.

No white flash from Thaeroval’s sword.

No old man emerging with one ruined sleeve ending where his forearm had been.

Cyrandor’s blood had dried into the hem of Seralune’s cloak. Each step drew the stiffened cloth against her ankle.

Nereth stopped several paces ahead.

“If you intend to turn back, Your Highness, do it before I forget which direction that is.”

The flame above her palm bent towards the current. It was scarcely larger than the flame upon a lampwick, but the low passage gathered its light and spread it across the water. Damp hair clung to Nereth’s neck. The bruise left by the palace guard had darkened around her upper arm.

Seralune faced forwards again. “I was listening.”

“To the water?”

“To what might be following us.”

“If your brother decides to follow, listening will not help.”

Nereth’s gaze touched the stone above them.

“Neither will running.”

She continued before Seralune could answer.

The maintained channel had changed slowly enough that Seralune could not name the point at which the palace ended.

First the metal cages disappeared from the lamps. Then the lamps themselves became fewer, until Nereth had raised one hand and made a small flame where the next should have been. The fitted floor had given way to stone worn shallow by years of water. Pipes entered the walls without emerging again. Marks left by tools had softened, then vanished beneath moss.

Now even the walls seemed uncertain whether they had been built.

Roots crossed them in pale cords. Some disappeared into the mortar. Others entered one side of the passage and emerged from the other, holding pieces of stone between them as though the aqueduct remained standing only because the growth had not yet decided to let it fall.

Seralune stepped over one. Her bare foot landed upon something sharp beneath the water.

Pain opened through her sole. She caught the wall.

Nereth turned at once.

Seralune steadied herself against the stone. “I can walk.”

“I know.” Concern briefly overcame the impatience in Nereth’s voice. “Lift your foot.”

Seralune lifted her foot. A narrow cut crossed the skin beneath her toes. It was not deep. Blood thinned into the current and disappeared.

Nereth lowered the flame and examined it.

“The stone cut you,” Nereth said. “It is shallow, but this water has passed through places neither of us understands.”

“You do not need to waste your dress on it.”

Nereth tore a strip from the inner edge of her own underskirt. “If it festers, I will be the one carrying you. I have no intention of discovering whether I can.”

She folded the strip twice, pressed it against the cut and tied the ends above Seralune’s foot.

Her fingers were quick. They did not tremble.

“You did not leave me before,” Nereth said.

Seralune watched the knot tighten.

“No.”

Nereth finished the knot without looking up. “Cyrandor paid for the time it cost us.”

“Thaeroval chose to take his arm.”

“I know who swung the sword.” Nereth pulled the knot tight. “I keep thinking he might have reached the stairs if you hadn’t stopped for me.”

Nereth rose and returned to the current before Seralune could see whether anything else had entered her face.

They walked on. The passage divided twice. Nereth lowered her flame to see the water and followed the current at each fork. The third division offered no current at all. Three black mouths waited beyond a chamber where silt had buried the floor and roots hung through the ceiling in motionless curtains.

Nereth stood among them and lowered her flame towards the buried floor. “The current is gone.”

“Then the laundry channels have ended.”

“Along with the only route I knew.” Nereth’s frustration showed in the flame, which drew thin and high above her palm before she steadied it. “From here, we are following whatever survived beneath them.”

She lifted the flame towards the left-hand opening. Broken stone blocked it several paces in. The right descended too sharply for the light to find its floor.

The centre carried no water, only a line of pale mineral dried along one wall.

Nereth looked at Seralune. “Which one did Cyrandor tell you?”

“He did not.”

“Then we are guessing.”

“He expected to be here.”

Nereth lowered her flame. She did not ask again.

Seralune touched the signet beneath her cloak. The closed lily pressed into her palm.

“Why did you help him?”

Nereth’s eyes moved from the passages to her.

“You asked me that in the stair.”

“I asked whether you wanted to come,” Seralune said. “I never asked why you stayed when the guards found us.”

Nereth stepped into the dry channel.

Seralune followed.

“He taught me to read,” Nereth said.

The answer arrived after Seralune had begun to believe none would.

“When I entered service, the palace knew I could warm washing water. That was considered sufficient education for someone like me.” Nereth kept her eyes upon the passage. “Cyrandor found me crying over a laundry inventory. He taught me every word upon it, then made me write a better one.”

“How old were you?”

“I was ten.”

“How long ago?”

“Eight years.”

Nereth lifted the flame as the passage rose.

“He asked my name before asking what my fire could do. I remembered.”

“So you came because he asked you.”

“He did not ask.” Nereth’s mouth tightened. “He was bleeding upon the floor and looked towards the stair. It was the last thing he needed from me.”

The ceiling rose after several paces. Water had once passed high enough to polish the lower walls, leaving them smooth beneath Seralune’s fingers. Higher up, shallow carvings repeated along the stone.

Two curved lines.

A descending leaf.

Another pair of lines.

Another leaf.

She stopped beneath them.

Nereth’s flame moved onwards, drawing its light away from the carvings.

“Wait.”

Nereth turned. The flame cast its light across the carvings again. “What did you find?”

Seralune touched the nearest carving.

She remembered standing beneath an open sky while masons cut the same leaves into pale stone.

She had been small enough that the aqueduct wall rose like a cliff beside her. Her mother had held one of Seralune’s shoes in each hand because Seralune had insisted upon walking through the unfinished channel barefoot. Thaeroval had followed along the upper edge, pretending he had not been told to keep her from falling.

Workers had lowered the final arch while court officials spoke about water and the southern orchards.

Seralune remembered none of their words.

She remembered the mason who had carved one leaf upside down.

She had found it before anyone else.

Her mother had made them leave it.

Seralune searched the wall until the flame caught upon a leaf whose point faced upwards.

There.

Her fingers settled into the old mistake.

“I know this place.”

Nereth came closer.

“This was the southern aqueduct,” Seralune said. “Before the palace grew across the lower gardens.”

“You remember it?”

“I watched them finish this section.”

Nereth looked along the stone.

No surprise entered her face. It had been used too often since Seralune awakened. Something quieter took its place.

“How far did it run?”

“Beneath the orchards. Then along the southern slope.” Seralune closed her eyes and tried to place the old mountain beneath the palace she had seen from her locked room. “There was a spillway above the coast.”

“The coast is where Cyrandor meant us to go.”

“Yes. If the spillway survived, it should bring us above it.”

“I hope the spillway was built as well as these walls.”

Seralune’s hand remained upon the carving.

“The mason was called Avarin.”

Nereth glanced at her.

“He put the leaf upside down,” Seralune said.

The smallest curve touched Nereth’s mouth.

“Then I am glad no one corrected him.”

She turned south, and Seralune followed the leaves.

---

The aqueduct remembered distance better than Seralune’s body did.

The channel descended through chambers broad enough to hold the palace room where she had awakened. Arches repeated into darkness, each one carrying the same carved leaves. In places, the roof had split and roots filled the opening. Elsewhere, the floor dropped away beneath pools whose green depths hid the stone below.

They walked until Seralune’s feet stopped feeling cold.

Then until they stopped feeling like feet.

They rested once upon a dry ledge while Nereth pulled the wet cloth tighter around Seralune’s cut. They rested again after the aqueduct narrowed and forced them to climb over stone brought down by an old collapse. Neither slept.

At the first ledge, Nereth drank from a narrow fall entering through clean stone before allowing Seralune to do the same. By the second, green light travelled beneath the water, and neither of them touched it.

When Seralune closed her eyes, she saw Cyrandor’s arm strike the floor.

When Nereth closed hers, her fingers moved as though still holding Seralune’s hand.

The distant bells returned sometime later.

Their notes reached the aqueduct through layers of earth, too faint for Seralune to count. A low note. Silence. Then several others answering from farther down the mountain.

“Evening?” Seralune asked.

Nereth leaned against the wall with her flame cupped close to her chest.

“Unless Natharul has changed what the bells mean.”

“Everything else has.”

“That is true.”

The bells faded, and they continued into the night.

The first flower appeared where the carved leaves ended.

It rose from a crack above the water, white petals folded around a centre that gave off the same green light as the channel. No stem held it. Fine translucent roots joined the flower directly to the stone.

Seralune approached. The petals opened.

Light moved across her face.

Nereth’s hand closed around the back of her cloak.

“Do not touch it.”

“I only wanted a closer look.”

“Everything behind us should have cured you of wanting closer looks.” Nereth did not release the cloak.

Seralune straightened, and Nereth released her cloak. The flower remained open towards her. When Nereth passed, its petals folded at once.

Nereth stopped, and the flame above her palm moved nearer.

The flower withdrew into the crack so quickly that its roots made a faint scraping sound.

**Krik-krik-krik.**

Other sounds answered from beyond the arch.

Seralune lifted her gaze. Dozens of flowers covered the next chamber.

They grew across the walls and beneath the surface of the water, their pale petals turning the aqueduct into a buried garden. Long leaves drifted upon the current without being carried by it. Fungi climbed the pillars in translucent shelves. Light passed through them in slow waves, green deepening towards violet before fading back again.

For the first time since entering the waterways, Nereth’s flame was unnecessary.

She closed her hand. Darkness should have followed.

The garden continued breathing light.

Seralune stepped through the arch. Every flower opened.

Nereth remained behind her. The nearest leaves tilted towards Seralune’s legs. They did not touch. Fine roots lifted from the water and followed the movement of her cloak before settling again.

“It opened for you,” Nereth said, unease replacing the dryness in her voice.

Seralune watched a thin stem lean after her hand. “It closed when you approached.”

“Then watch what it does now.”

Seralune looked back. Nereth had not entered.

“What is it?”

“I don’t know,” Nereth admitted. “That is why I wanted you away from it.”

Seralune watched the nearest petals turn towards her. “We cannot flee from everything we do not understand.”

“No. But we can stop offering it our hands.”

Nereth summoned her flame again and carried it into the chamber.

The flowers nearest her closed. Several roots withdrew from the water. Beneath a translucent shelf of fungus, something small and many-legged disappeared into a hollow in the wall.

Nereth watched it go. “They do not like fire.”

“Then keep it close,” Seralune said.

Nereth lifted her hand between them.

The growth made room for Seralune.

It retreated from Nereth. Seralune reached inward.

Mana waited beneath her skin as it always had—vast, silent and without edge. The flowers brightened before she touched it.

She let the thought go.

Their light dimmed with her.

Nereth saw. “What did you do?”

“Nothing.”

“They brightened when you did that.” Nereth kept away from the nearest flower. “Whatever it was, please don’t do it again.”

A shape moved beneath the water.

Nereth’s flame lengthened. The creature crossed between Seralune’s feet without touching either. It was no larger than Seralune’s hand, with a broad translucent body and too many delicate fins. No blood travelled through it. Pale roots filled the places where veins should have been and opened through its back in a fan of leaves.

It circled Seralune once, then swam towards Nereth.

She lowered the flame. The creature stopped.

Its root-filled body tightened. The fins along its back spread, and the water around it began to tremble.

Nereth closed her hand. The flame vanished, and the creature loosened and turned away.

Neither woman spoke until it disappeared beneath the flowers.

Seralune looked at the pale garden again.

Beautiful had not become the wrong word.

It had become insufficient. They moved more carefully.

Beyond the garden, roots covered the carved walls completely. Some were narrow enough to see light passing through them. Others had grown thick as Seralune’s waist, entering the floor and emerging several arches ahead. They crossed against the current as though water no longer told them which direction the world moved.

The first body appeared beneath one of them.

Seralune mistook it for stone.

A hand emerged from the growth, its fingers open towards the water. Grey skin remained between bands of translucent root. No decay marked it. The body lay upon its back beneath the surface, its chest split from throat to stomach by a pale trunk.

Seralune stopped. Nereth stepped in front of her.

The corpse wore the remains of a worker’s tunic. Metal buttons had turned green against the cloth. Roots entered beneath the jaw and passed into the open mouth.

“How long?” Seralune asked.

“Long enough that we cannot help him.”

Nereth did not look at her when she said it.

Farther ahead, another hand broke the surface.

Then part of a face.

Then three bodies gathered around the base of a pillar, held upright by growth entering their backs.

Their eyes were closed. One mouth opened.

“Help…”

The voice belonged to a child.

Seralune’s heart struck once against her ribs.

Nereth caught her wrist before she moved.

The corpse’s mouth remained open. Roots trembled beneath the skin of its throat.

“Help…”

The word came from another body across the chamber.

Not the same voice. The same pain.

A third corpse drew air it could not need.

“Please…”

Nereth pulled Seralune backwards. “They are dead.”

“Something isn’t.”

“That is what concerns me.”

The cry came again, farther south and beyond the bodies.

A frightened, breathless sound followed by something scraping against stone.

Seralune looked into the next arch.

“I can hear it suffering.”

“That may be why it wants us to hear,” Nereth said. Her fear had become too immediate to hide beneath impatience.

“What if it really is hurt?”

Nereth’s fingers tightened around her wrist.

“No,” Nereth said. Her attention remained upon the next arch. “But the dead are carrying its voice for it. Whatever waits there knows how to make you approach.”

“I heard you,” Seralune said, unable to turn from the cry. “I have to see. If we can help it—”

Another cry travelled through the rooted dead.

It entered the corpse nearest Seralune as a vibration before sound left its mouth.

The body had become an instrument.

Whatever waited ahead was pulling breath through the dead to make itself impossible to ignore.

Nereth’s face had gone still.

“We keep south.”

“I am going to see what it is.”

Nereth stared at her. Anger sharpened her face because fear had nowhere else to go.

“And if the suffering is bait?” Nereth asked.

“Then something alive is being used to set it.” Seralune’s gaze remained upon the next arch. “I will not walk away without seeing whether it can be freed.”

Seralune met her eyes. “You may stay here.”

Nereth stared at her for one disbelieving breath.

Then she released Seralune’s wrist and stepped past her.

“Oh, certainly. I’ll wait here and listen to you scream next.” Nereth brought her flame forward. “Stay behind me.”

“Nereth—”

“Let me look before you touch anything. Please.”

Nereth entered the next arch.

Seralune followed.

---

The cry led them away from the current.

A narrow branch of the aqueduct climbed into a chamber where the water had withdrawn centuries before. Roots covered the floor so densely that stone appeared only in small dark spaces between them.

The pale creature lay near the centre. It resembled a salamander from a distance; closer, the resemblance failed.

Its skin was translucent enough for Seralune to see the movement beneath it. Roots had replaced one of its rear legs and entered the lower body through an opening that had healed around them. A green sac expanded where lungs should have been. Each time it drew breath, light travelled through the roots beneath the floor.

The dead behind them answered.

“Help…”

The creature’s own mouth opened.

A thin childlike cry escaped it.

Pain shook the small body. It tried to pull itself forwards, but the roots holding its hindquarters tightened and drew it back.

Seralune crouched several paces away. Nereth remained standing.

“There,” Seralune whispered. “It is trapped.”

“It is watching your hand,” Nereth warned. “Not your face.”

“It is frightened.”

“Frightened things still bite.”

Seralune lowered one hand towards the floor.

The roots nearest her opened a path.

Not quickly. Not with alarm.

They lifted from the stone and folded aside as though recognising the place her palm intended to touch.

Nereth’s breath changed. “Your Highness.”

Seralune looked at the creature. Its dark eyes followed her hand.

“I won’t hurt you.”

“Do you believe it understands Elven?”

“I believe it understands that.”

She moved closer. The creature stopped crying.

For one breath, no voice travelled through the corpses behind them. The entire buried growth seemed to hold itself still.

Seralune could see where the roots entered the animal. Its flesh had grown around them. Pulling it free would tear away half its body.

She could not leave it like this.

Mana stirred beneath her skin.

The growth brightened. Nereth stepped forwards.

“Do not.”

“I have not done anything.”

“Then why is everything getting brighter?”

Seralune heard it then.

Not the creature.

The roots.

A low vibration moved through them, too deep to become sound. It entered the soles of her feet and climbed the bones of her legs.

**HMMMMMMMM.**

The light beneath the salamander’s skin became violet.

Nereth seized Seralune’s shoulder.

The creature moved.

It did not leap at Seralune.

Its body passed her outstretched hand and struck Nereth’s face.

Teeth entered beneath her left eye.

**TCHK.**

Nereth screamed.

Her flame erupted between them. Orange light filled the chamber. The creature tore free before the fire touched it, taking a narrow piece of flesh with its mouth.

It vanished into the roots.

Nereth dropped to one knee.

Both hands rose towards her face. One stopped before touching the wound, fingers curled against the air.

Blood ran between Nereth’s fingers. Seralune reached for her.

“Let me see.”

Nereth struck the hand away.

“Move.”

The word broke inside her teeth.

Something passed beneath the skin of her cheek—a black-purple line pushing away from the bite towards her temple.

Nereth saw Seralune watching. “What?”

Another line divided beneath her eye.

It pulsed once, then again.

Nereth’s breath vanished. Her back arched so violently that her shoulder blades drew together.

A sound left her throat without becoming a scream. She folded around her stomach, one hand digging into the stone while the other clawed at the cloth above her ribs.

Seralune caught her before her forehead struck the roots.

“Nereth.”

“Inside.”

The word came through a locked jaw.

“What is inside?”

Nereth’s fingers tightened against her own side.

“Biting.”

The roots beneath them began to move.

**Krik.**

A pale cord withdrew into the floor.

**Krik-krik.**

Another followed.

The chamber was not retreating. It was opening.

Shapes moved inside the walls.

Seralune pulled Nereth’s arm across her own shoulders and forced her upright.

Nereth’s knees failed. She recovered before Seralune could take all of her weight.

“South,” Nereth gasped.

“The channel is behind us,” Seralune said, tightening her hold beneath Nereth’s arm.

“Then get me back to it.”

Nereth forced the first step. Seralune carried the next.

The first creature emerged from the wall as they reached the lower passage. Its body had once belonged to something with four legs. Roots lifted its chest in place of bone. A ring of hollow tubes opened where its face should have been.

Nereth raised one shaking hand. Fire gathered.

The black-purple lines beneath her face contracted.

Nereth screamed and the flame collapsed.

The creature lunged.

Seralune pulled her sideways. Rooted jaws closed against the edge of her cloak and tore through the cloth.

Nereth drove her heel into its body.

The growth beneath its skin broke with a wet crack. It fell, folded around itself and began rising again before they had passed it.

More movement followed them. Not hurried. Certain.

The roots knew where Nereth was.

They ignored Seralune even while she carried her.

---

The pain came in waves.

Seralune learned to recognise each wave before it took Nereth.

The markings beneath her face pulsed first. Her left eye lost focus. Then the muscles along her neck tightened until her head drew towards one shoulder. Whatever followed travelled downwards.

Nereth’s steps shortened. Her breath stopped. Her fingers dug into Seralune’s cloak as though something beneath her skin had begun pulling in the opposite direction.

Then the wave broke, and air tore back into her.

They gained several paces before the next.

The aqueduct descended around them. Flowers opened for Seralune and closed after Nereth passed. Pale animals gathered inside the walls, their root-filled bodies turning together whenever black-purple light moved beneath her skin.

Seralune tried to count the waves.

She lost the number after nine.

At the next arch, Nereth pulled away from her.

“I can walk alone,” Nereth said. Pride forced her upright where strength could not.

“Not yet.”

She made it three steps.

The fourth never reached the floor.

Nereth struck the wall shoulder-first. Her hands flattened against the stone. A narrow shape moved beneath the skin of her forearm, travelling from wrist to elbow. It crossed the veins rather than following them.

Nereth stared at it. “No.”

Heat entered the passage.

Seralune felt it before flame appeared.

Orange fire crawled between Nereth’s fingers and spread across the wall.

The corruption beneath her face tightened.

Her knees bent as something seemed to drag along the inside of her arm. The skin rose behind it in a thin moving ridge.

Nereth screamed then.

Not loudly.

The sound came from too deep within her for volume. It tore once through her chest and left her without enough breath for another.

Seralune caught her waist. Nereth turned.

Her forearm struck Seralune’s throat and drove her into the wall.

Stone entered the back of her head.

The passage blurred. Nereth held her there.

One eye found Seralune. The other had filled with branching darkness, its green iris divided by black-purple veins.

“Nereth.”

“Do not.”

The pressure against Seralune’s throat increased.

Nereth’s face twisted as another wave moved through her. Anger arrived with it—too large for the narrow passage, too sharp to belong only to pain.

“Cyrandor lost his arm because a princess decided she could not bear to leave anyone behind.”

Seralune stopped reaching for Nereth’s wrist.

“You couldn’t leave me. So he had to stay behind!”

The words hurt because they had somewhere true to land.

Cyrandor had told her the choice was hers.

She had chosen Nereth. He had stood before Thaeroval because of the time that choice had cost.

Seralune offered no defence. Nereth saw that too.

For a moment, the pressure weakened.

Then something dropped from the ceiling behind Nereth.

Roots struck stone. Seralune saw the ring of hollow roots where a face should have been.

“Nereth.”

This time the name was a warning.

The creature struck Nereth from behind and hooked two pale limbs over her shoulders.

Her arm left Seralune’s throat. She turned with a cry and caught the thing around its narrow body.

Fire burst from both hands.

**FWHUMM.**

Orange flame opened through the creature. The roots inside it writhed towards Nereth instead of away. Their tips entered the skin around her wrists.

She tore her hands free.

The creature fell burning between them.

The flame lasted only a heartbeat.

Darkness swallowed it. Nereth stumbled over the body and struck the opposite wall. The black-purple marks had reached beneath her jaw.

Seralune drew breath through her bruised throat.

Then she took Nereth’s arm and continued south.

Neither spoke about Cyrandor. The accusation walked with them.

The roots followed.

The pain had become constant by the time the aqueduct widened again.

Nereth could no longer hide where it moved. One hand pressed beneath her ribs, fingers splayed as though holding something inside. Her ribs barely moved beneath her palm.

“It is pushing,” Nereth said.

“What is pushing?” Seralune asked.

Nereth’s laugh broke before it became sound.

“I don’t know.” She pressed harder beneath her ribs. “I only know it wants more room.”

Her legs folded, and Seralune lowered her beside the wall.

The chamber ahead had once distributed water between four descending channels. The central basin remained dry. Roots filled all four mouths and crossed the floor in a single pale knot.

Shapes gathered beyond them. Not one creature. Many.

Their movement made no sound until the first touched the stone.

**Tik.**

A claw.

**Tik. Tik.**

Another.

Nereth tried to rise. Her body refused. Seralune removed the cloak and gathered it across her knees.

Cyrandor’s blood had turned the lower edge stiff and dark. The rooted jaws that had caught Seralune in the passage had torn one side from hem to waist. She found an unbloodied section near the shoulder and pulled.

The cloth resisted. She wrapped both hands around it, and the cut across her palm opened.

Fabric tore.

**RRRIP.**

Nereth saw the strip. “No.”

“The wound is still open.”

“That is not a wound anymore.” Nereth’s fingers tightened against the floor. “It is where the thing entered.”

Seralune’s grip loosened around the cloth. “Then tell me what I can do.”

Nereth’s fingers closed weakly around Seralune’s wrist.

“Do not touch it.”

The first creature entered the green light.

Seralune looked towards the four channels. All were blocked.

Behind them, pale bodies crowded the passage they had crossed.

Nereth’s blood continued down her neck.

Seralune placed the folded cloth beneath Nereth’s left eye.

Cold entered the cut in Seralune’s palm the instant the cloth touched Nereth’s wound.

Not across the skin.

Into it.

Her mana moved. She had spent her entire life unable to give it shape. Physicians had placed crystals in her hands and watched them crack. Tutors had asked for flame, water, wind, earth—anything the world would recognise.

Nothing had ever answered. Now something did.

The black-purple veins beneath Nereth’s face opened towards Seralune like roots finding water.

Power left her.

It did not trickle.

It poured.

Seralune tried to pull her hand away.

Her fingers remained pressed against the folded cloth.

She tried to move her arm.

Nothing.

Her thoughts reached every part of her body and found the path between command and movement severed.

Nereth’s eyes widened. “Your Highness?”

Seralune could not answer. The mana inside her had never possessed an edge.

For the first time, she felt its movement because something else had given it a direction.

Light raced from the wound through Nereth’s face. Black-purple branches crossed her throat, entered her shoulders and divided beneath both arms.

Nereth’s back lifted from the wall. Her mouth opened around a scream. No sound came.

Every root in the chamber brightened.

The light moved outwards from Nereth: through the knot beneath them, into the blocked channels, along the walls and away.

The aqueduct answered from distances Seralune could not see.

**HMMMMMMMM.**

The vibration became pressure. Pressure became hunger, and the creatures stopped approaching.

One by one, their bodies turned towards Nereth.

Her fingers curled against the floor. Nails split. Blood appeared beneath them. The corruption climbed around her ribs in pulsing bands, forcing each breath wider than the last.

Nereth jerked against her hand. Heat surged through the cloth, and Seralune tried again to pull away.

Nereth looked at her.

Pain had stripped every title and duty from her face. She was eighteen years old. Frightened. Furious. Dying beneath a city that had measured her usefulness before she had learned to read.

Her mouth opened. “Seralune.”

The name barely existed.

Then Nereth burned.

---

The first flame made no sound.

Violet-white light filled the centre of her body. Orange fire opened around it, too bright to cast shadows and too dense to move like flame.

The black-purple bands beneath Nereth’s throat flared. Wherever the fire pressed back towards her chest, they brightened and drove it out along her arms into the roots. Her collar caught. Skin blistered above it, but beneath the branching her chest continued to rise.

Then the aqueduct drew breath.

**WHUMMMMMMM.**

Fire entered every root at once. The knot beneath them turned white, and the creatures arched backwards as light split through their translucent bodies. Hidden roots shone through flesh and stone, revealing the growth within the walls before it burned.

The force reached Seralune and divided before her face.

Flame curved past both shoulders, above her head and beneath her frozen hand without touching skin. Her hair lifted inside heat that should have turned it to ash. No black-purple branching ran beneath her skin. Nothing stood between her and the fire.

Nereth disappeared within the white.

The world broke.

**KRRRRAAAAAAAM.**

The channels split. Collapse travelled south from arch to arch, forcing water into walls of steam. The roots holding the dead burned away, and bodies fell from the stone with their mouths open. This time, no voices came.

The floor threw Seralune against the wall. Her hand tore free from Nereth, and movement returned too late to become balance. Something heavy struck her thigh. Another piece of stone opened her brow, though the fire passed over both wounds without entering them.

The whole chamber tilted. Beneath it, a deeper pressure gathered along the southern channels.

**GRRNNNNNNK.**

The roof tore open to a sky paling towards morning. Trees fell through the gap before the force hurled the upper slope outwards. Farther down the mountain, violet-white columns erupted from the buried channels. Each eruption shook the ground beneath Seralune, lifting broken stone before it had settled from the last.

Seralune fell back into the place from which the force had torn her.

The stone around it had blackened. Beneath her, a perfect circle remained wet, no wider than her outstretched arms. Green moss clung to the floor. A single white flower stood at its edge, untouched inside a chamber where everything beyond it had become ash.

Seralune stared at the flower. Everything around it was burning.

Then the ceiling came down.

She rolled towards Nereth. A slab struck the place where her head had been. Its broken edge caught her shoulder and drove her against the floor. Pain opened from neck to wrist.

This pain reached her. It proved the rest had not been a dream.

“Nereth!”

Steam swallowed the basin. Seralune crawled through it. Blood from her brow entered one eye. She wiped it away with the back of her wrist and found ash, shattered root and stone hot enough to blister beneath her hands.

The torn cloak had caught beneath one of her knees. She dragged it free, pulled it across her mouth and kept searching.

Nothing answered. A shape lay beyond the edge of the untouched circle.

Seralune reached it. Nereth had fallen upon her side. Most of the grey and green cloth across her back had burned away. Her hair remained dark on the right and singed almost to the jaw on the left. Black-purple veins covered that side of her face, branching from beneath the eye towards her temple and down her throat.

The bands that had driven the fire away from her chest still pulsed beneath her skin. The light inside them had faded to a thin, uneven flicker.

She did not move. Seralune placed two fingers beneath her jaw.

No answer. Seralune moved her fingers and pressed harder.

There—a pulse. Too fast, but alive.

Seralune lowered her forehead against Nereth’s shoulder.

The relief lasted one breath.

Stone shifted above them.

**Krrrk.**

A crack opened across what remained of the wall.

**Krrrk.**

The basin leaned towards the crater.

Seralune lifted Nereth’s head. Her left eye opened without finding anything. A narrow ember appeared between two of Nereth’s fingers and died.

“Nereth.”

Her mouth moved. No sound followed.

Seralune tore another strip from the cloak and pulled it across the burned cloth covering Nereth’s left shoulder and back. There was too little fabric and too much damage. She tied what she could beneath Nereth’s right arm, then pulled the remaining cloak around Nereth’s shoulders.

Nereth began to shake. Not lightly.

Her teeth struck together. Her body drew inward as though the white fire had left winter behind it.

Seralune took her hands. They were cold.

A drop of black-purple light moved beneath the skin of Nereth’s wrist.

It travelled towards her elbow, then disappeared.

Seralune looked at the ash surrounding them. Every root around them had burned away. Something inside Nereth continued.

The wall cracked again. Seralune placed Nereth’s arm over her own shoulders and rose.

Her legs folded beneath the weight.

Both women struck the floor.

Pain crossed the cut in Seralune’s foot, her bruised thigh and her injured shoulder. She pressed one palm against the stone, found Cyrandor’s dried blood upon the cloak between them and forced her knees beneath her again.

Nereth’s cheek fell against Seralune’s neck.

Seralune stood. “Come.”

Nereth did not answer.

Seralune tightened her hold. “That was not a request.”

The words had no authority over a body that could not obey.

They gave Seralune enough to take the first step.

---

The eruption had made a road from everything it destroyed.

That road climbed through broken chambers towards the open sky, a slope of shattered stone, steaming roots and earth that shifted whenever Seralune trusted it with her weight.

She carried Nereth as far as her strength allowed.

Then dragged her.

Then carried her again because the stone tore through the remaining cloth around Nereth’s legs.

The crater breathed heat beneath them. Water poured from broken channels and vanished into cracks before reaching the bottom. Steam rose in white sheets. Beyond it, portions of the aqueduct continued collapsing into themselves.

**Krrr-rrrum.**

The sound followed them upwards.

Seralune chose each step by testing it before placing Nereth’s weight upon it. She learned which blackened roots still held beneath stone and which became ash beneath her fingers. She learned that violet fire remained inside some cracks after the visible flame had gone.

She learned how heavy an unconscious person became when the world wanted both of them below.

Halfway up, Nereth woke. Her breath entered with a small broken sound, and the arm across Seralune’s shoulders tightened.

“Do not try to stand,” Seralune said, frightened by how little strength had returned to Nereth’s body.

The corrupted eye opened but failed to focus upon her. “I could not if I tried.”

Nereth’s voice scarcely reached the air.

Seralune adjusted her grip around Nereth’s waist. The movement drew a sharp breath through Nereth’s teeth.

Nereth blinked until the corrupted eye found Seralune.

“What…” Nereth began.

Nereth looked down. The crater answered before she finished.

A section of the southern wall collapsed. Stone fell through white steam and struck the ruined aqueduct below.

**GRRRAAAM.**

The mountain shook, and Nereth’s fingers opened.

A small flame appeared above Nereth’s open palm.

Orange. Its centre flashed white. Both women froze before the flame vanished.

Nereth stared at her hand.

“Do not call the fire again,” Seralune said, tightening her hand around Nereth’s wrist.

“I did not call it.” Fear stripped the dryness from Nereth’s answer.

Seralune looked from Nereth’s palm to the corruption beneath her eye. “Then tell me as soon as you feel it return.”

A laugh tried to leave Nereth. Pain caught it.

She folded against Seralune, one hand pressing beneath her ribs. The black-purple veins along her neck pulsed with no rhythm Seralune could follow.

“Inside,” Nereth whispered. “It is still…”

Her jaw locked.

Seralune held her until the wave passed.

“Still biting?”

Nereth opened her eye. “Smaller teeth.”

The answer frightened Seralune more than a scream would have.

She drew Nereth higher against her shoulder.

They climbed.

The first morning bell sounded before they reached the top.

Its note travelled from somewhere beyond the crater, across the mountain and through the exposed earth. It was not the old eastern council bell Seralune remembered. It rang higher, urgent enough that another tower answered before the sound had faded, then another.

Natharul was waking to the wound beneath it.

Seralune looked back once. The palace rose far above them.

Distance and smoke hid the lower walls, but the highest towers remained visible against the paling sky. Lights moved across bridges. Too many. Bells called from one level to the next, carrying alarm down the mountain.

No riders appeared upon the broken slope.

No dark figure stood above the crater with a sword capable of cutting the distance between them.

Thaeroval would know.

He would feel the mountain break.

He would see where the earth had opened and understand that Seralune had passed beneath it.

She waited for guilt to tell her to turn back.

Nereth’s weight shifted against her.

Seralune faced the climb. A tree had fallen across the upper edge of the crater. Its roots remained half-buried upon one side while its crown reached down towards them. The exposed roots offered the first holds she had seen that might bear both their weight.

Her injured palm slipped, and she struck one knee against the slope.

Nereth made a quiet sound beside her.

“Leave me here,” Nereth said.

“No.”

“You answered before considering it.”

“I considered it beneath the palace.”

“That was before I tried to crush your throat.”

Seralune tightened Nereth’s arm around her own shoulders.

“You were being eaten alive.”

“I remember,” Nereth said.

The words carried no request for forgiveness.

Seralune gave none. They climbed another step.

“Cyrandor is alive,” Seralune said.

Nereth looked towards the palace.

“He was when we left.”

“Thaeroval chose his arm.”

“He still took it.”

“Yes.”

Nereth’s eye closed. The bells continued.

Seralune felt the accusation return between them.

*So he had to stay behind.*

She did not push it away.

She placed the next foot higher.

Nereth remained silent for several breaths.

“If this is how you rescue everyone, Your Highness, I understand your brother’s concern.”

The words were faint and dry enough to break something inside Seralune that had held since the palace.

She almost laughed. It hurt too much.

“I had begun to worry the fire had changed your disposition.”

“No fire is powerful enough.”

That was almost reassuring. “When we reach the harbour, I’m finding you a bed,” Seralune said. “You can spend the whole day telling me what I did wrong.”

Nereth’s good eye opened by half.

“Only a day?” Nereth whispered.

It closed again. Seralune kept talking as she pulled them towards the tree, hoping for another answer.

They reached the fallen tree.

Seralune pulled Nereth beneath it and found solid earth beyond the crater’s edge. Wet grass pressed cold beneath her knees.

For a while, she could go no farther.

Nereth lay against her shoulder, breathing shallowly. The left side of her face had swollen around the bite. Beneath the skin, black-purple branches continued towards her hairline. They did not fade with the approaching light.

Seralune looked south. The mountain fell away beneath them.

Terraces that had once held orchards had returned to forest, then broken into rocky slopes descending towards the sea. Far below, roofs gathered around a narrow harbour. Boats rested beside long wooden piers. No name reached Seralune with them.

Beyond the settlement waited the ocean.

It filled the horizon. Seralune had seen it before—from high palace balconies and from the southern road as a child, Thaeroval riding ahead and turning every few moments to make certain her pony followed.

Never like this.

No wall framed it.

No window decided how much she was permitted to see.

Morning reached the water one pale line at a time.

Against the light, the sea appeared without border—too large to imprison and too distant to promise safety.

Somewhere beyond it lay Mydea.

Somewhere within Mydea, her mother had left a trail that might still remember her.

Seralune tightened the remnants of the cloak around Nereth and rose once more.

Behind them, Natharul rang every bell it possessed.

Ahead, the ocean accepted the morning without asking what had broken beneath it.

Against Seralune’s shoulder, the darkness beneath Nereth’s closed eye pulsed again.
