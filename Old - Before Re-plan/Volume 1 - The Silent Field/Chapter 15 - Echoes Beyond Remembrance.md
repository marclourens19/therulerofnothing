# Echoes Beyond Remembrance

The chain lifted from the earth.

Alaric watched the last loose link straighten beside the broken leg. Somewhere beyond the machine, soldiers called to one another. Wood creaked beneath the weight they had begun to raise.

The pale reflection of his bandages trembled upon the orb.

“Take your hand away,” Silas whispered.

Alaric withdrew it as a thin line of soil fell from the armour above the opening. Silas returned the sword to its sheath and leaned closer to the bent fastening. The outer plate had bent one edge of the fastening inwards, leaving a narrow crack across the metal holding the enclosure shut.

“I might be able to break it here,” Silas whispered, indicating the crack with his left hand. “Stay behind me while I try.”

“With fire?”

Silas nodded.

Alaric looked through the narrow slot. The clear curve inside it remained whole.

“Won’t that break the orb as well?”

“It might. I’ll keep the flame outside the box, but there’s still a risk.”

A soldier shouted for the others to pull again. The machine shifted, opening a narrow gap beneath its fallen leg. Alaric felt the movement through his knee as a pebble dropped from the broken edge and struck his sleeve.

Silas looked at him. “We either try now or leave it.”

Alaric thought of the hands waiting upon the other side, of the timbers being pushed beneath the wreck. Once it rose, anyone standing beyond it would be able to see them.

He moved back.

“Try.”

Silas turned his left palm towards the damaged fastening, keeping his burned right hand tucked against his chest. A small flame appeared above his open fingers.

It made the darkness beneath the machine seem deeper. Alaric could see the damp hair beside Silas’s scar and the way he held his breath.

Silas angled his palm across the outer face of the fastening. Alaric ducked behind his shoulder as the flame struck in a short, hard burst.

Heat washed across his cheek.

**KRAK.**

The report filled the space beneath the wreck. A bright fragment struck the outer armour and fell into the dirt beside them. The bent fastening tore free.

Silas pulled his hand back as the loosened edge sprang outwards.

“Stop pulling!” someone shouted. “Hold it there!”

The chain shuddered.

“Get the timber under it!” the same soldier shouted.

The work lamp swung away from the break in the armour. Boots hurried towards the soldiers holding the chain.

For an instant, flame ran along the opening Silas had made. Most of it passed outside the enclosure. One narrow tongue slipped through the gap and disappeared.

Orange light answered from within.

Alaric began to rise. Silas’s flame had gone out, but a thread of orange still moved inside the orb’s dark centre. Its light scarcely reached beyond the clear shell.

Silas caught Alaric’s sleeve. “Mind that edge.”

The torn metal held a dull red line. Alaric drew his hands back from it and reached through the wider opening above. His left palm found the orb’s underside. He brought his right hand around the nearer curve, keeping his elbow close enough that his shoulder could bear the movement.

The shell was warm through the linen, not hot enough to hurt. Alaric lifted it. Something beneath it resisted, then released with a small scrape.

The orb rose between his hands. He eased it past the broken edge, afraid of touching the metal, afraid of dropping it, afraid that he would find a crack upon the side he had not yet seen.

“Come on,” Silas whispered. “We’ve got to move.”

Alaric drew the orb against his coat.

It was out.

He had it.

The orange thread opened inside the black centre.

His heartbeat struck the back of his eyes.

**THOOM.**

Pain drove through his skull.

Alaric tried to say Silas’s name. His arms closed around the orb instead. He felt its curve press beneath his ribs, the hard ground reach his knee, fingers catching the back of his coat—

Someone took his hand.

---

Warm fingers slipped between his.

A young woman stood inside a doorway, laughing as she drew him over the threshold.

“You’re late again.”

“I know,” he answered, trying not to smile.

She gave his hand another tug. He laughed and let her draw him closer.

The door closed behind him. Lamplight lay across the edge of a bed. A curtain lifted beside the window, and a loose latch tapped against the wood.

Her hands were around his left hand now, lifting it between them.

There was no cloth around his fingers.

She brought his palm against her cheek.

A strand of black hair lay across his thumb. He moved it aside without thinking, already knowing how she would turn her face into his hand.

She did.

Green eyes met his. She was close enough for him to see the fine hair beside the pointed tip of her ear.

Her thumb moved against the inside of his wrist, and he let his hand settle more fully against her cheek. She smiled at him.

The latch tapped again.

She began to speak. Her fingers tightened a little around his wrist—

---

Dirt entered his mouth.

His heels dragged through broken stalks. The coat pulled tight beneath his left shoulder, then loosened as the movement stopped.

“Alaric.” Silas’s voice came from behind him. “Alaric, look at me.”

The room was gone. The warmth remained against his hand.

He tried to breathe. Nothing came deeply enough.

His arms had folded around something hard. The orb lay against his chest, held between his forearms and the gathered front of his coat. One hand had caught inside a fold. His fingers would not open.

Alaric turned his head. Silas crouched behind him, his left hand gripping the coat beneath Alaric’s uninjured shoulder. From beyond the wreck came the scrape of timber and a shouted warning not to let the leg fall. The light remained upon that side, where the soldiers were working.

Silas pulled again. Pain dragged through Alaric’s ribs, and the sound he made stopped him at once.

“Can you breathe?”

Alaric tried. His chest rose against the orb, but the breath caught before he could answer.

Silas gathered more of the coat beneath Alaric’s back. “All right. I’ve got you.”

Alaric’s heels scraped from the stalks onto the hard ruts of the path. Silas drew him across while the voices beyond the wreck shouted for another timber.

A lamp swung around the machine’s broad foot.

Silas pulled him behind the detached plate’s folded edge. Alaric’s heels cleared the path as light reached the ground they had crossed.

Silas lowered him into the hollow and turned towards the light. Alaric could not see the person carrying it. He could see Silas watching, his jaw set, his chest rising too quickly beneath his coat.

The lamp continued towards the damaged machine. Silas turned back at once.

“Can you hear me?”

Alaric tried to nod. Silas’s left hand came against his jaw, holding him still. Dirt marked the fingers. One trembled against his skin.

A little air entered and caught. Alaric’s body tried to pull more before he could let it out.

“Fuck.” Silas glanced towards the edge of the plate, then back at him. “Come on, Alaric. You’re here with me. Just one breath.”

Alaric stared at him.

He knew this face.

The scar upon his cheek. The dirt in his stubble. He had followed this man down the bank. Redd was waiting above it with Wena.

The next breath entered thinly, hurting all the way down.

Silas’s fingers loosened. “That’s it. Again.”

Alaric loosened his hold upon the orb as he breathed, easing the shell away from the ache beneath his ribs.

Wetness reached his chin.

He thought he had bitten his mouth until another drop fell from beside his nose. Tears crossed his cheeks and gathered against Silas’s fingers.

He could still see her.

Alaric shut his eyes, frightened that trying to hold the face would destroy it. But he could still see the hair caught across his thumb, her fingers around his wrist.

He tried to hear what she had been about to say. Only her laughter returned.

A sharp order sounded beyond the plate. Other voices answered, their syllables overlapping in the way he had learned to fear.

Alaric opened his eyes.

Silas had drawn back to look past the broken edge. Across the path, soldiers were gathering around the machine. One held a lamp above the opening while another pointed towards the torn fastening.

Someone called for a guard. Silas turned back to Alaric.

“Did I burn you?”

Alaric shook his head.

“Then what happened?”

“I remembered—”

His voice failed. He swallowed dirt and tried again.

“Someone.”

Silas went still for a moment.

“From before?”

Alaric nodded.

Silas looked at the orb, then at his face. He seemed about to ask something else. A pair of boots crossed the path, and the question never came.

“We can talk once we’re clear,” Silas whispered. “Can you stand if I help you?”

Alaric looked towards the slope.

“I can try.”

Silas offered his left forearm. Alaric tucked the orb against his coat with his right arm and caught the offered sleeve with his left hand. His shoulder hurt beneath the weight of the shell. He kept it close as Silas helped him rise.

The injured ankle almost gave beneath him. Silas braced him until he could shift onto his other foot, then guided him along the plate’s shadow into the dip beneath the bank. Alaric limped beside him, unable to straighten without pulling at his ribs.

Across the path, a soldier bent towards the breached enclosure.

Behind them, a soldier called out. Several voices answered at once.

Silas did not look back.

---

By the time they reached the roots below the bank, Alaric could no longer lift the injured foot properly.

Silas stopped beneath the thorn and called Redd’s name once, softly. A branch moved above them, and Redd appeared upon the upper slope. His gaze passed over the orb beneath Alaric’s arm and went straight to his face.

“What happened?”

“Help me get him up,” Silas whispered.

Redd braced against an exposed root and offered his right arm. Alaric caught it with his left and brought his good knee onto the root. Silas steadied him from below while Redd drew him into the hollow. Alaric kept the orb against his coat.

Wena rose at once, but the rope stopped her before she could reach him. A thin whine escaped her, and Alaric lowered his free hand towards her nose.

“I’m here.”

He meant to say more. Her tongue crossed the cloth around his fingers, and the rest caught in his throat.

Redd settled him against the roots and looked at Silas.

“He fell when he picked it up,” Silas whispered. “Get the sack. We need to get him out of here.”

Redd reached for the provisions. Wena pushed her head beneath Alaric’s left hand. He let his fingers sink into her fur and kept them there while she nosed against his sleeve.

Redd took Alaric’s black clothes from the sack and spread their covering across his knees.

“Let me wrap that before we move.”

Alaric looked down. A faint orange line remained inside the core. Its light reached the pale bandage beneath it and stopped there.

“Wait.”

Redd’s hands stayed open.

“It happened when I touched it,” Alaric said. “I don’t know if—”

“I don’t know what it’ll do to me either.” Redd glanced at the bandages around Alaric’s hands, then drew the cloth over his own. “Just lower it onto this for now. We can’t carry it around with that light showing.”

Alaric lowered the orb onto Redd’s cloth-covered knees, keeping his hands against the shell.

“Can you turn it a little?” Alaric whispered. “I couldn’t see the back.”

He loosened his hold so Redd could turn it through the cloth. The curve that had faced the machine was smooth, unbroken.

“I can’t see any cracks,” Redd said.

Alaric made his fingers open. Redd wrapped the orb until no light showed, returned the black clothes to the sack and cushioned it among them, away from the water bottle.

Silas loosened Wena’s tether from the alder while Redd pulled the sack’s straps tight. The dog crossed the last of the space between them and settled against Alaric’s legs.

Redd put the ash stick into Alaric’s left hand. “Ready to try?”

Alaric looked past him, towards the dark trees behind the hollow.

“No.”

The answer surprised him.

Redd shifted closer.

“We’ll go slowly.”

He shouldered the sack and steadied Alaric’s left arm as he rose upon the stick. Silas took Wena ahead, leading them down through the fold in the ground.

At the first turn, a shout reached them from the field. Alaric could not make out the words. By the next, he could hear only the river.

---

Alaric remembered little of the walk downstream beyond Redd’s hand beneath his elbow and the pauses when his foot would go no farther.

At the old crossing, Silas led Wena over the first channel. Redd stayed behind Alaric until they reached the willows, then helped him sit upon a fallen trunk. From there, Silas could watch the stones they had crossed.

Alaric rested the stick against his knee and took the water bottle from Redd. He drank without spilling any. His hands obeyed.

Silas passed Wena’s rope to Redd and crouched beside the trunk.

“How’s your head now?”

“It’s still hurting. Not like before, though.”

Silas studied him in the moonlight reaching through the willows, then sat back upon his heels. Redd took the bottle when Alaric had finished and put it away before going to watch the farther bank. Wena settled beside his feet.

Alaric looked at Silas’s right hand. The edge of the dressing had darkened where the man had held it against his coat.

“Did I hurt your hand?”

“No.”

“You were pulling me.”

“I used the other one.”

Silas said it without impatience. That made Alaric look up. Silas rubbed his left palm over his mouth before speaking again.

“I thought you were dying.”

Silas looked towards the crossing. “You weren’t answering me. I got you out, and I still couldn’t tell if you were breathing.”

“I’m sorry.”

Silas’s eyes returned to him.

“I’m not asking you to apologise.”

Alaric looked down at the stick between his hands.

“If it starts again, can you warn me?” Silas asked, more quietly.

“I don’t know if I’d have time. There was light inside it. Then my head hurt, and I wasn’t there any more.”

Silas waited.

Alaric had expected the room to vanish as the other fragments had, leaving only the ache and the certainty that he had lost something. But he could still see the doorway.

“I was in a room. There was a woman with me.”

“Someone you knew?”

“I knew her there.” Alaric paused, trying to explain the difference. “I wasn’t trying to remember her. I just knew her. The way I know you’re Silas when I look at you.”

Silas nodded slowly.

“But now I don’t know her name,” Alaric said.

He looked towards the water beyond the willow branches.

The woman had been an elf. He could remember the pointed ear beneath her hair as clearly as he remembered the pressure of her cheek against his palm.

Alaric rubbed his thumb over the bandage across his palm. The cloth caught upon a small hard ridge of dried blood.

“She’d been waiting for me,” he said. His voice had begun to shake again. “She took my hand before I’d said anything.”

Silas looked down at the hand moving over the bandage, then sat beside him upon the trunk.

The wind moved through the willows. A thin branch tapped another above his head.

Alaric looked up before he could stop himself.

There was no window.

Only the dark leaves and Redd standing beyond them with Wena’s rope held loosely in one hand.

Alaric lowered his gaze.

“I could feel her.”

“Do you need a moment?”

Alaric nodded and bent his head. Her laughter came back with his own beneath it. He could remember the sound he had made so easily in that room, but when he tried to speak, the next breath broke.

For a while, he let the river cover it. Silas stayed beside him.

When he could, Alaric wiped his face upon the sleeve of the coat. The cloth smelled of dirt and smoke. He lowered it before the smell could become the cabin upon the farm.

“What if that’s all I get?” he asked.

Silas leaned forwards, resting his left forearm upon one knee.

“I don’t know,” Silas said. “I can’t tell you there’ll be more. But you haven’t lost what you saw tonight.”

Alaric looked towards the sack. Silas followed his gaze.

“I wasn’t going to touch it again,” Alaric said.

“Good.” The word left Silas more quickly than anything else he had said. “Keep it wrapped. Let’s have someone who understands these things look at it before either of us touches it again.”

“Helmi?”

“That’s who Darcy told us to find.”

“Do you think he could tell me who she is?”

“I don’t know. Darcy said he could read the core. We’ll have to ask him about the rest.”

Alaric nodded.

Silas got to his feet. Redd passed him Wena’s rope and came back to the trunk.

“Do you think you can manage the other side?” Redd asked.

Alaric took up the stick.

“I think so, if we go slowly.”

Redd offered his arm, and they followed Silas towards the stones.

---

Beyond the second channel, Silas led Wena up the far bank. Redd took Alaric’s stick and helped him climb the root shelf. Alaric reached the grass breathing hard, but upright; Redd handed the stick back to him.

They continued through the lower ground, keeping away from the road they had watched before crossing towards the farm. No wagons passed now. The silence made Alaric listen for them all the same.

Kelmend’s lights appeared through a break in the trees.

There were more of them than he remembered.

Silas stopped.

Across the low ground, lanterns moved between the abandoned houses. Two riders waited where the narrow path entered the old row. Beyond them, a pale rectangle opened in a wall as someone forced a door inward and carried a light inside.

Redd came to stand beside Silas. “That’s the path to my cottage.”

“I know,” Silas said.

“They weren’t there when we left.”

Silas watched a rider turn towards the houses. Pale hair caught the nearest lantern before the man passed out of its light.

“They’re searching this side now.”

Alaric looked beyond the riders. He could not distinguish Redd’s house from the others at that distance, but he remembered the wooden catch upon its door, Redd testing it once before leaving.

The sack shifted upon Redd’s shoulders. His fingers caught beneath its strap and remained there.

“They’ll find the armour.”

Silas did not answer immediately. “We can’t go back for it.”

“I know.”

Redd kept watching.

The door of another house opened. Light moved across the floor inside, then disappeared as its bearer went farther in.

Alaric remembered the table standing too close to the bed, the cup Redd had held against his mouth. He had slept while Redd kept the fire going and brought him water.

“Is there anything else you need from there?”

Redd’s hand moved to the sword at his hip.

“There’s plenty I’d like to take.” Redd kept watching the houses. “But I’m not going back while they’re there.”

Alaric nodded. Beyond the riders, Kelmend’s wall cut across the light from the streets.

“What about Marta?”

Silas turned towards the glow above Kelmend’s wall. For several breaths, he said nothing.

Marta had gone back to the inn. Alaric watched Silas looking towards it and wished he had asked more gently.

“We can’t go back to her inn,” Silas said at last. “Not with them searching like this.”

“Does she know we’re out here?”

“She knows we wanted to reach the farm. I’ll get word to her when I can.”

He remained facing Kelmend after he had finished speaking.

Redd glanced towards him, then looked down the slope again. Neither man moved towards the houses.

Farther along the old row, a torch rose above a low wall. Silas drew Wena back beneath the trees.

“We need to go.”

“Which way can we go?” Alaric asked.

“There’s a track beyond the fields. It joins the inland road without taking us through those houses.”

“Towards Favale?”

“Yes. If we keep to it.”

Alaric looked at the sack upon Redd’s back.

The orb was inside, unbroken as far as they could tell. Darcy had asked him to find it and take it to her father. Alaric could still remember the two circles upon her paper.

He could remember a face now, too.

“Will you take me there?” he asked Silas.

Silas looked at him. “All the way to Helmi?”

“If you can.”

Silas’s left hand rested around Wena’s rope. He let it slide a little as she moved towards Alaric.

“We’ll need more food and somewhere you can rest properly. And I need you to tell me when that foot won’t carry you any farther.”

“I will.” Alaric met his eyes. “I don’t think I could make it by myself.”

Silas nodded. “All right. I’ll take you.”

Redd shifted the sack higher. “You’ll have to put up with me as well.”

He was attempting a smile, though his gaze moved once more towards the old houses.

“I’d like you to come,” Alaric said.

Redd looked back at him.

“Good. I’d rather hear what Darcy’s father makes of that thing than wait for you to come back and tell me.”

His fingers loosened from the strap.

“And I’d rather not be here when they finish looking through my kitchen.”

Alaric managed a small smile. Redd answered it, tiredly, and turned from the houses.

Silas checked the ground beyond the trees. “Come on. We’ll find somewhere to stop before morning.”

He led them away from the lights.

Redd took Wena’s rope so Silas could move ahead to watch the track. The dog remained close to Alaric, matching the short distance between his steps. When he stopped to settle the stick, she stopped too.

At the edge of the last field, Alaric looked back. He could no longer see the old houses. Kelmend remained only a glow beneath the clouds, and beyond the river, smoke lay across the place where Gerolt had found him.

He did not know where the woman was, or how far he would have to go to find her. But when he thought of the doorway, her face was still there.

Redd had stopped to tighten one of the sack’s straps. Alaric waited beside the hedge. Beyond it, the track dipped into ruts he could not cross safely upon the stick alone.

“Redd?”

Redd looked up.

“Could you give me a hand here?”

“Of course.”

Redd came beside him and settled his free hand beneath Alaric’s left elbow. Wena waited while they found their footing. Ahead of them, Silas held a branch aside.

Alaric leaned upon Redd’s arm and took the next step.
