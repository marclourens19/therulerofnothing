# What Remained

Every time Alaric remembered waking, Redd was there.

Once, with a cup held to his mouth and a hand under his head. Water had run down his chin. Redd had wiped it off with the corner of a cloth and waited before letting him drink again.

Another time, Alaric had found himself sitting on the edge of the bed with no idea how he'd got there. His foot was resting on Redd's knee. The cloth round his ankle was coming loose under patient fingers.

"Leave that to me. You'll have both hands bleeding again."

He remembered doing as he was told.

There had been darkness in the window. Then light. Wena's weight against the bed frame, a bowl in his lap, someone reminding him to swallow what was already in his mouth before he tried to talk.

Now amber light lay across the ceiling, and Redd was sitting on the chest with his back to the wall. His chin had dropped towards his green waistcoat. One hand hung open by his knee.

Alaric watched him breathe.

He knew this room. The sword above the chest. The table too close to the bed. Under the front window, Silas sat with one boot braced against the wall and his bandaged hand resting on his thigh. His eyes were shut, but they opened the moment Alaric moved.

"How long?" Alaric asked. His voice came out rough.

Silas lowered his boot. "Two days."

Alaric looked at the window. "Since we got here?"

"Since you lay down."

The scraps of light and dark rearranged themselves. He remembered the cup again. Had he thought every waking was the same afternoon?

"We were going to leave."

"You couldn't get your boot on that evening. Yesterday you made it as far as the door, and Redd had to bring you back." Silas sat forward carefully. "We waited."

Redd stirred on the chest. His eyes opened, found Alaric sitting up, and stayed open.

"You're making more sense," he said, rubbing a hand down his face. "That's encouraging."

"What was I saying?"

"Mostly that you could manage." Redd pushed himself up. "Usually while I was managing for you. Water?"

Alaric nodded. When Redd brought the cup, he closed his own hands round it. The fresh cloth pulled against tender skin, but his fingers held.

Redd didn't let go until he was sure.

"Have you been here the whole time?"

"It is my house." It came with a small smile. Then Redd noticed Alaric hadn't smiled back. "Silas took over when he was here. I've slept."

Alaric looked down at the water.

He remembered being angry in Darcy's cell because she wouldn't leave with him. He remembered expecting everyone who helped him to want something back. Redd had wanted to see a battlefield. Instead, he'd spent two days holding a cup for someone who kept spilling it.

"Thank you."

Redd tapped the rim with one finger. "Finish that. You can thank me by not falling over when I turn my back."

Alaric drank it and handed the cup back.

Wena got up from beside the bed. Her ears lifted as he lowered a hand towards her, and she pushed her head into the space under his fingers. When he moved the blanket, she stepped back to let him put his feet down.

His ankle had changed colour. The swelling no longer hid the shape of it, though the skin above the wrapping was still dark. He tested the floor with his heel. The pain that answered was duller than he remembered. He waited for it to settle, then put the rest of his foot down.

Silas watched without reaching for him.

Alaric stood.

His ribs tightened. The room drifted a little further away. He kept one hand on the bed frame and let it come back.

Then he took a step. Not far, and not well.

But the ankle held.

Redd pulled the chair out of his way. Alaric reached the table, rested there, and came back before either of them could ask him to. Sitting hurt almost as much as standing had, but he sat without anyone catching him.

"Better," Silas said.

Alaric nodded, breathing carefully round the ache under his shoulder.

"You went out."

"Last night." Silas moved his right hand higher on his knee. "As far as the farm."

"What did you see?"

"Soldiers. Mydeans on the road, elves nearer the field. Wagons coming in while I watched. They've cut paths through the wheat and put lamps round the bigger wrecks."

"Are they taking them away?"

"Not while I was there."

Alaric pressed his foot into the floor again. Gerolt's token sat in the pocket at his hip, its edge catching between cloth and skin when he leaned forward.

"I want to see it."

Silas's face didn't change. "You could barely stand a moment ago."

"I know." Alaric made himself hold his eyes. "I'm not asking you to take me through the soldiers. I just want to see what's there. Where they are."

He'd meant to stop. The rest came out quieter.

"It's the only place I know I was before any of this."

Silas studied him. Behind him, the light under the shutter had narrowed to a thin yellow line.

"There's a bank above the field, with trees behind it. We can look from there without walking into their lamps."

"Tonight?"

"After dark. Slowly. And if that foot won't carry you, we turn back while we still can." Silas leaned in. "We're only going to look. I need you to understand that before we set foot outside."

Alaric thought of the machines. Of Darcy drawing a dark circle inside another on the back of a sheet.

Then he thought of the cabin.

"I understand."

---

While the last daylight lasted, Redd cut the rough end off an ash stick, shaved a knot away near the top, tested the grip with his palm and wrapped it in a strip of old linen.

"Try that."

With the stick in his left hand, Alaric could take weight off his foot without lifting the bad shoulder.

"Too long?"

"No. It helps."

"Good. I can make it shorter. Putting the end back on would take more skill than I've got."

Alaric ate the bread Redd had saved for him, slowly enough that nobody had to remind him to. Across the room, Silas buckled the stolen sword belt round his borrowed coat, using his left hand and his teeth to work the loose end through its loop. The right hand stayed wrapped. He could bend the fingers a little now, but he always stopped before they closed.

Redd had dried Alaric's boots and loosened the fastenings. He helped him work the swollen foot into one, pausing each time Alaric's breath caught, and left that side looser than the other.

The charcoal messenger's coat came out of the bundle on the chest. The high collar brushed Alaric's jaw, and for a heartbeat the dry room smelled of the river.

He pulled the front closed.

Redd packed the black clothes with the last of the provisions, added Marta's clean dressings and a stoppered water bottle, and set his travelling sack by the door. Then he lifted one corner of the sacking that covered the armour.

"Those stay," Silas said.

"I wasn't going to wear it. I was wondering what I'd say if anyone asks why I own it."

"Don't let them see it."

"That was my first thought as well."

Redd pushed the plates against the wall and tucked the sacking over the last exposed edge. When he straightened, his eyes went to the sword above the chest.

He took it down carefully. Alaric expected him to check the blade. Instead, Redd only pressed his thumb against the mended mouth of the scabbard before he buckled the belt round his waist. He adjusted it twice, settling it where it wouldn't knock his knee.

Wena was at the door before anyone called her. Redd found a length of soft rope in the kitchen, tied it to her collar, and held the free end out to Alaric.

"Near the roads. She'll smell something we can't and be halfway to it before we notice."

Alaric passed it to Silas. "I can't hold her if she pulls."

Silas took it without a word.

Outside, the last light had drained out of the grass. Redd shut the cottage door and dropped a simple wooden catch across the outside. It would stop the wind blowing it open. Alaric could see it wouldn't stop much else.

"Ready?"

Alaric looked at the dark window above the bed he'd borrowed.

"Yes."

They left the cottage standing quietly among the others.

---

Kelmend stayed behind the low ground longer than he expected. He couldn't see its walls anymore, but their light held against the underside of the clouds. A bell reached them after the path had narrowed to a track between hedges. He tried to count the strokes and lost one under Wena's breathing.

Silas didn't ask him to hurry.

That helped, until Alaric noticed how often Silas was shortening his own stride. Redd walked behind with the sack, close enough to offer an arm where the ground dipped. Neither of them mentioned the pace. Alaric found that harder to bear than being told he was slow.

At the first low wall, he tried to step over it on his own. His boot caught the top stone.

Redd had him under the left elbow before the stumble reached his ankle. He waited while Alaric freed the boot, and stayed beside him until both feet were on the ground.

"Thank you," Alaric muttered.

"You can stop saying that every time." Redd hitched the sack higher. "Tell me before you try the next one. That'd help more."

At the next wall, Alaric waited.

They were resting under a stand of hazel when lights appeared on the road above them.

Silas lowered the hand holding Wena's rope. Redd crouched. Alaric sat back against the roots instead of trying to copy him. Through the branches he could see the road where it crossed a gap between two fields.

Horses first. Then a wagon under a dark covering. Then more riders.

Pale hair caught the lanterns. A voice passed down the line, its syllables overlapping in the way he remembered far too well. One rider turned in the saddle to answer. The wagon rolled on.

Nobody searched the hedge.

Alaric kept waiting for a face wrapped in white cloth. The last rider went by without one.

Silas waited until the wheels had faded before he stood. "More of them than last night."

"All going the same way," Redd said. He'd stopped smiling. His fingers rested on the strap across his chest, well away from the sword.

Alaric pushed himself up on the stick. "Towards the farm?"

"That road reaches it. We'll stay below it."

He led them off the rise and along the bottom edge of the hazel. The detour took them through grass wet enough to darken their boots, but no more lanterns crossed their path. After a while, running water began to fill the gaps between their footsteps.

Alaric knew the sound before the trees opened.

His hand tightened on the stick.

---

The river was wider here. It split round a low island of willow, the two channels spreading over pale shelves of stone before joining again further down. Whenever the clouds thinned, moonlight caught the white edges of the current.

Alaric stopped above the bank.

He could feel soaked cloth dragging at his legs. His body remembered that weight more clearly than it remembered the walk that had brought him here.

Silas pointed downstream. "There."

At first Alaric saw only another line of broken water. Then he made out the tops of broad stones laid across the near channel. Some leaned on their neighbours. Others had sunk until the water split round them, but their tops stayed above it. The line reached the island. Past the willows, a second stretch ran on to the far bank.

"There used to be a road," Silas said. "The bank went, and they stopped mending this end once the bridge was built."

Redd went to the edge and studied the way down. Roots held a narrow shelf where the earth had fallen away. "You crossed here last night?"

"Both ways. Step where I step."

Silas went down first with Wena. She took one look at the water and planted her front legs until he stopped pulling and crouched beside the first stone with the rope slack. She smelled the air. Then the stone. One paw touched it, reluctantly. Then the other. Silas rose and led her towards the island, letting her choose her footing and keeping her away from the edges.

Redd had stayed beside Alaric. "I'll take the stick while you get down."

Alaric handed it over. He turned sideways and used the roots, lowering his good foot onto the shelf before bringing the other after it. His shoulder pulled when he reached too far. Redd told him to stop, moved in closer, and steadied his left arm until he could let go.

Nobody fell.

Alaric stood at the crossing and waited for his breathing to understand that.

The first stone was wide enough for both boots. Redd gave the stick back and followed a pace behind as Alaric tested the next stone with the end of it.

Water filled the gap. Only a gap. He could step across it.

He watched the water anyway.

"Keep your eyes on the stone," Redd said quietly. "The water keeps moving. That doesn't."

Alaric lifted his eyes and put his foot where the stick had been.

The stone held.

They crossed slowly. Once, spray struck the side of his boot, and he stopped so suddenly that Redd nearly walked into his back. But no water got in above the leather. The cloth round his ankle stayed dry.

On the island, Wena pressed her nose to his knee and waited until he touched her head. Silas let them rest among the willows before leading them over the second channel.

The far bank was lower. Alaric was on grass before he noticed, when the stick sank into soft earth.

He turned round. The stones were still where he'd left them.

Silas was watching his face. "We'll come back this way if we need to."

Alaric nodded. He couldn't have answered yet. His throat was too tight.

Redd passed the water bottle round. They checked their wrappings were dry and went on upstream under the trees.

This time the river stayed beside them.

---

The smell reached them before the lights did.

He'd thought he remembered it. He'd carried pieces of it into the inn and into the fort. It had followed him into Redd's bed every time his thoughts went back to the dagger. But the air moving through these trees was thicker than memory. Sweet, under the smoke, in a way that made his mouth fill with spit.

He stopped to swallow.

Redd took out the bottle again. Alaric shook his head. He couldn't imagine putting anything in his mouth.

Wena had lifted her nose. She pulled once against the rope, then again when Silas didn't follow. Her ears pointed at a break in the trees, and a low sound started in her throat.

Alaric moved in beside her. "I know," he whispered, though he didn't know whether he did.

Beyond the trees, light lay against the underside of smoke.

Silas steered them away from it along a shallow fold in the ground. They climbed slowly, using roots where the soil had slipped. Twice, Alaric had to stop. The second time he said so before Redd had to touch him. Nobody objected.

The fold ended behind a bank thick with alder and thorn. Through the branches Alaric could see a pale tent roof and the top of something metal.

Silas turned back. "Leave the sack here. Nothing above the bank unless I say."

Redd lowered the provisions into a hollow behind the roots. Alaric laid the stick beside them. Silas tied Wena's rope to the low trunk of an alder, leaving her enough length to lie in the hollow but not to reach the top.

She tested it at once.

Alaric crouched beside her with more difficulty than he wanted either of them to see.

"Stay with me, girl."

Her nose moved against his collar. He rested his forehead on hers for one breath, then looked up as Silas beckoned.

Silas lay flat along the top of the slope with his burned hand held clear of the soil. Redd settled below his left shoulder. Alaric eased down beside them on his good side, close enough to keep one hand on Wena's neck.

Gerolt's farm lay under the lamps.

The wheat had been cut into roads.

Wide strips crossed the field, the stalks crushed under wheels and hooves. Tents stood on ground where the crop had still been standing when he ran. Soldiers moved between them with poles, coils of rope, and lamps whose pale light didn't flicker the way flame did.

The machines had guards. Every large wreck sat inside its own ring of lamps, and soldiers stood at the edge of each ring, facing outwards, as if the dead metal might be stolen in the night.

The dead had hooks.

Beyond the tents, soldiers were working along the edge of the ruin in pairs. One knelt and cut away straps and buckles. Whatever armour came loose went into a cart. Whatever was left, the other man caught by the belt with a long iron hook and dragged to a second cart, where two more soldiers swung it up onto the pile without breaking rhythm. The pile had been stacked in layers, crossways, the way Alaric had seen men stack firewood behind the inn.

He searched for the horse that had died with its forelegs stretched out in a stride it never finished.

He couldn't find it.

Some part of him had expected it to be waiting exactly where he'd left it. The thought shamed him. Soldiers were walking through the field now, lifting things, moving them, stacking them. Whatever he had failed to understand wasn't being kept safe until he came back.

Then he found the cabin.

It stood off to their left, past an open stretch between two tents. At first he recognised only the stone hearth. The roof had come down round it. Black beams lay across the place where the table had been, and one surviving stretch of wall leaned inwards under its own weight.

There should have been a window beside it. Gerolt had stood there with one hand braced on the frame.

Alaric's fingers closed in Wena's fur.

He could remember Gerolt's mug more clearly than the shape of the cabin from outside. The dent near the rim. The broad hands round it. The way he'd set it down and kept hold of the handle after it had reached the floor.

Two soldiers had come to the ruin with a lamp and the long hooks. They didn't hurry. One of them kicked a charred beam aside and bent to look under the next. The other set the lamp on the hearthstone.

They pulled something out from under the fallen wall.

At that distance, it was only a dark shape. Long. Heavier at one end. It came free of the beams all at once, and the soldier with the hook staggered a step and laughed at himself, and his partner laughed with him. Then they dragged it across the open ground towards the nearest cart, one hook through what was left of the belt, the way they'd dragged all the others.

Under Alaric's hand, Wena went rigid.

Every muscle in her body locked at once. Her nose was pointed at the dark shape bumping over the stubble. Her ears had gone flat against her skull, and from somewhere deep in her chest came a sound he had never heard her make. It wasn't a growl or a whine. It was thin and high and broken, and it was getting louder.

Alaric got his hand round her muzzle and held it shut.

She fought him. Not hard enough to break free. Hard enough that he could feel the sound going on inside her, shaking through his palm and up his arm, while the soldiers reached the cart and swung the shape up onto the pile and went back for the next.

"Alaric." Silas's voice was very low. "Keep her quiet."

"I know." He could barely hear himself. "I know. It's him."

"You can't know that from here."

"She does."

Silas didn't answer.

Wena had stopped fighting. She lay pressed against Alaric's side with her head still pointed at the cart, trembling from nose to tail. He kept his hand closed round her muzzle, because letting go would have meant letting her make the sound, and he wasn't sure he could stand to hear it.

He'd been inside that house for less than a day.

Gerolt had lived there. There had been mornings before Alaric. Work waiting in the fields, a dog under the table, and food Gerolt had meant to eat himself. He had brought a stranger inside. Now soldiers laughed as they dragged him over the stubble on a hook.

If he'd left Alaric where he found him —

The thought closed round his throat.

Another room followed it. Stone under his shoulder. A man trying to force his wrist down. His own hands pushing, because he wanted to breathe.

"We can't go down there now," Silas said, more quietly.

"I know."

He did. He'd agreed before they left. But agreeing inside a warm room hadn't meant watching Gerolt thrown on a cart.

He made himself stay where he was, with his hand round Wena's muzzle.

Further out in the field, a lamp moved across seamless silver.

Pain tightened behind his eyes.

He shut them at once. It felt as if something had been put inside his skull while he slept, and was now being pressed against the bone.

His heartbeat struck straight through it.

**THOOM.**

He gripped his own sleeve instead of reaching for his head, and waited with his mouth open, letting each breath come in without forcing it.

When he opened his eyes, the lamp had moved on. The machine was still there. The ache stayed behind his eyes, and he waited for it to ease before he looked again.

Beside him, Redd drew breath to speak.

Then the ground began to hum.

---

At first Alaric thought the sound was inside his head. It was too low to separate from the pressure behind his eyes. He felt it through the ground, a slow vibration that made a dry leaf tremble against his sleeve.

Wena lifted her head out of his hand.

Below, the soldiers had stopped working. One left a pole standing crooked in the earth and ran towards the centre of the camp. Another started pulling people back from the broad cleared strip beside the tents. A rider in a silver-bordered cloak turned his horse towards the sky.

The stars were going out above the far trees.

Not behind cloud. A dark curve was moving across them, edged with lights. Alaric followed it until his neck hurt. Behind it came another sweep of metal, then another, each overlapping the last.

He waited for the gap between the ships.

There wasn't one.

The ship at Fort Kelmend had strained at its chains like something the town was afraid to let go. He'd been able to see both ends of it. Here, armour went on past the branches whichever way he turned his head.

Its front edge had crossed the far fields before the back of the hull even cleared the trees.

All of it was one ship.

Its body rose broad and heavy above an underside crowded with curved ribs. Some ribs held deep hollows where blue-green light ran through the dark. Others swept down into armoured housings whose points hung under the hull like the towers of a fortress built upside down, towards the earth.

Along its flanks, thick metal mouths jutted from between the plates, each set in its own ringed mounting. Alaric remembered the opening on the dead machine below. These weren't broken. They were in rows.

The hum deepened.

**KRRRMMMM.**

Air pressed down through the alders. Leaves flipped their pale undersides towards the ship, then flattened against the branches. Alaric ducked his head as dust and dry grass swept over the bank.

Wena shoved against his chest. He wrapped his arm round her shoulders and kept his face in her fur until the first rush passed.

When he could see again, the ship had moved past the nearer trees. Its stern kept coming for so long that he had time to watch a soldier run between two tents before the last armoured point cleared the treetops.

The camp's lamps traced the cut roads below it. The ship reached past them on both sides. Its underside covered several fields, and the tents beneath it looked like scraps of pale cloth.

Gerolt's cabin had seemed small with soldiers round it. Now Alaric had to hunt between their lights to find the hearth at all.

Redd's mouth had fallen open.

Silas pressed his left hand into the bank and drew himself lower. "That's one of Natharul's royal warships," he murmured. "Stay under the branches."

The ship slowed above the far side of the camp. Blue-green light brightened between its ribs and spilled over the layered armour. It drifted, then held, the same row of lights hanging over the same trees.

The wind against the bank eased. The hum didn't. Alaric felt it in his ribs every time he breathed.

Under the hull, a line of white appeared. It widened as two curved plates drew apart. For a moment there was only light inside the opening. Then something dark came loose from it and began to sink.

Another ship.

It looked small enough to fit between two of the great hull's hanging ribs. As it came lower, its windows separated into single lights, and Alaric made out an enclosed cabin, a pointed prow and short armoured fins curving down each side. It was longer than Gerolt's house.

Above it, the opening closed.

The smaller craft crossed towards the cleared strip beside the tents. Soldiers waved the last wagon out of the way and pulled back behind a line of lamps. Short metal legs unfolded beneath the descending hull.

**KLANG.**

The last leg locked. Dust rolled out across the cut stalks, and the waiting soldiers lifted their cloaks against it, then stepped forward when it cleared.

A door opened in the craft's side, and a short stair lowered to the ground. Light fell across the steps. Figures moved inside.

Two came down first. Their armour caught the lamps in narrow white lines. More followed and formed a path from the foot of the stair to the waiting soldiers. A standard opened above them: dark green round a pale branching shape, with a closed crown in gold above it.

Silas went very still.

"What?" Alaric whispered.

"The king's standard."

Another figure appeared at the top of the stair.

He wore a long dark mantle over armour that took the light without shining as brightly as his guards'. Pale hair lay against the collar. When he turned towards the field, a narrow band of gold caught the light across his brow.

The soldiers below him bowed. Not all at once. The elves went first, and the Mydeans a moment after. One human soldier stayed upright long enough for the man behind him to touch his sleeve.

Then he bowed too.

Alaric stared.

He'd known kings existed. The word belonged with crowns and kingdoms and all the things other people seemed to understand without needing them explained. It didn't belong to a man walking down a stair into Gerolt's wheat.

The king reached the ground. The rider in the silver-bordered cloak had dismounted and came forward with his head lowered, one arm held out towards the field. The king listened briefly, then walked past him.

Everyone turned to follow.

A scraping sound came from beside Silas.

Alaric turned his head.

Redd's hand had closed round his sword.

---

The blade was out no further than the width of his palm. Even that caught the light through the leaves.

Redd didn't seem to notice. He was staring down the slope with his lips pulled back from his teeth. His left hand pushed into the ground as he started to rise. Soil gathered against his fingers. A small stone rolled uphill towards his wrist.

Alaric felt the earth shift under his knee.

"Redd —"

Silas moved before the name was out.

He drove into Redd from the side, caught the back of his coat with his good hand and forced him down below the crest. Redd hit the soft earth, and his breath broke in his throat. Silas came down on top of him, left forearm across his chest, burned hand clamped tight against his own body.

The half-drawn sword scraped through the grass.

Redd tried to push up.

"Stay *down*," Silas hissed. There was more anger in his voice than Alaric had heard since the fort.

Redd's fingers dug into the soil. A narrow ridge rose under his palm, no higher than the roots round it, and Alaric felt Wena tense against his arm. He held her before she could make a sound. He could feel the breath gathering in her chest.

"Quiet, girl. Please."

Overhead, the warship's hum held steady. Someone called out by the foot of the stair. Nobody on the lit strip was looking at the bank.

Silas pressed Redd harder into the earth. "You draw that and you get all of us killed. Put it away."

"He gave the orders." Redd's voice was low and strangled, every word forced out between his teeth. "The attacks. All of it. He sits up there giving orders, and —"

He tried to wrench his shoulder free. Silas stopped him with his weight.

"You can't reach him. His guards can reach us." Silas's face was close to his. "Put it *away*."

Redd's right hand shook round the hilt. The ground under his left moved again.

Alaric remembered those fingers holding the cup steady against his mouth. Redd had waited when he couldn't swallow. He'd stayed while Alaric slept.

Now Alaric was afraid to move anywhere near him.

"Redd," he said quietly. "Wena's going to bark."

Redd's eyes moved to him.

Alaric kept his hand on Wena's neck. "Please stop."

For several breaths, Redd did nothing.

Then his left hand opened. The raised soil broke apart beneath it. Small stones settled into the hollow his fingers had made. He looked down at the sword, and the blade slid back into its sheath, the sound almost lost under the throb overhead.

Silas didn't let go straight away. "Are you staying down?"

Redd nodded once.

Only then did Silas lift his arm. He shifted back carefully, his breath catching when the dressing on his right palm brushed his coat.

Redd stayed where he'd been pressed into the bank. Dirt clung to his cheek and the open edge of his waistcoat. He wiped his left hand on the grass, but kept the other on the sheathed sword.

Alaric wanted him to take his hand off the hilt. He didn't know how to ask.

After a while, Redd moved it himself and laid it flat beside the other.

Nobody spoke. Wena's breathing eased against Alaric's sleeve. Silas checked the field before he let either of them move back up to the gap in the thorn.

Redd came last.

---

The king had reached the dead.

He walked along one of the cut paths with the officer a step behind his shoulder. Guards went ahead of him, turning their lamps so the light fell wherever he was about to put his feet. The soldiers with the hooks stepped out of his way before the guards reached them.

Alaric couldn't hear what was said. He could see the officer talking, one hand pointing at the carts, then at the torn ground beyond them. The king kept walking. Once, he stopped beside something on the ground and bowed his head. Then he went on.

An attendant kept pace behind him with a pane of black crystal in a narrow silver frame. Nothing showed on its surface.

The group turned towards the machine nearest the bank. It sat at the edge of the ruin, half sunk on one side. From where Alaric lay, its front was in shadow. Then a guard carried a lamp round the far corner, and he recognised the long opening, blackened at the edges.

He'd seen it while sitting in the blood beside Gerolt.

The ache behind his eyes sharpened. He pressed his face into his sleeve until it eased, then lifted his head again. He wouldn't look towards the place where the green circle had been. He wasn't sure he could bear to find it changed. Or exactly the same.

Below, two soldiers moved aside to let the king approach the wreck. The officer was still talking.

The king stopped before he reached them.

At first Alaric thought something on the ground had blocked him. But the guard ahead went on to the machine with no trouble and lifted his lamp. Pale light ran across the silver side without catching a single seam.

The king didn't follow.

The officer took another step before he noticed. He turned back at once. Whatever he said was lost under the throb overhead.

The king lifted one hand.

The officer's pointing arm dropped.

Alaric edged closer to the gap in the branches. He couldn't make out the king's expression. The light was behind him now, and his face was dark under the thin band at his brow.

But the raised hand stayed in the air after everyone had stopped.

The attendant came closer with the framed crystal. The king lowered his arm sharply, and the attendant stopped too.

For several breaths, nothing happened.

Then the king walked to the machine alone.

He didn't take the open path to its front. He went along its side, past the officer without looking at him, and stopped where the silver curved under a broken edge. He pointed. A guard brought the lamp closer.

Whatever was there, the wreck hid it from Alaric.

The king bent towards it. He reached out and rested his hand flat against the metal.

Then he stepped back.

It wasn't the measured step of a man making room for his guards. His whole body moved, so suddenly that the officer behind him reached for his arm. The king tore himself away from that hand and took another step back.

The guards closed round him. Steel came out under the lamps.

Alaric held his breath.

Nothing came out of the wreck. No light opened inside it. The long black mouth stayed empty, and the silver flank kept the same pale reflection it had held before the king touched it.

Silas's good hand tightened on the roots. Beside him, Redd's hands stayed flat on the ground.

The king stood between his guards, one arm pressed close to his side. The officer asked something. Alaric heard his voice rise without catching a single word.

No answer came.

Instead, the king pointed at the machine. The officer turned, hesitated, and took the lamp from the nearest guard.
