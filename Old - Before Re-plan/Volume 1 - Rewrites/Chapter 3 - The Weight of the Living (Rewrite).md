# The Weight of the Living

Alaric ran.

His name chased him through the wheat in Gerolt's voice.

*Take Wena and run, Alaric.*

Stalks whipped his face and folded under his arms. Their heads burst against his chest, shedding dry husks that stuck to Gerolt's blood all down his clothes. The ground gave way under every other step. His legs hadn't recovered from whatever had torn his name out of him, and the dark kept squeezing in at the edges of his sight, then easing off, then squeezing in again.

Somewhere behind him, Wena was forcing her own path through the wheat, her breath coming in frightened little bursts. Every time that sound faltered, he nearly turned around. Ahead, the forest showed only in glimpses between the stalks. He kept running at it without any idea how much ground was left.

The iron token bit into his palm.

Behind him, steel rang against steel.

Once.

Again.

A layered cry went up over the wheat, and a horse screamed with it, the two sounds twisting together until Alaric couldn't tell where one stopped and the other began.

He looked back.

Torchlight moved around the broken cabin in scattered pieces. One rider was fighting a rearing horse. Another shape crossed the ruined doorway.

Alaric searched the chaos for Gerolt —

— and something hit the cabin.

The night went orange.

Flame punched up through the roof and rolled along the beams. The last shutters blew outward. A wave of heat swept through the wheat, bending every stalk towards him, and hit his back hard enough to throw him onto his hands.

Soil filled his mouth.

He rolled onto one hip, still clutching the token. Smoke was dragging itself up through the open roof. The horses nearest the cabin scattered, and one tore loose without its rider, saddle twisted under its belly, plunging blind through the wheat. Shouts chased it. The layered voices crashed over one another, all their earlier control gone.

Wena barked.

It was a high, broken sound. She was standing a few paces behind him, staring at the fire.

Then she turned and ran back towards it.

"Wena!"

She didn't slow down.

Alaric jammed the token deep into the pocket at his hip and lunged. He caught the leather at the back of her neck, and her weight wrenched his arm straight. He went down against her side, one knee slamming into the ground, and she dragged him several paces through the dirt.

"Stop — stop —" He dug his knee in, fighting to keep hold.

Wena twisted under him, paws tearing furrows towards the cabin. The sound coming out of her throat wasn't a growl, and it was too desperate to be a whine.

Alaric wrapped both hands around her collar. Gerolt's blood had dried dark in the fur beneath her jaw.

"He's —"

He couldn't finish it.

The cabin burned behind her. Its roof sagged inward, sparks swarming up into the dark. Alaric kept looking at the doorway, waiting for Gerolt to step through it and call her. If he'd only call her, she'd stop fighting. Alaric could let go.

Only the elves were shouting.

Hooves hit the earth again.

Alaric hauled Wena's head around until she was facing him.

"He told me to take you." He tried to make her look at him instead of the fire. "He *told* me."

She fought him one more time.

"I don't know where else to go." He had to stop and breathe. When he tried again, his voice shook. "I need you to come with me. Please."

Wena stared past him, at the burning cabin.

Alaric let go of her collar.

She stood there between the two of them for one heartbeat, the fire at her back, the forest past him.

Then her ears went flat. She turned away from the cabin and ran, and Alaric ran after her before she could change her mind.

The forest looked close right up until he tried to reach it. Wheat snagged at his knees. Every breath scraped deeper than the last. Behind him, the scattered hoofbeats were pulling together into a single rhythm.

Wena veered hard to the right and vanished.

"No, this way —" He reached towards the gap she'd left, terrified she was doubling back again.

He stumbled after her and found a narrow drainage cut running under the wheat. Wena was already down in it, slinking towards the trees with her belly low to the ground.

She knew this land. He didn't. He dropped into the cut and followed her.

The earth down there was wet and uneven. His ankle rolled once in water hidden under the grass and threw him against the bank, but he forced the leg back under him and kept moving.

An arrow hissed overhead and buried itself in the wheat. A second one thunked into the bank right beside his hand, spitting dirt across his knuckles.

The stalks behind him all bent at once.

Wind tore across his back, ripping wheat out of the ground, and the force drove him flat. He caught himself on the stones, and fresh pain lit up his already bloody hands.

Wena stopped at the treeline and looked back.

"Go," Alaric gasped, waving her on. "*Go!*"

She waited until he'd forced himself upright. Then she slipped between the trees, and he staggered in after her. A branch raked his cheek. His shoes kept catching on roots he couldn't see. Ahead of him, Wena moved without a moment's hesitation. Behind them, the first horse crashed into the trees, hit wood, and screamed.

The elves shouted to each other in their layered tongue. Harness creaked. Boots hit the ground.

They were coming on foot.

Alaric caught himself against a trunk and listened. Leaves shifted behind him under careful steps. Ahead, a steady roar was swallowing all the smaller sounds of the forest. He turned towards it, trying to hear past his own ragged breathing.

Water.

Gerolt's voice came back to him through blood and fire.

*West. Across the river.*

Wena had stopped farther down the slope. She looked back at him once, then slipped between two low trees. Alaric pushed off the trunk and followed.

The ground dropped away steeply. The air turned damp, cold against the sweat on his face, and the roar grew until it filled every gap between the trees.

Then the forest ended.

The river ran below him, swollen past its banks. Moonlight broke across the black current and foamed white wherever it hit the exposed rocks.

Off to his right, a bridge crossed it, barely more than a dark line between the banks, its wooden rails silvered by the moon. Relief loosened something in his chest.

Then torchlight moved among the trees on the path leading to it.

Two flames. Then a third. Riders, heading for the bridge, and already closer to it than he was.

Behind him, a branch shifted under a foot that wasn't Wena's.

In front of him, the river was dragging whole trees along.

Wena went to the water's edge and stopped dead. Her ears pinned back. She lowered her nose towards the current, then flinched away when it slopped over the bank and touched her paws.

Alaric knew what swimming was. He could picture it: arms moving through water. He couldn't remember ever having done it.

He looked once towards the bridge. The torches were moving faster now.

No time.

He pressed a hand over his pocket. The token was still there. He shoved it deeper, suddenly terrified the river would take that from him too.

Then he stepped in.

Cold clamped around his ankles. The second step took it to his knees. The current shoved at his legs and the stones rolled under his feet, so he turned half sideways and started working towards the far bank.

Wena stayed behind on the shore, trembling.

"Come on." He reached back towards her with one hand, the other arm out for balance. "Come on, girl."

She whined and backed away from the water.

"Wena, please." His fingers stretched for her collar. She was just out of reach.

A voice called out from the trees, layered, meaningless to him. Another answered it, close enough that Alaric heard the speaker draw breath first.

He stepped deeper.

The river climbed past his thighs, filling his shoes, dragging his clothes tight around his legs. He put one foot forward and found nothing under it.

He dropped.

The current had him before he could take a breath.

Water closed over his head. The riverbed smacked his knee and vanished. His shoulder slammed into stone and sent him spinning until he had no idea which way was up. Something tore along his ribs, and he kicked off it and broke the surface.

He dragged in half a breath and half the river. The coughing folded him in two before he could get another. The current shot him between two rocks, churning white around his chest.

His hand hit wood. He grabbed it before he even knew what it was.

A root, reaching out from the western bank, stripped pale where the river had worried its bark away. Alaric caught it with both hands, and the current hauled the rest of him downstream. His shoulders burned. One hand started to slide.

He clenched harder, until the torn skin of his palm split open again.

There was mud between the roots. Another arm's length and he could get a knee against the bank. He tried to pull himself closer —

Something barked upstream.

He twisted around, and his grip slipped on the wet root.

Wena was in the river.

Only her head was above the surface. Her forelegs were striking at water that gave her nothing to stand on. The current tore her away from the far bank and carried her towards him, and then past him.

"Wena!" It scraped his throat raw. Her eyes found his.

Alaric shifted one hand along the root and reached out with the other.

His fingertips brushed the wet fur behind her ear.

Then she was gone.

She spun away past him. Her head went under. It came back up several paces downstream, lower in the water than before.

The western bank was right there under his knee.

He could climb out. Right now.

He thought of Gerolt's bloody thumb, moving once between Wena's eyes.

*Take Wena.*

Alaric let go of the root.

The river snatched him up again.

He kicked towards the place she'd gone under, blind in the spray. The current rolled him sideways. When her head broke the surface beside him, he grabbed before she could disappear again.

His fingers went through fur and hit leather.

He locked both hands around her collar.

Wena's paws clawed at his chest. One caught under his jaw, and her weight shoved his face under the water, but he didn't let go.

Something broad and dark lay across the river ahead.

A fallen tree.

Its lowest branches trailed down into the current, but the trunk was wedged tight between the bank and a cluster of rocks. Alaric twisted in the water so that Wena hit it first. Her paws found bark. She scrambled, slipped, found it again.

He hit the trunk shoulder-first. It knocked every scrap of air out of him, but he kept one hand locked on her collar and hooked the other arm over the bark.

For a few breaths, neither of them moved. The river pulled. The tree held. Alaric hung between them, coughing water against the wood.

Wena dragged herself higher.

He shoved from underneath her chest until her forelegs reached the bank. She clawed up through the mud and vanished among the roots. Then Alaric hauled himself along the trunk, one hand at a time, until his knees hit solid earth.

He crawled out of the river.

Wena had collapsed beside him. Water ran off her coat in dark streams, and her sides heaved too fast, every breath ending in a small, wet cough.

He reached for her —

Hoofbeats broke through the trees across the river.

Alaric caught her under the chest and dragged her back towards the eroded edge of the bank. The river had eaten the earth out from under a mass of exposed roots, leaving a hollow between mud and water barely big enough for the two of them.

They crawled in.

Alaric pulled Wena against him and pressed his back into the wet earth. River water rose around his hips. Roots crossed his face like the bars of a cage.

Torchlight reached the far bank and gathered around the footprints he'd left in the mud.

Wena tensed to shake herself dry. Alaric wrapped both arms around her and closed one hand gently over her muzzle.

"No," he breathed into her wet fur, holding tighter as a torch swung their way. "Not yet. Not yet."

Two of the torches turned for the bridge. The rest spread out along the river. One passed close enough to lay a trembling line of light across the water, then moved on downstream.

Hooves thudded over the bridge.

The first horse came into the western trees and walked directly over their heads. Soil pattered down beside Alaric's face. A hoof sank into the earth beside the roots, so close he could hear the leather creak and the animal breathing.

The rider said something. An answer came from farther inland.

The horse moved on. Another followed it.

Then there was only the river.

Alaric kept holding Wena long after she'd stopped struggling.

At last she pulled her muzzle free of his hand and turned towards the far bank. Past the trees, an orange glow stained the low clouds. A band of smoke crossed the moon.

Wena stared at it.

Her ears pricked at every distant crack of timber, every fading hoofbeat, every voice the river broke apart. She waited, pressed against Alaric's side, watching the place Gerolt should have come from.

He didn't come.

Alaric didn't know how long they stayed under those roots. The nearest torches disappeared. His hands had gone numb around her, and when he tried to uncurl them, pain crawled back into his fingers.

He laid one hand between Wena's eyes, along the path Gerolt's thumb had taken.

She looked at him.

"Come on, girl," he whispered. He couldn't make it sound like Gerolt. "We have to go."

He crawled out of the hollow, and Wena followed him.

They moved west under the trees with the river at their backs. His shoes squelched water with every step. His clothes clung to him, heavy and cold, and the tear along his ribs sharpened every time he tried to breathe all the way in.

He kept one hand pressed over the token in his pocket.

*Kelmend. The inn by the southern gate. Marta.*

He repeated it silently, over and over, afraid of losing even one piece of what Gerolt had given him. Wena stayed close enough that her wet coat brushed his leg.

Then she stopped.

He nearly walked into her. Her head had dropped low, and a growl was moving through her chest, so quiet he felt it before he heard it.

She was staring into the trees ahead, away from the river. Alaric followed her gaze and saw nothing.

Then a man shifted beneath an alder.

He'd been leaning against the trunk with one boot braced on the bark behind him, so still that his weather-dark cloak had turned into another piece of shadow. Younger than Gerolt, older than Alaric, with dark stubble roughening his jaw. One hand rested close to the short blade at his hip. Not touching it. Close.

The man looked Wena over. Then Alaric. His eyes paused on the hard shape under the wet cloth at Alaric's hip before sliding past him to the lights still searching the river.

Alaric waited for him to offer help.

The hand stayed beside the blade.

"So," the stranger said over the roar of the river. "You're the one they're hunting."

---

There was no sound.

Just for an instant, the dark had been white.

Then it wasn't.

Seralune woke up certain that something had broken.

For a while, she couldn't move. Stone pressed against her cheek. The cold had soaked into that whole side of her face and into the fingers curled underneath it. Her other arm was somewhere behind her, trapped under her own weight and a tangle of hair.

She opened her eyes.

The dark didn't change at all.

She held her breath and listened for anyone nearby. When she couldn't hold it any longer, the sound of her own breathing filled the room again, and it was the only sound there was.

She dragged her arm free and pushed up onto one elbow. The chamber rolled around her, and her stomach lurched with it. She put her head back down until the stone stopped tilting under her palm.

"Brother?"

Her voice went out into the dark and came back in pieces. She waited, one hand braced against the floor to stop herself swaying.

He didn't answer.

She knew her name. That much was easy.

She knew the south garden by the smell its soil kept after rain. She knew which window held the evening sun the longest. She knew the tired edge her mother's voice got after a day of court, and the two quick knocks her brother always gave before walking in without waiting — as if warning her first made it polite.

All of that came to her without any effort at all. She tried to picture her mother coming through the door right now, annoyed that someone had left her daughter alone in the dark.

"Mother?" Louder this time. The chamber handed the word back to her, empty.

Seralune tried to stand. Her knees folded before she was halfway up, and both palms smacked down onto the stone. Pain shot up her wrists: sharp, ordinary, real. She tried again before it had even faded, frightened now by how weak she was.

Her breath started to come short. The dark seemed to press in closer every time she tried to fill her lungs. She groped for the edge of whatever she was lying on and found only more stone.

*Count.*

Her brother's voice, as clear as if he were kneeling right next to her.

*Slowly. One breath first.*

She tried. The first breath caught halfway in, and she had to start over.

One.

She held the next one, then let it out as slowly as she could.

Two.

She kept her palms flat on the stone and counted until the shaking eased.

The dark was still there. But it had stopped creeping closer.

She reached inward, and her mana answered at once, the way it always did — no distance, no delay. There was so much of it she had never once found where it ended. It filled the hollows under her ribs, pressed behind her eyes, gathered beneath her skin, all of it waiting for a direction she had never, ever been able to give it.

She asked it for the smallest thing she could think of.

*Light.*

The power rose. She held one hand in front of her face and strained to see even its outline.

Not a spark.

She'd failed at this a thousand times, under the patient eyes of physicians and tutors. It had never hurt like this. Back then, she could at least walk out of the lesson and find the sun waiting for her beyond a window.

Here, there was only the dark, and all that useless abundance filling her up with nowhere to go.

She let her hand drop. Sitting there waiting for something she'd never been able to do wasn't going to open any doors. She let the mana settle and started searching the floor instead.

There were lines cut into the stone around her, shallow grooves. She followed one with her fingertips until it met something smooth set into it. Metal, maybe, though it wasn't as cold as metal should be. The strip had split cleanly near the middle, and its two ends curled away from each other like something that had snapped under strain.

There were more breaks beyond it.

She crawled along the pattern, tracing curves she couldn't see. Some ran on unbroken under her fingers. Others ended in cracks, all of them radiating out from the place where she'd been lying. A loose fragment shifted under her palm and dropped against the floor.

**Tink.**

She snatched her hand back. That tiny sound carried much farther than it should have, and left her crouched there listening to the dark again.

This wasn't her room. It wasn't any room in the palace she'd ever seen.

She tried to remember what had happened before she woke.

Her father's council bell, ringing from the eastern tower. Her mother, complaining that the kitchens had ruined the pears again. Her brother, leaning in her doorway, promising he'd be back before evening.

Every one of those memories was perfectly clear. It was their *order* that was wrong. She kept trying to put herself somewhere after Thaer left — doing something ordinary, anything, that might lead to whatever had happened — and every single time, she ended up back here, with the stone under her hands.

Her fingers found the edge of the carved circle. She lowered one foot off it. The floor was waiting a short drop below.

Her legs shook when she put her weight on them. This time, they held.

She stood with both arms stretched out into the dark and shuffled forward until her fingers struck a wall.

The stone was so smooth she couldn't find a single joint in it. She kept one hand flat against it and followed it.

Seven careful steps to a corner.

Six more to another.

The chamber was smaller than its echoes had made it sound. No curtains. No shelves. No furniture. No window. Nothing broke the wall at all until her fingers slid into a narrow seam running straight up and down.

"Hello?" She put her face close to the seam and listened for footsteps. There were always people outside her door. Always. Someone should have heard her by now.

"Can anyone hear me?" She straightened up, even though her knees were trembling. "I need someone to open this door."

Nothing came back from the other side.

She felt around the seam for a latch and found only wall. She shoved against it with one shoulder. Then both palms.

It didn't move.

"*Brother!*"

Her voice cracked on the second syllable.

She hit the stone once, flat-handed.

"I'm here," she said, and it came out much quieter than she'd meant it to. She pressed her ear to the seam. "I'm here, Thaer."

After that, time stopped having any shape.

She walked the chamber again. Counted the corners. Counted her steps. Counted her breaths, until the numbers started repeating and stopped meaning anything at all. Twice she went back to the broken circle and sat down beside it, and twice she got up again when sitting still became unbearable.

She called out until her throat felt scraped hollow.

Then, at last, a faint vibration, under her hand, in the wall.

She froze.

It came again. Closer. And behind it, hurried footsteps.

Someone was running towards her.

"Here!" She pounded on the seam, not caring anymore that it hurt. "I'm in here! *I'm in here!*"

The footsteps didn't slow down.

They stopped right on the other side of the wall.

One breath. Nothing.

Then something hit the other side.

**KRRRACK.**

The whole chamber shook. Dust sifted down over her face. A thin line of light split the seam from the top towards the floor, and she stumbled back from it.

A second blow. The door tore out of its frame and smashed into the chamber wall hard enough to shatter a corner of it, and white light flooded through the opening.

Seralune cried out and threw her arms over her eyes.

After all that dark, the light had *weight*. It forced tears out under her eyelids and glowed red through the fingers she held over her face.

Someone was standing in it.

Tall. Unmoving. One arm hanging at his side, a lowered blade still in his hand.

She looked at him through her fingers. She couldn't make out his face yet, but the light caught the narrow point of one ear through his hair, and the line of his shoulder, and she knew that shoulder. She knew him before her eyes could even stand to look any higher.

"Thaer."

It came out of her with all the breath she'd been holding.

She dropped her hands and ran to him, her knees nearly giving out from the sheer relief of it. The floor shifted under her weak legs and she stumbled through the last step and caught herself against his chest, both arms wrapping around him.

The dark plates of his armour were cold against her cheek. Underneath them, his breathing was going far too fast.

But he was here.

Whatever had happened, whatever this place was, she wasn't alone anymore.

She pressed her face against him and waited for his arms to close around her.

They didn't.

She tipped her face up towards him.

He wasn't looking at her.

His arms stayed down at his sides. All the colour had gone out of his face. His eyes were fixed past her — on the broken circle, and the hollow at its centre — and his whole body had gone rigid inside her embrace, as if whatever he was seeing had burned away every sense except sight.

"Thaer?"

He didn't answer. He didn't even blink.

And for the first time in her whole life, Seralune saw fear in her brother's eyes.
