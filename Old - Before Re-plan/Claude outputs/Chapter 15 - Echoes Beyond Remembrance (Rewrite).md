# Echoes Beyond Remembrance

The chain lifted off the earth.

Alaric watched the last loose link straighten beside the broken leg. Beyond the machine, soldiers called to each other. Wood creaked under the weight they'd started to raise.

The pale reflection of his bandage trembled on the orb.

"Take your hand away," Silas whispered.

Alaric drew it back as a thin trickle of soil fell from the armour above the slot. Silas slid the sword back into its sheath and leaned in to the bent fastening. The outer plate had forced one edge of it inwards, leaving a narrow crack across the metal that held the enclosure shut.

"I might be able to break it here." He touched the crack with his left hand. "Stay behind me while I try."

"With fire?"

Silas nodded.

Alaric looked through the slot. The clear curve inside was still whole.

"Won't that break the orb too?"

"It might. I'll keep the flame outside the box. There's still a risk."

A soldier shouted for the others to pull again. The machine shifted, opening a narrow gap under its fallen leg. Alaric felt it through his knee. A pebble dropped from the broken edge and struck his sleeve.

Silas looked at him. "We either try now or we leave it."

Alaric thought of the hands waiting on the other side, and the timbers being pushed under the wreck. Once it rose, anyone standing past it would see them.

He moved back.

"Try."

Silas turned his left palm towards the damaged fastening, his burned right hand tucked against his chest. A small flame appeared above his open fingers.

It made the dark under the machine seem deeper. Alaric could see the damp hair by Silas's scar, and that he was holding his breath.

Silas angled his palm across the fastening's outer face. Alaric ducked behind his shoulder as the flame struck out in one short, hard burst.

Heat washed across his cheek.

**KRAK.**

The crack of it filled the space under the wreck. A bright fragment struck the outer armour and fell into the dirt beside them. The bent fastening tore loose.

Silas snatched his hand back as the freed edge sprang outwards.

"Stop pulling!" someone shouted. "Hold it there!"

The chain shuddered.

"Get the timber under it!"

The work lamp swung away from the break in the armour. Boots ran towards the soldiers on the chain.

For an instant, flame ran along the gap Silas had made. Most of it stayed outside the enclosure. One thin tongue of it slipped through and disappeared.

Orange light answered from inside.

Silas's flame had gone out, but a thread of orange was still moving in the orb's dark centre. Its light barely reached past the clear shell.

Alaric started to rise. Silas caught his sleeve. "Mind that edge."

The torn metal held a dull red line. Alaric kept his hands away from it and reached through the wider gap above. His left palm found the underside of the orb. He brought his right hand round the nearer curve, keeping his elbow close so his shoulder could bear it.

The shell was warm through the linen. Not hot enough to hurt. He lifted. Something underneath held, then let go with a small scrape.

The orb rose between his hands. He eased it past the broken edge, afraid of touching the metal, afraid of dropping it, afraid of finding a crack on the side he still hadn't seen.

"Come on," Silas whispered. "We've got to move."

Alaric drew it against his coat.

It was out.

He had it.

The orange thread opened inside the black centre.

His heartbeat struck the backs of his eyes.

**THOOM.**

Pain drove through his skull.

He tried to say Silas's name. His arms closed round the orb instead. He felt its curve press under his ribs, the hard ground come up to meet his knee, fingers catching at the back of his coat —

Someone took his hand.

---

Warm fingers slid between his.

A young woman stood in a doorway, laughing as she drew him over the threshold.

"You're late again."

"I know," he said, trying not to smile.

She gave his hand another tug. He laughed and let her pull him closer.

The door closed behind him. Lamplight lay across the edge of a bed. A curtain lifted beside the window, and a loose latch tapped against the wood.

Her hands were round his left hand now, lifting it between them.

There was no cloth round his fingers.

She brought his palm to her cheek.

A strand of black hair lay across his thumb. He moved it aside without thinking, already knowing how she would turn her face into his hand.

She did.

Green eyes met his. She was close enough for him to see the fine hair beside the pointed tip of her ear.

Her thumb moved against the inside of his wrist, and he let his hand settle more fully against her cheek. She smiled at him.

The latch tapped again.

She began to speak. Her fingers tightened a little round his wrist —

---

Dirt got into his mouth.

His heels were dragging through broken stalks. The coat pulled tight under his left shoulder, then went slack as the movement stopped.

"Alaric." Silas's voice, behind him. "Alaric. Look at me."

The room was gone. The warmth was still in his hand.

He tried to breathe. Nothing went in deep enough.

His arms had folded round something hard. The orb lay against his chest, held between his forearms and the bunched front of his coat. One hand had caught in a fold. His fingers wouldn't open.

He turned his head. Silas was crouched behind him, his good hand fisted in the coat under Alaric's uninjured shoulder. From beyond the wreck came the scrape of timber and someone shouting not to let the leg drop. The light was still on that side, where the soldiers were.

Silas pulled again. Pain dragged through Alaric's ribs, and the noise he made stopped Silas at once.

"Can you breathe?"

Alaric tried. His chest rose against the orb, but the breath caught before he could answer.

Silas gathered more of the coat under his back. "All right. I've got you."

Alaric's heels scraped off the stalks onto the hard ruts of the path. Silas hauled him across while the voices beyond the wreck shouted for another timber.

A lamp swung round the machine's broad foot.

Silas dragged him behind the folded edge of the broken plate. Alaric's heels cleared the path as the light reached the ground they'd crossed.

Silas lowered him into the hollow and turned to face the light. Alaric couldn't see who was carrying it. He could see Silas watching, jaw set, chest rising too fast under his coat.

The lamp went on towards the damaged machine. Silas turned back at once.

"Can you hear me?"

Alaric tried to nod. Silas's left hand came against his jaw and held him still. Dirt marked the fingers. One of them was trembling against his skin.

A little air got in and caught. His body tried to pull more before he could let the first out.

"Fuck." Silas glanced at the edge of the plate, then back at him. "Come on, Alaric. You're here. You're here with me. One breath."

Alaric stared at him.

He knew this face. The scar on the cheek. The dirt in the stubble. He'd followed this man down the bank. Redd was waiting at the top of it with Wena.

The next breath went in thin and hurt all the way down.

Silas's fingers loosened. "That's it. Again."

Alaric eased his grip on the orb as he breathed, letting the shell come away from the ache under his ribs.

Something wet reached his chin.

He thought he'd bitten his mouth until another drop fell from beside his nose. Tears were running across his cheeks and collecting against Silas's fingers.

He could still see her.

He shut his eyes, afraid that trying to hold her face would break it. But he could still see the hair caught across his thumb, her fingers round his wrist.

He tried to hear what she'd been about to say. Only her laughter came back.

A sharp order sounded beyond the plate. Other voices answered it, their syllables overlapping in the way he'd learned to be afraid of.

Alaric opened his eyes.

Silas had leaned back to look past the broken edge. Across the path, soldiers were gathering round the machine. One held a lamp over the gap while another pointed at the torn fastening.

Someone called for a guard. Silas turned back.

"Did I burn you?"

Alaric shook his head.

"Then what happened?"

"I remembered —"

His voice gave out. He swallowed dirt and tried again.

"Someone."

Silas went very still.

"From before?"

Alaric nodded.

Silas looked at the orb, then at his face. He seemed about to ask something else. A pair of boots crossed the path, and the question never came.

"We'll talk once we're clear," he whispered. "Can you stand if I help you?"

Alaric looked at the slope.

"I can try."

Silas held out his left forearm. Alaric tucked the orb into his coat with his right arm and caught the sleeve with his left hand. His shoulder ached under the weight of the shell. He kept it close as Silas got him up.

The bad ankle nearly folded. Silas braced him until he could shift onto the other foot, then steered him along the plate's shadow into the dip below the bank. Alaric limped beside him, bent, unable to straighten without pulling at his ribs.

Across the path, a soldier bent to look into the broken enclosure.

Behind them, someone shouted. Several voices answered at once.

Silas didn't look back.

---

By the time they reached the roots under the bank, Alaric could no longer lift the bad foot properly.

Silas stopped beneath the thorn and called Redd's name once, softly. A branch moved above them, and Redd appeared on the upper slope. His eyes went over the orb under Alaric's arm and straight to his face.

"What happened?"

"Help me get him up."

Redd braced against a root and reached down with his right arm. Alaric caught it with his left and got his good knee onto the root. Silas steadied him from below while Redd pulled him into the hollow. Alaric kept the orb against his coat the whole way.

Wena was up at once, but the rope stopped her short. A thin whine got out of her, and Alaric lowered his free hand to her nose.

"I'm here."

He meant to say more. Her tongue went over the cloth round his fingers, and the rest stuck in his throat.

Redd settled him against the roots and looked at Silas.

"He went down when he picked it up," Silas said. "Get the sack. We need to get him out of here."

Redd reached for the provisions. Wena pushed her head under Alaric's left hand, and he let his fingers sink into her fur and kept them there while she nosed at his sleeve.

Redd pulled Alaric's black clothes out of the sack and spread their covering across his knees.

"Let me wrap that before we move."

Alaric looked down. A faint orange line was still there inside the core. Its light reached the pale bandage underneath and stopped.

"Wait."

Redd's hands stayed where they were.

"It happened when I touched it," Alaric said. "I don't know if —"

"I don't know what it'll do to me either." Redd glanced at the bandages round Alaric's hands, then pulled the cloth over his own. "Just put it down on this for now. We can't walk about with that light showing."

Alaric lowered the orb onto the cloth over Redd's knees, keeping his hands against the shell.

"Can you turn it?" he whispered. "I couldn't see the back."

He loosened his hold so Redd could turn it through the cloth. The side that had faced the machine was smooth and unbroken.

"I can't see any cracks," Redd said.

Alaric made his fingers let go. Redd wrapped the orb until no light showed, packed the black clothes back into the sack, and bedded the orb among them, well away from the water bottle.

Silas untied Wena's rope from the alder while Redd pulled the straps tight. The dog crossed the last of the space between them and pressed against Alaric's legs.

Redd put the ash stick into Alaric's left hand. "Ready to try?"

Alaric looked past him, at the dark trees behind the hollow.

"No."

It surprised him.

Redd moved closer. "We'll go slowly."

He shouldered the sack and steadied Alaric's left arm as he got up on the stick. Silas took Wena ahead, leading them down the fold in the ground.

At the first turn, a shout reached them from the field. Alaric couldn't make out the words. By the next, he could only hear the river.

---

Alaric remembered little of the walk downstream except Redd's hand under his elbow, and the stops when his foot wouldn't go any further.

At the old crossing, Silas took Wena over the first channel. Redd stayed behind Alaric until they reached the willows, then sat him down on a fallen trunk where Silas could keep an eye on the stones.

Alaric leaned the stick against his knee and took the water bottle. He drank without spilling any. His hands did what he told them.

Silas passed Wena's rope to Redd and crouched beside the trunk.

"How's your head?"

"Still hurting. Not like before."

Silas studied him in the moonlight coming through the willows, then sat back on his heels. Redd took the bottle when Alaric had finished and went to watch the far bank. Wena lay down by his feet.

Alaric looked at Silas's right hand. The edge of the dressing had gone dark where he'd been holding it against his coat.

"Did I hurt your hand?"

"No."

"You were pulling me."

"I used the other one."

He said it without any impatience at all. That made Alaric look up. Silas rubbed his left palm over his mouth before he spoke again.

"I thought you were dying."

He looked away at the crossing. "You weren't answering me. I got you out, and I still couldn't tell if you were breathing."

"I'm sorry."

Silas's eyes came back to him.

"I'm not asking you to apologise."

Alaric looked down at the stick between his hands.

"If it starts again," Silas said, quieter, "can you warn me?"

"I don't know if I'd have time. There was light inside it. Then my head hurt, and I wasn't there anymore."

Silas waited.

Alaric had expected the room to vanish the way the other fragments had, leaving only the ache and the certainty that he'd lost something. But he could still see the doorway.

"I was in a room. There was a woman with me."

"Someone you knew?"

"I knew her *there*." He tried to find the difference. "I wasn't trying to remember her. I just knew her. The way I know you're Silas when I look at you."

Silas nodded slowly.

"But now I don't know her name."

He looked at the water beyond the willow branches.

She'd been an elf. He could remember the pointed ear under her hair as clearly as the press of her cheek against his palm.

He rubbed his thumb over the bandage on his palm. It caught on a small hard ridge of dried blood where the token had cut him.

"She'd been waiting for me." His voice had started to shake again. "She took my hand before I'd even said anything."

Silas looked at the thumb moving over the bandage. Then he sat down beside him on the trunk.

The wind moved through the willows. A thin branch tapped against another above his head.

Alaric looked up before he could stop himself.

There was no window. Only the dark leaves, and Redd standing past them with Wena's rope loose in one hand.

He lowered his eyes.

"I could *feel* her."

"Do you need a moment?"

Alaric nodded and bent his head. Her laughter came back to him with his own underneath it. He could remember how easily he'd laughed in that room. When he tried to speak, the next breath broke.

For a while he let the river cover it. Silas stayed beside him.

When he could, he wiped his face on the sleeve of his coat. It smelled of dirt and smoke. He lowered it before the smell could turn into the fire on the farm.

"What if that's all I get?"

Silas leaned forward, his left forearm on one knee.

"I don't know. I can't tell you there'll be more. But you haven't lost what you saw tonight."

Alaric looked at the sack. Silas followed his eyes.

"I wasn't going to touch it again," Alaric said.

"Good." It came out faster than anything else Silas had said. "Keep it wrapped. Let someone who understands these things look at it before either of us touches it again."

"Helmi?"

"That's who Darcy told us to find."

"Do you think he could tell me who she is?"

"I don't know. Darcy said he could read the core. We'll have to ask him the rest."

Alaric nodded.

Silas stood. Redd handed him back Wena's rope and came over to the trunk.

"Think you can manage the other side?"

Alaric took up the stick. "If we go slowly."

Redd offered his arm, and they followed Silas towards the stones.

---

Past the second channel, Silas led Wena up the far bank. Redd took the stick and helped Alaric up the root shelf, and he reached the grass breathing hard but upright. Redd gave the stick back.

They kept to the low ground, well away from the road they'd watched on the way out. No wagons passed. The quiet made Alaric listen for them anyway.

Kelmend's lights showed through a break in the trees.

There were more of them than he remembered.

Silas stopped.

Across the low ground, lanterns were moving between the empty houses of the old quarter. Two riders waited where the narrow path met the row. Past them, a pale rectangle opened in a wall as someone kicked a door in and carried a light inside.

Redd came up beside Silas. "That's the path to mine."

"I know."

"They weren't there when we left."

A rider turned his horse towards the houses. Pale hair caught the nearest lantern before he passed out of its light.

"They're searching this side now," Silas said.

Alaric couldn't tell Redd's cottage from the others at that distance. But he remembered the wooden catch on its door, and Redd testing it once before they left.

The sack shifted on Redd's shoulders. His fingers hooked under the strap and stayed there.

"They'll find the armour."

Silas didn't answer.

They found it.

A shout went up from the far end of the row. Lanterns converged. Someone came out of a doorway near the end with something held high over his head, and the light caught it. A dented breastplate, turning slowly in his hands so the others could see.

More voices, overlapping. One of the riders called an order.

Someone threw a lantern through the window.

For a moment there was only a small light moving inside the room, as if someone were walking about in there with a candle. Then the light spread. It found the bed under the side window. It found the shelves. It climbed the inside of the shutter that didn't match the other one, and came out through the gaps between the boards.

The roof went last. Two colours of tile, and the flames came up through both.

Nobody spoke.

Alaric watched the fire eat the cottage from the inside, and thought of the step inside the door that Redd kept meaning to fix. The chair with the leather under its short leg. The cup with the broken handle Redd had kept for himself. The bed Alaric had slept in for two days while Redd sat on the chest and held water to his mouth.

Redd's hand had moved to the sword at his hip. Not to draw it. He only held the mended mouth of the scabbard, the way he had before they left, as if checking it was still there.

"I'd only just got the roof right," he said.

His voice was quite steady. That was the worst part.

"Redd," Alaric said. "I'm sorry."

"It's all right." It plainly wasn't. Redd didn't look away from the fire. "It was a house. I've got the sword."

The riders sat their horses and watched it burn. Nobody went to put it out. Further along the row, a second door gave way, and another lantern went in.

Silas watched them for a long moment. Then he turned his head towards the glow above Kelmend's wall, where the town's streets were.

"What about Marta?" Alaric asked.

For several breaths, Silas said nothing at all.

"That was fort armour," he said at last. "They'll want to know who carried it out of the fort, and who hid him after." He was still facing Kelmend. "We can't go back to her inn. Not with them like this."

"Does she know we're out here?"

"She knows we wanted to reach the farm. I'll get word to her when I can."

He stayed facing the town after he'd stopped speaking. Alaric had never seen him stand still for so long without watching something that could hurt them.

Redd glanced at him, then back at the burning row. Neither of them moved towards it.

A torch rose above a low wall further along. Silas drew Wena back under the trees.

"We need to go."

"Which way?" Alaric asked.

"There's a track past the fields. It joins the inland road without taking us through those houses."

"Towards Favale?"

"Yes. If we keep to it."

Alaric looked at the sack on Redd's back.

The orb was inside, unbroken as far as they could tell. Darcy had asked him to find it and carry it to her father. He could still see the two circles on her paper.

He could see a face now, too.

"Will you take me there?" he asked Silas.

Silas looked at him. "All the way to Helmi?"

"If you can."

Silas's hand rested round Wena's rope. He let it slide a little as she moved towards Alaric.

"We'll need more food, and somewhere you can rest properly. And you tell me when that foot won't carry you any further."

"I will." Alaric met his eyes. "I don't think I could make it on my own."

Silas nodded. "All right. I'll take you."

Redd shifted the sack higher. "You'll have to put up with me as well."

He was trying to smile. His eyes went once more to the fire.

"I'd like you to come," Alaric said.

Redd looked at him.

"Good. I'd rather hear what Darcy's father makes of that thing than sit about waiting for you to come back and tell me." His fingers let go of the strap. "Besides. I've nowhere else to sleep now."

It was almost a joke. Alaric managed something like a smile for it anyway. Redd answered it, tiredly, and turned his back on the old quarter.

Silas checked the ground past the trees. "Come on. We'll find somewhere to stop before morning."

He led them away from the lights.

Redd took Wena's rope so Silas could go ahead and watch the track. The dog kept close to Alaric, matching the short distance between his steps. When he stopped to settle the stick, she stopped too.

At the edge of the last field, Alaric looked back.

He couldn't see the old houses anymore. Only the glow of them, low and orange, where Redd's roof had been. Kelmend was a paler glow above the clouds. And beyond the river, smoke still lay across the place where Gerolt had found him.

He didn't know where the woman was, or how far he'd have to go to find her. But when he thought of the doorway, her face was still there.

Redd had stopped to tighten a strap. Alaric waited by the hedge. Past it, the track dipped into ruts he couldn't cross safely on the stick alone.

"Redd?"

Redd looked up.

"Could you give me a hand?"

"Of course."

Redd came and settled his free hand under Alaric's left elbow. Wena waited while they found their footing. Ahead, Silas held a branch aside.

Alaric leaned on Redd's arm and took the next step.
