# Chapter 17 — Story Design

_Status: story architecture approved through question-and-answer design on 2 September 2026. Title remains open. This chapter is inserted between **Memories Intertwined** and **Into the Great Expanse**._

---

## Chapter Identity

### Placement

This is a full numbered chapter, not **Chapter 18.5**, a bonus chapter or an optional interlude. Its events are essential to the main narrative.

The chapter takes place on the third night after Seralune escaped through Waluna and continues until shortly after dawn. It occupies the same general period as the closing portion of Chapter 16. The king returns from the Silent Field during these hours. Chapter 18 then returns to Alaric and compresses the following week of travel towards the Great Expanse.

The previous sequence has been rearranged accordingly:

- **Chapter 15:** Alaric's fragmented memory.
- **Chapter 16:** Seralune experiences the connected fragment.
- **Chapter 17:** Thaeroval questions Cyrandor, receives the king's command and reveals the full scale of his sword.
- **Chapter 18:** formerly Chapter 17, Alaric's journey into the Great Expanse.
- **Chapter 19:** formerly Chapter 18, Alaric discovers Foramen and Liluth arrives.
- **Chapter 20:** returns immediately to the confrontation at Foramen.

### Narrative distance

The chapter stays beside **Thaeroval** throughout but does not enter his private thoughts. Use externally focused third-person narration:

- reveal him through speech, decisions, omissions and treatment of other people;
- describe only outwardly observable behaviour and the physical world around him;
- do not state what he feels, believes, remembers or intends as direct internal fact;
- do not enter Cyrandor, Leorin, the king, soldiers or wardwrights internally;
- do not head-hop;
- do not compensate for the lack of interior narration with explanatory dialogue.

Readers should understand Thaeroval because his actions contradict and expose one another. His refusal to answer the order to kill Seralune, his willingness to reseal her, his protection of Cyrandor and his concealment of the ward evidence must carry the character work.

### Primary purpose

Chapter 17 must accomplish seven connected tasks:

1. establish Thaeroval as Natharul's **First Blade** and a major tragic antagonist;
2. show that he genuinely loves Seralune while believing love gives him the right to confine her;
3. allow Cyrandor's escape effort to produce a concrete consequence and investigative lead;
4. return the king from the Silent Field with partial but terrifying knowledge of the ancient battlefield;
5. give Thaeroval a direct command to recover and reseal Seralune;
6. reveal the overwhelming scale and permanent cost of Thaeroval's conceptual severance;
7. begin an intelligent pursuit towards Waluna and Mydea.

This is not a conventional duel. Hundreds die before Thaeroval arrives; the creature dies in one strike. The chapter's tension comes from what Thaeroval chooses to do, what his power costs and what kind of pursuer leaves Natharul afterwards.

### Beginning state

- Cyrandor has survived the loss of his arm and remains inside a guarded infirmary.
- Thaeroval has ordered that no one question or move him before he wakes.
- Thaeroval followed Seralune's trail through the ruptured aqueduct in Chapter 11 and lost it at Waluna's harbour.
- He strongly suspects she escaped aboard a ship but does not know which vessel carried her.
- Natharul has not identified Seralune's route beyond Waluna.
- Thaeroval already suspected that the queen opposed the original sealing, but he does not know the complete structure of her inherited order.
- The king remains away from Natharul, returning aboard the enormous royal warship after inspecting the Silent Field.
- Nereth's eruption destroyed much of the visible corrupted growth but awakened the intelligent source beneath it.

### Ending state

- Cyrandor has confirmed that the queen wanted Seralune beyond Natharul's control and that the intended trail begins somewhere in Mydea.
- Thaeroval has discovered that Seralune carries the queen's lily signet.
- That signet gives him a credible route through Waluna's witnesses and harbour records towards the correct vessel.
- The king has connected Seralune's awakening to the unexplained arrival of armies from the Great Remaking.
- Thaeroval has accepted the duty to find and reseal Seralune.
- He has not promised to kill her if she remembers or cannot be contained.
- The source of the aqueduct corruption is dead, together with every root and corrupted creature sustained through it.
- Thaeroval has cut a permanent wound through Natharul's southern mountain, coast and seabed.
- His full severance has permanently diminished his instinctive compassion for strangers.
- The king has witnessed enough of his son's power to fear the weapon he commands.
- Thaeroval has concealed evidence that Seralune's lower seal fed the corruption.
- He leaves alone for Waluna aboard a small royal aircraft, carrying a restraint derived from the original sealing design.

### Emotional movement

Controlled regret → an accusation Thaeroval cannot answer → frightening confirmation from the ancient battlefield → duty accepted over Seralune's freedom → public salvation through impossible power → a private human loss no one names → pursuit beginning beneath a permanent scar.

### Target length

Approximately **5,500–6,500 words**.

Do not allow the chapter to expand to 7,500–9,000 words simply because it contains several major events. The creature dies in one strike. Keep the interrogation and royal confrontation substantial but responsive, and enter the massacre after it has already begun.

## Causal Foundation

### What caused this chapter

- Seralune escaped because Thaeroval and the court intended to confine and reseal her.
- Cyrandor obeyed an inherited order established by the queen and lost an arm helping her reach the lower passages.
- Nereth's desperate fire eruption ruptured the aqueduct and awakened the corruption's hidden source.
- The king recognised the Silent Field's dead, armour and machines as belonging to the Great Remaking.
- Seralune's awakening and the battlefield's appearance occurred too closely for the king to dismiss the connection.

### What becomes possible

- Thaeroval can trace the queen's signet from Waluna to a specific departing vessel.
- Natharul can begin a second, more dangerous pursuit distinct from Liluth's hunt for Alaric.
- Readers can understand why the king fears Seralune without receiving the full Ancient Sin.
- Thaeroval's future behaviour can reflect a specific permanent emotional loss.
- Leorin can begin suspecting that Thaeroval concealed evidence from the lower wards.

### What becomes impossible

- Thaeroval can no longer treat Seralune's departure as confusion that will resolve by itself.
- The court can no longer assume the corruption beneath the palace was a contained or local problem.
- Thaeroval cannot recover the part of his compassion spent through the full cut.
- The king can no longer regard his greatest weapon without also fearing him.
- Seralune's escape can no longer remain solely a family matter; it has become a royal military objective.

## Thaeroval — Chapter-Specific Presentation

### Identity and appearance

- **Full name:** Thaeroval.
- **Royal position:** prince of Natharul and Seralune's elder brother.
- **Formal military title:** **First Blade of Natharul**.
- **Affinity:** Dark, one of the Veiled Four.
- **Apparent age:** approximately twenty-five to thirty.
- **Actual age:** more than one thousand years.
- He has not visibly aged since Seralune last saw him.
- He is Natharul's strongest living warrior and the crown's most destructive instrument.

### Voice and behaviour

Thaeroval is quiet, precise and controlled. He does not perform menace, shout to establish authority or fill silence with explanations. He is warm only with Seralune. With everyone else, care appears chiefly as protection, orders and duty.

He still considers himself a good man and a hero. Do not state that belief directly. Show it through his assumption that preventing disaster justifies deciding what happens to Seralune.

His defining flaw remains intact:

- he will not draw his full blade against someone too weak to survive it;
- he considers restraint, confinement and removal of choice to be merciful alternatives;
- his love for Seralune overrides her wishes;
- he believes she will forgive him once she understands what he prevented;
- duty remains stronger than doubt.

### The cost already paid

Full conceptual severance has been eroding Thaeroval's emotional humanity throughout the millennium. This is why he can remain courteous, dutiful and protective while connecting warmly only to Seralune.

The chapter must not explain this mechanism. It is author-only and should be inferred from contrast.

## Scene One — Cyrandor Wakes

### Immediate want

Thaeroval needs to learn where Seralune went and who prepared her escape. Cyrandor wants to protect the queen's plan and prevent Thaeroval from reaching Seralune.

### Opening and staging

- Open at night inside the guarded infirmary.
- Do not open with Thaeroval walking through corridors or reflecting upon Chapter 11.
- Cyrandor is conscious but extremely weak.
- The stump below his elbow remains properly treated and bound.
- Guards remain outside the room.
- Leorin and a physician may be present for the first moments. Thaeroval confirms Cyrandor can survive questioning, then dismisses them.
- Leorin obeys but notices that Thaeroval wants no official witness.
- Thaeroval remains alone with Cyrandor.

### The address

Cyrandor calls him **Prince Thaeroval**. The title is formally correct and personally cold. He does not call him Thaer, First Blade or use the warmth of a household servant addressing someone he once knew.

Thaeroval calls Cyrandor a traitor. The accusation should arise from the immediate betrayal of the palace and crown, not become a speech about loyalty.

### The apology and its limitation

Thaeroval apologises aloud for taking Cyrandor's arm. The apology is real.

During the exchange, he reveals that he particularly regrets Seralune witnessing it. Cyrandor recognises the imbalance: Thaeroval is more troubled by what the violence placed between him and his sister than by what Cyrandor suffered.

Do not have Cyrandor announce that lesson. Let his next answer become colder or allow him to refuse reassurance.

### Information and half-truths

Cyrandor reveals only what pressure and visible evidence force him to admit:

- the queen established the inherited order;
- she wanted her daughter carried beyond Natharul's control;
- the order endured until Seralune awakened;
- its available trail begins in Mydea;
- Cyrandor does not know the queen's present location.

He does not know Alaric. No one remembers Alaric, and the interrogation must not hint that Cyrandor retained protected knowledge of him.

Cyrandor does not name Favale, Neldor, Nyana or any destination beyond Mydea.

### The signet clue

Thaeroval notices that an old chain associated with the queen no longer carries its pendant. He recognises the absence and asks what Cyrandor removed from it.

Cyrandor attempts a half-truth. Under continued questioning, he confirms that Seralune carries the queen's lily signet. He does not deliberately provide the ship or route.

This is the scene's necessary practical gain. The signet was shown in Waluna and can later distinguish one ship from every other vessel that departed after the eruption.

### The personal wound

Thaeroval maintains that Seralune fled before he could explain why renewed containment was necessary. He believes she would have understood.

Cyrandor does not argue abstractly about freedom. He answers with two events he witnessed:

> “When she woke, she ran into your arms. The next time she saw you, she ran the other way.”

Preserve the meaning. Refine the exact sentence only if the surrounding exchange demands more natural rhythm.

Thaeroval receives no internal reaction. His behaviour after the line must carry its effect.

### Scene turn

Thaeroval entered needing a destination. He leaves with Mydea and the queen's signet as a credible trail, but also with proof that his sister fled him knowingly rather than through simple confusion.

## Scene Two — The King Returns

### Transition

The royal warship reaches Natharul near dawn. Its arrival is experienced physically:

- light disappears from palace windows beneath its hull;
- glass, hanging metal or fitted doors tremble beneath its engines;
- attendants and guards change direction before a complete order reaches them;
- the king summons the First Blade before fully disembarking.

Do not repeat the warship's complete visual introduction from Chapter 13. Thaeroval and Natharul know this vessel. Emphasise its effect upon the palace and the urgency of its return.

### Staging

The king meets Thaeroval aboard the warship or in a royal chamber connected to its landing structure. Battlefield ash and traces of blood remain upon his clothing. He has not rested or changed.

The king's word is sufficient evidence. He does not carry a convenient machine fragment into the conversation.

### What the king knows

Through personal recognition, the king knows:

- the armies upon Gerolt's farm belong to the Great Remaking;
- the armour, standards and war machines are approximately one thousand years old;
- the forces appeared in Mydea without any known march, deployment or warning;
- Seralune awakened within the same narrow period.

He believes the awakening and battlefield are connected. This is his conclusion, not confirmed narration.

The king does not remember or name Alaric. He does not explain the full Ancient Sin, the divided soul or the erasure ritual.

### The king's fear

The king is terrified. Express this through what his authority does:

- he locks the chamber;
- dismisses people he would normally permit to remain;
- interrupts reports;
- demands orders be executed before explanations are complete;
- treats uncertainty as a reason for harsher action.

He remains intelligent. His conclusion is incomplete but grounded in what he personally recognises.

### The command

The king orders Thaeroval to:

- locate Seralune;
- bring her back by any means necessary;
- kill anyone knowingly obstructing her recovery;
- restrain and reseal her;
- kill her if her memories return and containment becomes impossible.

The king uses Thaeroval's identity as a hero against him. He makes the possible victims of another catastrophe part of the command, forcing duty and love into the same decision.

### Thaeroval's answer

Thaeroval accepts the duty to find and return Seralune. He intends to reseal her and believes explanation itself may be dangerous.

When the king asks what he will do if Seralune remembers and cannot be contained, Thaeroval does not answer.

Do not turn this into open rebellion. He does not denounce his father, release Seralune or reject the mission. His silence preserves only the one act he cannot promise.

Thaeroval does not tell the king that the queen created Cyrandor's order.

### Scene turn

Seralune's disappearance becomes a royal recovery operation. Thaeroval chooses duty and containment over her freedom, while withholding one fact from his father and refusing one final command.

## Scene Three — The Southern Terraces

### Cause and arrival of the report

Before the royal confrontation can fully settle, reports reach the warship from Natharul's southern slope.

Ordinary soldiers were sent first because the initial reports described surviving corrupted fauna emerging from the ruptured aqueduct. No one understood that Nereth's eruption had awakened the source sustaining the entire growth.

Within minutes, the deployment becomes a massacre.

Thaeroval goes immediately. The king remains aboard the warship and witnesses the later cut from above.

### The massacre

Enter after most of the killing has occurred. Do not narrate hundreds of individual deaths.

The southern terraces contain:

- hundreds of Natharul soldiers dead or dying;
- soldiers killed by companions rather than by a visible attacker;
- weapons remaining in bodies held by familiar hands;
- survivors unable to trust what they see or hear;
- black-purple roots emerging through wounds and armour;
- voices of dead friends calling from lightless places;
- corrupted fauna moving through the blood and broken formation;
- civilians, physicians or trapped soldiers still alive beyond the creature.

The violence may be gruesome, but every detail must clarify the creature's ability, the speed of the collapse or what Thaeroval chooses. Do not stack gore merely to advertise darkness.

### The unnamed source

No surviving Natharul record has classified or named the creature. Soldiers call it **the thing**, **the creature** or describe what they saw. Do not invent an official monster name in this chapter.

Its visible form draws from the approved reference without reproducing it exactly:

- somewhat larger than a normal person rather than colossal;
- a narrow, elongated black body;
- white eyes;
- a crowded pale mouth;
- long hooked limbs;
- smoke-like strands or roots trailing from its back;
- a shadow extending far beyond the scale of the body casting it.

Its restraint of scale is important. A roughly human-sized creature has destroyed hundreds without needing the spectacle of a giant.

### Ability

The creature intensifies fear, grief, suspicion, resentment and violent instinct already present in the people around it. It does not create complete false histories or turn innocent minds into empty puppets.

Its power expresses itself through:

- familiar voices coming from shadows;
- allies appearing threatening at the worst possible instant;
- grief becoming certainty that someone nearby caused the death;
- existing prejudice becoming permission to attack;
- one soldier's violence producing terror and retaliation in the next;
- movement in which the creature appears to enter one shadow and emerge from another.

It uses mind games rather than physical dominance. Each death and new source of fear strengthens the conditions it exploits.

The creature does not know Seralune's identity, call her its source, recognise Thaeroval as the seal's architect or explain its origin. It does not speak in Seralune's voice.

### Thaeroval's resistance

The creature attempts to find an emotional connection it can distort. Thaeroval's mind has already been diminished by centuries of severance. Its influence finds too little ordinary continuity or compassion upon which to build.

Do not explain this in narration. Show only that:

- shadow-voices reach him and fail to change his pace;
- soldiers around him respond while he does not;
- the creature approaches or tests him, then reacts as though something expected is absent;
- Thaeroval continues towards it.

Seralune remains the protected attachment within him. The creature does not reach, imitate or expose her in this scene.

### The surviving soldiers

Thaeroval places himself between the creature and those still alive. The survivors respond with worshipful relief. To them, the First Blade's arrival means the killing has ended.

Do not make them explain his reputation. Their behaviour—lowered weapons, repeated title, attempts to crawl behind him or immediate obedience—must establish it.

### Scene turn

The soldiers' strength and numbers have become weapons against one another. Thaeroval arrives as their unquestioned salvation, but the means by which he saves them costs something they cannot see.

## Scene Four — One Cut

### No conventional fight

Thaeroval does not test lesser attacks, exchange blows or struggle for advantage. He is emotionally exhausted by Cyrandor, Seralune and his father's command. Once the creature continues killing, he chooses the full blade immediately.

The creature dies in one strike.

### Presentation

The attack should appear almost unnaturally plain:

1. Thaeroval fully draws the single-edged sword.
2. The ordinary act of drawing produces no pressure, preserving continuity with his controlled Chapter 5 strike.
3. Thaeroval deliberately releases Dark through the exposed edge; black light consumes its reflections.
4. Only then do nearby bodies and mechanisms fail beneath the pressure. Do not establish this as a separate named power.
5. He performs one horizontal cut.
6. A single line appears.
7. The world separates along it.

Do not surround the motion with shouted names, elaborate spell terminology or decorative sound effects. **THOOM remains exclusive to Alaric's heartbeat and cannot appear.**

### What is severed

The line passes through:

- the creature's body;
- the corruption sustaining it;
- every root and corrupted creature linked to that source;
- Natharul's southern mountainside;
- the coastal water beyond it;
- the seabed;
- the clouds upon the horizon.

The creature's body falls in two physical halves. It does not vanish without evidence. Across the aqueduct and tunnels, every connected root and corrupted organism dies at once.

The sea remains divided for several seconds. Its floor becomes visible before the water collapses together.

### Range and function

Thaeroval's sword can sever anything, including physical matter, magic, distance, causality, memory, identity, Affinity and metaphysical bonds. For the present design:

- it only severs, ends, removes or disconnects;
- it cannot create, heal, repair, reveal hidden information or restore what has been lost;
- physical distance does not protect a target;
- the target does not have to be immediately visible;
- full severance creates permanent consequences even when Thaeroval remains physically unharmed.

Do not explain this catalogue in prose. The chapter demonstrates only the physical and sustaining connections cut in this one attack.

### Collateral consequence

Thaeroval directs the line away from surviving soldiers, and the blade itself kills none of them. His frustration causes the severance to continue much farther than the immediate target required.

The aftermath remains dangerous:

- part of the mountainside begins collapsing;
- divided water returns with destructive force, but Waluna's outer headland absorbs the first impact before a reduced surge reaches the harbour;
- soldiers and wardwrights must move the wounded;
- Natharul receives a permanent scar through mountain, coast and seabed.

The severed source no longer controls the connected growth, but corruption already established within a living host is not cured. A soldier carrying black-purple veins remains visibly infected and is isolated from the other wounded. This preserves the rule established through Nereth rather than allowing the source's death to erase infection.

Do not immediately absolve the attack because no ally stood directly upon its line.

### The permanent emotional price — author only

The full severance takes no visible stamina, blood or mana from Thaeroval. He remains standing normally.

Its price is irreversible: this cut removes more of his instinctive compassion for strangers. He can still understand that civilians should be protected. He can still perform the duties of a hero. Their pain no longer reaches him in the same human way.

His love for Seralune remains protected for now. Whether he could one day sacrifice that final attachment is deliberately unresolved and must not be answered in this chapter or fixed prematurely in the bible.

Do not name or theorise about the cost on-page. Establish it through contrast:

- before the cut, Thaeroval apologises to Cyrandor and makes sure the wounded will receive treatment;
- after the cut, injured strangers and collapsing terrain affect his decisions as obligations rather than people;
- his voice and actions lose a degree of human response without turning him into a different personality instantly.

### Scene turn

Thaeroval saves the surviving soldiers effortlessly. Natharul sees its perfect hero. The act quietly makes him less capable of understanding the people he saves.

## Scene Five — Evidence, Restraint and Departure

### The lower-ward evidence

After the source dies, black roots remain visibly fused to conduits built into Seralune's lower ward system. Their direction and construction allow the cause to be inferred: the machinery intended to carry excess mana away from Seralune fed the corruption beneath Natharul over centuries.

The chapter does not need a neutral magical lecture. A wardwright may begin reporting what the conduits show. Thaeroval recognises enough to control the information.

He:

- orders surviving wardwrights to report only to him;
- takes or suppresses their written findings;
- prevents the conclusion from reaching the king;
- does not threaten indiscriminate execution or cut the evidence from the mountain.

Leorin notices the restriction and suspects concealment without learning the full truth.

Thaeroval's concealment is a deliberate moral compromise. He protects the legitimacy of the ancient decision while preparing to repeat it.

### The king's response

The king witnessed the cut from the royal warship before completing his return. He is relieved that Natharul's greatest weapon still obeys and frightened because the same blade could divide the kingdom.

Do not have the king praise the spectacle. His altered treatment of Thaeroval should show that fear: greater care with distance, fewer attendants, or the choice to personally deliver the restraint rather than risk another hand carrying it.

### The restraint

The king personally gives Thaeroval a prepared restraint intended to hold Seralune long enough for renewed sealing.

- Its shape, metalwork or repeating arcs visibly resemble the original broken circle.
- The resemblance should create recognition without a mechanical explanation.
- Thaeroval accepts it.
- Acceptance communicates his decision more strongly than a speech about bringing Seralune home.
- He carries no tool intended to kill her.

### Brief return past Cyrandor

On the way to the aircraft, Thaeroval passes the infirmary. Cyrandor remains awake inside.

Thaeroval pauses at the open doorway but does not enter or speak. He then continues.

Keep this brief. It exists to show that Cyrandor's accusation remained with him and that the man passing after the full cut is not emotionally identical to the man who apologised before it. Do not add another argument, demand or forgiveness scene.

### Pursuit method

Thaeroval travels alone aboard a small royal aircraft he can operate himself. Do not give him a distance-cutting form of travel; it would create unnecessary problems for later geography and pursuit.

His first destination is Waluna. From there he intends to:

1. identify who saw or accepted the queen's lily signet;
2. examine departures during the hours following the eruption;
3. identify Neldor's merchant vessel;
4. follow its northern Mydean destination;
5. reach Seralune before ordinary Natharul forces can harm her.

He does not yet know Favale is Seralune's intended destination.

### Final image

End with Thaeroval's small aircraft crossing above the lasting line of his cut.

The sea has closed over the wound. The mountain has not.

Do not force that exact phrasing if it reads as an authorial maxim. The physical contrast should provide the ending: water moving again below, the divided mountain remaining open behind, and Thaeroval carrying the restraint towards his sister.

## Dialogue Guardrails

- Follow [[Chapter Craft Writing Rules]] and the revised Chapter 14 conversational standard without exception.
- Dialogue must sound like people addressing one another under immediate pressure, not characters debating the novel's themes.
- Cyrandor is weakened but not reduced to cryptic one-liners.
- Thaeroval's control should appear in precision, not endless refusals to answer.
- The king is frightened but remains intelligent and authoritative.
- Each royal order must follow from the evidence the king actually possesses.
- Do not let the king explain the complete Great Remaking merely because he lived through it.
- Preserve ordinary connecting language and natural contractions where each speaker would use them.
- Keep speakers unmistakable.
- Do not overdecorate dialogue tags with eyes, hands, breath, mouths or silence.
- No character should explain that Thaeroval is losing his humanity.
- Do not make Cyrandor say **freedom**, **choice**, **control** or **protection** repeatedly as thesis vocabulary. His strongest argument is what Seralune actually did.

## Prose and Pacing Guardrails

- Use externally focused third person without becoming a screenplay or an omniscient historical account.
- No direct access to Thaeroval's thoughts.
- Do not convert every unspoken response into a gaze, tightened hand, rigid body or silence filling the room.
- Reserve fragments and isolated lines for the king's final command, the creature's arrival and the single severance.
- The massacre should be horrifying but selective. A few exact images are stronger than a catalogue of bodies.
- The creature dies immediately when Thaeroval attacks. Do not manufacture resistance to extend the scene.
- Let the cut's physical sequence remain comprehensible: body, mountain, coast, sea, seabed, clouds, collapse.
- Do not use **THOOM**.
- Do not name attacks or use modern buzzwords.
- Do not explain the cost after showing the before-and-after contrast.
- End after the pursuit begins. Do not add another viewpoint, Seralune reaction or distant Alaric disturbance.

## Mystery and Reveal Ledger

### Developed

- **The Silent Field:** the king identifies its armies and machines as belonging to the Great Remaking.
- **Seralune's sealing:** its lower wards are shown to have fed the corruption beneath Natharul.
- **The queen's opposition:** Cyrandor confirms she created an order intended to take Seralune beyond Natharul's control.
- **Thaeroval's power:** readers see one unrestricted physical severance and its geographical scale.
- **Thaeroval's pursuit:** the lily signet provides a credible first investigative step.

### Partially answered

- **Why the king fears Seralune:** he personally remembers the age represented by the battlefield and believes her awakening signals its return.
- **Why Thaeroval rarely uses his full blade:** the before-and-after contrast implies a personal cost without explaining it.
- **Origin of the aqueduct horror:** the roots are visibly linked to the lower wards, but the complete magical process remains unresolved.

### Preserved

- Alaric's identity and erased history.
- The divided soul.
- The four Affinities Alaric once carried.
- The full Ancient Sin and the lovers' responsibility for ancient destruction.
- The precise ritual by which Alaric was erased.
- The queen's present location.
- Seralune's ship and destination from Thaeroval until he investigates Waluna.
- Whether Thaeroval could ever sacrifice his final attachment to Seralune.
- A direct explanation of the sword's emotional price.

### Consequences continuing

- Cyrandor survives but remains imprisoned and permanently maimed.
- Leorin begins watching Thaeroval's concealment more carefully.
- The king fears both Seralune's return and Thaeroval's unrestricted strength.
- Natharul bears a permanent geographical scar.
- Thaeroval becomes a pursuing antagonist rather than a stationary obstacle.
