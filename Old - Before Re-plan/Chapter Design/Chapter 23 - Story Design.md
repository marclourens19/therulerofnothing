# Chapter 23 — Story Design

_Revision 2, 22 September 2026, for Joshua's approval. Built from three rounds of Joshua's answers, including the escalation round asked for after his direction that the coming chapters "really pick up in intensity, brutality, character writing and questions". Nothing is drafted as prose until this design is approved._

---

## Working Status

- **Viewpoint:** Alaric, close third throughout
- **Working title:** Open (ten options follow the finished chapter)
- **Primary location:** the southern half of the Great Expanse, from the colossi and the buried channel to the rim of the central crater and back, ending within sight of the Expanse's far edge
- **Time:** from dawn on the third day after Foramen to dawn on the sixth
- **Target length:** approximately 5,500–6,500 words. It's a heavy chapter; Chapter 24 can run shorter to balance.
- **Primary register:** thirst, guilt and fracture inside a group that has only just learned to trust itself. The Expanse is a place that punishes wanting.
- **Combat:** none. One struggle between friends, and one killing that isn't a fight.

## Joshua's Decisions (locked)

1. **Main pressure: Alaric leads them wrong.** After Chapter 22, Silas trusts Alaric's sense of direction and gives him the lead. Pulled by the woman's voice, Alaric bends their course towards the heart of the Expanse, and the mistake costs water they can't spare.
2. **He walks in his sleep.** On the night he keeps quiet about where the voice is, Alaric walks towards it in his sleep. At dawn Silas and Redd catch him at the crater's rim with sand pouring past his boots, and that's how they find the crater. His daytime choice to stay silent still stands; now the pull can move his body.
3. **Alaric asks Silas to teach him how to stop someone** (disarm or hold, without killing). Silas says there's rarely time for it, then teaches him. Early attempts fail.
4. **Redd learns about Marta from Silas** on the worst night of thirst, when he asks Silas to talk about Marta's kitchen again and Silas can't.
5. **Silas tells the gorge story** that same night: the order he gave, the trap, Marta's father dying because of him. It's the first time Alaric and Redd hear it.
6. **The forty: the Expanse took them, and they turned on each other.** Natharul's relief party took the direct route to cut the survivor off at the far edge. The voices, the thirst and their own circling tracks broke them, and they killed each other. One rider is still alive.
7. **The rider:** Alaric stops Redd from killing him, then Silas kills the rider anyway.
8. **What the rider says:** the orders ("the survivor of the field", alive, for the king's questioning), and at the end only water and his mother.
9. **The core does nothing.** It stays cold and dormant; the pull is in Alaric, not the object.
10. **The hook:** on the last night, the silent voice speaks once, clearly: *"——Alaric."* It's broken exactly the way the voice that gave him his name was in Chapter 2. The chapter ends on it, still inside the Expanse, with the edge in sight. They walk out in Chapter 25.

## Current Position (handoff from Chapter 22)

Chapter 22 ended late on the second day out of Foramen, beside Kurdag's buried channel near the two colossi. Every skin and bottle is full. Silas has shown them the line south. Three dry days lie ahead if the paths stay where Kurdag left them.

Carried into this chapter:

- **Alaric:** Silas's spare sword; the satchel with the wrapped, cold, dormant core; his cleaned blade still kept at a distance; his knee already mending faster than it should (not remarked on).
- **Silas:** sword; Fire『Affinity』(hands sore from burning the creature's grip in Ch 22); the water count; what Liluth said about Marta; the gorge, which he has never told either of them.
- **Redd:** his father's sword; four clawed furrows on his forearm, bound with a blanket strip; the smallest full skin; still hears his father at times. He doesn't know about Marta.
- **Wena:** rope; drinks from the tin dish.
- **Food:** the bread is gone. What's left of Foramen's roasted roots and a little dried fruit.
- **Knowledge:** forty Natharul riders are heading for the marker (Aelis, Ch 20). Liluth wanted the survivor alive. Alaric heard a woman's voice in the night (Ch 22), wanted to answer, and didn't.

## Chapter Purpose

1. Complete the Expanse's final stretch, with its worst costs coming from inside the group.
2. Make Alaric wrong and make him pay. His Chapter 22 clarity earns the lead; his private wish to find the voice spends that trust, and then the voice takes his body out of his control.
3. Rehearse the climax in miniature: he realises the voice pulls him away from his friends, and turns his back on it. Nothing is explained.
4. Destroy the forty through the Expanse and their own collapse, which vindicates Alaric's long-way choice in Chapter 20.
5. Advance two mysteries hard. Natharul's king wants Alaric alive and nobody knows why; and the voice knows his name.
6. Open Silas up. Marta's truth and the gorge story come out on the same night, and both cost him.
7. Set Silas's cold, early decision against Alaric's newly learned restraint, so Silas is proven right in a way that discomforts Alaric (bible), and readers now understand why he makes that kind of choice.

**Gained:** the forty are gone; the edge is in sight; Alaric knows the king wants him; Alaric and Redd finally know who Silas is.
**Lost or damaged:** a day and more of water; Silas's trust in Alaric's judgement, and in his own watch; Redd's peace; Alaric's trust in his own body; the belief that the voice is only a sound.

## Causal Chain

**What caused this chapter?**

- Chapter 20: Alaric chose Kurdag's slow western route. Aelis revealed the forty. Liluth taunted Silas about Marta, and Alaric killed her deliberately.
- Chapter 22: Alaric kept his bearings better than Silas and Redd, and heard a woman's voice he wanted to answer. Redd was clawed. Silas anchored Redd with Marta's kitchen while hiding what Liluth said, and learned to ignore footsteps that weren't there.

**What becomes possible?**

- Alaric enters Favale knowing Natharul's king wants him, and knowing the voice that named him is out there.
- Redd carries Marta, Hob's children and a dying Natharul boy into a city where an elven princess is heading for the same streets (readers know).
- Silas's history is in the open, which makes his warnings in Favale land harder, and makes ignoring them cost more.

**What becomes impossible?**

- Silas can't pretend his bearings, or his watch, are sound in the Expanse.
- Alaric can't treat the voice as harmless, or trust himself asleep.
- Redd can't go back to not knowing.
- Natharul's forty will never reach the far edge.

**Who behaves differently afterwards?**

- **Alaric** distrusts the pull, and keeps someone awake beside him at night. He knows a learned skill can fail against someone he loves.
- **Silas** takes the lead back and makes the hard call early again. Two failures of trust (Alaric's lead and his own watch) confirm his worst belief: his judgement has to stay in charge.
- **Redd** has seen what Natharul does, what grief makes him want to do, and what that looks like when someone else does it.

## Plain Scene Skeleton

**lesson (fail, fail, half) → lead given → drift → voice withheld → sleepwalk → found at the rim at dawn → confession → lead taken back, water re-counted → Marta told → the gorge told → spiral of tracks → the forty killed each other → survivor → friend stopped → enemy killed anyway, water spilled → thirst march → silence → the edge at dawn → his name**

Compared with the previous five chapters:

- **Ch 18:** route choice → inn → training → entering the Expanse
- **Ch 19:** arrival → suspicion → acceptance → the smooth edge → alarm
- **Ch 20:** stand-off → battle → deliberate kill → dispute → departure
- **Ch 21:** landfall → inspection → farewell → city → road
- **Ch 22:** meal → displacement → shelter → siege and pull → landmark → water

No attack on the group, no siege, no inspection, no escape. The pressure is a protagonist's mistake and loss of control, two confessions, and a moral split over an enemy who can no longer hurt anyone. The training beat returns for restraint rather than commitment, and pays off within the chapter.

**Deliberate echo check:** the forty killing each other echoes the Chapter 17 soldiers. The difference must be clear on the page. Chapter 17 was a creature magnifying fear. Here, nothing is magnified: thirst, the spiral and each rider hearing a different voice calling a different way turn soldiers into men fighting over water and direction. No outside force makes them do it.

## Scene Architecture

### Scene 1 — Before the Heat

**Opening:** open mid-lesson, with Alaric on his back in the sand for the third time. The colossi stand over them in the grey before sunrise. Not waking, not walking.

**Immediate want:** Alaric wants to learn how to stop someone without killing them.

**Pressure:** Silas thinks it's a luxury ("there's rarely time for that"), and every movement costs water, so it has to be done before sunrise. Alaric's body wants to commit, the way Silas taught him in Ch 18. Now he has to stop halfway.

**Required developments:**

- Alaric asks without explaining why. Silas knows why and doesn't say so. Liluth isn't named.
- One concrete technique, such as stepping inside the swing, trapping the sword arm at the elbow and turning the wrist until the blade drops. The exact move is set in drafting, and the same move comes back in Scene 5.
- Two clear failures with specific corrections. The third attempt half-works.
- Redd heckles, then volunteers as the one-armed "villain" (warm and funny). He lets Alaric win once, and Alaric knows he did.
- Silas: "It works on someone who doesn't want to hurt you." This pays off in Scene 5.

**Turn:** Silas hands Alaric the lead. "You kept your head better than either of us. Keep it today." It's a real change in trust, delivered as an instruction.

### Scene 2 — Leading

**Immediate want:** Alaric wants to deserve the lead.

**Pressure:** the dark sand forks, and each fork has to be chosen. The ground keeps shifting. Redd and Silas both hear things, while Alaric feels sure of one line.

**Required developments:**

- Compressed travel showing his small competences: naming targets before he walks (from Ch 22), reading the dark strip, checking behind. Redd calls him "Captain Al" once.
- At each fork, the east-leaning branch feels right. A careful reader can notice the bend; Alaric doesn't.
- **Night 3:** high stone for shelter; creatures heard far off. The woman's voice returns, clearer than in Ch 22 but still without words, and it comes from ahead, the way he's been leading.
- He doesn't answer her. He also **doesn't tell Silas where she is.** He tells himself the dark sand runs that way anyway; the truth, which he half-knows, is that he wants to know who she is. He falls asleep having decided to keep the same line tomorrow. That's his mistake, and it's a choice.

**Turn:** the decision to keep quiet.

### Scene 3 — The Rim

**Immediate want:** Silas and Redd want Alaric back alive.

**Pressure:** it's dark, creatures are about, the water is limited, and Alaric's tracks run straight towards the place everyone has been told to avoid.

**Required developments:**

- **Silas's watch:** footsteps in the small hours. For two nights he has heard footsteps that weren't there, so he doesn't turn round. That's his failure. Before dawn, Wena whines at an empty blanket and wakes Redd.
- **The chase:** Alaric's tracks run straight, with no curve. Silas carries a flame in his palm and pays for it. Wena follows the scent (a dog, not a sense of magic). Creature tracks cross Alaric's line more than once, and his prints walk on untouched. Nobody can explain it, and nobody tries.
- **The rim at dawn:** they find him standing at the edge of the central crater, with sand pouring past his boots and over the lip along its whole visible length, vanishing without filling anything. The scale is a landscape, not a building, and the sound is endless rain. Silas's fist closes in his collar and he wakes, with no memory of walking. The voice is quiet now that he's close enough to see where it came from.
- **Confession, at the rim:** "I heard her. Last night. She was ahead of us, and I kept walking towards her." / "And after that?" / "I don't remember." Nothing is shouted.
- Silas takes the lead back without a speech. Redd tries a joke and can't finish it.
- Silas re-counts the water out loud. They've spent the best part of two days going the wrong way: three dry days have become four, on water meant for three. Half shares from now on, and Wena gets a share. Alaric's knee, which he struck against stone in the dark, is fine by noon. Nobody mentions it.
- No memory, no headache, no core reaction at the crater, and no name for it. Kurdag's word, "the crater", is enough.

**Turn:** Alaric turns his back on the place the voice came from. It's the first, smallest version of the choice he'll make in Favale, and nobody calls it that.

### Scene 4 — The Worst Night

**Immediate want:** get through the night without anyone breaking.

**Pressure:** thirst, half shares, Redd hearing his father again, Silas worn thin by the night before. Redd asks Silas to talk about Marta's kitchen again, "like the other night".

**Required developments:**

- Silas starts, and can't. Redd notices, because Redd notices when people stop.
- **Marta:** Silas tells him plainly what Liluth said: "Your innkeeper didn't make a sound. Not one. I had to ask a peat-cutter instead." He adds only what he knows: Marta was hurt, and she held. He doesn't invent details.
- Redd's first response is pride: "Course she didn't." Then he understands, and asks what it would take to make a peat-cutter talk. He answers himself: they'd have used his children. (Readers know from Interlude II that he's right.)
- Redd asks Alaric, "You knew?" Alaric says, "Yes." Neither Silas nor Alaric defends keeping it back.
- **The gorge:** Redd asks the question that turns the night: why didn't Silas go back for her? Silas tells them both, in a few plain lines:
  - twenty years ago, in the Mydean Campaign, his company scouted Natharul raiders;
  - a Natharul unit fell back into a dry gorge, and he ordered pursuit against every warning;
  - Natharul held both ridges and closed the ends, and everyone died but him, including Marta's father;
  - he came back alone and told her it was his order, and she shut her door;
  - if he'd gone back to Kelmend that night, they'd have had him and her both.
  No self-pity and no asking for forgiveness. It's the most he has ever said at once.
- Nobody absolves him. Redd goes quiet, then says something about his own village that makes Silas's story his too (Natharul burned it), or says nothing at all. Wena goes to Redd.

**Turn:** the group knows who Silas is, and the crack between them is also, strangely, a closeness. Redd's hatred of Natharul now has Marta's name on it.

### Scene 5 — The Forty

**Immediate want:** get back to Kurdag's line before the water goes.

**Pressure:** in the morning, tracks cross their path: many horses, shod, curving. Silas and Alaric see the curve at the same time (Kurdag's warning pays off). They don't follow it. From a rise they see where it goes: round and round, tightening.

**Required developments:**

- **The forty turned on each other** (the Tobin bar: plain, heavy, no lingering):
  - riders dead of sword wounds from their own side;
  - water skins slashed;
  - two men who died locked together;
  - a knife still in a rider, and the hand that put it there within reach;
  - horses dead in the spiral, one with its throat cut for blood;
  - drag marks leading away where the creatures have taken some of the dead.
  Fewer than forty bodies can be seen. The picture says they fought over water and over which voice to follow.
- **Silas reads it:** they took the straight line through the middle to be waiting at the far edge first. It was a sound plan by people who didn't know Kurdag's warnings (Craft Rule 18: a reasonable decision with terrible consequences).
- **The survivor:** a young Natharul rider pinned under his dead horse, lips split, eyes clouded. His sword lies near his hand, and its blade is dark. Whether he fought or only defended himself is never answered. He sees Alaric and knows him, because who else would be walking out here.
- **The orders**, in the human tongue: the survivor of the field, alive, for His Majesty's questioning. "Which king?" "There's only one."
- **Redd:** hearing Natharul, the king and "alive", the morning after Marta and Hob's children, he draws his father's sword and goes for the helpless rider.
- **Alaric stops him** with the Scene 1 move. It half-works: Redd is stronger and doesn't stop being strong because he's grieving. They both go down, and the sword ends up in the sand. Redd doesn't fight Alaric beyond that; he lies there and breathes.
- Alaric kneels by the rider with the open skin. The rider's voice drops into Elvish.
- **Silas kills him** quickly, with a knife, while Alaric is lifting the skin. Alaric's hand jerks, and the water spills into the sand beside the dead man. Silas rights the skin before much more is lost.
- Alaric asks what the rider said. Silas: "He was asking for his mother." Redd hears it.
- **Aftermath:** Silas explains once. The rider would have lasted till dark, the creatures come at dark, and Alaric would have stayed with him. Silas is right on every count, and after last night Alaric understands exactly why this is how Silas protects people. He can't forgive him for it yet. "I stopped Redd." "I know. I didn't need you to stop me."

**Turn:** the forty are finished. Natharul's king wants Alaric, and nobody knows why. Each of the three men has just done, or tried to do, something the others can't agree with.

### Scene 6 — The Last Night, and Dawn

**Immediate want:** reach water.

**Pressure:** compressed thirst march. Wena lies down in the heat and won't get up, and the skins are nearly dry.

**Required developments:**

- Alaric pours his share into his cupped hands for Wena. Redd, who hasn't spoken since the rider, gives Alaric part of his own share without a word, or hands back the sword Alaric dropped. It's a first repair, and nobody announces it.
- Silas carries Wena for a stretch, then has to stop because his hands are too sore. He doesn't say so.
- **Night 5:** Alaric asks Redd to sit up beside him, and admits why: "In case I walk." The voice doesn't come. Alaric finds himself listening for it, and dislikes that he is.
- **Dawn, Day 6:** from high stone, a darker line along the southern horizon, living ground, with a thread of smoke above it. The edge is in sight. It's the first relief in two days: Redd almost laughs, and Silas closes his eyes.
- **The hook:** as they get up to go, from behind them, from the heart of the Expanse, one word, clear, broken the way it was in Chapter 2: *"——Alaric."* He has never told anyone where his name came from. Nobody else hears it. He doesn't answer. **End.**

**Ending image:** the relief of the green line, then the name. The chapter ends on a revelation and a disturbing implication, not a threat. They walk out in Chapter 25.

## Character Movement

### Alaric

- **Starts:** earned clarity; a secret wish to answer the voice; wants a way to stop people that isn't killing.
- **Mistake:** keeps the voice's direction to himself and leads towards it.
- **Loss of control:** walks to the crater's rim in his sleep.
- **Cost:** water; Silas's trust; trust in his own body.
- **Choices:** confesses; turns away from the voice; stops Redd; tries to give the enemy water; asks Redd to keep watch over him.
- **Ends:** knows the king of Natharul wants him, and that the voice knows his name, the name he thought was his alone.

### Silas

- Gives his trust and it fails; then his own watch fails. Both confirm his flaw that his judgement has to stay in charge, and that feeds his next cold decision.
- Tells Redd the truth about Marta, then tells them both the gorge story. It's the most exposed he has ever been.
- Kills the rider, as mercy and pragmatism at once. He's proven right in a way that discomforts Alaric.

### Redd

- Starts warm (Scene 1), ends quiet.
- Learns about Marta and immediately understands what was done to the peat-cutter.
- Hears the gorge story, tries to kill a helpless elf, is stopped, then hears the boy ask for his mother.
- A first wordless repair in Scene 6. His hatred is sharpened and informed, and not endorsed.

### Wena

- Wakes Redd to Alaric's empty blanket and tracks Alaric in the dark (by scent, as a dog would). She collapses from the heat in Scene 6, and survives.

## Dialogue and Voice Notes

- Revised Chapter 14 remains the dialogue reference.
- Nobody lectures about killing, mercy or choice. The argument over the rider takes five or six lines at most, then behaviour.
- The gorge story is plain, short and in Silas's voice. There's no speech quality to it; it sounds like a report he's been rehearsing for twenty years and never delivered.
- Silas speaks less as he gets thirstier. Redd's jokes stop in Scene 3 and don't come back.
- The rider speaks the human tongue "like something distasteful" (Voice Standard), then Elvish. Silas understands some Elvish from his intelligence work in the Mydean Campaign (consistent with the bible). Alaric doesn't.
- Oaths: "By the Four" at most once.

## Magic and Mana Guardrails

- **The voice:** the same woman as in Ch 22. For most of the chapter it has no words, and it has a direction (towards the centre). At the end it says exactly one word, his name, broken as in Chapter 2. Nothing identifies her, nothing explains it, and Alaric doesn't connect it to Seralune (he doesn't know she exists).
- **The sleepwalk:** Alaric remembers nothing of it. Show no dream content, or at most one sensory scrap (warmth, lamplight).
- **Alaric's resistance:** he stays oriented while awake. The pull isn't confusion; it's wanting, and then, asleep, something stronger than wanting.
- **The creature tracks** crossing his untouched line are shown and not explained. They don't settle how the creatures hunt (a World Bible open question).
- **The core:** dormant, cold and unremarked. No reaction at the crater.
- **Silas's Fire:** only the palm-flame on the night chase, and it costs his sore hands. No fire kill.
- **Redd's Earth:** none.
- **THOOM:** at most once, at the rim or at Redd's attack, and only if it's genuinely warranted.
- **The crater:** unnamed. Kurdag's "the crater" is enough.

## Final Chapter Shape

**At dawn under the colossi, Alaric asks Silas to teach him how to stop someone without killing them, and half-learns it. Silas, trusting Alaric's clarity, gives him the lead. Alaric follows a line that feels right, hears the woman's voice ahead of them in the night, and says nothing. In the small hours he walks towards her in his sleep while Silas ignores the footsteps. At dawn Silas and Redd catch him at the rim of the central crater, sand pouring past his boots. He confesses, Silas takes back the lead, and the water is re-counted for a journey a day longer than they packed for. That night Redd learns what Liluth did to Marta, and Silas finally tells them about the gorge. In the morning they find the forty, dead at one another's hands in a spiral of their own tracks, and one rider alive who knows Alaric and says the king wants him. Redd goes to kill him; Alaric stops Redd; Silas kills the rider anyway, and the water Alaric was offering runs into the sand. They march on nearly dry and carry the dog. At dawn the edge of the Expanse is in sight, and as they rise to walk towards it, the voice behind them says his name.**
