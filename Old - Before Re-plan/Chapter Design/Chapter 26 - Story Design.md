# Chapter 26 — Story Design

_Draft for Joshua's approval, 23 September 2026. Built from ten rounds of Joshua's answers, under his direction for the final arc: a living world (LOTR, FFXIV), brutal and realistic action, characters at their best, more characters talking, and web/light-novel rhythm. Nothing is drafted as prose until this design is approved._

---

## Working Status

- **Viewpoint:** Alaric, close third throughout
- **Working title:** Open (ten options follow the finished chapter)
- **Primary location:** Helmi's house at the foot of the western aqueduct, the street and steps outside it with Nell still hanging on the arch, then the curfew streets of western Favale
- **Time:** the morning after the hanging (Ch 25) until the small hours of the next night. This is Seralune's Day 7. **The Longest Light is five days away** when the chapter opens.
- **Target length:** as long as the story needs [Joshua, 23 Sept: longer is fine if it makes the storytelling better]. Likely 6,500–8,000 words, for a full day, four long conversations, the street, the fight and the run. It can be split for posting at the evening bell.
- **Primary register:** grief turning into purpose, a friendship coming apart, then violence that forces it back together without mending it
- **Combat:** one fight in a narrow house at night. Brutal, fast and realistic. Four of the temple watch die.

### Style targets for this chapter [Joshua, 23 Sept]

- **More talking.** At least 30% of the words in dialogue. Most scenes have three or more people talking to one another, not only to Alaric.
- **Shorter paragraphs.** An average of about 16 words, and almost none over 80. Quick exchanges, with a line of action between lines of speech (web/light-novel rhythm).
- **The world alive around them.** The bells run the day, banners for the Longest Light go up, street talk carries news, and the city carries on under a hanged woman.
- **The fight** is close, confused and exhausting. Wounds stay and change what people can do. There's talk in the pauses, but no choreography.

## Joshua's Decisions (locked, ten rounds)

**The door and the message**

1. **Helmi tries to shut the door.** Strangers have only ever meant the temple. Alaric gets the first line of Darcy's message out through the gap, "Water always finds its way to the ocean", and the door stops.
2. **The second line breaks him.** "Then tell him I'm still waiting" comes later, at the table set for two. Thirty years of setting out her cup, and she's been waiting too.
3. **Helmi becomes the archivist again.** His hands remember the work. He unwraps the core like a relic, fires questions at Alaric, gets out his old tools and makes a plan for the Library. Grief turns into purpose, sharp and a little frightening.

**Helmi**

4. **His wife died, and he's alone.** Darcy's mother held on for a few years after they took Darcy, then died. The second cup was never hers.
5. **His voice: precise and dry, with Darcy's mind.** Exact words, routes and costs, and a dry humour that surprises people. He corrects Redd's grammar and apologises for it. His cracks show in his hands, as hers do.
6. **He kept a way in: a friend who still owes him.** She's the **Keeper of the Vaults**, a woman his age and once his closest friend. They were young archivists together, and she held Darcy as a baby. Now she's sharp, careful and high up, and she has spent thirty years grateful and ashamed in equal measure.
7. **The debt:** the night before Darcy's examination, the two of them tried to cheat the silver cup. It failed. Helmi swore he'd acted alone, lost everything, and kept her name out of it.
8. **He learns about Darcy:** where she is (flown to the frontier), who has her (Gilmot), and that she stayed so the Faint quarter wouldn't burn. His daughter chose other people's lives over her own freedom, which is just what he'd have wanted and exactly what he feared.

**The core and the Library (lore)**

9. **The core can only be read in the Vaults.** There's a reading pool or stone there that works nowhere else. In Ch 26 Helmi can only confirm it's **real**.
10. **What the core will prove (Ch 28): it's a thousand years old.** The Silent Field's machines come from the age of the Great Remaking, which means something moved them through time.
11. **Helmi doesn't notice yet that Alaric has no mana.**
12. **The Library's barrier: the church guards it.** The temple owns the Library. Priests and the temple watch hold its doors, and only the sworn may enter the Vaults. Pilgrims may see the public halls, and nobody sees below.
13. **The record of two figures (Ch 28)** is a painted page in a Great Remaking chronicle. The crowned elven woman is **deliberately unclear**: it could be Seralune or the queen. **Both marks are unnatural.** No tool made either of them, both are wrong in the same way, and the Keeper is afraid of the page.

**The world**

14. **The Longest Light:** midsummer, the last day of pilgrim season. It's the one day a year the saint is carried down the western stair among the sick. All Favale is in the streets, and **it's where the tear happens.** Five days away.
15. **The informer is a Faint street child** paid in bread to sit on the aqueduct steps and count who goes in and out of certain doors. The child stays in the background: a face on the steps that readers notice.
16. **City life:** the pilgrim season building to the Longest Light (banners, sun-glass lanterns, rumours of what the saint will do this year), and the temple's bells running the day (the dawn bell, the morning bell for hangings, the evening bell for curfew).
17. **The Natharul carriage: street talk only.** A Natharul envoy is lodged at the old palace, and the king has been seen bowing. Nobody knows why they've come.

**The characters**

18. **Alaric and Redd are still cracked, and they argue.** They're polite and careful all morning. Then they have it out over Nell and who's worse. It gets personal: Redd's mam and da against Alaric's silence. Neither wins, and each is right about the other. Then the fight forces them together, which isn't forgiveness.
19. **Alaric steps in, and it costs him.** When the watch come, he acts first, before Silas. It's clumsy, real and bloody, and he gets hurt.
20. **Alaric tells Helmi only where the core came from**, the field, and nothing personal. There's no memory, no woman and no voice.

**The fight and after**

21. **A night knock, then it turns.** Four of the watch come after the evening bell: routine, polite and bored, to question "the archivist's guests". It turns, and the fight fills the narrow house: stairs, kitchen, a lamp knocked over.
22. **Who kills:** Silas kills two, fast and cold, before anyone else has moved. Alaric kills one: messy and close, a man trying to kill Helmi. Redd kills one: his first human kill, and it's nothing like killing an elf in his head. All four of the watch die.
23. **Alaric's wound:** he catches a blade meant for Helmi across his hand and forearm. It's deep and bloody and ruins his grip.
24. **The house is left as it is:** bodies on the kitchen floor, the table still laid for two. Helmi takes the core, the small cup and Darcy's coat, and doesn't look back.
25. **They run with Helmi through the night towards the Keeper.**
26. **The ending: Alaric's hand.** In the dark he unwraps the cut to check it and finds it already closing. He wraps it again before anyone sees, and a new secret starts.

## Chapter Purpose

1. Deliver Darcy's message and bring Helmi back to life as the archivist, Darcy's father, and a voice to love before he dies.
2. Set up the Library: the church's barrier, the Vaults, the Keeper and the debt, and the core confirmed as real.
3. Make Favale live around them: the bells, the Longest Light coming, street talk, the envoy, and a city going about its day under a hanged woman.
4. Have it out between Alaric and Redd, then put them in a fight together.
5. Answer the arch. This time Alaric steps in, first, and pays for it.
6. Land a brutal, realistic fight with lasting consequences: four of the temple watch dead in the Holy Capital, and the group now fugitives.
7. Start a new secret: Alaric's wound closing too fast.

**Gained:** Helmi, alive in every sense; the Keeper; the core confirmed; the Longest Light as the clock.
**Lost:** Helmi's house and thirty years of waiting in it; the group's safety in Favale; Redd's innocence of killing his own kind; Alaric's grip; the last of their anonymity.

## Scene Architecture

### Scene 1 — The Door

**Opening image:** the door closing, and Alaric's voice through the narrowing gap. (Ch 25 ended on the open door. Ch 26 opens on it shutting.)

**Immediate want:** Helmi wants them gone, and Alaric wants the door open.

**Required developments:**

- Helmi: "Please go." The door starts to close. Alaric, fast, through the gap: "Water always finds its way to the ocean."
- The door stops. Behind it, a long silence. Then a hand (the cracks show in his hands) opens it again.
- Silas's eyes are on the aqueduct steps, where a small Faint child sits under the arch with a heel of bread, watching doors (background only).
- They're let in fast: "Inside. All of you. The dog too."

**Turn:** the first line of a thirty-year-old promise opens a door that has been shut to everyone.

### Scene 2 — The Table Set for Two

**Immediate want:** Helmi wants every detail, and Alaric wants to give them without breaking him.

**Required developments (dialogue-led, four voices):**

- The kept house: scrubbed, silent and perfect. The small cup. The coat. Redd, too loud for the room, then too quiet.
- Helmi questions Alaric like an archivist cross-examining a source: where the cell was, what she wore, how she looked, her hands, her voice. "Did she still fold paper when she was thinking?" Alaric: "Under one finger." It lands.
- The truth, in his own words: the frontier, **Gilmot**, "the captain holding the leash", and that **she stayed so the Faint quarter wouldn't burn.** Helmi's reaction is precise and dry, then it isn't.
- Redd, trying to help, asks about the second cup: "Your wife's?" Helmi: "My wife died twenty-six years ago. She never used that cup." It's the only answer he gives about her.
- **Then Alaric gives him the second line: "Then tell him I'm still waiting."** This is where he breaks. It isn't a speech. It's his hands, the small cup, and one line he can't finish.
- Helmi corrects Redd's grammar at some point and apologises for it: "Forgive me. Habit. My daughter used to do it to me." It's the first time he laughs, surprised at himself.

**Turn:** thirty years of waiting, and she was waiting too.

### Scene 3 — The Archivist

**Immediate want:** Helmi wants to see what his daughter sent them to find.

**Required developments:**

- "You said she sent you with something."
- Helmi gets out his old tools from a locked chest: cotton gloves, lenses, a silver probe and a book of Vault marks. He unwraps the core like a relic. His hands stop shaking the moment they have work.
- He fires questions: where exactly, how deep, who else touched it, whether it was ever wet or heated. Alaric tells him **only the field** (Gerolt's farm, the machines, the soldiers stacking the dead). Nothing personal.
- **Helmi's verdict: "It's real."** He means real in an archivist's sense: not a Campaign forgery or a Natharul trick. He can't read what's in it here. "Water holds a shape. But only still water, and only the right water. The pool I need is under the Library."
- **The barrier, from someone who lived inside it:** the Library is the temple's. Priests and the watch hold the doors. Pilgrims get the public halls, and only the sworn go below. "I haven't been sworn for thirty years."
- **The Keeper:** "There is one person." Her name, and what she is now. The debt, told drily and briefly: "The night before the examination we tried something foolish with the silver cup. It didn't work. I told them I'd done it alone." Silas: "Did she let you?" Helmi: "She let me."
- **The plan:** they'll go to her, but not today, in daylight, with a dog the whole road saw. At first light, or the night after.

**Turn:** grief has become purpose, and it's slightly frightening how fast.

### Scene 4 — The Street Under the Arch

**Immediate want:** Silas needs bread and news, and Alaric and Redd are sent out together, or go out together to get away from the house.

**Pressure:** the city, and each other.

**Required developments (the living world):**

- **The bells:** the midday bell, and the city pausing for it.
- **Banners for the Longest Light** go up on poles along the old river road, and sun-glass lanterns are hung and tested.
- **Street talk (overheard and joined in):**
  - "Five days."
  - What the saint will do this year: "They say she'll walk the whole stair."
  - The Natharul envoy lodged at the old palace, and the king seen bowing: "They say he bowed first."
  - "There were two witches, they're saying. Only hanged the one."
- Children playing "witch and watch" in the road under the arch, with a stick for a spear.
- **Nell is still hanging,** and the city walks under her. A pilgrim touches his sun token and doesn't look up.
- **The argument: Nell, and who's worse.** It starts quietly and gets ugly: Redd's mam and da in the yard against Alaric's silence on the road. Redd: "At least I said what I thought." Alaric: "You thought she deserved it." Redd: "I thought they all did." Neither wins. Each is right about the other, and both of them know it.
- They go back to the house without speaking. Helmi, at the window, has heard enough to know.

**Turn:** the friendship is as bad as it's been, just before they need it most.

### Scene 5 — Curfew

**Short.**

- **The evening bell:** curfew. Shutters come down up and down the road. The street empties, and the lanterns on the banner poles are lit for practice.
- Supper at the table for two, with five at it (four people and a dog under it). Helmi lays the small cup out as he always has, then stops, and puts it in his coat pocket instead. He doesn't explain.
- Helmi and Silas, briefly and drily, two men who've each lost the person they built a life round. (Optional; a few lines.)

### Scene 6 — The Knock

**Immediate want:** get through the night and reach the Keeper at first light.

**Pressure:** the child counted them in. And the watch remember the dog.

**Required developments (the fight, at the Tobin bar):**

- **A knock after curfew:** four of the temple watch. The one in front is polite, tired and routine: "Evening, archivist. You've guests. We'll just have a word."
- **Proposal:** one of them is the watchman who fitted Nell's noose, the patient man from the parapet. Alaric knows his face before he knows why.
- Questions at the table set for two: who they are, where they came from, why they're here in pilgrim season. Silas answers for them, smooth and dull. It's nearly working.
- **The turn: Wena.** The noose-fitter looks under the table. "That's the dog. The one that went for the witch." In a city that has just hanged a witch, a dog that knew her is evidence. The polite one sighs as if this is going to make his night longer, and the watch move: to take all of them, the archivist first, "for harbouring".
- **Alaric acts first, before Silas.** A watchman's blade comes out towards Helmi, and Alaric gets between them. He catches the blade across his hand and forearm. It's deep, bloody and immediate, and his grip goes.
- **Silas kills two in seconds,** fast and cold and without a word, the way he killed the rider.
- **Close, confused and ugly in a narrow house:** a lamp goes over and is stamped out, a chair breaks, the stairs are too narrow to use, and the kitchen is where it ends.
- **Redd kills one.** A watchman gets behind Alaric while Alaric is on the floor. Redd takes him, with his father's sword or his wild Earth (the flagstones heaving up). It's his first human kill, to save the friend he was shouting at hours ago. It's nothing like killing an elf in his head.
- **Alaric kills one: the noose-fitter** (proposal), on the kitchen floor, left-handed, with the man's own knife, while the man is trying to get past him to Helmi. It's messy and close, and it takes too long. Afterwards Alaric can't stop seeing the face, the same patient face from the parapet.
- **Helmi doesn't fight.** He stands in his kitchen with his back to the table for two.
- **Talk in the pauses, all of it real:** the polite watchman bargaining ("Lad, lad, put it down, there's no need"), someone begging, Silas's single word, and Redd swearing at Alaric while he saves him.
- **Wounds stay:** Alaric's hand is useless, Redd is splattered and shaking, and Silas has a cut he doesn't mention.

**Turn:** the man who stood still on the road didn't stand still in the kitchen, and four men are dead on the floor of a house kept for two.

### Scene 7 — The Cup, the Coat and the Core

**Required developments:**

- Silence, and breathing. Nobody says anything useful.
- Helmi looks at his kitchen for a long moment. Then, precise: "They'll be missed at the change of watch. That's the second bell after midnight. We have until then."
- He takes three things: **the core, the small cup and Darcy's coat.** He folds the coat once and puts it inside his own. **He leaves the table as it is,** laid for two, with the bodies on the floor. He doesn't look back.
- Redd and Alaric look at each other once. It isn't forgiveness. Redd tears a strip from his own sleeve and binds Alaric's hand, badly and tight, without a word. Then he turns away.
- Silas, to Helmi: "Where?" Helmi: "The Keeper."

### Scene 8 — Alaric's Hand

**Required developments:**

- The curfew streets: shuttered, lantern-lit banner poles, the arch behind them with Nell on it, and the child's step empty.
- They hide from a curfew patrol in a water stair beneath the aqueduct, or behind the Longest Light scaffolding, while boots pass.
- In the dark, alone for a moment, Alaric unwraps Redd's strip to look at the cut.
- **It's already closing.** Hours ago it was open to the bone. He doesn't know what that means, and it frightens him more than the fight did.
- He wraps it again, tighter, before anyone sees.

**Final image:** a secret in his own hand, and Redd's knot holding it shut.

## Dialogue and Voice Notes

- **Helmi:** exact, dry and sometimes funny. He cross-examines like an archivist, corrects grammar and apologises, and uses Darcy's habits. His cracks show in his hands. He's never eloquent about grief.
- **Redd:** his jokes carry the morning, then drop away in the argument. He swears in the fight. After it, he says nothing at all.
- **Alaric:** few words, but he doesn't back down in the argument.
- **Silas:** smooth and dull for the watch, blunt with everyone else.
- **The watch:** polite, bored and procedural, until they aren't. Cruelty is calm: "for harbouring".
- **Street voices:** earthy Mydean, "By the Four", gossip about the envoy and the saint, bargaining. Children's games.
- **Dialogue target:** 30% or more, as set above.

## Final Chapter Shape

**Helmi tries to shut the door, and Alaric gets Darcy's first line through the gap: "Water always finds its way to the ocean." Inside the house kept for two, Helmi questions him about his daughter like an archivist, down to how she folds paper, and learns she's at the frontier, owned by Gilmot, and stayed so the Faint quarter wouldn't burn. The second line, "Then tell him I'm still waiting," breaks him. Then his hands remember the work. He unwraps the core like a relic and pronounces it real, but it can only be read in the Vaults under the temple's Library, where only the sworn may go. There's one person who can get them in: the Keeper, who owes him for a night thirty years ago when they tried to cheat the silver cup. Outside, the city counts down five days to the Longest Light, gossips about the Natharul envoy and the bowing king, and walks under Nell's body. Beneath her, Alaric and Redd finally have it out, and nobody wins. After the curfew bell, four of the temple watch knock politely, and one of them knows the dog that went for the witch. Alaric steps in first this time and catches a blade meant for Helmi. Silas kills two, Redd kills the man behind Alaric, and Alaric kills the man who fitted Nell's noose, on the kitchen floor. Helmi takes the core, the small cup and his daughter's coat, and leaves the table laid for two. In the dark, hiding from a patrol, Alaric unwraps his hand and finds the cut already closing, and wraps it again before anyone sees.**
