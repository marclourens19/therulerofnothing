"His legs just wouldn't move" is exactly right, though, so both options keep it.

> **Now:** He had the sword and wanted to help. Alaric tried to stand, but his knees would not unlock.

## What I understand

In my own words, so you can correct me where I've got it wrong.

1. **The world crowned strength in God's empty place.** A test at ten measures a child's Affinity, and institutions announce what life that child may have. Power decides who stays with their family, who works, whose pain counts and who gets called a person.
2. **Volume 1 asks: what is your worth when everyone tells you you're nothing?** The world tells Alaric he's nothing, and tells Seralune she's dangerous and must be decided for. The volume tests the question and doesn't answer it.
3. **Alaric's Volume 1 is a false growth.** He learns, correctly, that his choices hurt people. Then he concludes, wrongly, that he must make every important choice alone. He chases his past because he thinks it will tell him what he's worth, keeps missing the life forming around him, and ends believing "I am nothing" and "I killed thousands". His worth never comes from the cosmic role he turns out to have; it comes from what he chooses.
4. **Seralune wakes in a prison built by people who love her.** She wants freedom and her mother's answers, comes to believe she is the problem, and ends Volume 1 held by the church, not remembering him. Her later danger has to grow out of her compassion, never out of madness.
5. **The shared flaw of the whole cast is care turning into authority.** Thaeroval seals his sister to keep her safe, the ancient pair each decided for the other, Silas ends threats before anyone else can choose, Redd and Freya lean on each other, and Nereth mistakes obedience for loyalty. Love is where the philosophy shows.
6. **One soul, two people.** The fading world tried to make one remedy and made two people, born at the same moment. The shared soul explains why their magic fits. It never makes them love each other; present Alaric and Seralune owe each other nothing because of the past.
7. **The road is long and lived-in.** A *Final Fantasy XV* road trip through a world with *Lord of the Rings* depth: the group becomes the place Alaric belongs, through meals, watches, jokes and arguments that later events can spend.
8. **The ending is approved in direction.** They survive, stop being special, and choose each other as ordinary people. Magic ends because many people choose to help, not because two saviours decide for everyone.
9. **What Volume 1 asks the reader to give up** (a proposal, but I think it's right): the expectation that finding out who Alaric *was* will tell us what his life means now.

## Chapter 4: three things that are new on the page

**My recommendation:** keep all three as canon, and decide what the crystal was doing to her mana when we reach the chapter that explains the seal. 6c fits the handoff's idea (§15.5, unapproved) that her "yesterday" may be years before the final battle.
