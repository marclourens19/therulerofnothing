# Chapter 8: Design

Started 30 September 2026. **Status (1 October):** the author's working design, `Chapter 8 - Working Design.md`, replaces the six-scene shape and round 1 below. The chapter is now the journey to Marta's door, and Marta herself moves to the next Alaric chapter. Claude's review of it and round 2 are at the end of this file. Round 1 is kept as a record; its Marta questions (E–H, J, K) move to the next Alaric chapter.

**The target:** 4,000–5,000 words, and longer if the story needs it. Claude's estimate for the shape below is **4,500–5,500**. Chapters 5, 6 and 7 all came in a quarter under Claude's budgets, so read it as a range.

## Where it starts

- **Chapter 6 ends at dusk on Day 2,** on the grass above Kelmend. The royal warship has just passed over the town, going east towards the river and the farm. Kelmend's bell is ringing, and the torches on the wall have "stopped going on and started moving". Silas asked, "All those bodies. You said they were on Gerolt's land?", then stepped back off the open ground: "Alaric. Get Wena into the trees."
- **The state they're in:**
  - Alaric's ankle is swollen where the ogre held it ("It's not broken. You'll walk on it.").
  - Silas's palm is burned raw from the ogre fight, wrapped in a strip of cloth Alaric held for him.
  - Alaric has Gerolt's sword.
  - Wena is with them. Silas wanted to leave her, because they're looking for "a boy with a dog".
- **What Silas promised** (Chapter 6): "There's a way along the south wall… I'll have a look at the inn. If it's clear, we both go in, and you show her the sword yourself. If there are riders, we wait." And Alaric's condition: "If you tell me what you're checking."
- **What Alaric knows:**
  - Gerolt said "Marta, at the inn by the south gate. Show her that. Tell her the old fool sent you."
  - Silas said her name to his blade, and won't say how long he knew Gerolt ("Leave it at that for now").
  - The elves who hunted him were Natharul's, and a rider was at the bridge talking to Kelmend's guards (Chapter 3).
  - He believes Gerolt died because of him.
- **Chapter 7 happened the same morning,** far away. Nothing of it reaches Kelmend.

## What's fixed

- **Marta** is Gerolt's niece and keeps the inn by the south gate. Her father was Gerolt's brother, and he died in Silas's gorge. Silas still loves her; they were going to be married before the gorge. **Everything else about her is undecided** (Decisions).
- **Silas's private reason** for wanting to see her first: he loves her and wants to get close to her again. It's never said on the page.
- **"Pay a small answer on Silas, Gerolt and Marta soon, most naturally when Marta sees Silas at her door"** (Decisions, after Chapter 3).
- **Kelmend:**
  - It has a Faint quarter built against the outside of its wall. Alaric saw its low roofs from the slope in Chapter 6.
  - Redd and Freya grew up there. Kelmend's guards hold inspections every year, and Redd hid Freya from them.
  - The class system: Exalted, Eminent, Common and Faint. Every child is tested at ten, and Faint children are taken "under what's called protection".
- **"Empty" is Alaric's own word.** In Chapter 1, Gerolt says "You're not Faint. You're…", and the boy says "Empty?" Gerolt doesn't deny it. The outline's "Gerolt's word comes back" is wrong. It's the boy's word, and in Chapter 8 someone else says it back to him, or he has to say it himself.
- **The outline's job for Chapter 8:**
  - **Its question:** "What is he, to people who can see it?"
  - **Its turn:** "He accepts shelter that has to be earned."
  - **"Empty" stops being a private word and becomes a danger.**
  - **The problem it leaves open:** Darcy, and the price on the Faint quarter.
- **The structural map:** the sword proves Gerolt sent him, but it "should not earn unlimited trust. Alaric's conduct determines whether Marta continues helping him." And "Empty" becomes "a social and legal verdict as well as a magical description."

## A proposed shape (six scenes)

All in Alaric's eyes, from dusk to late on the night of Day 2.

1. **Under the ship** (500–700 words).
   - They lie in the trees while the ship goes east over the river, towards the farm. The bell stops. The wall's torches are still moving.
   - Silas asks about the field again, properly this time: how many, what they wore, what was left standing. He listens to every answer. He doesn't say he believes him; the questions say it.
   - Alaric understands where the ship is going. Gerolt is lying out there.
2. **Along the south wall** (700–1,000).
   - In full dark they go down across the fields and along the backs of the houses built against the wall. Alaric sees the Faint quarter close up: how the houses lean on the stones, what the lanes smell like, who is still awake. That's the world without explaining it.
   - After the bell, soldiers are walking the lanes with lamps. There's one near miss, with Wena.
   - Silas keeps his word: he tells Alaric what he's checking, and they go to the inn together.
3. **The door** (800–1,200).
   - The inn's back door. Silas knocks. Marta opens it and sees Silas first. That's the small answer: what her face does, and what she says to him.
   - Then Wena, then the sword. Alaric holds it out himself: "The old fool sent me."
   - She knows the sword. "Where is he?" Alaric tells her. That's the chapter's first heart: he has to tell Gerolt's niece that her uncle is dead, and he believes it's his fault.
4. **The kitchen** (1,000–1,400).
   - She lets the boy and the dog in. Whether she lets Silas in is her call.
   - She sees to Alaric's ankle, and asks her questions while her hands work. He tells her what happened, briefly, since the reader has seen it all. He tells her it was his fault.
   - Then she finds out what he is. She reaches for his Affinity, or tests it, and finds nothing: not weak, nothing. "Empty" comes back. It isn't pitiful, it's dangerous: "Nobody can know."
5. **The patrol** (600–900).
   - Soldiers at the front of the inn after the bell. They're asking, on Natharul's word, about a boy with a dog.
   - Marta handles them without lying badly, using her standing in the town. Alaric listens from where she's hidden him, and understands what she risks by keeping him.
6. **The price** (400–700).
   - Alaric offers to go, so she isn't in danger because of him. That's the act that earns the shelter: he chooses her safety over his own.
   - She keeps him, but it has a price, and it's the next chapter's problem: Darcy, and what Kelmend has promised to do to the Faint quarter.

## What Alaric can and can't know

- He sees faces, hears words, and feels his ankle and his guilt. He doesn't know what the gorge was, only that something between Silas and Marta is old and bad.
- He doesn't know what the ship is beyond "Natharul's", or who is on it.
- He doesn't know how Affinity is read. He only sees what Marta does, and what her face does after.

## What could make this chapter 100/100?

1. **The door has to pay off three chapters of "Marta".** Readers have waited since Chapter 2. The moment needs one thing only she would do.
   - **Challenge:** the old version gave her a list of reactions in order (Alaric, Wena, Silas, the token). One true reaction is stronger than four.
2. **Her grief should be small and exact,** and the reader should feel it through Alaric, who caused it (as he believes). The most painful thing in the scene isn't her tears. It's that she has to comfort him, or refuses to.
3. **"Empty" must cost something concrete.** Not a speech about the law, but an action: she moves him out of sight, changes a plan, lies to a soldier, or looks at the door the way Gerolt did in Chapter 1. That echo would be the chapter's best line if it's earned.
4. **Silas and Marta: one line that lands, not a backstory.** The reader should come away knowing she has a right to shut the door on him, and that he'd stand outside it all night.
5. **Alaric has to earn the shelter by choosing,** not by being liked. Offering to leave is the proposal. The challenge is that it mustn't read as noble posing. It has to come from guilt, from not wanting another person to die for him.
6. **Too much for one chapter:** Marta, the grief, the verdict of "Empty", Silas's answer, a patrol, and Darcy's price. **Challenge:** Darcy should only be a hook at the end: a name, a date, a threat. It isn't explained here.
7. **The world through what's done, not explained.** The Faint quarter at night does the work of a page of exposition, if the details are chosen well.

## Round 1 questions

**A. Where it opens.**
- *Recommended:* straight on from Chapter 6: in the trees, under the ship.
- *Alternative:* start later, with them already at the wall in full dark. That's faster, but loses Silas's first real questions about the field.

**B. Silas starts to believe him.**
- *Recommended:* he asks properly about the field and listens, but doesn't say it.
- *Alternative:* he says it: "I thought you were lying."

**C. The way in.**
- *Recommended:* through the edge of the Faint quarter at night, so the reader sees it before Chapter 9 needs it.
- *Alternative:* along the outside of the wall, keeping to the dark, and the quarter waits for Chapter 9.
- Should Redd or Freya be glimpsed here, unnamed? *Recommended:* no. They get their own entrance in Chapter 9.

**D. Silas's promise.**
- *Recommended:* he keeps it. They go to the inn together, and Alaric shows her the sword himself. After Chapter 6, keeping it is the first small repair between them.
- *Alternative:* Silas goes to the door first anyway, and Alaric has to push in.

**E. Marta, who she is.** All of this is new, so it's your call.
1. **Her age and look.** The old version: about forty, red-haired, elegant. Is that kept?
2. **Her Affinity.** The old version: Eminent Wind, strong enough to sense the shape of someone's Affinity. Is that kept? It's the easiest way for "Empty" to be found out.
3. **Her manner.** The old version: sharp-tongued, observant, suspicious of motives, with influence from information and long friendships, not only her Affinity. Is that kept, or reset?
4. **Does Wena know her?** In the old version Gerolt sold her produce for the inn, so Wena runs to her.

**F. Marta and Silas at the door (the small answer).**
- What does she say to him first?
- Does she let him in?
- *My suggestion:* she says his name, and nothing else, for long enough that Alaric looks at Silas. She lets the boy and the dog in, and leaves Silas standing outside until Alaric says something, or until the patrol forces it.
- The old version: she turned cold, and refused to let him use the boy as an excuse to come back. That boundary is hers, and the story shouldn't treat it as something romance overcomes.

**G. Who tells her Gerolt is dead, and what does Alaric confess?**
- *Recommended:* Alaric tells her, and he tells her it's his fault.
- What does Marta say to that? It's the emotional centre, and it should be yours first.

**H. How "Empty" is found.**
- *Recommended:* Marta senses it (if E2 holds).
- *Alternatives:*
  - a test at the table, like Gerolt's flame in Chapter 1;
  - a guard's inspection that he only just avoids.
- And who says the word: Alaric, as in Chapter 1, or Marta, because Gerolt told her in a letter or a message? *Recommended:* Alaric. It's his word, and hearing someone else take it seriously is the turn.

**I. The patrol.**
- Keep it? *Recommended:* yes.
- Is it Mydean guards asking on Natharul's word, or an elf with them?

**J. What earns the shelter.**
- *Recommended:* he offers to leave so she isn't in danger, and that's what makes her keep him.
- *Alternative:* she names a price first, and he agrees to it.

**K. The ending and Darcy.**
- Does Chapter 8 end on Darcy's name and the threat to the Faint quarter, or on something quieter (the attic, Marta and Silas arguing low downstairs), with Darcy in Chapter 9?
- Who is Darcy to Marta now? The old version: her childhood friend. That's your call.

**L. Title.** Later. A note from Chapter 6's design: the old title *The Road Owed to the Dead* fits Chapter 8, "because that's where the road ends".

## The author's working design (1 October)

Kept word for word in `Chapter 8 - Working Design.md`. In short: sixteen beats from the trees above Kelmend to Marta's back door, dusk into night on Day 2, with Silas and Alaric working together under pressure for the first time. There are two destroyers: the king's, which Seralune watched leave in Chapter 5, and a regional one already in Mydea, which is the ship over Kelmend in Chapter 6. The search behind it comes from a neighbour who saw Gerolt leave with a boy and a dog, and from the bodies found in the forest. Silas can't knock for himself, so the chapter ends with Alaric knocking.

It's a strong design. It's honest about what each person can know (Liluth never saw his face; the neighbour saw from two fields away by firelight), every obstruction changes the route, and Alaric is given real work instead of another "wait here". Chapter 2 does have the neighbour's lit window ("Two fields over, a single window was lit."), and the bodies match the page: two riders burned by Gerolt, three killed by Silas, one more on the far bank in Chapter 3.

**Length.** The budget is 5,200. Per-scene budgets have run about a quarter high on every chapter so far, so expect **4,000–5,000** at the density you like. That's on target. If it comes in short, the place to grow it is the guards (beats 9–11) and the talk in cover (beat 13), not more hiding.

## Claude's review: round 2

### 1. Marta is the first person they'd question

She's Gerolt's niece and keeps an inn in the same town. Anyone investigating a dead farmer asks who his family is, and Kelmend's guards know Gerolt (the couple at the fence call him "the old fool"). As designed, the search doesn't think of her, so the knock is safer than it should be.

*Recommended:* use it. In beat 9 the guards mention that someone is to question "the farmer's niece" at the inn tonight. It's close to the old canon, where soldiers said the scarred elf would question Marta after closing.
- Alaric learns Marta is Gerolt's niece. That's a small answer, and it hurts: he's about to tell Gerolt's family.
- Beat 13 stops being an abstract fear. They're already coming to her, so he either warns her or brings the danger to her door himself.
- The knock becomes a race.

*Alternative:* officials have already told her at dusk that Gerolt was found dead. She knows before Alaric knocks, and the next chapter's heart becomes "how" and "it was my fault", not "he's dead".

### 2. A destroyer stationed in Mydea breaks a settled rule

Decisions: Natharul's scouts are all over Mydea and "never seen", "to keep people calm". Silas's shock in Chapter 6 comes from seeing a Natharul warship openly over Mydea. If it's been anchored in Mydea with the king's permission, Kelmend would have seen it, and so would Silas.

*Recommended:* the regional destroyer is stationed just over the border, on Natharul's side, out of sight of Mydean towns. Tonight is the first time anyone in Kelmend has seen one, which is why the bell rings. That keeps Silas's shock, the quiet cooperation at the bridge in Chapter 3, and your two-ship idea.

### 3. Which ship looks like what

Chapter 5's ship is "long and dark and plated all over", with blue-green light underneath. Chapter 6's has "dark red plating", "spines beneath" and the same blue-green light. Decisions gave the *Agrius* look (red hull, spines) to the royal warship and left a note to reconcile it with Chapter 5.

*Recommended:* the *Agrius* look belongs to the regional destroyer. Chapter 6 already describes it, so nothing needs rewriting, and the reconcile note closes: they're two different ships. The shared blue-green light makes them the same kind of ship.

### 4. The ending: the knock, or the door opening

The design ends on the knock. Readers have waited for Marta since Chapter 2, and Chapter 6 already ended on a threshold. Decisions also says the small answer about Silas and Marta comes "most naturally when Marta sees Silas at her door".

*Recommended:* the door opens. She sees Silas first, over Alaric's shoulder, and the chapter ends on what her face does, or on his name and nothing else. It's one beat, it pays the reader before the cliffhanger (your 100/100 point 9), and the next chapter opens with her.

*Alternative:* end on the knock, as designed.

### 5. Silas's way through the wall (Your call)

"A former maintenance access… from earlier work" gives Silas a past job in Kelmend. That's new backstory.

*Recommended:* keep it unexplained on the page. He knows the way, and Alaric can see he's used it before. If you want a reason, it's yours to give.

### 6. Messages

*Recommended:* no fast messaging on the page in Volume 1. Reports travel by rider, and by ship where there is one. That keeps the delays and the half-informed guards your design wants, and it needs no new lore. How the king hears about Seralune can be settled when a chapter needs it.

### 7. What the search orders say

*Recommended:* find the boy with the big dog and bring him in alive. A helper is only a rumour among the guards ("whoever cut down the three by the river"), not an order.

### 8. Showing the search

*Recommended:* only through what Alaric sees and overhears, with no interlude. Your design leans this way already.

### 9. The outline moves along by one

With Marta in the next Alaric chapter, the order becomes: Chapter 9, Marta; Chapter 10, Seralune; Chapter 11, the Faint quarter, with Redd and Freya. *Recommended:* yes. I'll update the outline once you confirm.
