# Chapter 5 – The Shape of Absence

"What's wrong?" she whispered.

At last, he looked at her. He went to sheathe his sword without looking, but the point missed and scraped across the plates at his hip, and he had to look down to find the sheath. She had watched him put that sword away all her life, and he had never once needed to look.

Then his arms came round her.

They closed so hard that they drove the breath out of her. One arm locked across her back and his hand held her head against his chest, and the edges of his armour dug into her ribs and caught her cut hand between them. It hurt. She let it, for as long as she could bear it.

"Thaer," she said into his chest. "You're hurting me."

He let go at once, but he didn't step back. His hands stayed on her shoulders, not quite steady, while he took her in: her face, the blood dried down her wrist, her bare feet on the stone. He was still breathing hard.

"How long have you been awake?"

"I don't know." Her voice came out cracked and dry, and she had to swallow before she could go on. "All day, I think. I called and called, and nobody came."

His hands tightened on her shoulders. "I'm here now, Seralune."

She had waited all day to hear him say it.

"Where were you? I thought you'd never come."

His eyes went back to the crystal.

She turned round, and for the first time she saw it. It stood in the middle of the chamber, far taller than Thaer, and the light from the doorway ran along its smooth sides. The opening she had reached into went up one side of it, taller than she was, and the pieces that had broken away lay all round it, glittering on the floor. She had spent the whole day alone with it, and never once seen it.

"What is that thing, Thaer?" She could still feel it, cold, drawing at her through her fingers, and she pressed her hand into her dress. "What is this place?"

He didn't answer. He took her by the arm, turned her away from the crystal and walked her out through the doorway he had cut, over the two halves of the door lying on the floor.

There was a passage outside, with lamps burning along its walls. Her eyes watered, and she blinked until she could make out the stone, and the stairs at the far end of it, going up out of sight. She didn't know any of it.

She stopped walking. His hand dragged at her arm for a step, and then he stopped too.

"Where are we? What happened to me? Thaer, please—"

"Not here." His eyes went past her to the broken doorway. "No more questions, Seralune. Not here."

Then there were footsteps above them, a great many, coming down fast. Thaer let go of her arm and stepped in front of her, with his hand on his sword.

Guards came down into the lamplight with their swords drawn. The first of them saw her and stopped so suddenly that the ones behind walked into his back, and after that none of them came any further. They stood on the stairs and stared at her. The nearest one's knuckles had gone white on his hilt, and the one behind him backed up a step without seeming to know he'd done it.

Her mana crowded up under her ribs.

An old man walked down between them. The guards stood aside for him without being told, and he stopped at the bottom, in the lamplight. His hair was white and thin, and his face was deeply lined. She didn't know him.

He bowed to her, low. "Your Highness."

Then he turned to Thaer. "Thaeroval." He looked from the ruined door to the sword at Thaer's hip. "What have you done?"

She looked at the old man properly. There was something in his face under all those lines, and the longer she looked, the more of it she knew, until she could see the young man she remembered.

"Leorin?"

He glanced at her, and turned back to Thaer. "You should never have opened that door."

"Would you rather I'd left her in there?"

Leorin didn't answer.

*He'd have left me in there.*

"I'm standing right here, you know. You can just ask me."

Nobody did. Leorin's eyes came to her then, and stayed longer than they had yet, before they dropped to the floor. Thaer took her arm again, and this time she was glad of it.

"Come with me, Seralune, and stay close. Don't speak to anyone until we're upstairs."

*Upstairs, then. He'll tell me everything then.*

Leorin stepped back against the wall to let them pass. As she went by him, he said, so quietly that she barely heard it, "Forgive me, Seralune." The guards on the stairs got out of her way.

The stairs went on and on. Thaer took them fast, with his hand closed round her arm, and he didn't look at her once. Her legs had been shaking since she stood up in that room, and her bare feet were numb on the stone. By the first turning, she was leaning on him with every step. The guards followed at a distance, with their swords still out, and Leorin's voice went on among them, too low to make out.

*Leorin isn't old. And my own cousin bowed to me like he'd never seen me before.*

She looked up at Thaer. He was watching the stairs ahead of them, and his face was the one she had seen this morning, not a day older.

*And Thaer hasn't changed at all.*

Partway up, she heard it. It was so faint at first that she thought it was her own blood in her ears. Then there was no mistaking it.

The falls.

*So I was in the palace after all. Somewhere very deep in it.*

The sound grew with every turn of the stairs, until it was under everything again, as it always had been.

At the top, that sound was the only thing she knew. The falls were off to her left, exactly where they should have been, but the corridor ran straight on where it should have turned towards them, and there were doors all along it where she remembered bare wall.

There were servants in the corridor, even at this hour, and they pressed themselves back against the walls as Thaer brought her past. Some of them bowed. Some forgot to, until the person beside them pulled them down. Not one of them would look at her for long, and she didn't know a single face.

At the end of the row, an old servant bowed like the rest, and as he straightened, he was smiling at her. Only a little.

*I've never seen him before in my life, and he's the first person who's smiled at me.*

She looked back for him at the corner. He was gone.

Her knees gave at the next stair. Thaer caught her against his side before she could fall, and kept walking, and she had to go with him.

Then cold air touched her face, and the sound of the falls was all around her.

They had come out onto a long gallery, open along one side to the night. The falls were right there beside the palace. The water poured down from high above them, white under the moon, and dropped past the gallery so far into the forest below that she couldn't see where it landed. The spray drifted across and settled cold on her cheeks.

The forest stretched further than she could see, and there were lights down among the trees where the city was, far more of them than she remembered, and they ran on and on to either side.

Something was moving over the forest.

It was so big that at first she took it for a cloud. Then she saw the light along its underside, lines of it, blue-green, running the whole length of it. It was long and dark and plated all over, and it hummed so low that she felt it in her chest more than she heard it over the falls.

She couldn't make sense of it at first. Then the shape of it came together, and she knew it for a ship. She had seen ships fly before, but never one that size.

Thaer had stopped beside her. He watched it until it was far out over the forest, going away from them, and he didn't say a word.

She looked away from it, up the falls to the top, where the royal tree had always stood.

It was still there, with its branches reaching out over the water on both sides, and it was so tall that she had to tip her head back to see the top of it.

*How in the world did it grow so big? It was nowhere near that size this morning.*

*A tree doesn't grow like that in a year. Leorin didn't go white in one. So it's been…*

She didn't let herself finish it.

She turned to him. "Thaer, how long was I in there?"

He wouldn't even look at her. He only took her arm again, and led her along the gallery and through a door into a room she didn't know.

Someone had made it ready. A fire was burning in the hearth and lamps were lit in the corners, and the covers on the bed had been folded back. There was a jug of water on the table, with a cup and a tray of bread and fruit beside it, and a basin on a stand by the hearth, with clean linen folded next to it. The shutters over the window stood open, so that the falls were loud in the room.

None of it was hers.

She went straight to the water. The first swallow hurt her throat, and she drank the whole cup anyway, and poured another with a hand that wouldn't keep still.

When she looked round, Thaer hadn't come any further than the doorway. "We're upstairs," she said.

"Get some rest. There are things I have to see to, and I'll be back as soon as I'm able."

He went out, and the door shut behind him. She started after him, and had to catch hold of the bedpost instead.

There was a maid standing in the corner by the door, with her hands folded in front of her and her eyes lowered. She must have been there all along.

"Where's my mother?"

"I couldn't say, Your Highness."

"Then fetch Elowen. She'll know."

"I've never heard of her, Your Highness."

Seralune stared at her.

*Never heard of Elowen? How? She's by my side every day. She sat up with me every night until I could read, with her finger under every word, and this girl has never even heard her name.*

*So she's new, or she's lying to me, or…*

She let out a long breath. "Well. This is getting me nowhere." The maid hadn't moved. "You do have a name, I take it?"

"Nereth, Your Highness."

Seralune couldn't keep still. She walked from the fire to the window and back again, and her legs still shook, and she walked anyway.

Then there were voices outside.

She crossed to the door and put her ear to the wood. Nereth was an arm's length away in her corner, with her eyes on the floor.

The voices were low, and they ran together through the wood, so that she couldn't make out a word. Footsteps went by somewhere beyond them, and the voices stopped until they had gone.

*Is that Thaer speaking? And who else?*

One of them rose, and the words came through whole.

"…needs to be sealed, Thaeroval, and tonight."

It was Leorin.

Then she heard Thaer, angry, and loud enough now that she knew him.

"At least wait until my father has returned. He'll have answers."

"He'll be gone for weeks. Until he's back, I speak for him."

"Not about her." Something struck the wall beside the door, so hard that the wood jumped against her cheek. "No one touches my sister."

After that the voices dropped too low to hear.

She stepped back from the door so fast that her legs nearly went, and sat down hard on the edge of the bed.

*Who? Me? Sealed? No.*

*Is that what that room was?*

*People don't sleep inside crystal. That's what I told myself.*

*Weeks. That ship Thaer watched go… Was Father on it?*

Outside, the voices had stopped. She sat with her hands pressed together in her lap, and made herself think.

*Very well. I won't say a word about what I heard. Let's see if my brother tells me himself.*

After a while there were two knocks, and Thaer came in before she could answer, the way he always had, and shut the door behind him. The knuckles of his right hand were scraped raw.

"What's happening, Thaer? Why is everyone acting like this?"

He stood just inside the door and said nothing.

"I don't know anyone here, Thaer. You're all I have, and you won't even look at me. Did I do something wrong?"

"No." It came at once.

For a breath he was her brother again, and she wanted so badly to believe him that it hurt.

"Then what was that room, Thaer?"

His eyes went to the tray on the table. "You haven't eaten."

She looked at him. He was still looking at the tray.

She was on her feet now. "I sat in that room all day, Thaeroval, and nobody came. Now I want answers, and I want them now."

At his full name, his eyes came up to hers at last.

"When you've slept—"

"Don't you dare send me to bed like a child!"

He turned for the door. She caught his arm, and the cut in her palm split open again under her grip, and she held on anyway.

"Leorin's an old man, Thaer. What happened while I was in that room?"

He stood with his back to her. His arm had gone stiff where she held it, and her blood was on his sleeve.

He turned round. "If it were up to me, you'd—" He stopped. "It isn't up to me."

His face closed again. "Seralune, get some rest. I'll be back in the morning."

He took her hand from his arm, gently, and looked at the cut. Then he let go of her and went to the door.

"You said you'd be back before evening, too."

His hand stopped on the door. Then he opened it and went out, and the lock turned.

She ran to the door, grabbed the handle and shook it with both hands. The cut split wider, and she didn't care. "Thaer! Thaeroval, come back here! Don't you ignore me!"

His footsteps stopped.

Thaer's voice came through the door, low: "Leave her be." His footsteps went on.

*It isn't up to him. Then who is it up to?*

She didn't sleep. She sat on the bed while the fire burned low, and Nereth stood in her corner with her hands folded.

*Father will listen to me. He has to.*

*And Mother? Mother would never let them do this to me.*

Sometime in the night, she felt a blow through the soles of her feet before she heard it, dull and heavy, and then another, and another, slow and steady.

*What are those noises? They're coming from under the palace, from where I just was.*

"What are they doing down there?"

"I couldn't say, Your Highness."

"Of course. No one can say anything." The next blow came up through the floor, and the one after it, and she was on her feet and shouting before she knew she was going to. "*Why can't anyone just say what is going on?*"

A key scraped in the lock, the door banged open, and two guards came in with their swords out.

Before Seralune could move, Nereth was standing between her and the swords. "Her Highness is tired," she said, in the same even voice she'd used all evening, with the swords a step from her. "She needs to rest. That's all."

"Nereth, get out of the way."

Nereth didn't move.

The second guard looked past Nereth at Seralune, and down at her bare feet, and lowered his sword. "Leave it."

"And if she—"

"Then I'll call you," Nereth said.

The first guard kept his eyes on Seralune all the way to the door. It shut behind them, and she heard the key turn.

"I'm sorry," Seralune said to Nereth's back. "I shouldn't have shouted at you. None of this is your doing."

Nereth turned round. "No, Your Highness. You shouldn't have."

Nereth's eyes had gone to Seralune's hand, where the cut had opened again. "You're bleeding. May I?"

Seralune held it out, and Nereth wet the linen at the basin, then cleaned the cut and bound it. Neither of them said anything. Nereth's hands were quick and careful, and they didn't shake, though the swords had been a step from her.

The binding was neat and firm. "Thank you," Seralune said.

Nereth bowed her head, and went back to her corner.

"Sit down, Nereth. You've been standing there since I came in."

"I'm quite all right, Your Highness."

"Sit down."

Nereth sat on the very edge of the chair by the hearth, with her back straight and her hands folded in her lap.

The blows went on. Seralune listened to them from the bed, and thought of the crystal standing split open in that room far below her, and of the word that had come through the door.

*Whatever they're doing down there, they're doing it to that thing.*

She got up and went to the window. The shutters still stood open, and the spray blew in cold off the falls. She leaned out as far as she dared. The wall dropped away beneath her, smooth and wet, all the way down to where the falls met the forest, so far below that she couldn't see the bottom.

She stayed there until the cold had got into her hands. Then she went back to the bed and sat with her back against the wall, and listened to every blow, and waited for morning, and for Thaer.

When the window began to grey, the blows stopped.

*They've stopped. Whatever they were doing, it's done.*

There were footsteps in the corridor.

Two knocks came.

The door stayed shut.

She got up. She had been waiting for him all night. "Thaer?"

The lock turned.
