# Chapter 2: What Changed from the Old Chapter

Written on 26 September 2026. This compares the new `Chapter 2 - The Price of a Voice.md` with the old version, `Old - Before Re-plan/Volume 1 - Rewrites/Chapter 2 - The Price of a Voice (Rewrite).md`. Most of the chapter changed, so the comparison goes scene by scene and shows the key passages as before and after. It doesn't go paragraph by paragraph.

- The quotes below show the chapter after revision 1, which took out every comparison Alaric couldn't make (see `Chapter 2 - Changes.md`).
- The first new version is saved unchanged as `Drafts/Chapter 2 - The Price of a Voice (Draft 1).md`. From now on, every round of changes uses a change list against it, as Chapter 1 did.
- Every decision this chapter carries is in `Chapter 2 - Design.md`.

## At a glance

- **Length:** the old chapter was 3,391 words; the new one is 2,907. It's shorter even though the ending moved in from old Chapter 3. The design bible allows "a short, devastating chapter", but tell me if you want more room anywhere, especially in the farewell.
- **Shape:**
  - four movements with no scene breaks: the door, the fight, the farewell and the wheat;
  - all in the boy's eyes;
  - he becomes "Alaric" in the narration once the name returns.
- **Gerolt:**
  - dry to the end, with his humour dropping only at "There you are";
  - he gives away the sword, so his last stand is a hand of fire.
- **Principle 5:** every old line that explained a moment has gone.

## Your call

These were new. Your answers are recorded after each one, and the changes they led to are in `Chapter 2 - Changes.md`.

1. **The second scout laughs** before we see her: "Somewhere beyond the wall, a woman laughed." It shows how sure of themselves they are, and it tells the boy there are two. **Answered:** keep.
2. **The elf's entrance.** "'There,' he said. 'Now you've no door to answer.' / He stepped over the threshold with his hands empty, and didn't look where he put his feet." His contempt is what gets him killed. **Answered:** cockier. He now laughs as he steps in: "Testing my patience, old man. Now you've no door to answer."
3. **The boy's only other sight of magic.** Right after the wind, one line: "Gerolt's flame had sat in his palm no bigger than a candle's." It's a single comparison, with no comment on it. **Answered:** keep.
4. **The sword skids** to "an arm's length from the boy's hand" in the fight. Later, Gerolt tells him to fetch it. **Answered:** keep.
5. **"Tell her the old fool sent you."** It hints that Marta knows him well, without saying she's his niece. **Answered:** keep, and the line now ends "in a small cough, and blood began to run from the corner of his mouth".
6. **"Took you three tries to sit up at midday."** This is Gerolt's dry reply when the boy offers to carry him. **Answered:** cut.
7. **The hot hand.** Gerolt's hand on his wrist turns from warm to hot, "hot, and getting hotter". Gerolt takes it away and closes it into a fist. It's the only sign on the page that the boy's staying holds back his fire. **Answered:** rework. "It doesn't sound like Cid speaking; it sounds like a computer monologue." Reworked: "Careful, lad," he said. "I run hot."
8. **"You're a stubborn little bastard, Empty."** This is the one time he uses "Empty" as a name. **Answered:** keep, but without "Empty". Gerolt never calls him Empty in this chapter.
9. **The last line:** "Alaric lay in the wheat with Gerolt's sword beneath him and watched the house burn." **Answered:** "perfect".
10. **Where the coat went.** In Chapter 1, his one coat hangs by the door. So the wind tears it off its peg, and the boy presses Gerolt's shirt to the wound. **Answered:** keep.

## 1. The door

**The threat.** The author asked for elves who are "full of themselves".

> **Before:** "Farmer. Open the door. Tell us what happened in your field, and no other household need answer for your silence."
>
> The boy understood every word of that. Each one was clean and exact and effortless, and each one dripped with the distaste of someone who thought talking to the people inside was already more courtesy than they'd earned.
>
> A man's voice. Young, maybe. Very used to being obeyed.
>
> **After:** "Farmer, answer this damn door before we start knocking down others because of your silence."
>
> He didn't raise his voice. Somewhere beyond the wall, a woman laughed.

The line is yours. The old paragraph told us he was contemptuous; now his calm and her laugh show it. The old second threat ("Every moment you keep us waiting is added to your village's debt.") is cut, because the threat has already been made once.

**Gerolt's choice** (question 8: the neighbours).

> **Before:** Gerolt's hand left his shoulder.
>
> **After:** Gerolt's fingers dug into the boy's shoulder. He looked at the door. Then he looked down at the boy, and kept looking, long enough that the boy felt it.
>
> His hand left the boy's shoulder.

**The sword.** "At the table… a floorboard" pays off the glance at the end of Chapter 1.

> **Before:** Gerolt picked it up like the years since he'd last held it hadn't happened.
>
> The boy stared.
>
> The grumbling old farmer who'd hauled him through the wheat was gone. Or maybe this man had been underneath him the whole time.
>
> **After:** Gerolt picked it up without looking at it.

The last line of the old passage explained what the moment meant (principle 5).

**The wind.**

> **Before:** **KRAAACK.** … Wind『Affinity』. / That was what Gerolt had asked him to reach for, beside the fire. Not some quiet idea you talked about over stew. A force that could rip a home apart before the people inside it had the breath to scream.
>
> **After:** An iron pot rolled across the floor in widening circles and stopped.
>
> Gerolt's flame had sat in his palm no bigger than a candle's.

Bold is kept for THOOM, so the sound effect has gone. The old passage explained magic; the new line only sets two sights side by side.

**The elf in the doorway.**

> **Before:** "Human," he called into the dark. "You were offered the mercy of answering alone."
>
> **After:** He laughed as he stepped over the threshold, his hands empty. "Testing my patience, old man. Now you've no door to answer."

## 2. The fight

**The first elf.** You asked: "let him choke on his own blood". Everything up to the clicking is kept; this is added after it:

> **New:** …every breath he took brought up more blood, frothing pink at his lips and running down the steel into the collar of his armour. He choked on it. He choked again, and his heels scraped at the boards, and the hand on Gerolt's wrist began to shake.

His scream now comes "in the layered voice, every throat of it at once".

**Breaking the silence.**

> **Before:** Silence would keep the boy hidden. / "GEROLT!" / The name ripped out of him.
>
> **After:** "GEROLT!"

Chapter 1 already showed him choosing silence, so the shout doesn't need a sentence setting it up.

**The second scout.** You asked for "a boy seeing someone getting chopped up with a sword".

> **Before:** The edge split her left brow, crushed through the eye beneath it and tore across the bridge of her nose, leaving her cheek hanging open to the teeth. Blood sheeted down her face. … She threw up an arm, and the blade broke straight through her metal bracer and bit into the bone. Her hand jerked once, then hung uselessly off the ruined limb.
>
> **After:** The edge opened her left brow, went through the eye beneath it and dragged across the bridge of her nose, and her cheek fell open to the teeth. What had been her eye ran down her face with the blood. She was still screaming when he swung again.
>
> She threw up her arm. The blade broke through her metal bracer and stopped in the bone. When Gerolt wrenched it out, her hand hung from the end of her arm at an angle no hand should make.
>
> He swung a third time, and the earth rose to meet it.

**Why they came.** This follows the agreed line table. He said "Natharul" in Chapter 1.

> **Before:** "Natharul scouts." Gerolt swallowed… "Saw the field, maybe. Saw my light." … "Doesn't matter now. She got away."
>
> **After:** "Doesn't matter now." Gerolt swallowed, and every line in his face tightened with it. "She got away."

**The echo of Chapter 1** (new):

> "I've got you," the boy said.
>
> Gerolt's hand settled over his instead of pushing it away.

## 3. The farewell

**The token becomes the sword.**

> **Before:** He pulled out a small iron token, dark with age and worn almost smooth. A split shield was stamped into one side. … "West. Across the river." … "Kelmend. Find the inn… near the southern gate." … "Ask for Marta. Give her that." … "Tell her… Gerolt sent you."
>
> **After:** "Sword," he said. … "Get it." / The boy brought it over. It was heavier than it looked, and the grip was slick. Gerolt didn't take it. He folded the boy's fingers around the grip and held them there. / "West," he said. "Across the river. Kelmend." … "There's an inn by the south gate. Marta. Show her that. She'll know it." … "Tell her the old fool sent you." It ended in a small cough, and blood began to run from the corner of his mouth.

**His peace, lost.** This is an echo of Chapter 1.

> **Before:** His eyes moved around the ruined cabin. The broken table. The dead hearth. The stool on its side. The boy could still see him sitting there with his mug, asking questions nobody could answer.
>
> **After:** Gerolt's eyes went around the cabin. The table on its side. The dead hearth. The stool knocked over, and his dented mug lying in the ash.

**Cut under principle 5:**

- "This man had carried him out of a field of corpses. Washed the blood off his face. Fed him…"
- "If he left, Gerolt would still be lying right here."
- "Don't make me die watching you stop."
- "That's why you have to take her."

**Kept word for word:** "It's noth—" / "Can't walk. Look at me, lad." / "Take Wena." / "You already did." / "I'm not leaving you." / "Wasn't asking." / "On that field… Found one lad still breathing." / "She'll stay if you do."

**"Empty."**

> **Before:** "Stubborn little bastard," Gerolt murmured. "All that emptiness, and still no room in you for sense."
>
> **After:** "You're a stubborn little bastard."

**The name.** It comes through Chapter 1's vision (the reaching hand and the mouth), with no gold.

> **Before:** The door hung whole on its hinges *and* lay shattered against the wall. The candle burned on the table *and* died between Gerolt's fingers. A warm wooden bowl sat in the boy's hands, hit the floor, and was whole again. … A single thread of light stretched across it, trembling under some impossible strain. At its furthest end, something moved.

>

>
> The hand reached out of the white. It opened, closed on empty air, and reached again.
>

>

**Cut under principle 5:**

- "It *caught*. / He didn't remember a face turning towards it. A hand writing it. Anyone calling it across a room. But every scattered piece of him answered to it anyway."

**Kept:** "My name." / "It's Alaric." / "Alaric." / "There you are."
