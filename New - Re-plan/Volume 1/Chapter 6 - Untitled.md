# Chapter 6

Silas nudged his boot with the toe of his own.

"Up, boy. You've had a few hours. We need to get moving."

Alaric's arm had gone numb under Wena. He eased it out from under her and sat up, and everything that had been in the river last night ached at once. His clothes had dried stiff on him while he slept, and they still smelled of the water.

For a few breaths he only hurt. Then he saw Gerolt's sword lying in the dirt beside him, where it had slid off his knees, and he remembered why he had it.

There was daylight at the entrance now, coming in through the gaps in the bush. The fire was cold, and the bedroll lay rolled up and tied with cord.

*We. So he meant it.*

"You're coming all the way to Kelmend?"

"I'm not sending you up to those guards on your own. On your feet, then."

Silas bent to pick up the sack. He still hadn't asked Alaric's name.

Alaric got up, steadying himself against the wall. The roof was too low for him to stand straight, so he stood bent under it and waited to find out whether his legs meant to hold him.

Silas had carried the sack over to the cold fire and crouched to open it. He picked at the knot with one hand. The other was wound round and round with a strip of cloth, and he kept it half closed and out of his way. Alaric remembered the elf, and the blade glowing in it, and Silas shaking that hand out afterwards with his breath hissing through his teeth.

Silas got the knot open and turned to reach for the water skin.

"Your dog's a thief."

Alaric looked down. Wena had her nose inside the sack.

"She's hungry."

"So am I. Get her head out of my breakfast."

He lifted the sack as Alaric caught her collar. Wena came away with a piece of bread between her teeth.

"Wena, don't—"

She swallowed it.

Silas looked into the sack. "Oh, for fuck's sake."

Alaric laughed, still holding her collar. Wena was trying to follow the sack again.

"Here." Silas broke an end off the next piece and held it out to Alaric. "Give her that before she takes the rest. And eat yours, will you? That's what I got it out for."

Alaric gave Wena the end, and she took it so fast that he checked his fingers afterwards. Then he ate his own. It was hard, and it had been in the sack a while, and he hadn't known how hungry he was until it was in his mouth. Silas watched him take the first bite, then went back to the water skin and didn't look again.

When the bread was gone, Alaric picked up the sword. There was no belt and no sheath to carry it in, only the blade and the grip. He took it by the grip, the way he'd carried it through the trees last night.

Silas glanced from the blade to him.

"Keep the point down. If you fall on that, I'm not carrying you."

He slung the bedroll over his shoulder, pulled the branches aside at the entrance and ducked out.

Outside, the morning was bright and cold. Last night there had been trees, and the river, and the fire behind them, and not much else he could make out. Now he could see where he was: the bush they had come through, a slope of trees going down to the water, and the river itself, running fast and brown below them. On the far side the trees climbed away from the bank, and beyond them, further off than he'd have guessed, smoke was still going up into the sky.

He stopped.

Gerolt had set that fire himself. It was still burning.

Wena stood beside him with her nose lifted towards the far bank, and didn't move.

*He's still over there, up in the trees where we left him. Somebody ought to bury him, and there's nobody left to do it but me.*

He looked at the river that had taken him under last night, and at the far bank, where the torches had been, and he didn't ask Silas to take him back.

"Boy."

Silas had stopped further along the path. Alaric kept looking across the river.

"Just a moment."

Silas didn't answer. His footsteps didn't start again either.

Wena leaned against Alaric's leg. He lowered his hand to her neck and stood with her a little longer.

*Kelmend. Over the river. Marta, at the inn by the south gate.*

Then he turned away.

"Come on, Wena."

He followed several paces behind Silas, with Wena at his knee.

The path, if it was one, ran along the slope above the river for a while, then turned away from the water into the trees. Whenever the ground rose, Alaric could see the smoke behind them between the trunks, and every time, he looked. The sword grew heavier as the morning went on. He changed hands, and changed back, and still it pulled at his arm.

Silas went round the brambles Alaric went through, and under the branches Alaric walked into. He never seemed to hurry, and somehow the gap between them kept getting wider anyway.

"How far is Kelmend?"

"Far enough that you'll want to walk faster than that."

"Could you just tell me?"

Silas glanced back. "Keep moving, and we'll get there before dark."

*Only the rest of the day, then. Lovely.*

Alaric stepped over the root Silas had gone round. "Wouldn't the road be quicker?"

"It would. It also takes you straight to the gate, and you saw who those guards were keeping company with."

"So how do we get in?"

"Not by the gate. I'll find us a way in when we're closer."

That was all. Silas walked on, and Alaric walked after him and thought about the bridge: the four helmets round the fire, the rider speaking down to them, and one guard nodding up at him and nodding again.

Twice after that, Silas stopped dead and stood with his head turned, listening, and Alaric stopped too and held Wena still. The second time, he heard it as well: horses, somewhere off to their left, a long way off and going away.

"Riders?" he whispered.

"On the road." Silas waited until the sound had gone. "That's why we're not on it."

A while later, Wena's head came up. She pulled towards a gap in the bushes beside the path, and Alaric caught her collar and smelled it a moment after she had. Something was dead in there.

Silas pushed through the gap ahead of them, and Alaric followed with Wena.

It had been a deer. Most of it was gone. What was left lay open on the ground with the ribs broken outwards, and the leaves around it had been flattened and churned and soaked with blood. Flies rose off it and settled again. In the soft earth beside it was a footprint, longer than Silas's boot and nearly twice as wide.

Silas stopped beside the remains of the deer. He turned one of its legs with his boot.

"Ogre."

"What's an ogre?"

"Big ugly bastard. Eats anything it can get hold of. Don't let your dog go pushing into the brush."

Alaric looked between the trees. "Is it still here?"

"That one's had its fill, at least. It'll be lying up somewhere, I'd say." Silas stepped past the carcass. "Come on. Keep her with you."

Alaric stood over the footprint before he followed. He kept one hand on Wena's collar for a long way after that, with the sword in the other, and looked twice at anything among the trees that was big enough to be lying up. None of it moved. By the time the trees began to thin, he found he had let go of her collar.

Near midday, Silas put a hand back to stop him and dropped to one knee at the edge of the trees. Alaric crouched beside him and pulled Wena down with him.

Below them, the ground opened into a field of cut stubble with a small house at the far side of it and smoke going up from its chimney. A few hens were scratching in the yard. Nearer, where the field met the trees, a man and a woman were mending a fence.

The woman held the fence rail while the man worked at its end.

"Up a bit," the man said.

"It is up."

"Up a bit more, then."

She lifted it, and said something under her breath that made him laugh, and he hammered.

Alaric watched them. They had a bundle of new stakes lying in the grass, and they didn't hurry. He wanted to go down to them. He wanted to stand at that fence and ask them something ordinary, and have somebody answer who wasn't dying, or chasing him, or Silas.

"Still smoking over at Gerolt's," the woman said.

"Burning off that back field again, I suppose. Told the old fool he'd smoke us out one day."

Alaric's hand closed on the grip of the sword.

*The old fool.* Gerolt had called himself that, at the river, with blood on his mouth.

"Someone ought to go over," she said. "See if he's all right."

The man looked towards the river. "Not past those riders. We'll leave it till they've gone."

*They don't know. They'll wait till the riders go, and then one of them will walk over there and find him.*

Alaric looked at Silas.

"Do you know them?" he whispered.

Silas's eyes stayed on the woman.

"Silas?"

Silas turned away from the fence.

"Move."

He went back into the trees, low and fast, and didn't wait to see if Alaric was following. Alaric caught up with him only when the farm was well behind them and he couldn't hear the man's hammer any more.

"You knew him, didn't you? Back there, when she said his name, you didn't hear a word I said."

"Watch where you're putting your feet."

"Silas, I asked you last night. You never answered."

"I heard you just fine. Leave it."

Alaric followed him over a fallen trunk.

"Last night you said I wasn't bringing any more elves to your door. This morning you rolled up your bed to walk me to Kelmend." He waited, and got nothing. "And when I said Marta—"

"She keeps an inn. Half of Kelmend knows her."

"Then tell me about her. Is there a reason I shouldn't go there? Because if you know one, you could just tell me."

Silas stopped and looked back at him.

"All right. Listen. Here's what we're doing when we reach Kelmend."

He pointed up through the trees, to where the ground climbed towards a bare rise.

"Kelmend's past that rise. You wait outside the wall with the dog. I'll take the sword to Marta and find out whether it's safe."

Alaric looked down at the hilt in his hand.

"He told *me* to show it to her."

"And if they're watching the inn? You walk in, they take you, and they take her along with you."

"Then we wait till it's dark. You check first. I'm not asking you to walk straight up to the guards." Alaric looked at him again. "But I'm going in with you, and I'm showing her the sword."

Silas looked down the slope.

"We'll see."

"Silas—"

"Stay here a minute. I need a look at what's below us."

He was already moving between the trees.

*We'll see.*

Alaric watched until he could no longer make out Silas's cloak.

He could sit down here and wait for him. It would be easy. He'd been doing what he was told all day.

*So he takes the sword to Marta, and I sit outside with Wena until he decides what happens next. What if she won't let him in? Am I supposed to go somewhere else because he says so?*

He shifted the sword in his hand. Gerolt had pressed that hilt into his fingers and told him where to go.

*I can keep off the road. The town's beyond that rise—he pointed it out himself. I'll find the south gate, and then the inn.*

Wena was looking in the direction Silas had gone. Alaric touched her shoulder.

"Come on, girl. This way."

She didn't move at first.

*And that ogre's eaten already. Silas said it'd be lying up somewhere. I just have to get through here.*

He went on, and after a few steps she followed.

The slope was steep, and his legs were heavy from the river and the long walk, but he climbed it faster than he'd walked all day. Nobody was ahead of him deciding where he went. It felt good, and he let it.

He had expected to see the town from the top. There were only more trees. The ground fell away on the far side into a hollow, where the trees grew close and the undergrowth was thick between them, and beyond it the land climbed again.

*Past the rise. He didn't say how far past.*

He went down into the hollow anyway. Halfway down, Wena stopped.

She stood with her head low and her legs braced, staring at the bottom of the hollow, and the fur along her back had risen. Alaric followed her stare. There was a fallen tree lying at the bottom, long and pale, with no bark on it.

Then Wena began to bark, and the fallen tree sat up.

It was bigger sitting than Silas was standing. Its skin was a dirty white all over. Its gut hung over a band of rags tied round its middle, and two tusks came up out of its lower jaw, one on either side of its mouth. Across its knees lay a club, a whole branch with the bark still on, studded with spikes. Slowly, it turned its head towards the barking, until it was facing straight up the slope at him.

*That's it. That's the—it was lying up, it was lying up right here, and I walked straight—*

"Wena!" It came out as a croak. "Wena, come on!"

He ran.

He went back the way he'd come, straight up the slope, because it was the only way he knew. Behind him, he heard something big get up out of the leaves, and branches cracking. Wena was barking at his heels, and then behind him, and then beside him again, and the sword kept catching on everything he passed.

*Stupid, stupid, he told you, he stood there and told you, and you—just run, run—*

"Silas!"

His legs were giving out. The slope was loose and wet, and his feet slid back at every stride, and the crashing behind him was getting closer, and there was a smell coming with it: the deer, and something worse. He grabbed a sapling to pull himself up the last of it.

A hand closed round his ankle.

It yanked him off his feet. His face hit the leaves, and then the ground dropped away from him, and he was upside down, swinging, his shirt falling down over his chest and the blood rushing into his head. The whole wood turned over. He still had the sword. He didn't know how.

It lifted him closer to its face.

"Good eating."

The words were clear. It sounded pleased, as though someone had put a bowl in front of it.

*It talks?*

"Put me down!" Alaric twisted towards the ground. "Put me down!"

"Put me down," it repeated.

Then its mouth opened.

It brought him in towards its mouth. He saw the tusks and the wet teeth between them, and he twisted round and swung the sword at its face, at anything. The blade hit something. It dragged, and caught, and tore free.

The ogre screamed.

Its head jerked back. Its other hand let go of the club, and the club dropped into the leaves, and it clapped the hand to its face. When the hand came away, blood was running out of a split in its cheek, just under the eye. It shook him, roaring, and the trees and the sky and the ground swung round and round, and Alaric held on to the sword with both hands.

Somebody was shouting.

Something black swung in at the edge of what he could see, and the hand round his ankle opened.

He fell. He hit the ground on his back, and the breath was knocked out of him and wouldn't come back. He lay with his mouth open and his chest locked, looking up through the branches, and couldn't do anything at all.

When he could turn his head, he saw Silas on the slope above him, and the ogre between them with its back to Alaric now. It was grabbing at Silas with its open hands, and Silas gave ground up the slope with his blade held out in front of him. Every time he tried to get in, a hand came at him, and he had to go back again, and the leaves slid under him. One hand caught his cloak and dragged him half off his feet before he tore free.

Wena was at its heels, barking and snapping and darting back.

The ogre turned from Silas and looked down at her.

Alaric was up before he'd thought about it. His ankle flared as soon as it took his weight, and he kept going anyway, in between Wena and the hand coming down for her, and he cut at it. The blade bit into the back of it. The ogre snatched it away with a howl, and its face came round to him.

Silas went for it.

He came low from the side the ogre had turned away from, with both hands on the hilt, and drove the blade under its ribs. Nearly all of it went in. The ogre's weight carried it backwards, and its back struck a tree, and Silas stayed with it, leaning his whole body on the hilt to keep it there.

It wasn't dead. It screamed and reached down with both arms, past Silas, for Alaric and Wena.

Alaric grabbed Wena's collar and pulled her back. His ankle gave way under him, and he fell in the leaves right beneath it, with Wena on top of him, and a hand came down at them: the one he had cut, with the blood running off the back of it. It missed his face by a few inches and raked the ground beside his head.

Silas was still there, against it. His feet were sliding. He was holding it against the tree, and it was pulling at the blade and at him, and he didn't let go.

*If he lets go—*

The black steel between Silas's hands and the wound began to glow.

The red crept up from where the blade went in. Smoke came out around it. The cloth round Silas's hand started to smoke as well, and his face tightened, and tightened, and still he didn't let go. It was what he'd done to the elf.

The hand above Alaric slowed. It came down on the leaves and lay there, and the ogre sagged against the tree, and slid, and stopped.

Silas let go of the hilt then. He stepped back from it with his hand held against his chest, breathing hard.

Alaric pushed himself up and pulled Wena round to look at her. She was shaking, and she was filthy with leaves and mud, and she licked at his face while he ran his hands over her, but none of the blood on her was her own, and she could stand on all four legs. He put his face down against her neck.

When he lifted his head again, Silas had wrapped the corner of his cloak round the hilt and was pulling the blade free. It came out slowly. He wiped it on the rags round the ogre's middle, and then he turned and looked Alaric over from his head to his feet.

"Would it kill you to listen, boy? Just once?"

*Nearly.*

Alaric tried to stand. His ankle gave, and he caught himself against a tree.

"I'm sorry. I thought I could get there."

"You nearly got both of you eaten." Silas came over. "Sit down. I want a look at that foot before you try walking on it."

Alaric sat. Silas pulled the boot off, none too gently, and turned the foot one way and the other with his good hand while Alaric hissed at the leaves. The skin round the ankle had gone red where the ogre had held it, and it was already swelling.

"It's not broken. You'll walk on it."

Silas sat down on the slope beside him. The cloth round his burned hand had gone brown and black where the hilt had been.

Alaric watched him pull the loosened cloth away from his hand.

"How long had you known Gerolt?"

Silas stopped pulling.

"I knew him for years." He looked down at the cloth again. "Leave it at that for now."

Alaric nodded. After a while, he said, "I wasn't trying to make things harder. I was trying to get to her."

"And I was trying to get you there without bringing those bastards down on her inn."

"Then why couldn't you tell me what you were doing? You said you were taking the sword and I had to wait outside."

"I wasn't going to leave you sitting there all night."

"How was I meant to know that? You said, 'We'll see.'"

Silas let the cloth rest across his knee.

"There's a way along the south wall. I was checking how we could get there. When we do, I'll have a look at the inn. If it's clear, we both go in, and you show her the sword yourself. If there are riders, we wait. All right?"

"If you tell me what you're checking. Don't just tell me to sit somewhere and disappear."

"Fine. But no more wandering off."

"I won't."

Silas got the last of the cloth free. The palm under it was raw and wet and blistered. Alaric looked away from it, then made himself look back. He had watched that hand stay on the hilt while the steel turned red.

Without a sound, Silas poured water from the skin over it, then nodded at the sack by Alaric's feet. Alaric found a strip of clean cloth in it and passed it across.

Silas tried to catch the loose end of the binding. It slipped out of his fingers.

"Here." Alaric reached for it. "Let me hold that."

Silas handed him the end and began drawing the cloth round his palm.

"It's Alaric, by the way."

Silas looked up.

"My name. You never asked."

"All right, Alaric." He drew the cloth across again. "Keep hold of that while I tie it."

Alaric held it steady. Silas finished the knot, then opened his hand a little and shut it again.

"That'll do. Give me the sack. We're not staying here."

Alaric gave him the sack. Then he got his boot back on, which was worse than having it off, and found the sword in the leaves where he'd fallen, and got up.

Wena wouldn't go near the ogre. She circled wide of it with her hackles up, and came and pressed against Alaric's legs on the far side of him.

"How did you find me?" Alaric asked.

"Your dog." Silas glanced back up the slope. "Half the forest heard her."

Alaric put his hand on Wena's head.

They went back down into the hollow and up its far side. Every step downhill jarred Alaric's ankle. After the first few he gave up trying to keep up and just walked, and Silas was never far ahead. Silas had slowed down. He didn't say so, and Alaric didn't either.

Once, a bird clattered up out of the undergrowth beside them, and Wena threw herself against Alaric's legs, and he had the sword up before he knew it. It was only a bird. His heart took a long time to believe it.

Alaric walked close enough to talk, and now and then Silas talked without being asked.

"Stream at the bottom of this."

And later, when they had reached it: "Once we're across, it's open ground most of the way to the wall. We'll wait in the trees till the light goes."

The stream ran fast between steep banks. Where the near bank came down low enough to get across, a track ran down to the water, rutted by carts, and the mud at the edge was full of hoofprints. Some of them were still filling with water.

Silas crouched over them.

"I'm going to have a look upstream, see if there's a better crossing. Give me a minute."

He set off along the bank and into the trees.

Alaric stood by the water with the sword. The hoofprints ran down into the stream and out again on the other side. He looked upstream, where Silas had gone. His ankle ached, but it would have taken him that far. He took a step after Silas, and Wena looked up at him.

He stopped, and stayed where he was.

Silas came back sooner than Alaric had expected.

"There's stones a bit further up, with trees right down to the water on both sides." He glanced at the hoofprints once more. "Come on, Alaric."

Alaric got across on the stones with only one wet boot. Wena didn't bother with the stones.

Across the stream, the trees went on a little longer and then began to thin, and the light turned gold and then red between them. The sun was low when they came to the last of the trees.

"There," Silas said.

Alaric came up beside him and out onto the grass at the top of a long slope, and saw Kelmend.

It lay at the bottom of the slope, across fields gone grey in the dusk. A wall ran round it, with roofs crowded inside and smoke going up from a great many chimneys. Outside the wall, all along the near side of it, there were more roofs. They were lower and closer together, and pressed right up against the stones. A road ran across the fields to a gate, and there were still people on it: a cart, and a few more on foot, all going in. Lights were coming on in the windows. On top of the wall, somebody was walking along lighting torches, one after another.

"South gate," Silas said, and pointed to the end of the wall nearest them, where the road went in.

Somewhere by that gate there was an inn, and Marta was in it.

Alaric didn't know what she looked like. He pictured it anyway: a door, and a room with a fire in it, and a woman he didn't know looking at Gerolt's sword and knowing it. *The old fool sent me.* He couldn't see any further than that. He didn't need to. After that, there would be somebody who knew what to do.

He let himself sit down on the grass. Wena lay down beside him with her head on her paws. A dog was barking somewhere down in the town, and Wena's ears lifted at it and then dropped again. The wind came up the slope smelling of chimney smoke and of somebody's supper, and Alaric was hungry again. Behind the town, the sun was going down.

"We can still get to her tonight, can't we?"

Silas was looking at the wall. "Once it's dark. If it's quiet, we go down along the south wall, and I have a look at the inn."

Alaric nodded. He watched the torches go on along the wall until they reached the far end of it.

He felt it before he heard it: a hum so low that it was in his chest more than his ears, and in the ground under him.

Wena's head came up off her paws.

Alaric turned to the trees, then to Silas, and then up, where Silas was looking.

Beyond the town, against what was left of the sunset, there was a shape too big to be anything. He couldn't tell how far off it was. Then it was over the wall, and the torches on top looked tiny beneath it. The hum grew until he could feel it in his teeth.

It kept growing. Alaric found the bow, then the dark red plating behind it, and the spines beneath. Blue-green light ran along its underside.

He was on his feet without having decided to stand. It was the biggest thing he had ever seen move, and it moved so slowly that it seemed to hang there. He forgot his ankle. He forgot to be afraid of it.

"What *is* that?"

Silas had come out onto the grass beside him. He was staring up at it, and his face had gone still, and he didn't answer straight away.

"Natharul's."

A bell began ringing in Kelmend. Alaric looked towards the town, then back up at the ship.

"Is it coming here?"

"No. It's going past us."

It went over them. The hum filled everything. Blue-green light slid across the grass, and across Wena, flat against Alaric's legs, and across Silas's upturned face. Then it was past. Down on the wall, the torches had stopped going on and started moving.

The ship carried on east, low and slow, turning first one way and then the other, out over the trees they had come through all day. Silas watched it all the way towards the river. Then he looked at Alaric, and at the sword hanging from his hand.

"All those bodies. You said they were on Gerolt's land?"

"Yes. I told you last night."

Silas looked towards the river again. Alaric waited for another question.

Instead, Silas stepped back off the open ground.

"Alaric. Get Wena into the trees."
