# Chapter 5

"I'm sorry," she whispered.

His eyes came down to her at last. He went to put his sword away without looking, and the point missed the sheath and scraped across the plates at his hip, and he had to look down to find it. She had watched him put that sword away all her life, and he had never once needed to look.

Then his arms came round her.

They closed so hard that they drove the breath out of her. One arm locked across her back and his hand held her head against his chest, and the edges of his armour dug into her ribs and caught her cut hand between them. It hurt. She let it, for as long as she could bear it.

"Thaer." She said it into his chest. "You're hurting me."

He let go at once, but he didn't step back. His hands stayed on her shoulders, and he looked her over, from her face to the dried blood on her wrist, and down to her bare feet on the stone.

"How long have you been awake?"

"I don't know." Her voice came out cracked and dry, and she had to swallow before she could go on. "All day, I think. I called and called, and nobody came."

He was staring at the crystal again.

"What is that thing, Thaer?" She could still feel it, cold, drawing at her through her fingers, and she pressed her hand into her dress. "What is this place?"

He didn't answer. He took her by the arm, turned her away from the crystal and walked her out through the doorway he had cut, over the two halves of the door lying on the floor.

There was a passage outside, with lamps burning along its walls. Her eyes watered, and she blinked until she could make out the stone, and the stairs at the far end of it, going up out of sight. She didn't know any of it.

She stopped, and made him stop with her.

"Where are we? What happened to me? Thaer, please—"

"Not here." His eyes went past her to the broken doorway. "No more questions, Seralune. Not here."

Then there were footsteps above them, a great many, coming down fast. Thaer let go of her arm and stepped in front of her.

Guards came down into the lamplight with their swords drawn. The first of them saw her and stopped so suddenly that the ones behind walked into his back, and after that none of them came any further. They stood on the stairs and stared at her. The nearest one's knuckles had gone white on his hilt, and the one behind him backed up a step without seeming to know he'd done it.

*What's happening? Why are so many people here?*

Her mana crowded up under her ribs.

An old man walked down between them. The guards stood aside for him without being told, and he stopped at the bottom, in the lamplight. His hair was white and thin, and his face was deeply lined. She didn't know him.

He bowed to her, low. "Your Highness."

Then he turned to Thaer. "Thaeroval. What happened here? Why has the door been cut down?"

She looked at him properly. There was something in his face under all those lines, and the longer she looked, the more of it she knew, until she could see the young man she remembered.

"Leorin?"

He glanced at her, and turned back to Thaer.

"I'm standing right here, you know. You can just ask me."

Nobody did. Thaer took her arm again.

"Come with me, Seralune, and stay close. Don't speak to anyone until we're upstairs."

*Upstairs, then. He'll tell me everything then.*

Leorin stepped back against the wall to let them pass, and the guards on the stairs got out of her way.

The stairs went on and on. Thaer took them fast, with his hand closed round her arm, and he didn't look at her once. Her legs had been shaking since she stood up in that room, and her bare feet were numb on the stone, and by the first turning she was leaning on him with every step. The guards followed at a distance, and Leorin's voice went on among them, too low to make out.

*That was Leorin. It can't have been, though. Leorin isn't old.*

*But it was him, I know it was. So why does he look so old? What happened?*

Partway up, she heard it. It was so faint at first that she thought it was her own blood in her ears. Then there was no mistaking it.

The falls.

*So I was in the palace after all. Somewhere very deep in it.*

The sound grew with every turn of the stairs, until it was under everything again, as it always had been.

At the top, the corridors were ones she knew. There were servants in them, even at this hour, and they pressed themselves back against the walls as Thaer brought her past. Some of them bowed. Some forgot to, until the person beside them pulled them down. Not one of them would look at her for long, and she didn't know a single face.

At the end of the row, an old man bowed like the rest, and as he straightened, he was smiling at her. Only a little.

*I've never seen him before in my life, and he's the first person who's smiled at me.*

She looked back for him at the next corner. He was gone.

Her knees gave at the next stair. Thaer caught her against his side before she could fall, and kept walking, and she had to go with him.

Then cold air touched her face, and the sound of the falls was all around her.

They had come out onto a long gallery, open along one side to the night. The falls were right there beside the palace. The water poured down from high above them, white under the moon, and dropped past the gallery so far into the forest below that she couldn't see where it landed, and the spray drifted across and settled cold on her cheeks.

The forest stretched further than she could see, and there were lights down among the trees, a great many of them, where the city was.

Something was moving over the forest.

At first she took it for a cloud, it was so big. Then she saw the light along its underside, lines of it, blue-green, running the whole length of it. It was long and dark and plated all over, and it hummed so low that she felt it in her chest more than she heard it over the falls.

It was a ship. She had seen ships fly before, but never one that size.

Thaer had stopped beside her. He watched it until it was far out over the forest, going away from them, and he didn't say a word.

She looked away from it, up the falls to the top, where the royal tree had always stood.

It was still there, with its branches reaching out over the water on both sides, and it was so tall that she had to tip her head back to see the top of it.

*How in the world did it grow so big? It was nowhere near that size this morning.*

*How long was I in that room?*

Thaer didn't let her stand there for long. He brought her along the gallery and in at a door at the far end of it, into a room she didn't know.

Someone had made it ready. A fire was burning in the hearth and lamps were lit in the corners, and the covers on the bed had been folded back. There was a jug of water on the table, with a cup and a tray of bread and fruit beside it, and the shutters over the window stood open, so that the falls were loud in the room.

She went straight to the water. The first swallow hurt her throat, and she drank the whole cup anyway, and poured another with a hand that wouldn't keep still.

When she looked round, Thaer hadn't come any further than the doorway. "We're upstairs," she said.

"Get some rest. There are things I have to see to, and I'll be back as soon as I'm able."

He went out, and the door shut behind him.

There was a maid standing in the corner by the door, with her hands folded in front of her and her eyes lowered. She must have been there all along.

"Where's my mother?"

"I couldn't say, Your Highness."

"Then fetch Elowen. She'll know."

"I've never heard of her, Your Highness."

Seralune stared at her.

*Never heard of Elowen? How? She's by my side every day.*

She let out a long breath. "Well. This is getting me nowhere." The maid hadn't moved. "You do have a name, I take it?"

"Nereth, Your Highness."

Seralune couldn't sit still. She walked from the fire to the window and back again, and her legs still shook, and she walked anyway. Nobody had told her anything, not Leorin or the guards or this maid, and not Thaer either.

*Why is he so on edge? He's never been like this, not once.*

Then there were voices outside.

She crossed to the door and put her ear to the wood. Nereth was an arm's length away in her corner, and she didn't move, and didn't look at her.

The voices were low, and they ran together through the wood, so that she couldn't make out a word.

*Is that Thaer speaking? And who else?*

One of them rose, and a few words came through.

"…needs to be… sealed…"

Then she heard Thaer, angry, and loud enough now that she knew him.

"…at least wait until… my father… returned… answers…"

She stepped back from the door so fast that her legs nearly went, and sat down hard on the edge of the bed.

*Who? Me? Sealed? No.*

*Is that what that room was?*

*People don't sleep inside crystal. That's what I told myself.*

The voices stopped. After a while there were two knocks, and the door opened before she could answer, the way it always had, and Thaer came in alone and shut it behind him.

"What's happening, Thaer? Why is everyone acting like this?"

He didn't answer.

"Did I do something wrong? Nobody will tell me anything, and you… you of all people."

His eyes went to the tray on the table. "You haven't eaten."

"You told me to follow you, and I followed. You told me to rest, and I rested." She was on her feet now. "I sat in that room all day, Thaeroval, and nobody came. Now I want answers, and I want them now."

"When you've slept—"

"Don't you dare send me to bed like a child!"

He turned for the door. She caught his arm, and he stopped.

"Seralune, get some rest. I'll be back in the morning."

He freed his arm and went to the door.

"You said you'd be back before evening, too."

His hand stopped on the door. Then he opened it and went out, and the lock turned.

She didn't sleep. She sat on the bed while the fire burned low, and Nereth stood in her corner and didn't move.

*Wait until my father returned. So Father's gone somewhere, and Thaer wants to wait for him.*

*And Mother? Mother would never let them do this to me.*

Some time in the night, she felt a blow through the soles of her feet before she heard it, dull and heavy, and then another, and another, slow and steady, from somewhere far below her.

*They're mending it, and it's for me.*

"What are they doing down there?"

"I couldn't say, Your Highness."

"Of course. No one can say anything." The next blow came up through the floor, and then Seralune was up off the bed, shouting. "*Why can't anyone just say what is going on?*"

The lock turned, the door banged open, and two guards came in with their swords out.

Before Seralune could move, Nereth was standing between her and the swords. "Her Highness is tired," she said, in the same even voice she'd used all evening, with the swords a step from her. "She needs to rest. That's all."

The guards looked past her at Seralune. Then they backed out, the door shut, and the lock turned.

"I'm sorry," Seralune said to Nereth's back. "I shouldn't have shouted at you. None of this is your doing."

Nereth turned round. She didn't seem to know what to do with her hands.

"…Your Highness."

After that, the blows went on, and Seralune sat on the bed and listened to every one of them, and waited for morning, and for Thaer.

When the window began to grey, the blows stopped, and there were footsteps in the corridor.

Two knocks came.

The door stayed shut.

She got up. She had been waiting for him all night. "Thaer?"

The lock turned.
