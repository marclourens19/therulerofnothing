# Chapter 9 Working Design

Recorded 2 October 2026. No title chosen and no chapter drafted. This file separates the author's latest character decisions and proposed events from recommendations still under discussion.

## Current author decisions

- Marta is Gerolt's niece and the same age as Silas. The current character handoff places Silas at approximately forty-five; no new exact age is selected here.
- Marta and Silas married and remain married. They separated after her father died, and she wanted nothing further to do with him. This supersedes the older intended-wife and former-fiancée descriptions.
- Marta is extremely beautiful and regarded as the most beautiful woman in Kelmend. Her reputation contributes to the inn's popularity.
- She wants to protect the life she has built. She is firm in her rules and beliefs, accustomed to being obeyed and a hub of information and secrets in Kelmend.
- Her voice draws on Baderon from Final Fantasy XIV: familiar and rough, with light dialect and direct authority. With Silas she is angry, profane and personally hostile; this is not her manner with everyone.
- The inn has overnight rooms. The front bar closes at night so guests can sleep; closing does not mean the entire building is empty.
- Adult sex workers live and work at the inn. Their relationships with guards and political figures are part of the information network. Their terms of work, freedom to refuse and involvement in gathering information remain to be decided.
- Marta supplies Avarice with information as an associate, not a member. She is neither automatically Gerolt's pupil nor a combatant because of this connection.
- Existing character material describes Alaric as about twenty. His embarrassment at attention can be written as a young adult's response, without making every worker interested in him.

## Author's proposed course of events

1. Alaric says Gerolt's instruction through the closed door: the old fool sent him. The footsteps inside stop, then Marta reopens and questions how he knows the words or why Gerolt sent him.
2. Marta admits Alaric and Wena to speak, while telling Silas to leave.
3. Alaric notices Marta's beauty. Some resident workers find him handsome, and he blushes at their attention.
4. Marta hears what happened to Gerolt. The familiar events may be compressed instead of narrated again in full.
5. She is distraught, asks whether Silas had anything to do with it, and hears that he brought Alaric to her. She suspects that he acted selfishly. This suspicion is Marta's interpretation, not narration establishing his motives.
6. The two local guards visit. Marta handles them, including an offer of drinks when they finish their next shifts. They become embarrassed, tease each other and leave.
7. She gives Alaric shelter and investigates what might help him.
8. Alaric and Silas have about two days to rest before a lead towards Darcy arrives. They then need to move quickly. Alaric acknowledges that he cannot manage the task alone, and Marta directs him to Silas.

These are the author's current scene directions. The practical reasons for the guards' departure, Silas's recovery location and the Darcy opportunity remain open.

## Recommendations for the arrival night

### The reopened door and Gerolt's death

The familiar words, sword and dog together make Marta hear Alaric. The words alone do not prove that Gerolt is dead or function as an infallible password.

Compress the full escape account, but keep the questions and answers that change Marta's understanding. She may care where her uncle is, what he said, how badly he was hurt and when Silas arrived. Choose the questions that belong to her; do not require a checklist of death details. The reader should experience the death anew through his family's response.

Alaric can explain the help he witnessed and disagree with Marta's interpretation of Silas. He cannot certify Silas's motives, explain an old marriage or absolve the gorge. Marta can retain her anger without being incapable of hearing facts.

### The inn as a household

Introduce the resident women through individual work, wants and judgments. One can handle a guest's immediate needs while another wants quiet near her rooms. Who gathers information, what she knows and what she chooses to pass on should differ.

Place the lighter teasing after the first grief and the immediate guard visit, or during the following day. A small awkward exchange can show Alaric is still capable of reacting to ordinary attention. It should not make the death announcement feel like a prelude to universal admiration.

The proximity to Chapter 8's auction makes the women's freedom or lack of freedom significant. Decide the actual arrangement before writing the inn as a refuge. Payment for sex and payment for intelligence need not be the same agreement.

### The guard visit

Marta's familiarity and offer of drinks can influence how the men conduct a completed interview. They need something plausible to report, and no direct sighting of Alaric, Wena or Gerolt's sword. Alaric should recognise the two voices from Chapter 8.

Do not have the guards abandon an explicit room-search order or an obvious clue solely because she is beautiful. The current order is to question her; the extent of their authority and instructions needs a choice. There is no established need for a new legal warrant system or magical charm ability.

Her information network can make maintaining a relationship with her useful to the guards. It also creates dependencies and vulnerabilities; it is not immunity from the elves or a later determined search. A bounded success tonight is enough.

### Rest and the double

The accepted working plan places Alaric's first sustained double encounter after genuine help from Marta. He first needs an ordinary reflection in which he can learn his face, naturally while washing. The double appears privately to him, exploits guilt about Gerolt and challenges the safety he has been offered. It does not deliver Darcy's intelligence or take over his choices.

The first night can complete Chapter 9, with the recovery interval shown when Alaric's viewpoint next returns. This chapter break is a recommendation, not an author decision. The two-day rest can also remain within Chapter 9 if the selected scenes retain their weight.

## Recovery and the next lead

Arrival is late on Day 2. Two full recovery days are Days 3 and 4. A new action can begin at the end of Day 4 or on Day 5; the exact departure remains unset. The interleaved Seralune chapters need clear temporal transitions.

Choose a few consequential recovery scenes rather than narrating every meal and hour. Let Alaric experience ordinary welcome and begin to settle, while the city's inquiry and both injuries persist. Any unusually rapid recovery needs to follow the established developing connection rather than become a convenient reset.

The Darcy opportunity should arise from fresh information, newly completed verification or a newly available contact. Explain what makes her useful regarding the battlefield and what makes the opportunity temporary. Marta should not hold a known urgent lead for two days and then demand haste.

Silas's initial exclusion requires a recovery location and a way to contact him. A separate nearby lodging, an arranged room elsewhere or later conditional admission are possible; none is selected. His return must respect Marta's boundary. The marriage does not supply permission to enter, and her practical recommendation need not mean forgiveness.

Alaric asking Silas for help is a small step towards trusting others. It does not complete the Volume 1 arc in which he later begins carrying responsibility alone. If the double has already urged withdrawal, asking for help can also be an early act of resistance.

2. Are the guards conducting an interview, inspecting the public rooms or ordered to search the overnight rooms?
3. What freedom and terms do the resident women have in their work and in collecting information?
4. What can Darcy actually help with, who supplies the lead and what closes the opportunity?
5. Does Chapter 9 finish on the first night or contain the recovery interval?
6. Why have Marta and Silas remained married, and how much of their past belongs on the page now?
7. Marta's Affinity and the circumstances in which she learns Alaric has none remain unsettled. If Silas is to hear this for the first time in the scene, he must be present.

No new age, hair colour, eye colour, Affinity, magical sensing, covert guild role or connection between Darcy and ancient Alaric is invented by this record.

## The review that came with it (the other workspace's assistant, 2 October)

Chapter 9 should make Alaric experience what it means to be taken in—and realise that the people helping him have lives he knows almost nothing about. Your vision gives us that. The marriage makes Marta's refusal much more personal: she can shelter Alaric without welcoming Silas back into her life.
I've recorded your decisions and the remaining questions in the [Chapter 9 working design](C:/Users/joshu/Documents/Codex/2026-09-25/files-pasted-by-the-user-the/outputs/Chapter 9 - Working Design.md).
There are several things I would tighten before drafting.
We should compress Alaric's account, but keep Marta receiving the news. The reader already knows how Gerolt died; the reader does not know what losing him means to her. That is the new scene.
Her questions should come from their relationship rather than become another battlefield explanation. Perhaps she initially assumes he survived because he sent the boy here. Perhaps she asks where he is before Alaric can finish. Choose one or two questions that reveal what she expected from her uncle, and let his answers destroy those expectations. Her grief need not become a long speech.
Her suspicion of Silas also gives Alaric something meaningful to do. He can defend the help he witnessed without understanding their marriage. Silas came back for him, fought for him and burned his hand protecting him. Alaric should not sit silently while Marta dismisses all that—but neither should his defence settle whether Silas also wanted a reason to return to her door. Both motives can exist.
The guards need more than beauty and promised drinks to justify leaving. Keep the embarrassment, swearing and teasing; those fit the men we established. But Marta should first give them an interview they can report as completed.
They came to question Gerolt's niece. She answers their questions, gives them no visible reason to search upstairs, and maintains the familiarity that makes them comfortable accepting her account. The drinks help preserve that relationship. They should leave believing they have done their job.
That makes Marta capable while preserving the danger: tonight she manages two local guards. A determined elf investigator could be another matter entirely.
The women at the inn need lives beyond finding Alaric attractive and collecting secrets. One teasing him is enough. Another might be tired, concerned about the visitors, or irritated that Marta is bringing trouble upstairs. Their disagreements would make the inn feel inhabited.
Their working arrangement matters especially after the auction. If Marta's house is meant to offer a different kind of life, show the difference through what these women can choose: customers, work, information they share, whether they stay. If their freedom is compromised, that becomes part of Marta's character and Alaric's growing understanding of the place. We should decide deliberately.
Alaric noticing Marta's beauty is natural. I would keep it brief at the reopened door, then let what she says command his attention. Save the lighter embarrassment with the women for after the immediate grief and guard visit, or the following morning.
Silas being excluded creates a practical question we cannot skip. Where does he recover? Marta cannot send him away and then have him quietly occupying an upstairs room without another decision between them.
My preference is separate lodging nearby initially. Later, recommending Silas to help Alaric becomes a revealing distinction: she trusts what he can do while remaining unwilling to resume their relationship. That is more interesting than treating her recommendation as forgiveness.
The sequence I would build around is:

1. Gerolt's words stop Marta's footsteps. She reopens the door and questions Alaric.
2. She admits him and Wena, refusing Silas entry.
3. She learns Gerolt is dead. We compress the familiar events around her specific questions.
4. She challenges Silas's involvement; Alaric tells her what he actually saw him do.
5. The guards arrive. The household helps conceal Alaric, Wena and the sword while Marta handles the interview.
6. Afterwards, Marta chooses to shelter him. He begins encountering the inn as someone staying there.
7. His first encounter with the double follows genuine help, as previously discussed. It attacks the safety he has just been offered.
8. Recovery gives that safety substance before fresh information creates the opportunity involving Darcy.

I support the two days of rest. Let something good last long enough for Alaric—and the reader—to care about losing it. His ankle and Silas's hand should improve without immediately becoming irrelevant.
We can use a few consequential scenes rather than account for every hour. Those days could fit within Chapter 9, or follow its first-night ending when we return to Alaric's viewpoint. Length alone should not decide the break.
The Darcy lead must be newly obtained or newly verified. Marta should not sit on an urgent opportunity for two days and then suddenly demand haste. We also need to establish what Darcy could know about the farm and why access to her is about to become difficult. Alaric admitting he needs Silas would then feel like a choice he has earned.
Before the dialogue round, I would settle these three things:

1. Where does Silas stay? Separate lodging nearby, somewhere Marta arranges, or later conditional admission to the inn? I favour separate lodging initially.
2. What are the guards ordered to do? Question Marta, or search the guest rooms too? I favour the interview already established in Chapter 8.
3. What can the resident women refuse? Customers, intelligence gathering, both—and are they free to leave? This will determine what kind of refuge Marta has actually built.
