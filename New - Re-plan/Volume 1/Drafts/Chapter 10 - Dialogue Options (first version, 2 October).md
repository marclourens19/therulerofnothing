# Chapter 10 — Dialogue Options

Options for "Beneath Natharul", written 2 October 2026 for the author to read through, choose and edit. Each section is one moment of the chapter in scene order, with two or three versions written as they would read on the page. Choose by section and letter (for example "1A, 2B, 3A and C"), mix lines from different versions, or rewrite anything.

These are passages, not the continuous chapter; the joining prose comes when it's written. They follow the agreed design. Nereth leads, and they go in circles. Seralune takes the lead and finds the way deeper. They find what lives down there, and the dead. Nereth's descent is built from the author's guide. Then come the "help me", the lizard and the bite, the fire through the mountain, the fresh air, and the walk down the mountain. The Thaer line at the hole is already approved, and every version of section 14 uses it.

## 1. The first turning

### A. Nereth chooses

The channel split round a pillar. Nereth stopped and lifted the lamp to one side, then the other, and the light gave out a few paces down each.

"This is where they turned us back, Your Highness."

"Do you know where either of them goes?"

"No." Nereth looked down the left-hand way for longer than the right. "The water came from this side when we cleared it, though. It has to come out somewhere."

"Then you choose. I've never set foot down here."

### B. "Then guess well"

The channel split round a pillar, and both ways went on further than the lamp could reach.

Seralune looked from one to the other. Upstairs she had been sure of every stair, and half of them had turned out to be walls.

"You'd better lead, Nereth. You've at least been this far."

"As far as this pillar, Your Highness." Nereth lifted the lamp. "After this, I'll be guessing."

"Then guess well."

Nereth took the left-hand way.

### C. Lost once before

The channel split round a pillar. Nereth stopped, and the lamp shook a little in her hand before she steadied it.

Seralune had been lost once before, when she was small. She had gone looking for something and turned round and not known any of the doors, and she had sat on a step and cried until her mother came with a lamp. Nereth had the only lamp now.

"Which way, Nereth?"

"Left, I think, Your Highness." Nereth was already going. "The water came from that side when we cleared it."

## 2. The circles

### A. The footprints

The lamp lit the ledge a few steps ahead and a few behind, and the rest was black. The water lay in the channel beside them without moving.

They had been walking a long time when Seralune saw the prints in the silt: soft soles, and harder ones beside them, going the way they were going.

"Nereth."

Nereth brought the lamp down and looked at them for a long while.

"Someone else has been down here."

"Those are mine." Seralune set her foot beside the nearest one, and it fitted. "We've been past here before."

"We haven't, Your Highness." It was the even voice she had used on the guard. "We've come straight. I've been counting the turnings."

"How many?"

"Six." Nereth looked back the way they had come. "Seven."

*Hours. We've been going round and round for hours, and she'd have kept on until we dropped.*

### B. Seralune's mark

At the third turning, while Nereth held the lamp up to choose, Seralune picked up a chip of stone and scratched a line on the pillar beside her, as high as she could reach. It took three tries before it showed.

Nereth watched her do it. "What's that for, Your Highness?"

"So we'll know if we see it again."

"We won't."

The ledge narrowed, and widened, and narrowed again, and the borrowed shoes began to rub her heels raw. Then the lamp found a pillar, and on the pillar, as high as she could reach, a pale scratch.

She put her finger in it. It was hers.

"Nereth, we've come back."

"That isn't yours." Nereth didn't look at it. "Yours was on the other side."

"It's mine. I made it, and you watched me make it."

"Then someone else has made one the same, Your Highness." Nereth held the lamp up to the left-hand way. "This way."

It was the way they had taken last time.

### C. "It won't happen again"

They had been walking a long time when Seralune saw the prints in the silt along the ledge, soft soles and harder ones beside them, going the way they were going.

She stopped. "Those are mine."

Nereth brought the lamp down to them. "Then somebody else down here wears your shoes, Your Highness, because I haven't brought you this way."

"Look at them, Nereth. That's the shoe you put on my foot this morning."

Nereth looked. Something crossed her face and was gone before Seralune could tell what it was.

"Then I've made a mistake." She said it too quickly. "It won't happen again."

It happened again.

## 3. The first signs

Choose one, two or all three; they can come in any order between the circles and Seralune taking the lead.

### A. Someone talking

Nereth stopped so suddenly that Seralune walked into her back.

"Do you hear that?"

Seralune held her breath. Somewhere ahead, water was dripping: one drop, then a long wait, then another.

"It's only the water."

"Under it." Nereth had her head on one side. "Someone's talking."

Seralune listened until her ears rang, and there was nothing under the dripping at all.

### B. Burning

Nereth lifted her head and breathed in.

"Can you smell that?"

Seralune could smell the water, and wet stone, and under them something sweet and spoiled that she didn't want to think about.

"Smell what?"

"Burning." Nereth turned slowly, and the lamp swung with her. "Something's burning. Can't you smell it?"

There was no fire anywhere but the lamp.

### C. The pattern

Nereth had stopped walking. She was staring at the wall beside them, where roots had begun to come through the stone, and she had her free hand pressed flat against her chest.

"Don't look at it, Your Highness."

"At what?"

"The pattern." Her voice was very quiet. "It keeps going. Every time I look, it goes further in."

Seralune looked. There were roots, and wet stone, and the lamplight sliding on both, and no pattern at all.

"Nereth, there's nothing there."

"Don't look at it," Nereth said again, and walked on with her eyes on her feet.

## 4. Her kindness, and the first snap

### A. The shawl

Nereth's hand had begun to shake in hers. Seralune started to pull the shawl from her own shoulders.

"Here. Put this round you."

"I'm quite all right, Your Highness."

"You're shaking. Take it."

"*Keep* it." It came out so hard that Nereth seemed to hear it herself, and she lowered her voice. "Keep it, Your Highness. You're the one who can hardly stand."

"I'm sorry. I only meant—"

"Sorry won't get us out of here."

### B. "Sit down, Nereth"

Nereth stumbled on the ledge and caught herself on the wall, and stood there with her eyes shut.

"Sit down, Nereth. Just for a little while."

"I'm quite all right, Your Highness."

"You said that last night, after you'd been standing for hours. Sit down."

"I said I'm all right!"

It went off down the channel and came back to them from both ways at once. Nereth put her hand over her mouth. Then she took it away again, slowly.

"Please stop telling me to sit down, Your Highness." Her voice was perfectly even. "I'm not a child."

### C. The lamp

"Let me carry the lamp for a while. Your arm must ache."

"It doesn't."

"Then let me carry it anyway. You've had it since the stair."

Nereth stopped and turned, and held the lamp up between them so that the light was in Seralune's eyes.

"Would you like to carry me as well, Your Highness? Would that make you feel better?"

Seralune didn't answer. Nereth looked at her over the flame a moment longer, then turned and walked on, and the light went with her, and Seralune had to hurry to stay in it.

## 5. Seralune takes the lead

### A. "I'd rather be lost somewhere new"

They came back to the pillar. Seralune knew it by the crack that ran up one side, the way she had known it the last time, and the time before.

"Give me the lamp. I'll go first."

"You don't know the way."

"Neither do you!"

It went off down both channels at once. Nereth's fingers tightened on hers until the bones ground together, then let go. Seralune made herself speak quietly.

"We've passed this pillar three times. I'd rather be lost somewhere new."

"I'm meant to go in front, Your Highness. That's what I'm here for."

"Then stay close behind me, and tell me when I go wrong."

Nereth gave her the lamp.

Their prints went left, and they went straight on. To the right, low down, roots hung over an opening she hadn't noticed before, and the silt in front of it was smooth.

*We've been left, and we've been straight on, and we haven't once been through there.*

She held the roots aside. They were cold, and softer than roots should be, and they moved a little after she had let go.

"Go on," Nereth said behind her. She was so close that Seralune could feel her breath on her neck. "Go on. Go on, then."

### B. "That's an order"

"Give me the lamp, Nereth."

Nereth held on to it. "You don't know the way, Your Highness."

"Nor do you. We've passed this pillar three times." Seralune held out her hand. "Give me the lamp. That's an order."

She heard herself say it, and it was too late. Only this morning, in her room, she had told Nereth she was sorry for giving her one.

"Yes, Your Highness," Nereth said, and put the lamp into her hand without another word.

Their prints went left, and they went straight on. To the right, low down behind a hanging mat of roots, there was an opening, and the silt in front of it was smooth. Seralune bent and went through. Nereth came after her, so close that her breath was on Seralune's neck.

### C. Nereth lets go

Nereth stopped at the pillar and didn't go on. She stood with the lamp held out in front of her, looking at the three ways, and didn't seem to see any of them.

"Nereth?"

"I don't know," Nereth said. "I don't know which one we came down."

It was the first time she had said it. Seralune took the lamp out of her hand, gently, and Nereth let her.

"Then we'll take the one we haven't been down."

There were prints going left, and prints going straight on. To the right, low down behind a mat of roots, there was an opening that nobody's feet had touched. Seralune went through first. Behind her, Nereth laughed, very softly, as if something had pleased her.

## 6. What lives down here

### A. Glimpses

The way went down. The lamp found roots on the walls, then roots over the walls, so thick that the stone only showed in patches. After a while there was a light that didn't come from the lamp, a pale glow in the roots themselves, so that the way ahead was never quite black.

Small white flowers grew out of them. When Seralune passed with the lamp, they turned on their stems to follow it.

There were sounds from all round them now. Something clicked in the roots overhead and stopped when she looked up. Something pale turned over in the water beside the ledge and went under again.

### B. The rat

Something came along the ledge towards them.

Seralune took it for a rat until the lamp found it. It had a rat's shape and a rat's tail, but roots had come out through its back and along its sides, with small white flowers growing among them, and where its eyes should have been there were more flowers, shut tight.

It came on without seeming to know they were there. It went past Seralune's feet without stopping. At Nereth's, it stopped.

It lifted its head. The flowers where its eyes should have been opened, one after the other.

Nereth kicked it into the channel. It sank without a struggle, and the flowers stayed open all the way down.

### C. The moths

Pale things began to come out of the roots and flutter round the lamp, small and soft, with wings like the petals of the white flowers.

When Seralune held the lamp out to see them better, they didn't come to it. They went past the flame, every one of them, and settled on Nereth: on her sleeves, her collar, her hair, the backs of her hands. Nereth didn't seem to feel them.

"Nereth, they're all over you—"

Nereth looked down at her hands, at the pale wings opening and closing on them, and smiled.

"They're only cold, Your Highness," she said. "They want to be warm."

## 7. The dead

### A. The servant in grey

The first of the dead was sitting against the wall with its knees drawn up, as if it had stopped to rest and never got up. Roots had gone in at its mouth and come out again between its ribs, where the cloth had rotted away. What was left of the cloth was grey.

Seralune looked down at her own sleeve.

"They sent people down here," she said. "You told me."

"Some of them didn't come back." Nereth stepped over its legs without looking down. "We were told they'd run off."

There were more of them after that. One lay face down in the channel with the roots holding it under. Two sat against the wall together, one with its head on the other's shoulder, the way people sit when they've been waiting a long time.

### B. Someone Nereth knew

The lamp found a shoe first, on its side on the ledge. Then the foot it belonged to, and then the rest of her, half grown into the wall, in grey.

Nereth stopped.

"She did the ironing," she said. "She used to sing while she did it. The same song, all day, until somebody threw something at her." She looked at the roots coming out of the woman's mouth. "They told us she'd run off with a soldier."

Seralune waited for her to say something else, or to kneel, or at least to look away. Nereth did none of those things. She stepped over the woman's legs and went on, and after a little while she began, very quietly, to hum.

### C. Nereth says nothing

The first of the dead was sitting against the wall with its knees drawn up, and its clothes were grey.

*They sent a few of us down once, to clear the channels.* Nereth had said that at the stair. She hadn't said how many of them came back up.

Seralune looked at Nereth to see whether she had understood it too. Nereth was looking at the dead servant the way she had looked at the laundry basket on the landing that morning, as something in her way. Then she stepped over it.

## 8. Nereth talks to someone

### A. "I'm going"

Nereth's lips were moving.

"I know," she was whispering. "I know. I'm going."

"Nereth? Who are you talking to?"

Nereth didn't answer, and she didn't look round at her name.

### B. Cyrandor

"I am," Nereth said.

She wasn't talking to Seralune. Her head was turned back the way they had come, as though someone were walking there.

"I am watching her. I haven't let go of her once. You can ask her."

"Nereth." Seralune's mouth had gone dry. "There's nobody there."

Nereth turned back and looked at her, as if Seralune were the one who had been talking to nobody.

"Of course there isn't, Your Highness. Who would there be?"

### C. Her own name

"Nereth."

Nothing.

"Nereth!"

Nereth went on walking. Seralune caught her sleeve and pulled her round, and Nereth looked at her face as if she were trying to remember where she had seen it.

"Your Highness," she said at last, slowly, the way you say a word in a language you're only learning.

## 9. The lash-out

### A. The wash passage, then everything

Nereth let go of her hand. She went to the wall, took hold of a root as thick as a rope, and pulled on it with both hands, the way she had pulled on the iron ring.

"Cyrandor, open it." She braced her foot against the stone. "Open the door!"

"Nereth." Seralune caught her arm. "He isn't here. There's no door. It's only the wall."

Nereth looked down at the hand on her arm.

"Why do you keep touching me?"

"I only—"

"Every time you touch me, they get louder." She tore her arm free. "What are you doing to me?"

"I'm not doing anything!"

Nereth flinched as if Seralune had hit her. When she lifted her head, it wasn't a face Seralune had ever seen her wear.

"You turned round."

"What?"

"In the wash passage. He was already coming back for me. All you had to do was keep walking, and he'd have got us both through that door. But you turned round, so he stayed." She came a step closer. "Do you understand that, Your fucking Highness?"

"I couldn't leave you there!"

"No, you couldn't. So he had to."

Her arm came across Seralune's throat and put her back against the wall. The lamp went out of Seralune's hand, and she heard it break on the ledge, and the flame went out.

In the glow from the roots, Nereth's face was a hand's width from hers. All along the wall behind her, the little white flowers had turned on their stems towards Seralune, every one of them.

"Do you know what he told me, before I'd ever set eyes on you? 'Watch over her. Even if she seems dangerous, she's important.'" She laughed, and it wasn't her laugh. "Why *you*? I could burn this whole place down round you, and you'd still be standing there saying sorry."

*Dangerous? How could I be dangerous? I've never had magic in my life.*

### B. Despair, then the threat

Nereth let go of her hand. She went to the wall, took hold of a root as thick as a rope, and pulled on it with both hands, the way she had pulled on the iron ring.

"Cyrandor, open it. Open the door!"

"Nereth, he isn't here." Seralune caught her arm. "There's no door."

Nereth stopped pulling. She stood with both hands still on the root, and her shoulders began to shake.

"He's dead," she said. "He's dead. They've killed him, and it's because of you."

"You don't know that. Thaer sent for him himself."

"You turned round!" Nereth turned on her. "He was coming back for me anyway. All you had to do was keep walking. But you turned round, so he stayed."

Her arm came across Seralune's throat and put her back against the wall. The lamp went out of Seralune's hand and broke on the ledge, and then there was only the pale glow from the walls.

"He's dead, and you're alive, and I'm down here holding your *hand*." Nereth's breath was hot on her face. Then her voice dropped, almost fond. "I could burn this whole place down round you. I can feel it. And you'd still be standing there saying sorry."

### C. Quiet

Nereth didn't shout. That was the worst of it.

She let go of the root and turned, and smiled at Seralune in the pale light from the walls. When she spoke, it was in her ordinary voice, the one she had used to tell the guards that Her Highness was tired.

"You turned round, Your Highness. In the wash passage. I watched you do it."

She knocked the lamp out of Seralune's hand without looking at it. It broke on the ledge, and the flame went out.

"He was coming back for me anyway. You only had to keep walking." She came closer, one step and then another, until the wall was at Seralune's back. "But you can't, can you? You can't leave anything alone. You have to touch it."

She put her hand flat on Seralune's chest, over her heart, and leaned on it.

"So he stayed," she said. "And here we are."

## 10. "Help me"

### A. The voice from the walls

"Help me."

It came from the wall at Seralune's shoulder. The dead servant sitting there hadn't moved, but its jaw hung open round the roots, and the voice had come out of it: a girl's voice, hoarse, as if she had been calling for a long time.

Nereth's arm went slack.

"Help me." It came again from further along, out of another of them, then from ahead, and from above, all in the same voice. "Help me. Please. Please, help me."

"Someone's alive," Seralune said. "Someone's down here."

Nereth was staring at the open mouth. "They're dead, Your Highness." It was nearly her own voice again. "All of them."

"Then who's calling?"

Seralune ducked under her arm and went towards the voice. She heard Nereth come after her. Then Nereth's hand found hers in the half-dark and held on, tight, and didn't let go.

### B. "I can't find the way"

"Help me."

The voice came out of the dead servant beside them, out of the open mouth with the roots in it. It was a girl's voice, young, worn down to almost nothing.

"Please. I can't find the way back. I keep coming back to the same place."

Beside her, Nereth had gone completely still.

"Help me. I can't find the way."

The next time it came from further along the channel, out of another of the dead, and then from further still, as if it were leading them.

"Someone's lost down here," Seralune said.

"Yes," said Nereth. "Us."

Seralune went after the voice anyway, and Nereth came after her, and caught her hand, and didn't let go.

### C. Seralune calls back

"Help me."

"Where are you?" Seralune called. "I can hear you. Where are you?"

"Help me."

"Keep talking! I'm coming!"

"Help me." The same two words, in the same voice, with the same catch in the middle of them, as if nobody had answered at all. "Help me."

Nereth had gone very still beside her. "It isn't listening to you, Your Highness."

"Then it can't hear me." Seralune was already moving. "It's hurt, Nereth. It's frightened and it's hurt, and it can't hear me."

## 11. The lizard

### A. "Frightened things still bite"

It was in a hollow at the end, caught in the roots: a lizard no longer than her hand. Roots had grown through it and out of it, and small white flowers had opened all along its back. Its sides went in and out, quick and shallow, and every time they did, the voice came out of the dead behind them.

"Help me."

"It's caught," Seralune whispered. "It's hurt."

"It's watching your hand." Nereth's breath was against her ear. "Not your face."

"It's frightened."

"Frightened things still bite."

Seralune reached for it anyway.

### B. Seralune talks to it

It was in a hollow at the end, caught in the roots: a lizard no longer than her hand, with roots grown through it and small white flowers open all along its back.

Seralune crouched in front of it.

"There," she said, softly, the way you speak to anything frightened. "It's all right. I'm not going to hurt you."

The lizard's head turned. It didn't look at her. It looked past her shoulder, at Nereth.

"Don't touch it," Nereth said.

"It's caught in the roots, Nereth. If I can just—"

"*Don't touch it.*"

Seralune put out her hand.

### C. The silence

When Seralune came close, the voice stopped.

It stopped all round them at once, in every open mouth in every wall, and the quiet afterwards was worse than any of it. The lizard lay in the roots with its sides going in and out, and the small white flowers along its back opened towards her, one by one.

It turned its head, slowly, and looked at Nereth.

"Your Highness," Nereth said, very quietly. "Come away from it."

Seralune reached for it.

## 12. The bite

### A. The voice comes out of her

Nereth wrenched Seralune's arm back, and the lizard came off the roots at Nereth's face.

She screamed until she had no breath left to do it. Then she went quiet, and that was worse. She knelt in the channel with both hands over the left side of her face, rocking, and when she opened her mouth, the voice that came out wasn't hers.

"Help me."

It was the voice from the walls.

"Nereth, no. Look at me. Nereth—"

Nereth's hand found her wrist and held on hard enough to hurt. For one breath, the voice was her own again.

"Seralune—"

Then the fire came.

### B. Laughing

Nereth wrenched Seralune's arm back, and the lizard came off the roots at Nereth's face and hung there by its teeth, under her left eye, until she tore it off and flung it away down the channel.

She didn't scream. She laughed.

Black-purple lines were spreading out from the bite, under the skin, into the veins round her eye. Nereth stood in the channel with her hand pressed to them and laughed until she couldn't stand, and went down on her knees, still laughing. Between her fingers, a light had started: a thin flame along her palm, white at the heart.

"Nereth, put it out. Please. Put it out."

"I can't." She looked up at Seralune, and she was smiling. "I don't want to."

### C. "Get it out"

Nereth wrenched Seralune's arm back, and the lizard came off the roots at Nereth's face.

She tore it off and flung it away, and then she was clawing at her own cheek with both hands.

"Get it out. Get it out of me. It's inside, it's going *in*—"

Seralune caught her wrists and held them, and Nereth fought her, and then all at once stopped fighting and stared at her with one eye. The other was swelling shut, and round it, under the skin, thin black-purple lines were spreading towards her temple.

"It's gone quiet," Nereth said. "Seralune. It's gone so quiet."

Then she opened her hand, and the fire came out of it.

## 13. The fire

### A. Light, then silence, then the falls

The light came first. It filled the channel from wall to wall, white at the heart and purple at the edges, and for an instant she saw every root and every one of the dead as clearly as if it were noon. Then she couldn't see anything.

There was no sound at all.

Then there was nothing but sound, bigger than the falls had ever been under her window, and the floor went out from under her.

The channel heaved. Stone came down somewhere behind them and went on coming down. The fire went away from them down the channel faster than she could have run, and everywhere it went, the roots went up, and the dead went up, and the walls lit white from inside.

Then it came back.

It came up the channel the whole width of it, and Seralune had time to hold Nereth tighter and nothing else. It reached them, and went round them. It went past on both sides, so close that she could have touched it, and she felt no heat from it at all.

Her mana moved, the way it had in the black, and settled again.

*It went round us.*

### B. The breath before

Before the fire, the air went.

It went out of the channel all at once, towards Nereth, and took Seralune's breath with it. Every white flower on the walls bent towards Nereth's open hand. Seralune's ears hurt. Then the pale light in the roots went out, all of it.

Then Nereth's hand opened.

Seralune never saw the fire leave it. There was white, and a weight that came down on her whole body at once, and a sound she felt in her teeth before she heard it. Then she heard it, and it didn't stop. The mountain answered. Far off down the channel, then nearer, then under her feet, stone broke and went on breaking, chamber after chamber, further than she could count.

Then the fire came back. She held on to Nereth, because there was nothing else to hold, and it went round them.

### C. Watching it go

The fire left Nereth's hand as a thread no thicker than a finger, white, and went away from them down the channel, and for a heartbeat that was all it was.

Then it reached the first chamber, and the chamber lit from inside. Then the next, and the next. Seralune watched the light go on away from her into the mountain, chamber after chamber, roots and walls and the dead all going up white, further than the channels she had walked, further than she had known there was any mountain to go through.

The sound reached her after the light did. She felt it before she heard it, in her teeth and the soles of her feet, and the ledge cracked under her, and the roof began to come down.

Then the fire came back up the channel towards them, filling it.

She pulled Nereth against her and turned her face away, and it went round them.

## 14. The hole

### A. Stars

When it had gone, the roots were gone with it, and so were the dead. The channel ran on ahead of her, black and bare and smoking, and the walls ticked as they cooled. A long way off, at the end of it, there was a ragged hole, and in it, stars.

Nereth lay across her knees and didn't move. Seralune put her ear to her mouth and felt her breathe.

She got her up somehow, onto her back, with Nereth's arms over her shoulders and her own hands locked under Nereth's knees, and walked towards the stars.

The hole was taller than the palace gates. The stone round it had melted and run and set again, and below it, where the side of the mountain had been, a long black scar went down the mountainside until she lost it, still burning in places.

All her life, people had said there was nobody in Natharul like Thaer. She had never seen him do anything like this.

### B. The ocean below

She came out onto the lip of the hole and had to stop, because there was no more mountain in front of her.

The fire had gone straight through it. Below her, the slope fell away in a long burnt furrow, wider than the court she had stood in last night, with fires still burning along its edges and smoke going up into the stars. At the bottom, a long way down, something pale moved and caught the starlight, over and over. It was a while before she understood that it was the ocean.

The wind came up the furrow and into her face. It was cold, and it smelled of salt and burning.

All her life, people had said there was nobody in Natharul like Thaer. She had never seen him do anything like this.

## 15. Fresh air

### A. "Your Highness"

The air changed first. It was cold, and it moved, and it smelled of salt and burning instead of rot.

"Your Highness."

Seralune nearly dropped her. Nereth's voice was hoarse, but it was her own again, and so was the way she said it.

"Put me down."

"No."

"I can walk."

"You can't hold your own head up."

Nereth tried to, and couldn't.

### B. Sorry

The air changed first. It was cold, and it moved, and it smelled of salt and burning instead of rot. Halfway down the furrow, Nereth's arms tightened round her neck.

"I'm sorry, Your Highness."

It was her own voice. Seralune had to stop walking.

"For what?"

"All of it." Nereth's breath caught. "I remember all of it."

Seralune hitched her higher on her back and went on.

"Sorry won't get us down this mountain," she said.

After a while, against her neck, she felt Nereth smile.

### C. The mark

Nereth woke without a sound. Seralune only knew it because the arms round her neck stopped hanging and began to hold on.

Neither of them said anything for a long way.

Where the ground levelled for a few steps, Seralune let her slide down and sat her against a rock, and knelt in front of her to look. Under her left eye, the bite had closed. Round it, under the skin, black-purple lines ran out towards her temple and into her hair, and they didn't fade when Seralune touched them.

Nereth caught her hand, held it away from her face, and then didn't let go of it.

"Don't, Your Highness," she said. "Please. Not yet."

## 16. Down the mountain

### A. "It felt like me"

"Leave me here, then."

"No."

"You didn't even stop to think about it."

"I thought about it under the mountain."

"Under the mountain, I had my arm across your throat."

"That wasn't you."

Nereth was quiet for so long that Seralune thought she had fallen asleep.

"It felt like me."

The sky was greying over the water. Seralune shifted her higher on her back and went on down.

"If this is how you rescue everyone, Your Highness," Nereth said into her hair, "I understand your brother's concern."

"When we get to the bottom, I'll find you a bed. You can spend the whole day telling me everything I did wrong."

"Only a day?"

### B. The ocean

The light came up out of the water before the sun did. First the sky went grey over it, then the grey went pale, and then the ocean was there below them, wider than anything Seralune had ever seen from her window, going on until it met the sky.

She felt Nereth lift her head to look.

Neither of them said anything. Seralune went on down the mountain with Nereth on her back, and Nereth looked at the ocean the whole way, and didn't put her head down again.

### C. Mother

At the bottom of the furrow, where the burnt ground gave way to grass, Seralune stopped to get her breath. Nereth had gone quiet again, asleep or near it, heavy on her back.

Behind them, high up, the hole in the mountain still smoked. Somewhere beyond it was the palace, and Thaer, and Cyrandor if he was still alive, and the room with the crystal in it that they had spent all night mending for her.

In front of her, the path went down towards the ocean.

*Inrandeel. Mother went to Inrandeel.*

She shifted Nereth higher, and went on down.
