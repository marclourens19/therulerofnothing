## 1. Into the channels

### A. The falls

The arch was behind them before Seralune had properly decided to go through it. After that there was the ledge, and the black water lying beside it, and Nereth's lamp making a small room of light that moved with them and never got any bigger.

She kept listening for the falls. She had listened for them all her life without knowing she did it, the way she breathed, and now she couldn't stop doing it on purpose. Every few steps her head turned of its own accord, and there was nothing: their feet on the stone, water dripping somewhere, Nereth's breathing beside her. Under all of it, where the falls should have been, there was nothing at all.

*Don't cry. The falls never stop, and I'll never stop looking for you.*

They did stop, though. You only had to go far enough down.

Nereth hadn't said a word since the arch. She walked half a step ahead with the lamp held out and her other hand in Seralune's, and every so often she looked back over her shoulder, the way she had at every turning since the iron door. There was never anyone there.

"Nereth."

"Your Highness."

"About Cyrandor—"

"Not yet." Nereth didn't turn her head. "Please, Your Highness. If I talk about him now, I'll stop walking. And I don't think I'll start again."

Seralune held her hand a little tighter, and didn't ask again.

The channel split round a pillar. Nereth lifted the lamp to one side and then the other, and neither way went anywhere the light could follow.

"This is as far as they sent us," she said. "After this, I don't know any more than you do."

"Then we'll both be guessing." Seralune tried to make it sound lighter than it was. "Which way would you guess?"

Nereth looked down the left-hand way for a long time. "The water came from that side, when we cleared it."

"Then that side."

### B. What she had done

For a long time, the only thing Seralune could hear was what she had done.

She had been free in the wash passage. The guard hadn't so much as glanced at her, and Cyrandor was already turning back for Nereth, and in a few more steps the crowd would have closed behind her. She had turned round anyway. She knew she would do it again, and that didn't help at all, because Cyrandor was on the other side of an iron door with its key at the bottom of a drain, and she was down here.

"Nereth, how far do these go?"

"I couldn't say, Your Highness."

"You said they sent you down once, to clear them."

"To the first turning." The lamp swung as Nereth looked back over her shoulder. "Somebody stood at the arch and called our names every so often, so that we'd know which way was out."

Nobody was standing at the arch now.

"When we're out," Seralune said, "when we're somewhere safe, I want to talk about him. I want you to tell me—"

"Not yet, Your Highness." It came very fast. Then, more quietly: "Please. Not down here."

At the first turning, Nereth stopped and held the lamp up to the two black openings, one after the other. Seralune watched her try to decide, and saw that she was trying even harder to look as if she knew.

"Left," Nereth said.

## 2. The circles

### A. The footprints

They walked for a long time.

The ledge was too narrow for two, so Nereth went first with the lamp, and Seralune came after her with one hand on Nereth's and the other on the wall. The wall was cold, and slick in places where something had grown over the stone. The water beside them didn't move. In the palace, everything ran: the falls, the channels in the courts, the fountain Elowen used to let her trail her fingers in when nobody was looking. This just lay there.

Her feet hurt in the borrowed shoes. She was so tired that twice she found she had stopped walking and didn't remember stopping, and both times Nereth's hand pulled her on.

Then she saw the prints.

They were in the silt where the ledge widened: soft soles, and harder ones beside them, going the same way they were going. She looked at them for a long time before she let herself understand them.

"Nereth."

Nereth brought the lamp down.

"Those are ours." Seralune put her foot beside the nearest soft print, and it fitted exactly. "We've been here. We've come round in a circle."

"We haven't, Your Highness." It was the even voice, the one Nereth had used to the guards and their swords. "We've come straight. I've been counting the turnings."

"How many?"

"Six." Nereth looked back the way they had come, and her lips moved. "Seven." Then, so quietly that she might have been asking herself, "Five?"

Seralune's mana crowded up under her ribs, the way it always did when she was frightened, so full she could hardly breathe round it. As always, it was no help at all.

The hand in hers had started to shake. Last night Nereth had stood a step from two drawn swords, and afterwards she had cleaned Seralune's cut and bound it, and her hands hadn't shaken then.

"It's all right," Seralune said. She didn't know why she said it. "We'll go back to the pillar and try the other way."

"Yes, Your Highness." Nereth was still looking at the prints. "The other way."

They went back to the pillar, and they tried the other way. A long time later the prints were there again, more of them now, trodden one over another, and Nereth walked straight across them without looking down.

### B. Her mark

At the third turning, while Nereth held the lamp up to choose, Seralune picked a chip of stone out of the silt and scratched a line on the pillar beside her, as high as she could reach. It took three tries before it showed.

Nereth watched her. "What's that for, Your Highness?"

"So we'll know if we see it again."

"We won't."

She said it so certainly that Seralune almost believed her, and was ashamed of how much she wanted to.

The ledge narrowed, and widened, and narrowed again. Seralune's heels were rubbed raw in the borrowed shoes, and she stopped feeling them, and then stopped feeling her feet at all. She thought about the room with the fire in it that she had walked out of this morning. She thought about the bed nobody had slept in, and wondered whether anyone had stripped it yet.

Then the lamp found a pillar, and on the pillar, as high as she could reach, a pale scratch.

She put her finger in it. It was hers. She had made it, and it had taken three tries.

"Nereth. We've come back."

"That isn't yours." Nereth didn't even look at it. "Yours was on the other side."

"It's mine. I made it, and you watched me make it."

"Then someone else has made one the same." Nereth held the lamp up to the left-hand way. "This way, Your Highness."

It was the way they had taken last time.

Seralune didn't move. Her mana had come up, cold and enormous under her ribs, and Nereth, who hadn't looked round once, stopped where she was.

"Don't," Nereth said, without turning. "Whatever you're doing. Don't."

"I'm not doing anything."

Nereth stood a moment longer with her back to her. Then she went on into the left-hand way, and Seralune, because the lamp was going and the dark was coming in behind it, went after her.

## 3. What Nereth hears

### A. Her name

It was Nereth who stopped this time, so suddenly that Seralune walked into her back.

"Listen."

Seralune listened. There was the water, dripping somewhere ahead: one drop, then a long wait, then another. There was her own heart, and Nereth's breathing, which had gone quick and shallow.

"I can't hear anything."

"Under the dripping." Nereth had her head on one side. "Someone's talking. There's more than one of them. Can't you hear?"

Seralune held her breath until her chest hurt. There was nothing under the dripping at all.

"Nereth, there's no one down here but us."

"They're saying my name." Nereth said it simply, the way she might have told her the water in the jug was cold. "They keep saying it. Why are they saying my name?"

Seralune's mana came up again, under her ribs and behind her eyes, and Nereth flinched and pressed her free hand flat over her ear.

"Louder," she whispered. "They've got louder."

### B. The pattern

Nereth had stopped walking. She was staring at the wall, where roots had begun to come through between the stones, and she had her free hand pressed against her own chest as if something in it hurt.

"Don't look at it, Your Highness."

"At what?"

"The pattern." Her voice was very quiet. "In the wall. It keeps going in. Every time I look at it, it goes further in, and it wants me to follow it."

Seralune looked. There were roots and wet stone, and the lamplight sliding on both. There was no pattern at all.

She made herself say it gently. "There's nothing there, Nereth."

"I know." Nereth didn't take her eyes off the wall. "I know there isn't. I can still see it."

Then she shut her eyes, and walked on with them shut, and let Seralune lead her by the hand past the place where it was.

## 4. The kindness

### A. The shawl

Nereth was shivering. Seralune could feel it through their joined hands, a fine, steady shaking that didn't stop, and she couldn't bear it.

She pulled the shawl off her own head. This morning Nereth had put it over her hair and tucked the ends in, then stood back to look, and tucked them in again. Seralune put it round Nereth's shoulders now and tried to do the same.

"There. You're freezing."

"I'm quite all right, Your Highness."

It was what she had said last night, sitting on the very edge of the chair by the hearth with her hands folded in her lap. It was even the same voice. But she was shaking so hard now that the shawl wouldn't stay.

"You're not. Hold still and let me—"

"*Don't.*" Nereth dragged the shawl off and pushed it back at her. "Don't fuss over me. I'm not something you have to look after."

"I didn't mean it like that. I'm sorry, I only—"

"Sorry." Nereth laughed, short and ugly. Seralune had never heard her make a sound like it. "You're always sorry. You were sorry about the order this morning, and you were sorry for shouting last night. What good has it done anyone?"

Seralune stood with the shawl in her hands.

"None," Nereth said, and her voice went even again, as if someone had smoothed it flat with a hand. "I beg your pardon, Your Highness. Shall we go on?"

### B. "Sit down"

Nereth's foot went off the edge of the ledge. She caught herself on the wall before she went into the water, and then she stayed there, leaning on the stone with her eyes shut, breathing as if she'd been running.

"Sit down, Nereth. Just for a little while."

"I'm quite all right, Your Highness."

"You said that last night, too, after you'd been standing in that corner for hours." Seralune put a hand on her shoulder. "Sit down."

"I said I'm all right!"

It went off down the channel ahead of them and came back from behind, as if the whole place had said it with her. Nereth put her hand over her own mouth.

For a long moment neither of them moved.

Then Nereth took her hand away, slowly, and when she spoke again it was perfectly calm. "Please stop telling me to sit down, Your Highness. I'm not a child, and you aren't my mother."

She didn't seem to know what she had said. She just picked up the lamp and walked on, and Seralune followed her, and didn't say anything at all, because she couldn't trust her voice.

## 5. Seralune takes the lead

### A. "That's an order"

When the pillar came round for the fourth time, Seralune stopped.

She knew it by the crack that ran up one side. She had known it the last time, and the time before, and both times she had said nothing: because Nereth was so sure, and because Nereth had been down here before and she hadn't, and because she was so tired that being led was easier than thinking. She had let everyone lead her since she woke. Thaer had taken her up the stairs, and Cyrandor had taken her down them, and Nereth had taken her into this.

*Very well. Not any more.*

"Give me the lamp, Nereth. I'll go first."

"You don't know the way, Your Highness."

"Neither do you!"

It went off down every channel at once and came back at them from all sides. Nereth's hand clamped on hers so hard that the bones ground together, and Seralune gasped. The grip let go.

"I'm sorry," Seralune said, and heard herself say it, and could have bitten her tongue. "Nereth, we've passed this pillar four times. Give me the lamp."

Nereth held on to it.

"Give me the lamp. That's an order."

This morning she had told Nereth she was sorry for giving her one, and asked her properly, and been glad she had. It had taken her half a day to forget.

"Yes, Your Highness," Nereth said, and put the lamp into her hand.

Seralune held it up. Their prints went left, trodden over and over, and they went straight on. To the right, low down, roots hung over an opening she hadn't seen before, and the silt in front of it was smooth.

*Left we've done, and straight on, and we've never once been through there.*

She pushed the roots aside. They were cold, and softer than roots should be, and after she let go of them, they went on moving for a while.

"Go on," Nereth said, close behind her. She was so close that Seralune could feel her breath on the back of her neck, quick and warm. "Go on, Your Highness. Go on. Go on."

She had never heard Nereth want anything before.

### B. Nereth lets go

Nereth stopped at the pillar and didn't go on.

She stood with the lamp held out in front of her, looking at the three ways, and she didn't seem to see any of them. Her face had gone very still, the way it went when she stood in her corner, and her lips were moving.

"Nereth?"

"I don't know," Nereth said. "I don't know which one we came down. I don't know which one goes out." She turned and looked at Seralune, and for the first time since the iron door, she looked frightened. "Your Highness, I don't know where we are."

Seralune took the lamp out of her hand, gently, the way you take something from a child who might drop it. Nereth let her.

"Then we'll take the one we haven't been down," Seralune said, as if it were simple.

There were prints going left and prints going straight on. To the right, low down behind a hanging mat of roots, there was an opening that nobody's feet had touched. Seralune held the roots aside and went through first.

Behind her, very softly, Nereth laughed, the way someone laughs at a thing that has pleased them.

## 6. Deeper

### A. The rat, and the servant in grey

The way went down. The lamp found roots on the walls, then roots over the walls, so thick that the stone only showed in patches. After a while there was a light that didn't come from the lamp: a pale glow in the roots themselves, so faint that she only saw it when she held the lamp behind her.

Small white flowers grew out of the roots. When she brought the lamp past, they turned on their stems to follow it. When she held it still, they went on turning, slowly, until they were facing her.

Something came along the ledge towards them.

She took it for a rat until the light found it. It had a rat's shape and a rat's tail, but roots had come out through its back and along its sides, with the little white flowers growing among them, and where its eyes should have been there were more flowers, shut tight.

It came on without seeming to know they were there. It went past Seralune's feet without stopping. At Nereth's, it stopped.

It lifted its head, and the flowers where its eyes should have been opened, one after the other.

Nereth kicked it into the channel. It went under without a sound, and the flowers stayed open all the way down.

The first of the dead was sitting a little further on, against the wall, with its knees drawn up as if it had stopped to rest and never got up again. Roots had gone in at its mouth and come out between its ribs, where the cloth had rotted away. What was left of the cloth was grey.

Seralune looked down at her own sleeve.

"They sent people down here," she said. Her voice sounded strange to her. "You told me. To clear the channels."

"Some of them didn't come back." Nereth stepped over its legs without looking down. "We were told they'd run off."

There were more of them after that. One lay face down in the water with the roots holding it under. Two sat against the wall together, one with its head on the other's shoulder, the way people sit when they've been waiting for someone a long time.

Nereth stepped over all of them.

### B. Someone Nereth knew

The lamp found a shoe first, on its side on the ledge. Then the foot it belonged to, and then the rest of her, half grown into the wall, in grey.

Nereth stopped.

Seralune waited for her to cry out, or turn away, or reach for her hand. Nereth did none of those things. She stood and looked down at the woman with her head a little on one side.

"She did the ironing," she said. "She used to sing while she worked. The same song all day, until somebody threw something at her to make her stop." She looked at the roots coming out of the woman's mouth. "They told us she'd run off with a soldier."

"Nereth, I'm so sorry."

"Why?" Nereth sounded honestly puzzled. "It's only her."

She stepped over the woman's legs and went on. After a little while, very quietly, she began to hum. Seralune didn't know the song. She didn't have to ask whose it was.

All round them, in the roots, the white flowers turned to listen.

### C. "I am watching her"

Nereth had begun to talk.

At first Seralune thought it was to her. Then she saw that Nereth's head was turned back the way they had come, as though someone were walking there, just out of the light.

"I am," Nereth was saying. "I am watching her. I haven't let go of her once. You can ask her yourself."

"Nereth." Seralune's mouth had gone dry. "Who are you talking to?"

"He said even if she seems—" Nereth stopped. She turned back, and looked at Seralune as if Seralune were the one who had been talking to nobody. "Talking to? No one, Your Highness. Who would there be?"

Seralune didn't answer her. She was looking past Nereth's shoulder, to where the lamp didn't reach, and she couldn't make herself stop.

## 7. The lash-out

### A. The iron door

Nereth let go of her hand.

She went to the wall, took hold of a root as thick as a rope, and pulled on it with both hands, the way she had pulled on the iron ring.

"Cyrandor, open it." She set her foot against the stone and hauled. "Open the door. *Open the door!*"

"Nereth." Seralune caught her arm. "He isn't here. There's no door. It's only the wall."

Nereth stopped pulling. She looked down at Seralune's hand on her arm.

"Why do you keep touching me?"

"I only—"

"Every time you touch me, they get louder." She tore her arm free. "Every time. What are you doing to me?"

"I'm not doing anything!"

Seralune's mana came up so hard that the lamp shook in her hand, and Nereth flinched as if she had been struck. When she lifted her head, it was a face Seralune had never seen her wear.

"You turned round."

Seralune couldn't speak.

"In the wash passage." Nereth came a step closer. "He was already coming back for me. You only had to keep walking, and he'd have got us both through that door. But you turned round, so he stayed. Do you understand that, Your fucking Highness? He stayed because of *you*."

She had thought it herself, all the way down from the arch. It was worse to hear it from someone else.

"I couldn't leave you there," she whispered.

"No. You couldn't. So he had to."

Nereth's arm came across her throat and drove her back against the wall. The lamp went out of Seralune's hand, and she heard it break on the ledge, and the flame went out.

In the pale glow from the roots, Nereth's face was a hand's width from hers. All along the wall behind her, the white flowers had turned on their stems towards Seralune, every one of them.

"Do you know what he told me," Nereth said softly, "before I'd ever set eyes on you? 'Watch over her. Even if she seems dangerous, she's important.'" She laughed, and it wasn't her laugh. "*You.* Why you? I could burn this whole place down round you, and you'd still be standing there saying sorry."

*Dangerous? How could I be dangerous? I've never had magic in my life.*

The arm pressed harder. Seralune's mana crowded up and up, behind her eyes, under her ribs, into her throat where the arm was, and Nereth's face twisted, and she leaned in with all her weight.

### B. "He's dead"

Nereth let go of her hand. She went to the wall, took hold of a root as thick as a rope, and pulled on it with both hands, the way she had pulled on the iron ring.

"Cyrandor, open it. Open the door!"

"Nereth, he isn't here. There's no door."

Nereth stopped pulling. She stood with both hands still on the root, and her shoulders began to shake.

"He's dead," she said. "He's dead, isn't he? They've killed him."

"You don't know that." Seralune's voice came out thin. "Thaer sent for him himself. Thaer wouldn't—"

"Thaer!" Nereth turned on her. "Your brother let them mend that thing for you all night and called it keeping you safe. And you turned round." She was crying now, without seeming to notice. "He was coming back for me. You only had to keep walking. You turned round, and he stayed."

Her arm came across Seralune's throat and drove her back against the wall. The lamp went out of Seralune's hand and broke on the ledge, and then there was only the pale glow from the roots.

"He's dead," Nereth said into her face, "and you're alive, and I'm down here holding your *hand*."

Then, all at once, her voice dropped into something almost fond.

"I could burn this whole place down round you. I can feel it, Your Highness. It's right here." She pressed her free hand flat against her own chest. "And you'd still be standing there saying sorry."

### C. Quiet

Nereth didn't shout. That was the worst of it.

She let go of the root and turned round, and smiled at Seralune in the pale light from the walls. When she spoke, it was in her ordinary voice, the one she had used last night to tell the guards that Her Highness was tired.

"You turned round, Your Highness. In the wash passage. I watched you do it."

She took the lamp out of Seralune's hand, held it up between them for a moment to look at her by it, and then let it drop. It broke on the ledge, and the flame went out.

"He was coming back for me anyway. You only had to keep walking." She came closer, one step and then another, until the wall was at Seralune's back. "But you can't, can you? You can't ever leave anything alone. You have to go to it. You have to touch it."

She put her hand flat on Seralune's chest, over her heart, and leaned on it.

"So he stayed," she said. "And here we are."

## 8. "Help me"

### A. The voice

"Help me."

It came from the wall at Seralune's shoulder.

The dead servant sitting there hadn't moved, but its jaw hung open round the roots, and the voice had come out of it: a girl's voice, hoarse and cracked, as if she had been calling for a very long time and nobody had come.

Seralune knew that voice. She had made it herself, against a door with no handle.

Nereth's arm went slack.

"Help me." It came again from further along, out of another of the dead, then from ahead, and from the roots overhead, all in the same voice. "Help me. Please. Please, somebody help me."

"Someone's alive," Seralune said. "Nereth, someone's alive down here."

Nereth was staring at the open mouth. "They're dead, Your Highness." It was nearly her own voice again. "All of them. Look at them."

"Then who's calling?"

She ducked under Nereth's arm and went towards it. She heard Nereth come after her. Then Nereth's hand found hers in the half-dark and closed on it, tight, and didn't let go.

### B. Her own voice

"Help me."

It was a girl's voice, young and worn thin, and it came out of the dead servant beside them, out of the open mouth with the roots in it.

"Please. I can't find the way back. I keep coming back to the same place."

Beside her, Nereth had gone completely still.

"Where are you?" Seralune called. Her own voice cracked. "I can hear you. Where are you?"

For a moment there was nothing. Then, from somewhere ahead, out of another of the dead, in a voice that was hers, exactly hers, cracked in the same place:

"*Where are you?*"

Seralune's hand went to her mouth.

"*I can hear you,*" said her own voice, further off now. "*Where are you?*"

"Someone's lost down here," she whispered. "Someone else is lost down here."

"Yes," said Nereth. "Us."

Seralune went after the voices anyway. She didn't know how not to. Nereth came after her, and caught her hand, and didn't let go.

## 9. The lizard and the bite

### A. "Frightened things still bite"

It was in a hollow at the very end, caught in the roots: a lizard, no longer than her hand. Roots had grown through it and out of it, and small white flowers had opened all along its back. Its sides went in and out, quick and shallow, the way a hurt thing breathes, and every time they did, the voice came out of the dead behind them.

"Help me."

Seralune's chest hurt.

"It's caught," she whispered. "Nereth, it's caught in them. It's hurt."

"It's watching your hand." Nereth's breath was against her ear. "Not your face."

"It's frightened."

"Frightened things still bite."

It was Nereth's own voice, dry and exact, the voice that had told her the linen stair was a wall. Seralune heard it, and knew it was right, and reached out anyway.

Nereth wrenched her arm back.

The lizard came off the roots at Nereth's face.

She screamed until she had no breath left to do it. Then she went quiet, and that was worse. She knelt in the channel with both hands over the left side of her face, rocking, and the lizard was gone, and Seralune hadn't seen where.

"Nereth. Nereth, let me see—"

Nereth lifted her head. Black-purple lines were spreading out from under her hands, into the skin round her eye, up towards her temple and into her hair.

She opened her mouth, and the voice that came out wasn't hers.

"Help me."

It was the voice from the walls.

"No," Seralune said. "No. Nereth, look at me. Look at me—"

Nereth's hand found her wrist and held on hard enough to hurt. For one breath, the voice was her own again.

"Seralune—"

It was the first time she had ever said her name.

### B. Laughing

It was in a hollow at the very end, caught in the roots: a lizard, no longer than her hand, with roots grown through it and small white flowers open all along its back.

When Seralune came close, the voices stopped. They stopped all round them at once, in every open mouth in every wall, and the quiet afterwards was the loudest thing she had heard since the arch.

The lizard turned its head, slowly, and looked past her at Nereth.

"Your Highness," Nereth said, very quietly. "Come away from it."

"It's hurt."

"Come away from it."

Seralune reached for it, and Nereth wrenched her arm back, and the lizard came off the roots at Nereth's face and hung there by its teeth, under her left eye, until she tore it off and flung it away down the channel.

She didn't scream. She laughed.

Black-purple lines were spreading out from the bite, under the skin. Nereth stood with her hand pressed to them and laughed until her legs went, and she knelt in the channel, still laughing. Between her fingers, a light had started: a thin flame along her palm.

"Nereth, put it out. Please. Put it out."

"I can't." She looked up at Seralune, and she was smiling, and her eye on the bitten side was all black. "I don't want to."

## 10. The fire

### A. Light, then nothing, then the falls

The light came first.

It filled the channel from wall to wall, white at the heart and purple at the edges, and for an instant Seralune saw everything at once, every root and every one of the dead, as clearly as if it were noon. Then she couldn't see anything at all.

There was no sound.

Then there was nothing but sound, bigger than the falls had ever been under her window, so big that it wasn't a sound any more but a weight, and the floor went out from under her.

The channel heaved. Somewhere behind them, stone came down and went on coming down. Ahead of them, the fire went away down the channel faster than anything she had ever seen move, and everywhere it went, the roots went up white, and the dead went up, and the walls lit from inside, chamber after chamber, deeper and deeper into the mountain, further than she had known there was any mountain to go through.

Then it came back.

It filled the channel from side to side, and it came back up it towards them. Seralune had time to pull Nereth against her and nothing else.

It reached them, and went round them.

It went past on both sides, so close that she could have touched it. Her hair didn't stir. Her skin didn't burn. She felt no heat from it at all, and in the middle of everything, her mana moved, the way it had in the black, and settled again.

*It went round us.*

Nereth hung against her, heavy and quite still.

### B. The breath before

Before the fire, the air went.

It went out of the channel all at once, towards Nereth, and took Seralune's breath with it. Every white flower on the walls bent towards Nereth's open hand. Seralune's ears hurt. Then the pale light in the roots went out, all of it at once, as if the walls had shut their eyes.

Nereth's hand opened.

Seralune never saw the fire leave it. There was white, and a weight that came down on her whole body at once, and a sound she felt in her teeth and the soles of her feet before she heard it. Then she heard it, and it didn't stop.

The mountain answered. Far off down the channel, then nearer, then under her, stone broke and went on breaking, chamber after chamber, further than she could count. The ledge dropped under her feet and caught again. Water that hadn't moved in a thousand years stood up out of its channel and went past her as steam.

Then the fire came back.

She held on to Nereth, because there was nothing else in the world to hold, and turned her face into Nereth's hair, and it went round them.

## 11. The hole

### A. Stars

When it had gone, the roots were gone with it, and so were the dead.

The channel ran on ahead of her, black and bare and smoking, and the stone ticked as it cooled. A long way off, at the end of it, there was a ragged hole, and in it, stars.

Nereth lay across her knees and didn't move. Seralune put her ear to her mouth, and for a long, terrible time there was nothing, and then there was a breath.

She got Nereth onto her back somehow, with Nereth's arms over her shoulders and her own hands locked under her knees. Her legs shook so badly that she thought they would fold, the way they had in the black when she tried to stand.

*I am not going to die in a hole I don't even know. And neither are you.*

She walked towards the stars.

The hole was taller than the palace gates. The stone round its edges had melted and run like wax and set again, and below it, where the side of the mountain had been, there was a long black scar running down the slope until she lost it, still burning in places.

All her life, people had said there was nobody in Natharul like Thaer. She had never seen him do anything like this.

### B. The ocean below

She came out onto the lip of the hole and had to stop, because there was no more mountain in front of her.

The fire had gone straight through it. Below her, the slope fell away in a long burnt furrow, wider than the court she had stood in last night, with fires still burning along its edges and smoke going up into the stars. At the bottom of it, a long way down, something pale moved and caught the starlight, and moved again. It was a while before she understood that it was the ocean.

The wind came up the furrow into her face. It was cold, and it smelled of salt and burning, and it was the first air she had breathed since the arch that didn't smell of rot.

All her life, people had said there was nobody in Natharul like Thaer. She had never seen him do anything like this.

On her back, Nereth's head moved against her shoulder, and was still.

## 12. Fresh air, and down the mountain

### A. "It felt like me"

They were a long way down the furrow when Nereth's arms tightened round her neck.

"Your Highness."

Seralune nearly dropped her. Nereth's voice was hoarse, and very weak, but it was her own again, and so was the way she said it.

"Put me down."

"No."

"I can walk."

"You can't hold your own head up."

Nereth tried to, to prove her wrong, and couldn't.

They went on. The stars began to go out over the water, one by one.

"Leave me here, then," Nereth said, after a long time.

"No."

"You didn't even stop to think about it."

"I thought about it under the mountain."

"Under the mountain, I had my arm across your throat."

"That wasn't you."

Nereth was quiet for so long that Seralune thought she had fallen asleep.

"It felt like me," she said.

Seralune didn't have an answer for that. She hitched Nereth higher on her back and kept walking, because walking was something she could do.

The sky was greying over the ocean when Nereth spoke again, into her hair.

"If this is how you rescue everyone, Your Highness, I understand your brother's concern."

It was so exactly Nereth, so dry and so tired, that Seralune laughed, and then found she was crying, and couldn't stop, and didn't try to.

"When we get to the bottom," she said, when she could, "I'll find you a bed. You can spend the whole day telling me everything I did wrong."

"Only a day?"

### B. Sorry, and the ocean

They were halfway down the furrow when Nereth's arms tightened round her neck.

"I'm sorry, Your Highness."

It was her own voice. Seralune had to stop walking.

"For what?"

"All of it." Nereth's breath caught against her neck. "I remember all of it. The wall, and what I said, and your throat—"

Seralune hitched her higher on her back and went on.

"Sorry won't get us down this mountain," she said.

There was a long silence. Then, against her neck, she felt Nereth smile.

The light came up out of the water before the sun did. First the sky went grey over it, then the grey went pale, and then the ocean was there below them, wider than anything Seralune had ever seen from her window, going on and on until it met the sky.

She felt Nereth lift her head to look at it.

*I've wanted to see what's past the falls since I was small.*

Neither of them said anything. Seralune went on down the mountain with Nereth on her back, and Nereth looked at the ocean the whole way, and didn't put her head down again.

- **The engine of the chapter.** Seralune's mana crowds up when she's frightened (Chapters 4 and 5), and every time it does, Nereth gets worse: "Louder." She can't stop being frightened, and she never works out the connection. The reader can feel it, and nothing says it.
- **Nereth's descent is her own habits breaking, in the steps of the author's guide:**
  - disorientation: the count, "Five?";
