# Chapter 8 – The Road Owed to the Dead

Alaric took Wena by the collar and pulled her back off the open grass. She came with her head still turned towards the sound, and he had to drag her the last few steps in under the branches before she would lie down. He sat beside her with his back against a trunk and Gerolt's sword across his knees.

He could feel the hum in the ground for a long time after the ship had gone. It grew fainter and fainter, until he couldn't tell whether it was still there or whether his chest was only remembering it. Down in Kelmend the bell was still ringing, and the torches on the wall were still moving.

Silas stayed out at the edge of the wood, where he could see both the town and the sky. He watched the ship until its light was almost lost beyond the trees. Then he crouched beside Alaric.

"What really happened on Gerolt's farm? Why were those elves chasing you? What did you two do?"

"What did we *do*?" Alaric stared at him. "He found me, Silas. He took me into his house and gave me something to eat. That's all. Then they came."

"And before he found you?"

"I woke up under a dead man! There were bodies all round me, and when I got out from under him there were more, everywhere I looked. Hundreds. Maybe thousands. I couldn't count them." His voice was rising. Wena lifted her head, and he put a hand on her neck. "Men, elves… things with horns and claws. Beastfolk, Gerolt called them, but these were wearing armour. There were silver machines among them, too. One was lying on its side. I don't remember anything before waking up there. I've told you that."

Silas glanced towards the town. "Keep your voice down."

"Then stop asking me as if I did something!" Alaric caught himself and lowered it. "Gerolt was just trying to help me. He hadn't heard a battle either. He didn't even know they were in his field until he went outside."

For a while Silas said nothing.

"I heard 'thousands' and thought you were talking shit," he said at last.

Alaric looked down at Wena. His hand was still gripping the loose skin at her neck; he let it go and stroked her instead.

The bell stopped. In the quiet it left behind, a dog was still barking somewhere in the town.

Then a light came back.

It was a single blue-green spark, far out over the trees where the ship had gone, and at first Alaric thought the ship was turning. It wasn't. The ship's light went on shrinking towards the river, and this one grew. It came back over the wood, low enough that Wena flattened herself against him, and on across the fields with a thin, high hum of its own, much fainter than the ship's. It passed over the end of the wall by the gate. Then it sank behind the roofs and didn't come up again.

Silas had seen it too. He watched the place where it had gone down, and up on the wall the torches began to drift towards that end, one after another.

*I thought it was leaving. It would go off east, everyone in Kelmend would stop looking at the sky, and we'd walk in while they were all still talking about it.* He watched the torches crowd together above the gate. *Except it left something behind.*

"What was that?" he whispered.

"Don't know." Silas didn't take his eyes off the town. "Whatever it is, it's come down by the gate."

They waited for full dark. The last of the red went out of the sky behind the town. Below them, the fields turned from grey to black, and the windows in Kelmend showed brighter for it. Silas opened the sack and felt about in it with his good hand, and came out with the last of the bread. He broke it against his knee and passed half to Alaric without a word.

Alaric gave Wena a piece of his. He would have liked to give her more.

"There's an old way through the south wall," Silas said. "Behind those houses. I'll check the other end's clear before we go through."

"And then Marta's?"

"Then we get close enough to see what's happening there. Her place'll be packed at this hour."

Alaric looked towards the gate. "Couldn't we just knock at the back? Tell her Gerolt sent us?"

"With half of Kelmend in there? Some bastard would fetch a guard for the price of his next drink. We'll wait till she's closing."

"Where?"

"Out of the light, for a start. I'll find us a spot where you can rest that foot while I watch the inn." Silas nodded at the sword in Alaric's hand. "Get the rolled bed off my shoulder. We need that blade covered before we go down."

Alaric reached for it. "I'll have to unwrap it before I knock."

"Yes. And tuck the point in properly. I'm not dragging you through Kelmend with the bloody thing in your leg."

Alaric laid the sword in the grass, picked the cord loose and unrolled the bed. It was one thick blanket, worn shiny along the edges, and it smelled of the cave. He put the sword down on it with the point towards him, folded the end of the blanket over it twice, and rolled the rest round the blade until the grip was buried too. Then he tied the cord round both ends. The bundle was longer than his arm, and it was heavier than the sword had ever felt on its own.

When he had pulled the last knot tight, Silas was unfastening his cloak.

"Put this on."

"Why?"

"Because nobody in Kelmend dresses like you." Silas held it out. "Hood up when we get near the houses."

Alaric took it. It was heavy, and still warm, and it smelled of smoke. When he got it round his shoulders, the hem lay in a heap on the ground beside him.

"I'll trip over it."

"Hitch it up, then."

There was no moon yet. They went down the slope with only the lights of the town to walk towards, Silas first, then Alaric with the bundle under one arm and the cloak bunched in his other fist, and Wena close against his legs. At the bottom, a cart track ran along the edge of the fields towards the wall. The carts had cut it into ruts and the ruts had dried hard, and after the first dozen steps every one of them seemed to be exactly where Alaric put his foot.

"Silas—wait. Just a moment."

Silas stopped. Alaric stood with his foot half raised, unable to put it down where he had meant to.

"It's these ruts. Every time I step in one…" He pressed his lips together until he could lower his heel. "I can walk. I just can't keep up with you over this."

"Get onto the grass, then." Silas stepped off the track himself, onto the strip along the edge of the field. "We'll take it slower. I need you walking when we reach the wall."

Alaric followed, watching where he put his foot. This time, when he looked up, Silas was beside him.

The houses along the wall were closer than they had looked from the slope. They were low and crowded, roof against roof, with the first row built right up against the stones of the town wall and the next row facing it across a narrow lane. Smoke came out of holes in the roofs as often as out of chimneys. Lamplight showed at the edges of shutters that didn't fit.

Where the lane went in among the houses, a lantern hung from a pole. Two men stood under it, and one of them had a spear.

Silas stopped, and behind him Alaric pulled the hood up.

"They don't usually put anyone on that lane," Silas said quietly. "We go round the back. Stay close, and keep her quiet."

*Usually.* Alaric looked at him, but Silas was already moving.

They went along the backs of the outer row, where every house had a patch of ground behind it: a few rows of something growing, a pile of firewood, a shed with a pig grunting inside it. A woman came out of a back door almost in front of them and scraped a pot into a bucket, and they stood still against a fence until she had gone in again. The smell she left behind was onions, and something cooked in fat.

Wena's nose went up. Alaric's stomach growled so loudly that he was sure the woman would come back out to see what it was.

Through a gap in the next shutter he could see four people at a table, and a man feeding a small child from his own bowl, blowing on every spoonful first. The child kept turning its head away, and the man kept waiting. Further along, two voices were arguing about whose turn it had been to fetch the water, and neither of them seemed to mind much.

Alaric slowed without meaning to. He wanted to knock at one of those back doors and ask if he could sit by the fire for a while, and have somebody say yes.

Behind the next fence, a dog got up on its chain and growled.

Wena's hackles rose under his hand. He closed his fingers gently over her muzzle and bent down to her.

"No. Not now, girl. Please."

She stood stiff against him. The other dog growled once more, then lost interest and lay back down. Wena let out a long breath through her nose.

Silas had stopped to wait for them. "Good dog."

"She can smell their supper."

"So can I. Keep walking."

At the end of the row there was a gap between the last house and a lean-to, just wide enough to turn sideways through. Beyond it, the lane curved out of sight of the lantern and ended at the wall. A woodpile had been stacked against the stones there, and behind the woodpile, low down, there was a door in the wall itself. It was barely higher than Alaric's chest, and an iron grate was set into it, black with age, with a bar across it.

Silas crouched and put his good hand on the bar.

"The staple's rusted through. Has been for years." He lifted, and the bar came up with it, staple and all. "Now the grate. It sits on pins. Take that side. Lift when I say."

Alaric put the bundle down, got his fingers through the cold bars and lifted when Silas said. The grate rose off its pins with a scrape that went straight through his teeth. They walked it sideways and leaned it against the woodpile.

Behind it was a passage through the thickness of the wall, short and low and smelling of damp and old ash. At its far end there were boards nailed across, with lantern light showing through the cracks. Silas went in bent double and pushed at the lowest board, and it swung inwards from a single nail at the top.

*He knew exactly which board.*

Wena went through without being asked. Alaric went after her with the bundle, and Silas came last and dragged the grate back across the opening behind them, swearing under his breath the whole time.

They came through into the back of a workshop. Shavings rustled under Alaric's boots, and there were barrels everywhere, finished and half finished, and hoops hanging on the walls. A window at the front looked out on a yard, and the lantern light came from there.

Silas's hand came back and caught Alaric's arm.

Out in the yard, by a brazier where the yard opened onto a lane, two men in helmets stood warming their hands. One of them was talking, loud and rough, and didn't seem to care who heard. The other was older, by his voice, and tired.

"They're fucking elves. They shouldn't be giving us orders in our own town."

"You gonna tell them that, then? Be my guest. I'd prefer to still have a home to go to."

The first man spat.

"I'm telling *you*. They say Gerolt put a few of the bastards down. Good for him."

"Keep your bloody voice down. We've still got the gate round to do."

"We should've been relieved an hour ago."

"I know. And now we've got the inn as well."

"What, Marta's?"

"The farmer's niece. Just inside the south gate. They want her questioned after she closes."

"What the fuck's she done?"

"How should I know? Finish the round, then go there. That's what I was told."

A buckle struck the wall. The first man swore under his breath.

"And we're still looking for this boy?"

"Boy and some mutt. Big one, apparently. Bring the boy in alive."

"Be nice if they told us what he looks like."

"Ask them yourself. Come on. Best get moving before they take it out on us."

Their boots scraped away across the yard. Silas kept his hand against Alaric's arm until the voices had gone.

Alaric found he was gripping Wena's collar so tightly that his knuckles hurt. He made himself loosen it.

*A boy and a big dog. That's all they've got.* He looked down at her. *And it's enough. Anyone who looks at us twice will know.*

*Good for him,* the loud one had said. Someone in this town had heard what Gerolt did and been glad of it. Alaric held on to that, because it was the only thing they had said that he wanted to keep.

Then the rest of it caught up with him.

*The farmer's niece. Marta's. They're going to ask her about Gerolt, which means she doesn't know yet. She's in there serving drinks, and she doesn't know he's dead, and the first she'll hear of it is from them.*

"Not through the yard," Silas said. "That's the way they've gone."

There was a side door at the other end of the workshop, away from the yard. It opened into a passage between two buildings, so narrow that the bundle scraped the walls on both sides, and at the end of the passage there was light and noise.

Silas stopped at the mouth of it and held out his good hand.

"Give her here."

"What?"

"They want a boy with a big dog. So we don't give them one." He nodded at the street. "I take her. You walk behind me, a few steps off, hood up. Don't come up beside me unless I stop."

Alaric looked down at Wena. She was looking at the street, with her ears up.

"She won't want to go with you."

"She'll pull. Let her pull at me."

Alaric took his hand off her collar. Silas hooked two fingers of his good hand through it in its place.

"Go on, girl," Alaric said. "Go with him."

Wena went, because Silas gave her no choice about it, and she looked back at Alaric twice before they were even out of the passage.

He let them get a few steps ahead, then followed.

The street was full. Lamps hung on poles along both sides of it, and under them there were stalls still trading: a man weighing nails on a scale, a woman with cloth over her arm arguing about a price, a boy turning meat over hot coals so that the smoke went into everybody's faces. A cart stood half unloaded by one of the stalls, with sacks still piled on it, and people went past it without looking. Gold changed hands everywhere. Alaric had never seen so many people in one place, and every one of them had somewhere to be.

At the far end of the street, where it met the gate, there were more torches than anywhere else, and a crowd held back by guards from something Alaric couldn't see.

"—came down right by the gatehouse. Elves, I'm telling you, three of them—"

"—it's the dead ones out past the river they're here about—"

"—they'll be putting soldiers in our houses next, you watch—"

He kept his head down. The cobbles were worse than the ruts, and the bundle kept knocking against his leg, and every time Wena looked back for him, he wanted to drop out of sight so that she would stop.

Ahead, the crowd thickened round a wooden platform built out from the front of a building. A man was calling numbers. Silas took Wena along the edge of it. Alaric had to wait while two men carried a barrel across in front of him, and while he waited, the crowd shifted round him until he was at the front of it.

The young woman on the platform was naked.

Alaric looked away, then looked back. There were scars across her shoulders and arms. She was trying to cover herself while the man beside her lifted the chain fastened round her neck.

Someone called a number. A voice on the other side of the crowd called a higher one.

The man pulled the chain to turn her towards them. She stumbled, and both hands went to her throat. When he stopped pulling, she tried to cover herself again.

Alaric found Silas a few paces away, holding Wena close.

"Th-that girl… what happened to her? Why are they giving him coins for her?"

Silas followed his look. "They're bidding for her. Whoever pays the most gets to drag her off."

"Takes her *where*?"

"Wherever he bloody wants. She doesn't get a choice."

Alaric turned back towards the platform. A man near the front was counting coins into his palm.

"But she doesn't want to go. Look at her. Why are they letting him do that?"

"Because she's Faint. Only Faint can be sold, and those guards know it as well as he does."

"Gerolt said they took children away. I thought…" He couldn't finish. "And the scars? Did that man do that to her?"

Silas looked at the man holding the chain, then at the woman.

"I can't tell you who made those scars." He moved closer so they could speak without raising their voices. "But look how he's using that chain. He doesn't give a shit who watches, because nobody here's going to stop him."

Wena pressed against Alaric's knee. He put his hand on her head, still watching as the man counting the coins came forward.

Then Silas's hand closed on his sleeve.

"Patrol. Get off."

Two guards were coming down the street towards them with a lantern, taking their time, looking at faces. Alaric stepped away from Silas into the crowd, and Wena came after him.

She hit the end of Silas's arm hard enough to pull him half a step. He hauled her back, and she twisted in his grip and whined, high and loud, with her eyes fixed on Alaric.

The lantern swung towards the noise.

*Don't look back. Don't—she's looking at me. As long as she can see me, she'll keep pulling.*

The half-unloaded cart was only a few steps away. Alaric stepped behind it.

Wena's whine went on a moment longer, and then it stopped.

Through the gap between two sacks he saw the guard with the lantern look at Wena, and then at Silas.

"That yours?"

"She's after the meat," Silas said.

The guard looked at Silas's face. He looked at the dog again, and then at the crowd, slowly, one face after another. His eyes went across the cart. Alaric kept still and didn't breathe, with the hood pulled forward and the bundle held against his chest.

"Keep her on a rope, then."

The guard moved on, and the other went with him. Alaric stayed behind the cart until their lantern was well past the platform, then came out and followed Silas without catching up.

His legs were shaking, but he kept them moving.

The inn stood on the corner nearest the gate, and it was easily the biggest building in the street. There was light in every window, upstairs and down. The front door kept opening, and every time it did, the noise came out with it: voices, laughing, somebody singing badly and somebody else telling him to stop. The smell of roasting meat came out too, and new bread, and Alaric was hungry all over again. People sat on the bench outside with their cups. A board hung over the door on a chain, too high in the shadow to read.

*Marta's in there. Somewhere in all that.*

Silas didn't slow down. He took them past the front and round the corner, into a lane that ran along the back of the inn. Here there was a yard with barrels stacked against its wall and a back door with a lamp over it. Across the lane from it stood an open shed with an old cart inside, and straw on the floor round its wheels.

"In there," Silas said. He let go of Wena's collar, and she was against Alaric's legs before he had taken a step. "I'm going up to the end of the lane, to see if anyone's watching the back. Stay here with her. You'll see me the whole time."

Alaric sat down in the straw behind the cart, with the bundle across his knees and Wena half on top of him, and watched Silas walk up the lane to the corner. He stood there a while, looking both ways. Then he came back.

"Nobody. Not yet."

He sat down beyond the cart wheel, where he could see the back door.

"The guards said his niece." Alaric looked towards the inn. "They meant Marta?"

"Yes. His brother's daughter."

Alaric looked down at the sword wrapped across his knees.

"How am I meant to tell her, Silas? I thought I'd show her this, say he sent me, and she'd…" He shook his head. "He was feeding me. He was sitting there, worrying about whether I'd eaten enough, and then those riders came. How do I tell her he's dead?"

Silas watched the inn for a moment before answering.

"What did he tell you to say?"

"That the old fool sent me."

"Then start there. Show her the sword and say what he told you. When she asks what happened, tell her straight."

"And the guards? If they find me in there, they're going to hurt her too, aren't they?"

"They could." Silas looked at him. "So she hears about them from you, before those bastards reach her door. Marta won't thank us for keeping her in the dark."

After that, they waited.

The singing stopped first. Then the front door began opening more often, and the voices that came out of it were saying goodnight. A cart went by at the end of the lane. Once, the back door of the inn opened, and a woman in an apron came out with a bucket and tipped it into the gutter, and Alaric was halfway onto his knees before Silas's hand pressed him back down.

"Not her."

The woman went in again. One by one, the windows upstairs went out.

Every time Alaric closed his eyes, he saw the platform again: the chain going tight, and the man counting coins into his palm, and the girl still trying to cover herself.

*Only Faint can be sold.* He thought of Gerolt by the fire, looking at the door and not at him. *What about someone with none? I asked him that. He said he didn't know what they'd do with me.*

*Well. Now I know what they do with the Faint.*

He pulled the cloak tighter round himself and made himself stop there.

Alaric listened for the loud voice and the tired one, somewhere out towards the gate. He heard boots once, and a shout, but they were far off, and they didn't come any nearer. *After the round, they said. How long does a round take? They were complaining about it, so it must be a long one. Unless that was only them complaining.*

He said the words over to himself, without making any sound. *The old fool sent me.* He would say that first. Then he would show her the sword. Then…

He couldn't get any further than that.

Downstairs, the front windows went out. Only one stayed lit, at the back, low down beside the door. Something was being scraped across a floor in there, chairs maybe, and a woman's voice said something short to somebody, and a door banged.

"Now," Silas said.

Alaric untied the cord and unrolled the blanket, and took Gerolt's sword out of it. Silas rolled the bed up again one-handed, badly, and stuck it under his arm. They crossed the lane together with Wena between them.

Halfway across, Wena's head came up. She trotted the last few steps ahead of them and put her nose to the gap under the back door, and her tail began to move.

*She knows this door.*

Silas stopped beside the door, but made no move to knock.

"What's wrong?" Alaric asked.

"Go on. You knock."

"Why? I thought you knew her."

"She won't open for me, boy."

Alaric turned towards him. "What do you mean, she won't open for you?"

"She told me never to show my face again. Not here. Not anywhere near Kelmend."

"And you're telling me *now*? After bringing me all the way here?"

Silas kept looking at the door.

"I put it off. You've got Gerolt's message. I reckoned she'd listen to him, even if she couldn't stand the sight of me."

Alaric looked down at the sword.

*Was that why he'd wanted to take it himself?*

"So what are you going to do? Wait out here?"

"Where do you think I'm going? I'm staying with you." Silas shifted closer, where the person opening the door would see him. "Go on, Alaric. Knock."

Alaric lifted his hand and knocked.

Inside, the scraping stopped. Footsteps came towards the door, quick and impatient, and a bolt went back.

The door opened a little. Wena put her nose into the gap, and the woman inside looked down.

"Wena? What are you doing here?"

She opened it further and bent to touch her. Wena pressed her head into the woman's hand. Alaric drew breath, ready with the words he had been trying to get right.

Then she looked up and saw Silas.

"Marta," he said. "Wait—"

"Get the fuck out of Kelmend, Silas. Right now." She straightened, her hand falling away from Wena. "I told you I never wanted to see your face again. Did you think I didn't mean it?"

"Marta, hear him out. He's—"

"Go!"

Silas drew Wena back as Marta pulled the door shut.

It slammed before Alaric could say Gerolt's name.
