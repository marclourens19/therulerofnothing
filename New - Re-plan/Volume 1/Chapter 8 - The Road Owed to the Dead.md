# Chapter 8 – The Road Owed to the Dead

Alaric took Wena by the collar and pulled her back off the open grass. She came with her head still turned towards the sound, and he had to draw her the last few steps under the branches before she would lie down. He sat beside her with his back against a trunk and Gerolt's sword across his knees.

He could feel the hum in the ground for a long time after the ship had passed. It faded little by little, until he couldn't tell whether it was still there or whether his chest was only remembering it. Down in Kelmend the bell was still ringing, and the torches on the wall were moving towards the gate. The carts on the road had stopped.

Silas stayed at the edge of the wood, where he could see both the town and the sky. He watched the ship until its light was almost lost beyond the trees. Then he crouched beside Alaric.

"What really happened on Gerolt's farm? Why were those elves chasing you? What did you two do?"

"What did we *do*?" Alaric stared at him. "He found me, Silas. He took me into his house and gave me something to eat. That's all. Then they came."

"And before he found you?"

"I've told you! I woke up in a field full of bodies, and I don't remember anything before it."

His voice was rising. Wena lifted her head, and he put a hand on her neck.

"Men, elves… things with horns and claws. Beastfolk, Gerolt called them, but these were wearing armour. There were silver machines among them, too. One was lying on its side."

Silas glanced towards the town. "Keep your voice down."

"Then stop asking me as if I did something!" Alaric caught himself and lowered it. "Gerolt was just trying to help me. He hadn't heard a battle either. He didn't even know they were in his field until he went outside."

For a while Silas said nothing.

"I heard 'thousands' and thought you were talking shit," he said at last.

Alaric's hand was still gripping the loose skin at Wena's neck. He let it go and stroked her instead.

The bell stopped. In the quiet it left behind, a dog was barking somewhere in the town.

Then a light came back.

It was a single blue-green spark, far out over the trees where the ship had gone, and at first Alaric thought the ship was turning. It wasn't. The ship's light went on shrinking towards the river, and this one grew. A narrow hull took shape round it. It came back over the wood, low enough that Wena flattened herself against him, and on across the fields with a thin, high hum of its own. It passed over the end of the wall by the gate. Then it sank behind the roofs and didn't come up again.

Silas had seen it too. He watched the place where it had gone down, and up on the wall the torches began to crowd together.

*I thought it was leaving.* Alaric watched the lights above the gate. *Except it left something behind.*

"What was that?" he whispered.

"Don't know." Silas didn't take his eyes off the town. "Whatever it is, it's come down by the gate."

They waited for full dark. The last of the red went out of the sky behind the town. Below them, the fields turned from grey to black, and the windows in Kelmend showed brighter for it. Silas opened the sack with his good hand and came out with a piece of bread. He broke it against his knee and passed half to Alaric without a word.

Alaric gave Wena a piece of his. He would have liked to give her more.

"There's an old way through the south wall," Silas said. "Behind those houses. I'll check the other end's clear before we go through."

"And then Marta's?"

"Then we get close enough to see what's happening there. Her place'll be packed at this hour."

"Couldn't we just knock at the back?" Alaric asked. "Tell her Gerolt sent us?"

Silas looked away, towards the town. "With half of Kelmend in there? Some bastard would fetch a guard for the price of his next drink. We'll wait till she's closing."

"Where?"

"Out of the light, for a start. I'll find us a spot where you can rest that foot while I watch the inn." Silas nodded at the sword across Alaric's knees. "Get the rolled bed off my shoulder. We need that blade covered before we go down."

Alaric reached for it. "I'll have to unwrap it before I knock."

"Yes. And tuck the point in properly. I'm not dragging you through Kelmend with the bloody thing in your leg."

Alaric laid the sword in the grass, picked the cord loose and unrolled the bed. It was one thick blanket, worn shiny along the edges, and it smelled of the cave. He folded several layers over the point and rolled the rest round the blade until the grip was buried too. Then he tied the cord round both ends. The bundle was longer than his arm, and it was heavier than the sword had ever felt on its own.

When he had pulled the last knot tight, Silas was unfastening his cloak. His own heavy blade hung at his hip beneath it.

"Put this on."

"Why?"

"Because nobody in Kelmend dresses like you." Silas held it out. "Hood up when we get near the houses."

Alaric fastened it at his throat. It was heavy and still warm, and the hem lay in a heap on the ground beside him.

"I'll trip over it."

"Hitch it up, then."

While Alaric gathered it clear of his feet, Silas took a narrow strip of cloth from the sack and tied it to Wena's collar. He passed the loose end across.

"Keep her close. I don't want her getting to a door before we do."

There was no moon yet. They went down the slope with only the lights of the town to walk towards, Silas first, then Alaric with the bundle under one arm and Wena's lead in his other hand. At the bottom, a cart track ran along the edge of the fields towards the wall. The carts had cut it into ruts and the ruts had dried hard, and after the first dozen steps every one of them seemed to be exactly where Alaric put his foot.

"Silas—wait. Just a moment."

Silas stopped. Alaric stood with his foot half raised, unable to put it down where he had meant to.

"It's these ruts. Every time I step in one…" He pressed his lips together until he could lower his heel. "I can walk. I just can't keep up with you over this."

"Get onto the grass, then." Silas stepped off the track himself, onto the strip along the edge of the field. "We'll take it slower. I need you walking when we reach the wall."

Alaric followed, watching where he put his foot. This time, when he looked up, Silas was beside him.

The houses along the wall were closer than they had seemed from the slope. They were low and crowded, roof against roof, with the first row built right up against the stones and the next row facing it across a narrow lane. Smoke came out of holes in the roofs as often as out of chimneys. Lamplight showed at the edges of shutters that didn't fit.

Where the lane went in among the houses, a lantern hung from a pole. Two men stood under it, and one of them had a spear.

Silas stopped, and Alaric pulled the hood up.

"They don't usually put anyone on that lane," Silas said quietly. "We go round the back. Stay close, and keep her quiet."

*Usually.* Alaric looked at him, but Silas was already moving.

They went along the backs of the outer row, where every house had a patch of ground behind it: a few rows of something growing, a pile of firewood, a shed with a pig grunting inside it. A woman came out of a back door almost in front of them and scraped a pot into a bucket. They stood against a fence until she had gone in again. The smell she left behind was onions, and something cooked in fat.

Through a gap in the next shutter, Alaric could see four people at a table and a man feeding a small child from his own bowl, blowing on every spoonful first. The child kept turning its head away, and the man kept waiting. Further along, two voices were arguing about whose turn it had been to fetch the water, and neither of them seemed to mind much.

Alaric slowed without meaning to. He wanted to knock at one of those back doors and ask if he could sit by the fire for a while, and have somebody say yes.

Wena's nose went up towards the window. Alaric kept the lead short and followed Silas past it.

At the end of the row, Silas turned into a gap between two houses. Further in, it narrowed into a passage that ended at the wall. Bundles of kindling stood along one side; a broken handcart had been pushed against the other. In the wall at the end was a narrow timber door.

Silas put a hand up, and Alaric stopped.

On the other side, something heavy knocked against the wall.

"Leave that one there. We haven't the cart for it yet."

Another man answered. Then came the scrape of wood over stone.

Silas pointed Alaric towards the broken handcart. They crouched behind it, and Wena settled under the axle against Alaric's calf.

"That door?" Alaric whispered.

"Loading yard on the other side. I need to see who's using it. Stay here."

Silas went to the door and put his eye to a gap where one of the boards had split. Lantern light showed through it. He listened for a while, then came back and sat beside the cart.

"Too many of them. We'll wait."

"How long?"

"Till they stop putting their goods where we'd have to walk."

Beyond the door Alaric could hear footsteps and wheels, and somebody telling somebody else they had brought the wrong cart.

"And if they don't?"

Silas took the sack off his shoulder. "Then we don't go through. Drink something."

Alaric drank from the water skin and eased his boot off. His ankle had swollen against the leather again. He left it bare in the cool air and stretched his leg beneath the cart.

The yard stayed busy. Whenever he thought the last load had gone, another came. He heard a man count sacks, lose his place and start again. He heard a wheel stick somewhere, and four people tell its owner four different ways to free it. Once somebody laughed so hard that Alaric smiled before he remembered where he was.

The voices in the houses grew quieter. Later came the sound of dishes being washed, then a window shutting. One by one, the lights along the row went out.

Wena slept on. Alaric was cold now, despite the cloak, and he had stopped trying to work out which set of footsteps might be the last. He could hear people going home in the lane beyond the houses. They called to one another, said goodnight and went through doors that shut behind them. Nobody came down the gap where he and Silas waited.

Silas loosened the binding on his palm. Alaric held the water skin and poured, a little at a time, while Silas wet the cloth. When they had finished, Silas put the skin back in the sack and rested his hand on his knee.

Alaric pulled his boot on before he was ready. He would rather be uncomfortable than have Silas say they could go and find him still sitting there with it off.

At last, the noise in the yard moved away from the wall. The wheels and voices were coming from one place now, further in. Silas went to the door again, eased the loose end of the split board aside and reached through with his good hand. A wooden bar scraped out of its bracket. He lowered it inside, pushed the door inwards a finger's width and listened before beckoning Alaric over.

Alaric stood up with the wrapped sword and Wena's lead. Silas was trying to get the sack out of his way without letting the door move.

"Give it here," Alaric whispered.

Silas let him take it. "Straight in. There's a shed opposite. Behind the barrels."

The opening was only a few steps deep, through the thickness of the wall. Alaric went sideways, carrying the sack against his stomach and the bundle upright. Wena followed at his heel. His boot caught on the raised threshold, and he lifted it clear without putting his weight on the injured foot.

They came out into a yard crowded with goods. To their left, a cart stood beneath a lantern in a wide entrance to a lane. Two men were loading it. Opposite, a packing shed opened onto the yard, with barrels just inside its folded-back doors. A narrow passage ran beside them to another door at the far end. Alaric could see light beneath it and hear a voice outside calling a price.

He crouched behind the barrels. Silas pushed the wall door shut, joined him and took the sack back over his shoulder.

"The cart entrance if it's clear," he murmured. "Through the shed if it isn't."

A loader took the lantern off its hook. Alaric kept a hand on Wena's collar, watching the light rise above the barrels. Then footsteps came through the cart entrance, and the lantern stopped moving.

"They're fucking elves. They shouldn't be giving us orders in our own town."

A guard stood just inside the entrance. The light caught the buckle at his shoulder as he leaned against the wall. Another followed him in, letting a loader drag an empty sack past before he answered.

"You gonna tell them that, then? Be my guest. I'd prefer to still have a home to go to."

The first man spat.

"I'm telling *you*. They say Gerolt put a few of the bastards down. Good for him."

"Keep your bloody voice down. We've still got the gate round to do."

"We should've been relieved an hour ago."

"I know. And now we've got the inn as well."

"What, Marta's?"

"The farmer's niece. They want her questioned after she closes."

Silas was watching the guards through the space between two barrels.

"What the fuck's she done?"

"How should I know? Finish the round, then go there. That's what I was told."

The first guard shifted. His buckle struck the wall, and he swore under his breath.

"And we're still looking for this boy?"

"Boy and some mutt. Big one, apparently. Bring the boy in alive."

"Be nice if they told us what he looks like."

"Ask them yourself. Come on. Best get moving before they take it out on us."

Their boots went back out through the cart entrance. Silas's hand was on Alaric's sleeve, and it stayed there until the voices had gone.

*A boy and a big dog. That's all they've got.* Wena had her head on her paws beside his boot. *And it's enough.*

*Good for him,* the loud one had said. Someone in this town had heard what Gerolt did and been glad of it. Alaric held on to that, because it was the only thing they had said that he wanted to keep.

Then he looked at the sword bundle.

*His niece.*

The loaders had stopped to listen too. One watched the entrance, then gathered the empty sacks and went out after the guards. The other put the lantern back on the cart and started securing its load.

Silas pointed along the passage inside the shed. Alaric got Wena up without speaking and followed him between the barrels. At the far door, Silas listened, then lifted the latch.

It opened into a covered walkway along the side of the building. A woman carrying a stack of wooden trays was coming towards them. Silas stepped back, and Alaric moved out of her way.

"Hold that, will you?" she said, looking at the open door rather than either of them.

Alaric kept it open while she turned sideways to get the trays through. She smelled of hot bread. Her sleeve brushed his hand.

"Thanks, lad."

Then she was in the shed, putting the trays down and calling to someone further inside. Alaric stood by the door with his hand still raised. She hadn't asked him anything else.

Silas drew him away before she could turn back. "This way."

Alaric followed him into the street.

Lamps hung on poles along both sides of it, and under them there were stalls still trading: a man weighing nails on a scale, a woman with cloth over her arm arguing about a price, a boy turning meat over hot coals so that the smoke went into everybody's faces. A cart stood half unloaded by one of the stalls, with sacks still piled on it. People went past without looking. Coins changed hands everywhere, and every person Alaric could see had somewhere to be.

At the far end of the street was the gate. There were more torches there than anywhere else, and a crowd held back by guards from something Alaric couldn't see.

"—came down right by the gatehouse. Elves, I'm telling you, three of them—"

"—it's the dead ones out past the river they're here about—"

"—they'll be putting soldiers in our houses next, you watch—"

Silas stopped in the shadow of a doorway and held out his good hand.

"Give her here."

Alaric's hand closed on the strip of cloth.

"They want a boy with a big dog. So we don't give them one." Silas nodded towards the street. "You go a few paces ahead. Don't go round a corner without me."

"She'll try to come after me."

"Then don't go running off. Stay where I can see you."

Wena pressed against Alaric's leg when he gave Silas the lead. He bent and touched her ear, then straightened.

"Go with him, girl. I'll be right here."

She watched his first steps away. Silas shortened the cloth and brought her to his knee.

Alaric walked behind a man carrying two baskets. The cobbles were worse than the ruts, and the bundle kept knocking against his leg. He held it higher, kept the front of the cloak shut and tried to move with the people round him.

Ahead, a driver was backing another cart into the gap beside the half-unloaded one. People had to step round its shafts. Alaric waited behind the baskets, then glanced back to make sure Silas could still see him.

Two guards were coming up from the gate with a lantern. Silas shortened Wena's lead again. She saw Alaric turn and tried to come to him.

He nearly said her name.

Instead, he stopped beside the nearest stall. The man with the baskets had put one down and was asking the woman about her cloth. Alaric turned towards it too, leaving the man between himself and Wena. If he kept walking, she would keep pulling.

Silas moved with her along the side of the other cart. Its sacks hid them from the patrol. Then Wena tried to get round its rear wheel and whined, high enough that Alaric heard her over the voices in the street.

The lantern turned.

*She can still see me.*

Alaric stepped behind the half-unloaded cart. He kept close to its load, with the hood pulled forward and the bundle against his chest. Wena's whine went on a moment longer, then stopped.

Through a gap between two sacks, he saw the guard look at the dog and then at Silas.

"That yours?"

"She's after the meat," Silas said.

The guard looked at his face, then towards the boy at the coals. Wena had put her nose down. Silas let the cloth slacken a little, and she sniffed along the edge of the cart.

The guard turned to the crowd and went over it slowly, one face after another. His eyes passed across the sacks. Alaric stayed still, with his boot braced against the wheel so that his ankle wouldn't give.

"Keep her close, then."

The guard moved on, and the other went with him. Alaric waited until their lantern was well past the next stall before coming out. Silas hadn't gone far. He was standing where Alaric could find him, with Wena beside his knee.

Alaric made his legs carry him past them. He didn't look at the dog, though he could hear her claws behind him.

Ahead, the street widened beside a square. People were gathered round a low wooden platform, and a man was calling numbers. Silas took Wena along the edge of it. Alaric had to wait while two men carried a barrel across in front of him, and while he waited, the crowd shifted enough for him to see the boards.

The young woman on the platform was naked.

Alaric looked away, then looked back. There were scars across her shoulders and arms. She was trying to cover herself while the man beside her lifted the chain fastened round her neck.

Someone called a number. A voice on the other side of the crowd called a higher one.

The man pulled the chain to turn her towards them. She stumbled, and both hands went to her throat. When he stopped pulling, she tried to cover herself again.

Her foot found a gap between the boards. She lifted it out carefully, looking down until she could stand with both feet together.

Alaric touched the fastening at his throat. The cloak reached almost to his boots.

Two guards stood at the corner of the platform. One was talking to a man beside him. The other had watched the woman stumble and was watching the bidding now.

Alaric's hand stayed at the fastening. The auctioneer called the amount again, impatiently, and the woman moved her arm higher across herself.

Alaric found Silas a few paces away, holding Wena close.

"Th-that girl… what happened to her? Why are they giving him coins for her?"

Silas followed his look. "They're bidding for her. Whoever pays the most gets to drag her off."

"Takes her *where*?"

"Wherever he bloody wants. She doesn't get a choice."

Alaric turned back towards the platform. A man near the front was counting coins into his palm.

"But she doesn't want to go. Look at her. Why are they letting him do that?"

"Because she's 『Faint』. Only 『Faint』 can be sold, and those guards know it as well as he does."

"Gerolt said they took children away. I thought…" He couldn't finish. "And the scars? Did that man do that to her?"

Silas looked at the man holding the chain, then at the woman.

"I can't tell you who made those scars." He moved closer so they could speak without raising their voices. "But look how he's using that chain. He doesn't give a shit who watches, because nobody here's going to stop him."

Wena pressed against Alaric's knee. He put his hand on her head, still watching as the man counting the coins came forward.

The auctioneer lowered the chain into the man's open hand. The buyer counted the coins into the auctioneer's palm, and the woman had to get down from the platform while they talked. She sat on its edge to do it. The chain tightened before she had both feet on the ground. She reached up and closed her hand round it, just under her chin. When he pulled again, it pulled her hand.

Alaric took a step.

Silas was beside him. He hadn't caught hold of Alaric or told him to stop. Alaric looked at the guards, then at Wena, then back at the woman.

The buyer was going towards the gate. She followed, keeping close enough that the chain hung loose for a few steps at a time.

*I'm going to walk away.*

"Which way?" Alaric asked. His voice sounded wrong to him.

Silas nodded down the street. "Past the next corner."

Alaric went with him.

A man outside a stall called a price, and Alaric turned before he could stop himself. The man was holding up a pot. Behind him, someone laughed and offered less.

Alaric faced forward. He found himself holding the cloak shut again, and let his hand fall.

The inn stood on the corner nearest the gate. There was light in every window, upstairs and down. The front door kept opening, and every time it did, the noise came out with it: voices, laughing, somebody singing badly and somebody else telling him to stop. The smell of roasting meat came out too, and new bread, and Alaric was hungry all over again. People sat on the bench outside with their cups. A board hung over the door, too high in the shadow to read.

*Marta's in there. Somewhere in all that.*

Silas didn't slow down. He took them past the front and round the corner, into a lane that ran along the side of the inn to its back door. Across from it was a narrow space between a closed storehouse and its yard wall. From there, they could see the back door beyond a stack of empty barrels and the light from a window beside it. Through the mouth of the lane, Alaric could see customers leaving the front step.

"Sit. Keep out of that window's light."

Alaric lowered himself onto a stone at the base of the wall, with the bundle across his knees. Silas passed him Wena's lead, and she was against his legs before he had taken it.

"I'm checking the lane past the back door. You'll see me."

Alaric watched him walk to the corner. Silas stood there a while, looking both ways, then came back.

"Nobody. Not yet."

He sat where he could see the door.

"The guards said his niece," Alaric said.

"His brother's daughter."

Alaric looked down at the sword wrapped across his knees.

"How am I meant to tell her, Silas? I thought I'd show her this, say he sent me, and she'd…" He shook his head. "He was feeding me. He was sitting there, worrying about whether I'd eaten enough, and then those riders came. How do I tell her he's dead?"

Silas watched the inn for a moment before answering.

"What did he tell you to say?"

"That the old fool sent me."

"Then start there. Show her the sword and say what he told you. When she asks what happened, tell her straight."

"And the guards? If they find me in there, they're going to hurt her too, aren't they?"

"They could." Silas looked at him. "So she hears about them from you, before those bastards reach her door. Marta won't thank us for keeping her in the dark."

After that, they waited.

The singing stopped. Customers came out in a group, stood on the step saying goodnight and stayed there talking. Alaric listened for the loud voice and the tired one from the yard. He heard boots once and a shout, somewhere out towards the gate, but they didn't come any nearer.

*After the round, they said. How long does a round take? They were complaining about it, so it must be a long one. Unless that was only them complaining.*

A woman came through the back door carrying a tub. Alaric was halfway off the stone before Silas's forearm stopped him.

"Not her."

The woman emptied the tub into the gutter and went back inside. Wena stood at the end of her lead with her ears up, watching her go.

Alaric shut his eyes. He saw the chain again, pulling tight before the woman from the platform had both feet down.

*Only 『Faint』 can be sold.* Gerolt hadn't known what they would do with someone who had none. Alaric pulled the cloak closer round himself. He didn't want to imagine what else they could do.

Across the lane, the back door shut. Someone at the front called a last goodnight, and the group on the step finally moved away. Silas watched them until they were past the storehouse.

Alaric said the words over to himself without making any sound. *The old fool sent me.* He would say that first. Then he would show her the sword. Then…

He couldn't get any further than that.

The ground-floor windows went out, all but one at the back beside the door. Something scraped across the floor inside, chairs maybe, and a woman's voice said something short to somebody.

"Now," Silas said.

Alaric's fingers were clumsy with the knot, and he had to pick it apart with his nails before he could unroll the blanket. When he lifted the sword free, the steel caught the light from the window. He turned it away and lowered the point.

He rested the sword across his knees while Silas rolled the bed again, keeping his burned hand clear. Alaric tied the cord round it for him. Silas tucked it beneath his arm and took Wena's lead with his good hand.

The front door closed. A bar dropped behind it.

Alaric stood up and waited until his leg would straighten. Then they crossed the lane together. Halfway across, Wena's head came up. She went ahead of them and put her nose to the gap beneath the back door, and her tail began to move.

*She knows this door.*

Silas stopped beside it, but made no move to knock.

"What's wrong?" Alaric asked.

"Go on. You knock."

"Why? I thought you knew her."

"She won't open for me, boy."

Alaric turned towards him. "What do you mean, she won't open for you?"

"She told me never to show my face again. Not here. Not anywhere near Kelmend."

"And you're telling me *now*? After bringing me all the way here?"

Silas kept looking at the door. Alaric waited for him to turn round and answer. Inside, the scraping started again; Silas glanced at the handle instead.

"I put it off. You've got Gerolt's message. I reckoned she'd listen to him, even if she couldn't stand the sight of me."

Alaric looked down at the sword.

*Was that why he'd wanted to take it himself?*

"So what are you going to do? Wait out here?"

"Where do you think I'm going? I'm staying with you." Silas shifted closer, where the person opening the door would see him. "Go on, Alaric. Knock."

Alaric lifted his free hand and knocked.

Inside, the scraping stopped. Footsteps came towards the door, quick and impatient, and a bolt went back.

The door opened a little. Wena put her nose into the gap, and the woman inside looked down.

"Wena? What are you doing here?"

She opened it further and bent to touch her. Wena pressed her head into the woman's hand. Alaric drew breath, ready with the words he had been trying to get right.

Then she looked up and saw Silas.

"Marta," he said. "Wait—"

"Get the fuck out of Kelmend, Silas. Right now." She straightened, her hand falling away from Wena. "Did you think I didn't mean it?"

"Marta, hear him out. He's—"

"Go!"

Silas drew Wena back as Marta pulled the door shut.

It slammed before Alaric could say Gerolt's name.
