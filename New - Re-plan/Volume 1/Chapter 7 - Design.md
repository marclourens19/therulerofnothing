# Chapter 7: Design

Started 30 September 2026. **Status:** written on 30 September as `Chapter 7 - Untitled.md` (about 3,850 words; the first draft is saved in `Drafts/`). A pass for errors, inconsistencies and POV slips followed the same day (21 changes, in `Chapter 7 - Changes.md`; now 3,829 words). Waiting on the author's review, one Your-call from the pass and a question about light in the aqueduct; the title is to come.

**The target:** 4,000–5,000 words, and longer if the story needs it. Claude will give a range, not a single number, because Chapters 5 and 6 both came in about a quarter under Claude's budgets.

## Where the story stands

- **Chapter 5** ends at first light on Day 2, in the room she doesn't know.
  - The blows under the palace have stopped (*They've stopped. Whatever they were doing, it's done.*).
  - Footsteps come, then two knocks, and the door stays shut. "Thaer?" The lock turns.
  - Guards are posted outside her door with a key, and they came in with swords drawn when she shouted.
  - Nereth stood between her and them, then bound her cut hand.
  - Seralune has been up all night, barefoot, in the dress she woke in.
- **What she knows:**
  - Many years have passed: Leorin is old, the tree is huge, the palace has changed, and nobody knows Elowen.
  - The crystal was made for her. Through the door she heard "the seal" and Leorin wanting it done "tonight".
  - Thaer said "If it were up to me, you'd—" and "It isn't up to me." Leorin speaks for the king while he's away.
  - The king's warship left last night. She wondered if her father was on it.
  - Her hopes: *Father will listen to me. He has to.* and *Mother would never let them do this to me.*
- **What she doesn't know:**
  - the number (a thousand years);
  - who sealed her, and why;
  - where her mother is;
  - who Cyrandor is.
- **Chapter 6** has the same ship over Kelmend at dusk on Day 2, searching for the farm. So Chapter 7 runs on the morning of Day 2, while Alaric is still asleep in the cave.

## Fixed for this chapter

- **Seralune's eyes only.** Her thoughts are Alisaie's: sure of what she knows, reasoning from it, then turning to the next practical step. A thought reasons; it doesn't just ask.
- **Cyrandor is behind the door.** He bows and smiles a little. She has never seen him, and he's the first person who has smiled at her. His knock is different from Thaer's, but she hears it as Thaer's because she's waiting for him. The narration mustn't claim it's the same knock.
- **He's the first to answer her:** the number, and her mother.
  - Her mother opposed the sealing, because she believed Seralune's mana could be controlled.
  - She ran away because everyone else agreed.
  - She always believed her daughter would wake.
  - She set trustworthy servants to report to her, and the duty passed down by word through the Order.
- **Cyrandor succeeds because he gives her information and a choice** (handoff).
- **Nereth:**
  - Cyrandor, her mentor and almost a father, secretly told her to watch over Seralune.
  - **Her first slip, from Ram to Revy, comes in the escape.**
  - **She must choose to come.** Seralune can't order her to flee and have the story call it freedom.
- **Thaer regrets it,** and delays resealing her while he hopes their father will change his mind. He wants her safe in his "chains", and still locks, guards and withholds while believing he's merciful.
- **No wardwrights, and no silver chain.**
- **Where it leads:** she goes into Mydea after her mother's trail, possibly by way of Inrandeel. By the end of Volume 1 the church holds her and Nereth.
- **Her mana acts on its own; she can't use it,** and she never tries to.
- **The old escape** (old Chapter 5, "The Last Keeper"), with what's left undecided:
  - Guards asleep outside.
  - The queen's signet ring, carved with a closed lily, that Seralune once cracked at seven.
  - Cyrandor telling her about the Order and Mydea.
  - Nereth taken by guards, and Seralune stopping for her ("Do you want to come with us?").
  - The old south stair.
  - Thaer alone at the end of a passage. Cyrandor holds a barrier and loses his arm below the elbow; Thaer can't strike his sister.
  - Down into the drainage channels.

## A proposed shape (for round 1 to change)

Five scenes, about 4,500–5,500 words. The numbers point to the questions below.

1. **The door** (about 700 words). "Thaer?" The lock turns, and it's an old man she has never seen, carrying something. He bows and smiles. Behind him, the guards (question 1). Her first reaction is Alisaie's: who are you, and why should she believe a word of it?
2. **What he tells her** (about 1,300). It's done in pieces, as she asks, with her testing each answer against what she knows. It is not a speech.
   - The proof (question 3).
   - A thousand years.
   - Her mother refused, and left.
   - The crystal is mended, and they mean to put her back in it this morning (question 11).
   - What it costs her: *Father will listen to me* breaks (question 2).
3. **The choice** (about 800).
   - Cyrandor offers her a way out and a place to start (her mother's trail), and doesn't make her take it.
   - She chooses.
   - Then Nereth. Seralune starts to give an order, and Nereth asks, "Is that an order, Your Highness?" (question 5).
4. **Out through the palace** (about 1,200).
   - The servants' ways that Nereth knows and Cyrandor doesn't.
   - A palace she no longer recognises.
   - The alarm when her room is found empty.
   - A close call (question 8).
5. **The stair** (about 1,000).
   - Thaer at the end of the passage.
   - She won't go back.
   - The cost, and Nereth's slip (questions 6 and 7).
   - Down into the water, and out (question 9).

## Round 1 (asked 30 September)

1. **How does Cyrandor get to her door past the guards?**
   - a. **They're asleep.** Something in their drink. Seralune checks that one is still breathing, which is her kindness. *Recommended:* it's simple, it's what the old version did, and it makes the Order capable.
   - b. He comes as himself, a trusted old servant with her breakfast, and the guard lets him in. Harder to get out.
   - c. One of the guards is the Order's.
2. **What does she learn about her family?** Canon says her mother ran "because everyone else agreed". Said plainly, that tells her that her father, and perhaps Thaer, agreed to seal her.
   - *Recommended:* Cyrandor says "everyone else" and doesn't name names, and Seralune works it out herself. *Father will listen to me* from Chapter 5 breaks here, and her mother becomes the one person who refused.
   - **Does she learn that Thaer sealed her?** *Recommended:* no, not in Volume 1's early chapters. Cyrandor doesn't know which hands did it, only that the court agreed. It keeps Thaer's regret from being explained away.
3. **What proves it's true?** The handoff says he should offer "something verifiable".
   - *Recommended:* something only she and her mother would know. The old version's signet ring (a closed lily, cracked at seven on a fountain's edge) did exactly this. *Your call:* keep the ring, or another object or words?
4. **Is her mother alive?**
   - *Recommended:* Cyrandor doesn't know. The last word the Order had placed her in Mydea, a long time ago, and then nothing. That gives Seralune a trail and no promise.
5. **Nereth's choice.** Seralune's flaw is taking control for other people's good.
   - *Recommended:* Seralune says "Nereth, come with us" as an order, without thinking. Nereth doesn't move: "Is that an order, Your Highness?" Seralune hears it, and asks properly.
   - Nereth's reason is her own: **she has wanted to see the world since she was a child** (canon). It can come out plainly or not at all.
   - This seeds Seralune's Volume 2 lesson without teaching it yet.
6. **Does Thaer confront her?** The outline left the old version's stair to round 2.
   - a. **Yes, at the stair, alone.** He tells her the one true thing he can, she chooses to go anyway, and he can't bring himself to strike her. *Recommended:* it's the chapter's big choice, made against someone she loves.
   - b. No. The guards and Leorin chase them, and Thaer arrives too late.
   - c. He lets her go.
7. **The cost.** The handoff says she "escapes, incurs real cost".
   - a. **Cyrandor stays behind to hold the way,** and his fate stays open. *Recommended.*
   - b. As the old version: Thaer takes his arm.
   - c. He dies.
   - Whichever you choose, **Nereth's slip** comes here. Her mentor pays, and the maid's voice cracks into something harder. Do you have a line for it?
8. **The close call in the palace.**
   - *Recommended:* guards have Nereth, as in the old version, and Seralune stops for her instead of running. That's Seralune taking control for someone else's good, her flaw, and this time it's right.
   - *Alternative:* they're nearly seen crossing a hall.
9. **Where does it end?**
   - a. **Outside:** at the foot of the falls, in the forest under the palace, with the bells above and Nereth beside her by choice. She turns towards Mydea. *Recommended:* something that can't go back.
   - b. In the channels under the palace, with the cost still fresh, as in the old version.
10. **Cyrandor's own reason.** He shouldn't be only a messenger.
    - *Recommended:* he has kept the queen's word his whole life for a woman he has never met, and today he spends it, and he sends away the girl he raised (Nereth). *Your call:* does he say why he believes in it, or only do it?
11. **Why this morning?**
    - *Recommended:* the blows under the floor were the crystal being mended. It was finished at first light, and they'll come for her once the court is up. That's why Cyrandor comes now, and it's cause and effect from Chapter 5's last lines.
    - This settles the open question Z, **did Thaer allow the repair?** The handoff's version: he refused to reseal her straight away, but let Leorin mend it "in case". *Recommended*, because it's Thaer's mercy with the lock still on it.
12. **Cyrandor's Affinity.** The old barrier was silver and spread under his skin. If he holds the stair, what does he hold it with?
    - *Your call.* Nothing is decided about him beyond his role.
13. **Her feet and her dress.** She's barefoot, in the dress she woke in, with a cut hand.
    - *Recommended:* Cyrandor brings plain clothes and shoes, a servant's grey, which is the "something he's carrying" in scene 1. It's also how they cross the palace unnoticed.

## What could make this chapter 100/100?

**The strongest opportunities:**
1. **The answers should cost her something.** Each true thing Cyrandor says should break a belief from Chapter 5:
   - "Father will listen to me";
   - "Mother would never let them";
   - "Thaer will explain in the morning".

   Her mother is proved right and gone. Her father, and everyone else, agreed. The information is the wound.
2. **Thaer at the stair can't be a villain scene.** He believes he's saving her. He should say one thing that's true and loving, and still ask her to go back into the room. She chooses against someone she loves, who loves her. That's what makes it cost her.
3. **Nereth's choice has to be hers.** "Is that an order?" lets the reader see Seralune's flaw and her better self in one exchange, and Nereth comes for a reason of her own.
4. **Cyrandor has to be a person.** He has a lifetime of keeping a promise to a queen he never met, and he's sending the girl he raised away with her daughter. If that shows in one small act, his cost lands.
5. **Keep it in her senses.** She was a princess here a thousand years ago. Every corridor that isn't where she remembers it, and every servant who bows, is a small loss she notices and reasons about (Alisaie), without stopping the escape.

**Honest risks:**
- **Scene 2 can turn into an information dump.** It has to be a conversation she drives, with her testing each answer. Cyrandor answers only what she asks and admits what he doesn't know.
- **Escapes turn into chase montages.** Two or three real obstacles, each solved by a person (Nereth's knowledge, Seralune's stubbornness, Cyrandor's cost), are better than many corridors.
- **The cost can read as punishment.** Whatever Cyrandor loses, he chooses it.
- **Copying the old version.** The arm, the barrier and Thaer at the passage end are strong, but they belong to a Thaer who was written differently. Keep only what the new Thaer would do.

## The author's review (30 September)

The author answered round 1 with a detailed design and lore review, kept word for word in `Chapter 7 - Design Review.md`. It keeps the five-part shape and expands it to 18 beats, about 5,000 words (review §9). It also names **three causal problems to solve before drafting**:
1. Who has ordered the resealing this morning, and what has Thaer actually authorised?
2. What makes the mother's trail usable? "Mydea, long ago" isn't a lead.
3. Why does Thaer fail to bring her back, when Dark can cut matter, magic and connections, and "distance and cover do not necessarily protect a selected target"?

### Where Claude agrees

- **All three problems are real,** and more dialogue or a bigger sacrifice wouldn't solve them.
- **Claude's scene 1 was wrong.** Chapter 5 already has the old servant who bows and smiles in the corridor ("*I've never seen him before in my life, and he's the first person who's smiled at me.*"). So the door opens on recognition, not a stranger. The same mistake as Chapter 6: the chapter wasn't searched before claiming.
- **Not every answer should break a hope.** Her mother's refusal gives her something to hold. Going back for Nereth is compassion, and it's right.
- **Keep the three questions about the sealing separate:** who agreed a thousand years ago, who allowed the repair, and who means to use it now.
- **Hand her the ring and let her recognise it.** Cyrandor doesn't recite the childhood story.
- **Thaer, version A:** he can stop her, and chooses not to finish it. It needs a real threshold, because he already took her by the arm in Chapter 5.

### Where Claude challenges it

- **The sample Thaer line has a continuity problem.** "I told you I wouldn't let him touch you." Thaer never told her. He said "No one touches my sister" to Leorin through the door, and she overheard. She then decided to keep it to herself: *Very well. I won't say a word about what I heard.*
- **That gives version A its threshold.** At the stair, Seralune spends the secret: "I heard you last night. 'No one touches my sister.'" To take her back now, he has to be the one who puts his hands on her and walks her to the crystal. He wouldn't let Leorin do it, and he can't make himself do it either. It's his own words, not a new motive, and it pays off her Chapter 5 decision. *Proposed.*
- **The guards.** The review's option b (a legitimate duty) solves more than one problem at once (see round 2, B).

## Round 2 (asked 30 September)

The review's four structural decisions first, then the rest. Claude's pick is marked; the author chooses.

- **A. Who is resealing her this morning?** *Recommended:* Leorin, before Thaer comes back. Thaer said "I'll be back in the morning". He allowed the repair "in case" but never agreed to use it, and Leorin moves at first light because Thaer isn't there. It keeps Chapter 5's disagreement, and it gives the chapter a race.
- **B. What Cyrandor knows, how he gets in, and why not last night.** *Recommended:* one order does all three. At first light, as the repair finished, the household was told to dress the princess and bring her down to the lower chamber at the first bell, and the task came to Cyrandor.
  - So he knows because he was told.
  - The guards unlock for him because he's expected. The two knocks are his, and the lock turning is the guard.
  - He couldn't come last night because the order didn't exist yet.
  - **How they leave:** 1. the guards wait outside to escort her, so they go out through a servants' door in the panelling that Nereth knows *(recommended)*; or 2. he drugged the guards' drink.
  - **The alarm:** they don't arrive at the lower chamber by the bell.
- **C. The lead.** *Recommended:* the queen's last message came back to the Order from **Inrandeel**, the forest of the independent elves that Seralune remembers. It came a few centuries ago, and then there was nothing. It's a real place she can go, and her memory of the elves there is her own reason to trust it (it may be wrong). *The author picks how long ago, and whether it's a message, a record or a person.*
- **D. Why Thaer lets her go.** *Recommended:* version A with the threshold above: "No one touches my sister." He asks her to come back upstairs. She asks him to come with her instead. Neither can. He doesn't follow, and doesn't order it in front of her.
- **E. Where Thaer is, and how he finds them.** *Recommended:* he was on his way to her room, as he promised. When the alarm goes, he goes where he knows the old ways lead: the one stair down from the old south wing, which is still the palace he grew up in. He knows the palace, and he knows her.
- **F. The cost.** *Recommended:* Cyrandor stays behind to stop the guards coming down after them, not to stop Thaer. His fate stays unknown. It follows from Thaer's choice, and it's Cyrandor's own.
- **G. Nereth's slip.** *Recommended:* it comes when she realises Cyrandor isn't following. It's one break, then back to getting them out. The line comes in the dialogue round.
- **H. Cyrandor and Nereth.** Canon says mentor, "almost a father", and that he taught her how to work in the castle. *Recommended:* he has been her mentor since she came into service as a child. Did he teach her to read, as in the old version? *Your call.*
- **I. Nereth's reason.** *Recommended:* the world past the falls, which she has wanted to see since she was a girl (canon), weighed against leaving him. She may say it plainly or not at all. Seralune accepts that staying is a real option.
- **J. The ring.** *Recommended:* one mark only, a nick in the stone's edge from the fountain when she was seven. She recognises it herself.
- **K. Elowen.** *Recommended:* yes, one question. Cyrandor has never heard the name. It's the moment "a thousand years" stops being a number.
- **L. The ending.** *Recommended:* in the forest below the falls, with the bells above them, and a next step that's practical, not symbolic (the city's edge, before the roads are closed). Mydea is where they're going, not where they are.
- **M. What Seralune learns about the family.** *Recommended:* her father agreed, and she defends him before she accepts it (the review's line). About Thaer, Cyrandor says what he knows, that he doesn't know who carried it out. If she asks and he doesn't know, that's honest, not a dodge.
