## The verdict

It's a good revision. Of the 49 changes, 41 make the chapters plainer, clearer about who's doing what, or more honest to the viewpoint, and they don't need anything from me. Two of them fix real problems:

- **Chapter 3, the sword.** Before, Alaric dropped Gerolt's sword at the river, and on the far bank Silas handed it back without anyone ever picking it up. Now Silas comes splashing back and snatches it up with a curse.
- **Chapter 6, the ogre.** Before, Silas named the ogre from a deer carcass, so Alaric walked into a danger he'd been told about by name. Now there are only broken trees and an honest "Can't tell", the creature has no name until after the fight, and Silas's surprise ("this far from the mountains") explains why he couldn't tell. The fallen tree that "sat up" now echoes the broken ones. In the fight, "the creature" is right, because Alaric doesn't have the word yet.

The best of the rest:

- **Gerolt answers the boy** (Chapter 1). "I don't know what they'd do with you" answers "What about someone with none?", which used to go unanswered.
- **"You'll need it."** (Chapter 2). Alaric refuses the sword out loud before Gerolt forces it on him.
- **Silas keeps his promise** (Chapter 6). "I don't like those tracks. I'll have a look upstream for a better crossing." He tells Alaric what he's checking, a few pages after Alaric made that the condition.
- **Seralune reasons out loud** (Chapter 5). "Leorin's an old man. You look exactly as you did this morning." She puts the evidence to Thaer and still doesn't say "years".
- **Her first plan** (Chapter 4). *Very well. If I'm alive, I can get up.* The old line repeated "freezing" from the thought before it.
- **The pronoun fixes** (Chapter 3). Four "He"s that could have been the rider, the elf or Alaric are now "the man".

Gerolt loses two jokes in Chapter 1, and I agree with both cuts. A joke about one syllable, aimed at a boy who can barely talk, was too sharp, and his humour is still there in "I'm too old to catch you twice in one day", in his back, and in "Calling louder is the whole of my strategy".

## Questions

Nine, by number. Each has my recommendation. Questions 1–7 are worth your time; 8 and 9 are small.

### 1. Chapter 1: Alaric's only joke

Your version of his outburst is simpler and more desperate, and I'd keep its shape. But it takes out "a headache that's at least probably my own". That was his only joke in the chapter, and it's the line the skill quotes to show that his humour survives the amnesia (principle 1). Without it, Chapter 1's Alaric has no humour at all.

*Recommended:* your version, with the headache back in place of "my head hurts so much I can barely think":

> "Then why can't I feel it? I can feel your fire, and the wind coming through your shutters, and a headache that's at least probably my own. I'm trying, Gerolt. There's nothing else."

"Your fire" sets up the joke the way "a fire that isn't mine" used to.

### 2. Chapter 1: Gerolt says the same thing twice

> "Long enough. And in all those years, I never saw men fight beside beastfolk."
>
> …
>
> "A human and a beastman, shoulder to shoulder." Gerolt was quiet for a moment. "I've never seen that, lad. I know what I saw, and I still can't make it fit."

"I've never seen that" repeats what he said a few lines earlier, and "shoulder to shoulder" repeats what the boy has just seen (his shoulder against the soldier's). The style check also flags the two shoulders and "for a moment". "I know what I saw, and I still can't make it fit" is the new thing, so keep that.

*Recommended:*

> "I know what I saw, lad, and I still can't make it fit."

*Alternative:* the old line, which ended on a question for the reader: "So either I've been wrong about how the world works for sixty years, or something out there made it true just long enough to kill every one of them."

### 3. Chapter 2: "his knees would not unlock"

> He had the sword and wanted to help. Alaric tried to take a step towards Gerolt, but his knees would not unlock.

On 29 September you called almost this exact line robotic: "He had the sword and wanted to help. He tried to go down to Gerolt, but his knees would not unlock." Two short sentences side by side read like a list, and "his knees would not unlock" reads like a report. You replaced it with "He tried to go back down to Gerolt, but his legs just wouldn't move." A few paragraphs later, "His knees went." repeats the knees.

*Recommended:* your 29 September line, keeping the sword you've added:

> He tried to go back down to Gerolt with the sword, but his legs just wouldn't move.

### 4. Chapter 4: what she remembers

> *Mother was in my room this morning. Thaer promised he'd be back before evening. I remember him leaving. Why can't I remember anything after that?*

These are four short sentences, three of them facts in a row, and the thought ends on a bare question. Her thoughts work best when they reason. The old one did: "I must have gone somewhere." The paragraphs after it go over the same memories in detail anyway (the sun on the floor, the fight, the two knocks), so this thought doesn't need to list them.

*Recommended:*

> *Mother was in my room this morning, and Thaer promised he'd be back before evening. I remember him leaving. I must have gone somewhere after that, so why can't I remember it?*

### 5. Chapter 6: Alaric forgets the broken trees

With the deer gone, his thought "And that ogre's eaten already. Silas said it'd be lying up somewhere." went too. That was where he weighed the danger and played it down. Your own rule from Chapter 6 was that he understands the danger and underestimates it, rather than not having it. Now, when he decides to go, he doesn't think about the broken trees at all, though he saw them that morning.

*Recommended (Your call, a new line):* one thought in his decision, so he chooses with the danger in mind:

> *I can keep off the road. The town's beyond that rise—he pointed it out himself. Whatever broke those trees was hours back. I'll find the south gate, and then the inn.*

It's true as far as he knows (the trees were before midday), it's wrong, and the fallen tree that sits up pays it off.

### 6. Chapter 6: "What the hell's one doing…"

This is the only "hell" in seven chapters. The world's oaths are its own ("By the Four", "the Last Dark"), and "hell" belongs to ours. Silas swears plenty, so the swearing isn't the problem, only the word.

*Recommended:* "What in the Last Dark is one doing this far from the mountains?" It hasn't been used yet in Chapters 1–7.

Your line also gives the world a new fact: **ogres belong in the mountains.** I've recorded it in Decisions.

### 7. Chapter 6: three "I wasn't"s in a row

> "I wasn't trying to make things harder. I was trying to get to her."
>
> "I wasn't about to lead those bastards to Marta's door."
>
> "Then why couldn't you tell me what you were doing? …"
>
> "I wasn't going to leave you sitting there all night."

Silas naming Marta, where Alaric only said "her", is good. But it's three "I wasn't"s in four lines, and Silas's two sound like the same answer twice.

*Recommended:* "And I'm not about to lead those bastards to Marta's door." It's still his plan, so the present tense fits.

Your other new "the man"s are needed: four fix a "he" that could mean someone else, and one starts the new sentence at the river. This one isn't needed, because only he can be letting go. The chapter is at 15 now.
