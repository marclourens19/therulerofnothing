# Chapter 9: Design

Started 1 October 2026. **Status:** round 1, waiting on the author. Nothing here is decided until it's answered. New material is marked **Your call**.

**The target:** 4,000–5,000 words, and longer if the story needs it. Claude's estimate for the shape below is **4,500–5,500**. Per-scene budgets have run about a quarter high on every chapter so far, so read it as a range.

## Where it starts

- **Chapter 8 ends late on the night of Day 2,** at Marta's back door. Wena knew her. Marta looked up, saw Silas, and told him to get out of Kelmend. "It slammed before Alaric could say Gerolt's name."
- **The state they're in:**
  - **Alaric:** he has Gerolt's sword in his hand, unwrapped. His ankle is swollen (the ogre, Chapter 6) and he's wearing Silas's cloak over the black clothes he woke in.
  - **Silas:** his palm is burned raw and wrapped. He has the rolled bed and the sack under his arm, and Wena's lead in his good hand.
  - Wena is outside with them, tail still going, nose at the door.
- **What's coming:** two guards are finishing a round of the gate, and then they'll come to question "the farmer's niece" after she closes. She has just closed. Alaric knows their voices: the loud one who said "Good for him" about Gerolt, and the tired one who's afraid of the elves.
- **What Alaric knows:**
  - Marta is Gerolt's niece, his brother's daughter (Silas, Chapter 8).
  - She told Silas never to show his face again, anywhere near Kelmend.
  - She doesn't know Gerolt is dead. He's carrying the first news.
  - Gerolt's message: "Tell her the old fool sent you." Silas: "Then start there. Show her the sword and say what he told you. When she asks what happened, tell her straight."
  - The search wants "a boy and some mutt. Big one," brought in alive. They don't know his face.
  - He believes Gerolt died because of him.
- **What he doesn't know:** the gorge, Marta's father, or anything about Silas and Marta beyond her anger. Nobody has told him the riders' bodies were found; he only knows the search is on.

## What's fixed

- **Marta** (Decisions):
  - She's Gerolt's niece, and keeps the inn just inside the south gate. It's always busy, and she closes late.
  - Her father was Gerolt's brother, and he died in Silas's gorge.
  - She told Silas never to show his face again. It's her own rule, not a warrant.
  - Wena knows her, because Gerolt visited often.
  - **Still undecided:** her age, look, Affinity, manner, and her part in Darcy's story.
- **Silas** still loves her. They were going to be married before the gorge. He accepts that she may take the boy in and still refuse him (Chapter 8, round 3).
- **The search:** bring the boy in alive. A neighbour saw Gerolt leave towards the forest with a boy and his dog, so the dog is likely known to be Gerolt's (Chapter 8's working design).
- **"Empty" is Alaric's own word** (Chapter 1). Gerolt said "You're not Faint. You're…", and the boy said "Empty?" Silas has never been told.
- **The double** (Decisions, handoff §23):
  - its first full appearance comes after Marta offers genuine help, "currently in Chapter 9";
  - before it, he needs a believable first sight of his own face, "such as while washing at the inn";
  - it attacks the safety he has just started to believe in, using a true fact and an unjust verdict;
  - only Alaric sees it, and others see his pain;
  - it can't move things or control him;
  - his first response is to hide it, because he's afraid of what another symptom means for the people sheltering him.
- **The outline's job for Marta's chapter:**
  - **Its question:** "What is he, to people who can see it?"
  - **Its turn:** "He accepts shelter that has to be earned."
  - **The change by the end:** "Empty" stops being his private word and becomes a danger.
- **The structural map** (discussion, not canon): the sword earns him a hearing, "not unlimited trust". What he does decides whether Marta keeps helping.
- **Chapter 10 is Seralune's,** and Chapter 11 is the Faint quarter, with Redd and Freya. Both are still working recommendations.

## After Chapter 8's final passes (1 October)

Chapter 8 is finished (5,268 words). Three things in it bear on this design:

- **The woman at the auction** now closes her hand round the chain so the buyer's pull lands on her hand. If Alaric remembers her in this chapter (at the table, or when Marta finds he's Empty), that's the image he has.
- **The guards no longer give the inn's address.** Nothing changes here: they know it.
- **Still open from Chapter 8: Marta's "Where's—".** If she starts asking about Gerolt when she sees Wena and breaks off at the sight of Silas, the slam is partly fear of the news. That's the setup for question 5 ("The last time you stood at my door, you'd come to tell me my father was dead"), and it makes question 1 land harder: Alaric's "The old fool sent me" through the door confirms what she was afraid of. *Recommended:* take both, or neither. Each one works without the other, but together they make one beat that pays off across the two chapters.

## The old version (raw material, not canon)

The old *Refuge* had several good beats:
- Wena runs to Marta first.
- Marta says Silas's name and nothing else.
- She lets Silas in only because a patrol is coming: "And the moment they're gone, so are you."
- She handles the guards in the doorway with her standing in the town.
- She talks about chickens while she cleans his hands, so he'll stop watching.
- "My uncle was right. You really are Empty." Gerolt had said it like a question; Marta says it like a verdict.

**What no longer fits:**
- Its characters are out of date.
- The token is now the sword.
- Gerolt never called him Empty.
- It ran at dawn, and this chapter is late at night.
- It explained the law at length ("protective stewardship").
- It ended on the Darcy mission.

Most of the old Marta (about forty, red-haired, Eminent Wind, sharp-tongued, influence through information and long friendships) is still undecided. Question 6 asks about it.

## A proposed shape (six scenes)

All in Alaric's eyes, from the slammed door to past midnight on Day 2.

1. **The shut door** (600–900 words).
   - Seconds after the slam. Silas stands back from the door and doesn't knock. Wena whines at the gap.
   - Alaric's choice: he knocks again, and says Gerolt's words through the wood. "The old fool sent me." *(Your call. It's the line Silas told him to start with, and it's the only thing that could open that door.)*
   - The bolt goes back. She sees the sword in his hand before he can lift it, and she knows it.
   - "Where is he?" He tells her, straight, in the doorway.
   - He tells her the guards are coming. That's the first thing he can do for her.
   - She lets all three in, Silas too, because a man standing at her back door is worse than a man inside it.
2. **The guards** (1,000–1,400).
   - She hides them. Alaric and Wena are somewhere he can hear the door; Silas is somewhere else.
   - The knock. Alaric knows the voices.
   - **Marta hears it a second time** (Your call). She has to hear her uncle's death from them as if it's new, with the boy who told her ten minutes ago listening behind a door. Her grief is real, and she's acting at the same time.
   - The guards' version adds something Alaric didn't know, or gets something wrong. *For example (Your call):* the official story says Gerolt attacked the riders unprovoked, and Marta can't defend him without giving herself away.
   - Then the question that matters: "Your uncle kept a dog, didn't he? Big one." Wena is in her house.
   - Marta lies for him. That's the genuine help, and it's a crime.
   - The loud one is kind to her about Gerolt; the tired one wants to be done. They go. "Somebody may want to see you tomorrow."
3. **The kitchen** (1,200–1,600).
   - The first quiet since Chapter 6. She sits down for the first time.
   - She sees to his ankle, and asks her questions while her hands work. What happened, and how he came to be at the farm. He tells her briefly, since the reader has seen it.
   - He tells her it was his fault. Her answer is the emotional centre, and it should be yours first (question 4).
   - **Silas's hand** (Your call): she puts the salve on the table and pushes it towards Alaric, not Silas. Alaric dresses it, as he held the cloth in Chapter 6. She won't touch Silas, and she won't let him go untreated either.
   - **The small answer on Silas and Marta** (question 5): one line that tells the reader she has a right to shut the door.
4. **Empty** (600–900).
   - She asks what his Affinity is, because she has to know what she's hiding.
   - "None." Gerolt had him reach for it, and nothing was there. He uses his own word.
   - Silas hears it for the first time.
   - How she confirms it depends on question 6. It isn't pitiful to her; it's dangerous. "No one can know." The cost shows in what she does, not in a speech about the law: she looks at the door the way Gerolt did.
5. **The price, and the room** (500–800).
   - He offers to go, so she isn't in danger because of him. It comes from guilt (one person has already died for him), not from noble posing.
   - She keeps him, on her terms. That's "shelter that has to be earned."
   - A room under the roof. She brings water to wash. He sees his face for the first time: a stranger's face, in plain words, with no meaning laid on it.
6. **The double** (400–700).
   - Someone in the room with his face.
   - At first it's desperate hope: it might be who he was, and it might explain him.
   - Then it turns. It takes the true things (Gerolt took him in and died; Marta has just lied to the guards for him) and draws the unjust verdict.
   - The pain: a headache like nothing before. Wena reacts to him, not to the figure.
   - **The ending:** Marta's voice at the door, "Alaric?", because she heard something. "I'm fine." It's his first concealment, and it mirrors the opening: the chapter began with him talking through a shut door to be let in, and ends with him talking through one to keep someone out. *(Your call.)*

## What Alaric can and can't know

- He can hear the guards but not see them, unless the hiding place lets him.
- He doesn't know what the gorge was, only what he sees pass between Silas and Marta.
- He doesn't know how Affinity is sensed. He only sees what Marta does and what her face does after.
- He has never seen his own face. The narration can't describe it before he does, and when he does, it describes only what he sees.
- He can't know the double is made by the world. To him it's himself, or who he was.

## What could make this chapter 100/100?

1. **The first ten lines have to earn the door opening.** Readers ended Chapter 8 on a slam. If Silas talks her round, or she opens it by herself, Alaric's choice is lost. "The old fool sent me" through the door works only if he says it because he can't think of anything else, not because it's clever.
2. **Grief she can't show.** The strongest idea here is Marta hearing the news twice: once from the boy, once from the guards, and having to act surprised the second time. It turns her grief into a test of nerve and lets the reader see both at once through a crack in a door. **The risk:** it reads as a device. It works only if the guards' version adds something new that hurts (a slander, a detail Alaric didn't know). If it just repeats what she's heard, cut it to a few lines.
3. **Wena is the evidence.** The search is for a boy and a big dog, and Gerolt's dog is in her kitchen. Every second the guards are inside, the dog is the danger, and Alaric has to keep her quiet without seeing the room. That's tension that comes from the story, not from a chase.
4. **Her answer to "it was my fault".** The most painful thing isn't her tears. It's whether she comforts him, refuses to, or does neither and gives him something to do, the way Gerolt did. This is the line to get right in the dialogue round.
5. **One line for Silas and Marta, not a backstory.** The reader should come away knowing she has a right to shut the door on him, and that he'd stand outside it all night.
6. **"Empty" costs something you can see.** Not "the law would…", but Marta checking the bolt, or moving him to a room with no window on the street.
7. **Don't let the double upstage Marta.** It comes in the last few hundred words, and it uses what she did for him. If it takes more than that, it belongs at the start of the next Alaric chapter instead (question 10).
8. **Too much for one chapter?** The death news, the guards, the grief, Empty, Silas, the face and the double. **Challenge:** Darcy stays out (question 11). If the draft runs long, Empty is the beat that could move to the next Alaric chapter, not the guards or the double.
9. **Silas doesn't win cleanly.** He doesn't get the door opened, doesn't get forgiven, and doesn't get to explain. What he gets is to stay in the room, and only because the guards made it necessary.

## Round 1 questions

Each has a recommendation. Answer by number.

**1. How the door opens again.**
- *Recommended:* Alaric knocks again and says Gerolt's words through the door: "The old fool sent me." She opens it.
- *Alternative:* Wena scratches and whines at the door until Marta opens it for her, and only then sees the sword.

**2. Where she hears it.**
- *Recommended:* in the doorway, with the sword in his hand. She asks "Where is he?" before he can start, and he tells her straight, as Silas said. Then he warns her about the guards.
- *Alternative:* she lets him in first, and he tells her at the table.

**3. The guards.**
- a) Keep the visit, with Marta hearing the news a second time? *Recommended:* yes.
- b) What the guards' version says (Your call). *Recommended:* the story going round is that Gerolt attacked the riders unprovoked, and she has to let it stand.
- c) They ask about Gerolt's dog? *Recommended:* yes.
- d) Where they hide. *Recommended:* Alaric and Wena behind a door off the kitchen, with a crack he can see through, and Silas in the cellar or the yard.
- e) The bodies by the river. Do the guards mention that some were cut down, not burned? Marta would know whose blade that sounds like, and Alaric would see her look towards where Silas is hiding. *Recommended:* yes, one line (Your call).

**4. "It was my fault."**
- What does Marta say? Give me your rough version, in your own words, and I'll bring it to her voice in the dialogue round.
- *My suggestion:* she doesn't answer it. She asks, "Did he say anything else?" He gives her the message again, and she goes on with his ankle.

**5. Silas and Marta: the one line** (Your call, new).
- *Recommended:* "The last time you stood at my door, you'd come to tell me my father was dead." It explains her fury without explaining the gorge. It gives Silas a reason to stand back from the door tonight, and Alaric sees Silas's face when she says it.
- *Alternative:* she says nothing to him all chapter except orders, and the reader learns it from that.

**6. Who Marta is.** All of it is your call. The old version is offered only as a starting point.
- a) Age and look. Old: about forty, red-haired, freckled, broad hands with an oven-burn scar.
- b) Her Affinity. Old: Eminent Wind, strong enough to sense the shape of someone's mana. If kept, it's how Empty is confirmed.
- c) Her manner. Old: sharp-tongued, observant, suspicious of motives, with standing in the town through information, long friendships and feeding the guards.
- d) What she wants (principle 6). *My suggestion:* to keep the inn and the people who depend on it safe, which the boy now threatens.

**7. How Empty is found.**
- *Recommended:* she asks his Affinity outright; he says "None", and uses his own word; then she confirms it (by sensing, if 6b holds).
- *Alternative:* she tests him the way Gerolt did, with a flame.
- And Silas: what does he say when he hears it? *Recommended:* almost nothing. Something like "That's why you never…" before he stops himself. You'll know his line better than I will.

**8. What earns the shelter.**
- *Recommended:* after the guards, Alaric offers to go, so she isn't in danger because of him, and that's what makes her keep him, on her terms.
- *Alternative:* she names her terms first, and he agrees to them.

**9. Others in the inn** (Your call).
- Chapter 8 had a woman with a tub, and a voice inside after closing. Does anyone live or work at the inn who's still there at this hour?
- *Recommended:* no. The staff have gone home, so Marta is alone. That keeps the scene to four people and a dog.

**10. The face and the double.**
- a) Where he first sees his face. *Recommended:* in the water in a basin, by lamplight. It's honest, and it isn't clear enough for him to be sure.
- b) The double at the end of this chapter, or at the start of the next Alaric chapter? *Recommended:* the end of this one, briefly. It attacks the safety Marta has just given him, which is what the plan says it's for.
- c) What it looks like. *Recommended:* exactly like him, in the black clothes he woke in, not Silas's cloak.
- d) What it says first. Give me your rough version. Its job is a true fact followed by an unjust verdict, using Gerolt and Marta.
- e) The ending: Marta at the door, "Alaric?", and he says he's fine. *Recommended:* yes.

**11. Darcy.**
- *Recommended:* not in this chapter. Marta has enough to carry, and the next Alaric chapter (the Faint quarter, Redd and Freya) is where Kelmend's price belongs.
- *Alternative:* her name only, at the very end, as a hook.
- Either way: who is Darcy to Marta? The old version had her as Marta's childhood friend. That's your call.

**12. Title.** Later, once it's written.
