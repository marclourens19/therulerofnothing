# Chapter 8 — Dialogue Options

These are alternatives for the dialogue pass, not edits to the existing chapters. Each option includes a little surrounding action so the exchange can be heard in context. Choose one option per section, or combine individual lines. Marta has a third option because her first response sets the next chapter's conversation.

## 1. Silas's new question about the field

### A — Alaric makes him acknowledge the earlier dismissal

“What else was on that field?” Silas asked. “Besides the soldiers. Carts, anything like that?”

Alaric turned from the light moving away through the trees.

“You could have asked me that last night. I was trying to tell you.”

“I should have.” Silas looked towards Kelmend, then back at him. “Tell me now.”

“There were machines. Silver ones. One was lying on its side, and another had legs taller than a man.” Alaric rubbed his thumb against Wena's collar. “I couldn't tell you how they worked.”

“Were there tracks coming into the field?”

“I looked for them. There weren't any. All that wheat round the edge was still standing.” He watched Silas's face. “That's what I've been trying to explain. I can't make it sound sensible. It wasn't.”

### B — Gerolt's judgment makes Silas reconsider

“What about banners?” Silas asked. “Did you see which armies they belonged to?”

“I wouldn't know their banners, Silas. You know that.” Alaric looked at him. “Gerolt didn't recognise them either.”

Silas turned away from the ship.

“He said that?”

“He said he could name every flag that had ever marched through Mydea. He didn't know a single one out there. Not the armour either.”

Silas stayed quiet long enough that Alaric went on.

“Last night you told me a battle that size would be the talk of Kelmend. You never asked what I'd actually seen.”

“I thought you'd got confused. You'd nearly drowned, and you were telling me thousands of men had died without anyone hearing them.” Silas looked towards the river. “I should've asked.”

## 2. The way in and the wait for closing

### A — Wena is Alaric's immediate concern

“We'll go down along the south wall,” Silas said. “There's an old way through behind those houses. I'll check the far end before we come out. After that, we're close to the inn.”

“Will Wena get through it?”

“There's room for her. Keep her with you while I have a look. If I need to go further, I'll tell you where.”

Alaric looked down at the lights near the gate.

“Then we can go straight to Marta?”

“We can get near her. Her inn'll be full at this hour. I want us at the back door when she's closing, with most of them gone.”

“How long do we have to wait?”

“A while yet. We find somewhere to sit first. I'm not having you stand on that foot all night.”

### B — Alaric wants to keep the sword ready

Silas looked at the blade hanging from Alaric's hand.

“We need that covered before we go down. Get the rolled bed off my shoulder. We'll wrap it in that.”

“Marta needs to see it.”

“And she will. You can unwrap it at her door. I don't want a guard asking why you're walking into town with a bare sword.”

Alaric reached for the roll.

“We're still using the way by the wall?”

“Yes. I'll check where it comes out, then we go through together. Keep the bundle with you while I look.”

“And we go to the back of the inn?”

“When she's closing. It'll be crowded now, and I want her to have time to hear you. We get close first, then find somewhere to sit till most of them have gone.”

## 3. Alaric admits he cannot keep the pace

### A — A plain request, with disappointment that resting did not help

Alaric lifted his foot out of another rut and had to stop before putting it down.

“Silas. Could you slow down a little?”

Silas turned. His eyes went to the foot Alaric was keeping off the ground.

“It's worse?”

“I can still walk. Just not this fast over this ground.” Alaric tried another step. “I thought it would be better after we'd sat down.”

“All right. Come beside me.”

Silas waited for him.

“We'll stop if you need to. Just let me know.”

When they moved on, Alaric could put his foot down carefully without having to hurry the next step.

### B — They change the footing as well as the pace

“Silas, wait. Please,” Alaric called.

Silas stopped. Alaric stood with his teeth pressed together until the pain eased enough to speak.

“I can't keep doing this. I put my foot in one of those ruts and it turns under me.”

“Come over here.” Silas moved towards the edge of the field. “Ground's flatter along this bit.”

Alaric followed him for a few careful steps.

“That's better. But could we go slower too?”

“We can. Take your time putting it down.”

Silas stayed level with him, looking towards the next houses while Alaric watched where he stepped.

## 4. The guards at the handover

### A — Sore feet, resentment and the remaining round

“Another round?” The rougher voice carried into the yard. “I can barely feel my bloody toes.”

“South Gate first, then the inn,” the other man answered.

“What do they want with an innkeeper?”

“She's the farmer's niece. The officer wants her questioned after she's closed. Finish the round, then we go over.”

“They say Gerolt burned some of them before they got him.” The first man lowered his voice. “Good for him. Those bastards had no business riding up to his house.”

“Keep that to yourself. I'm not answering for something you said.”

A boot scraped against the stone.

“Fine. What else?”

“We're still looking for the boy. Young, with a big dog. They want him brought in alive.”

“Did they give you a name?”

“No. That's all we have. So keep your eyes open, and don't stick him because he runs.”

“I heard you. Come on, then. My feet aren't getting any warmer here.”

### B — The angry guard is tired of taking Natharul's orders

“I thought we were getting relieved,” one of the guards said.

“So did I,” the other answered. “They've given us the inn as well.”

“What, the one just inside the South Gate? It's full.”

“After closing. The farmer's niece keeps it. We finish the gate round, then go and question her.”

The first man gave a short, unpleasant laugh.

“Gerolt kills a few of their riders, and now his family gets dragged into it. I'd have bought the old bastard a drink.”

“Would you shut up? You don't know who's listening.”

“They're Natharul's soldiers. They shouldn't be giving us orders.”

“And you're going to tell them that, are you? Because I want to get this done and get off the street.”

There was a pause.

“What about that boy?”

“Boy with a big dog. Same order: bring him in alive.”

“Half the people coming through have something at their heels.”

“Then look properly. I can't give you a better description than the one they gave me.”

## 5. After the auction

### A — Alaric checks whether he understood what he saw

When he was beside Silas again, Alaric looked back towards the people gathered round the steps.

“That girl. The man who gave them the gold… was he buying *her*?”

“Yes. They sell 『Faint』 people here.”

Alaric kept looking back.

“She was watching the crowd. Even after he took her arm. I thought she was waiting for somebody to come and get her.”

Silas followed his look. The crowd had already closed around the place where she'd been standing.

“What will happen to her?”

“She'll have to go with him. I can't tell you what he'll do when he gets her there, Alaric. I've never seen the man before.”

Alaric turned forward again, but he was still trying to find her between the people passing.

“He didn't even ask her.”

### B — Alaric asks why the guards permitted it

“Why didn't the guards do anything?” Alaric asked when they were together again.

Silas glanced towards the auction.

“About the girl?”

“She tried to pull her hand out of his. They saw her. They were standing right there.”

“They're allowed to sell 『Faint』 people, Alaric. Those guards aren't going to stop a buyer taking her away.”

“For being 『Faint』? That's what makes it allowed?”

“Yes.”

Alaric stared at him.

“But she didn't want to go. You saw that, didn't you?”

“I saw.” Silas looked back towards the steps. “If she'd kept pulling away, they'd have helped him take her.”

Alaric's eyes went to the guards again. One of them was still watching the crowd.

## 6. Marta is Gerolt's niece

### A — The family connection makes delivering the news harder

“The guards said his niece,” Alaric whispered. “They meant Marta?”

“They did. Her father was Gerolt's brother.”

Alaric looked down at the bundle across his knees.

“I thought I was coming here to tell her he sent me. Now I'm going to hand her his sword and tell her he's dead.”

“Start with what he told you to say. You don't have to get the whole thing out at once.”

“And then the guards come looking for me. What happens to her if they find me inside?”

Silas watched a customer leave the inn.

“They're coming to question her either way. But finding you inside could make it worse. I won't tell you otherwise.”

Alaric looked at him.

“I'll watch the approach while you knock,” Silas said. “And I'll be beside you when she opens.”

### B — Alaric asks what happens if she refuses him

“Marta's his niece?” Alaric asked.

“His brother's daughter. He visited often.”

Alaric put a hand on Wena's neck.

“Wena knows her, then?”

“She does. Gerolt brought her when he came.”

He stroked Wena once, then stopped.

“I thought she'd see the sword and know what to do. That's as far as I'd thought.” His eyes went back to the inn. “What if she won't let me in after I tell her?”

“I can't tell you what she'll do, Alaric.”

“Then what do I do?”

“If she won't have you, we find somewhere else. I'm not sending you back through these streets on your own.”

Alaric nodded, but he kept his hand on Wena.

## 7. At the private door

### A — Silas states the ban plainly and keeps his promise

“Go on, Alaric. Knock.”

Alaric looked at him instead of the door.

“Aren't you going to speak to her?”

“If she sees me first, she may shut it before you've said a word.”

“Why?”

Silas kept his eyes on the door.

“She told me never to come back. Not here. Not anywhere near Kelmend.”

Alaric looked down at Gerolt's sword.

*Was that why he'd wanted to take the sword himself?*

“You said we'd both go in.”

“I'm staying beside you. If she'll have me, I go in with you.” Silas looked at him then. “Show her the sword, Alaric. Let her hear what Gerolt told you.”

### B — Alaric challenges the late disclosure

Silas had stopped beside the door without raising his hand.

“What's wrong?” Alaric asked.

“You need to knock.”

“I thought you knew her.”

“I do. She told me never to show my face again. Especially not round Kelmend.”

Alaric stared at him.

“You brought me all this way, and you're telling me that now?”

“I kept putting it off. I thought she'd still hear you, with a message from her uncle.”

*Was that why he'd wanted to take the sword himself?*

“Are you going to wait somewhere else?”

“No.” Silas moved beside him, where whoever opened the door would see them both. “I'm here. Knock when you're ready.”

## 8. Marta's first reaction — the chapter's final line

### A — She recognises Gerolt's dog, then refuses its use as an excuse

The woman opened the door a little, and Wena put her nose into the gap.

“Wena? What are you doing—”

She opened it further and reached down. Wena pushed against her hand. Then the woman looked past Alaric.

“Marta,” Silas said.

Her hand stopped on Wena's neck.

“I told you never to come back, Silas. You don't get to bring my uncle's dog and pretend I didn't.”

### B — His familiar way of addressing her makes the anger personal

“Wena?”

The woman reached through the opening, and the dog pressed her head into her palm. She looked at Alaric, then at the man beside him.

For a moment she said nothing.

“Marta, could we—”

“Don't say my name like that. I told you to stay away from me, Silas. I meant it.”

### C — She takes charge of the conversation and excludes Silas

The door opened, and Wena went straight to the woman behind it. The woman bent to touch her, then looked up at Alaric.

“Where's Gerolt?”

Her eyes moved past him before he could answer.

“Marta,” Silas said.

She straightened, keeping one hand against Wena.

“You stay where you are, Silas. The boy can tell me why he's here. I haven't got a single thing to say to you.”
