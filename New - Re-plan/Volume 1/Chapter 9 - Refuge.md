# Chapter 9 – Refuge

Wena whined at the shut door. Behind it, footsteps were going away.

"Alaric—" Silas began.

Alaric didn't wait to hear what he would say. He put his free hand flat against the wood.

"The old fool sent me."

Inside, the footsteps stopped.

The door opened again, and Marta looked at him.

"Where'd ye hear that?"

"Gerolt. He told me to show you this." Alaric lifted the sword a little. "But before—"

She saw Silas still beside him.

"Are ye waiting for me to come out and drag ye away myself?"

"Please," Alaric said. "There's something I have to tell you."

"Aye, and I'll hear ye once he's—"

"There are guards coming here!"

Marta turned back to him. Alaric glanced down the lane and lowered his voice.

"We heard two of them. They're finishing their round, then they're coming here to ask you about Gerolt. And about us. They're looking for a boy with a big dog."

Marta looked at Wena. Then she looked at the sword, and her face changed.

"That's Gerolt's sword. Why've ye got it? Why's his dog out here with you and not with him?"

"Please, Marta. I'm begging you." He heard his voice crack and couldn't stop it. "Let me in and I'll tell you everything. If they find me out here—"

"If who finds ye?"

"The elves." His eyes went to the end of the lane. "Please."

Her hand tightened on the door. "I don't want trouble in this house, lad. Ye'll have to leave."

"I don't have anywhere else to go. Gerolt sent me to you."

Marta didn't move from the doorway. Wena had come forward to sniff her fingers.

She let out a long breath and stepped back.

"Oh, get in, then. And keep yer voice down."

Her eyes returned to Silas.

"That means the boy. Don't ye use me uncle to talk yer way back into this house."

"The message isn't mine, Marta. He had your name before I ever met him."

"Then let *him* speak. I don't want another bloody word out of you."

Silas handed Alaric Wena's lead with his good hand. Alaric wanted to ask whether he would still be there later, but Marta was waiting for him to move.

She let Alaric and Wena past her, then stood in the doorway again. Over her shoulder, Alaric could see Silas in the lane with the rolled bed under his arm.

"Ye aren't setting foot under me roof."

"I'll stay outside."

"Aye. Ye fucking will."

She shut the door before he could say anything else, and dropped the bar across it.

They were in a kitchen. A fire was burning low in the hearth, and a lamp stood on the long table among stacks of clean bowls. It was warm, and it still smelled of roasting meat and new bread.

Wena pulled the lead out of his hand. She went once round the kitchen with her nose down, past the hearth and under the table, and came back to the door. Then she lay down in front of it.

Marta pointed him to a chair. When she turned into the lamplight, Alaric saw her properly for the first time. *Prettiest woman in Kelmend,* the loud guard had said in the yard. He had thought the man was only talking. He sat down quickly and looked at the sword instead.

Marta looked at it too, lying across his knees.

"Ye said ye'd tell me inside. Why've ye got his sword? Where's Gerolt?"

"He gave it to me."

"Well, the old fool can come and tell me himself. Where is he?"

"He can't. Not anymore. He—he's dead."

Marta's face went still. Her lips were parted, but she said nothing.

Alaric lowered his eyes. Neither of them spoke.

"Look at me, lad."

He did.

"Tell me what happened. Don't just tell me he's dead."

"I only met him yesterday. I think they wanted to find out what happened on his farm, but they broke the door in, and he started fighting them. I still don't understand why. We ran, but they followed us, and there were arrows…" Alaric's fingers closed round the hilt. "He told me to run. I didn't. I couldn't. I couldn't leave him behind."

Marta kept staring at him. Alaric waited for her to say something, but her expression did not change.

"He stood there fighting them while ye—"

"I know." His face twisted. "I know I should have done something. I had his sword right here, and I couldn't even—"

Marta pulled out the chair beside him. She went to sit, and missed the seat.

Alaric reached towards her. She caught the edge of the table instead.

"Give me that," she said, looking at the sword. "Let me hold it a moment."

He laid it in her hands.

Marta ran her thumb over the worn grip.

"He only met ye yesterday?"

"That morning."

She nodded once, still looking down at it. When she tried to speak again, nothing came.

Wena got up from the door. She came and pushed her nose under Marta's hand, and Marta's fingers closed in her fur.

"And Silas?" Marta said at last. "Where was he while this was happening?"

"He came after Gerolt was hit. He killed the last of them, then dragged me away."

Marta looked towards the closed door. "And he brought ye here."

"Gerolt sent me. Silas helped me get here."

She didn't answer him.

"It was my fault," Alaric said. "If I'd gone when he said—"

"Don't."

He shut his mouth.

"I can't hear that just now." She swallowed. "I can't, lad."

A woman came in from the passage with a stack of mugs. Alaric knew her: she was the one who had brought the tub out to the gutter. She looked at him, then at the sword in Marta's hands, and her eyes stayed on Marta.

"What's happened?"

"Gerolt's dead."

The woman set the mugs down on the table one at a time.

"Oh, Marta."

She stayed beside Marta's chair with a hand on her shoulder. Marta covered it with her own, then let go.

"Ye said guards were coming," Marta said.

"After they've finished their round," Alaric said. "They're looking for me and Wena. They've been told to bring me in alive."

The woman's hand fell from Marta's shoulder.

"Marta, they're coming here looking for him. We've got guests upstairs. And the rest of us live here too. You can't just decide this and expect us to—"

"Gerolt sent him here. I'm not turning him out."

"Don't look at me like that. I'm not asking you to hand him over. I'm asking what happens to us if they find him."

Marta looked away from her, towards the passage.

"I haven't got an answer ye'll like."

"Then don't talk as if it's settled just because you've said so."

Alaric began to stand.

"I can leave. I didn't mean to bring all this here."

The woman put a hand out to stop him.

"Stay where you are a moment. You'll fall over before you reach the door."

She looked back at Marta.

"He can use my room while you talk to them. It's empty, and it's better than having him sitting here when they come knocking."

"Ye don't have to do that."

"I know. I'm offering." She took Wena's lead. "But we're talking about this again when they've gone."

Another woman had come to the passage door with a folded cloth over her arm. She spoke to the one holding Wena's lead.

"He's asking for you."

"Tell him I'm finished for tonight."

The other woman gave Alaric one look, then went back the way she had come.

Alaric got up more slowly this time.

Marta held the sword out, grip first. He took it carefully. She let go without looking at him.

He turned to the woman. "Thank you. I'm… Alaric."

"Rhose," she said. "Come on, then, Alaric. Lean on me if you need to."

At the passage door, Alaric looked back. Marta was still at the table, her hands empty in her lap.

The passage went past the common room, where the chairs stood upside down on the tables, to the stairs. Rhose went up them beside him with Wena's lead in one hand and his arm across her shoulders. Halfway up, his ankle turned on the edge of a step, and she took more of his weight until he had found his footing again.

At the top, a passage ran the length of the house, with shut doors on both sides. Behind one of them, somebody was snoring. Rhose opened a door halfway along and drew them in.

Her room was small. There was a bed with a dress laid over the end of it, a chest under the shutter, and a candle burning low on the chest. It smelled of something sweet that Alaric couldn't name.

"Sit down there," Rhose said, nodding at the floor beside the door. "And keep her quiet, whatever you hear."

Alaric lowered himself onto the boards with his back to the wall. He laid the sword across his knees and pulled Wena in against his side. Rhose blew out the candle. She opened the door a crack and stood at the gap.

"If they come up here…" Alaric whispered.

"If they do, let me answer." Rhose kept her eye to the gap. "You stay where you are."

*They'll knock at the front.* He thought of Silas, out in the lane with nowhere to go. *If they go round the back, they'll find Silas.*

He waited. Wena's ribs rose and fell under his hand. Somewhere along the passage, the snoring stopped and started again.

Then someone knocked downstairs, two hard knocks, and Alaric heard the front door open.

"We've shut the bar, lads."

"Not here for a drink."

Alaric knew that voice. It was the loud one from the yard.

"That makes a change. What can I do for ye?"

"Gerolt's your uncle, isn't he?"

"Aye. What's this about?"

"When was he last here?"

"His last delivery. If ye need the day, I'll check the book. Why are ye asking?"

"They found him in the woods by the river. He's dead, Marta. I'm sorry."

Alaric pressed his hand against his mouth. Rhose stood beside him, listening through the gap in her door.

Downstairs, a chair scraped across the floor.

"Where've they taken him?" Marta asked.

"The elves had him when the report came in. Nobody's told us where they've taken him since."

"The elves?"

"That's all we know."

"Can I see him?"

"I don't know, Marta."

There was no answer. After a while, the other man spoke. Alaric remembered him telling his companion to keep his voice down in the yard.

"Has anyone come from the farm tonight? A boy, especially. With a big dog."

"No."

Wena lifted her head. Alaric kept his hand on her neck until she put it down again.

"Anyone asking after Gerolt?"

"Not before you two. What d'ye want this boy for?"

"Orders are to bring him in alive. We haven't been told why."

"Haven't even given us a fucking face," the loud one said.

"Will you leave it?"

"I'm just saying."

"Well, ye can say it quieter," Marta told him. "There's people sleeping above yer head."

"Sorry."

"If he turns up, send someone for us," the other man said.

When Marta answered, her voice had changed. It sounded the way it had when she found Wena at her door.

"Aye. Come back tomorrow when ye've finished work. I'll put a drink aside for ye."

"A drink for me?" the loud one asked.

"Listen to him," the other man said. "She smiles at him once and he forgets I'm standing here."

"Fuck off. I was asking."

"Both of ye," Marta said. "And don't come arguing on me doorstep. I've enough of that without paying ye in drink for it."

The door closed below. Rhose stayed by her own door until the men's footsteps had gone.

Then she let out her breath and opened it wide.

Marta was in the common room with the lamp beside her. The chairs were still upside down on every table but one, and she was wiping that one. She didn't look up when they came down the stairs.

"I'll see who they woke," Rhose said, and went back up.

Alaric stood at the foot of the stairs with the sword. Wena went to Marta and lay down by her feet.

"I meant it before," Alaric said. "I can go. If I stay, they'll keep coming here."

"And where would ye go?"

He didn't answer. Through the passage, past the kitchen, he could see the bar across the back door.

"Ye warned me before ye came in. Ye can stay tonight."

"What about Silas?"

Marta kept wiping the table.

"What about him?"

"Will you let him in now they've gone? His hand's hurt. He burnt it getting me away from—"

"I heard ye, lad. He isn't staying here."

Alaric pulled at the edge of the cloak.

"This is his. I should give it back if he's still outside."

"Ye can give it back tomorrow. Silas has coin. Let him pay for a bed somewhere his wife doesn't have to look at him."

Alaric's hand stopped on the cloak.

"His wife?"

Marta set the cloth down.

"Aye. Me."

She sighed and picked it up again.

"Leave it at that, Alaric."

He nodded. He had more he wanted to ask about Silas. Now he could not think of anything to say without making it worse.

Rhose came down a little later. Marta put the cloth down.

"Take him up, will ye? Come back down when he's settled. We'll talk about it."

Rhose looked at her, then nodded. She lit a candle from Marta's lamp and took Alaric back up the stairs, past her own door, to the one at the end of the passage.

"This one's yours," Rhose said. "You can leave your things here now."

Alaric followed her in with the sword and Wena. There was a bed, a stool, and a jug and basin on a stand under the shutter. Rhose set the candle on the stand.

"Come on, sit down. Let me help you with that boot."

He sat on the bed and laid Gerolt's sword beside him. She bent towards his boot, and his eyes dropped to the cleavage above the low neckline of her dress. He turned away at once, his face burning.

"W-wait. I can do it myself, please."

She gave a quiet laugh.

"It's only your boot, Alaric."

She straightened slowly. Instead of stepping back, she stood close in front of him, near enough that her skirt brushed his knees, and put a hand on his shoulder.

"There's nothing to be embarrassed about."

He kept his eyes on the floor. Even his ears felt hot.

"I wasn't trying to stare."

"I know." She tipped his chin up with one finger until he had to look at her face. "Now I've had a proper look at you…" She smiled. "You're a handsome one, you know. Even under all that dirt."

He didn't know what to do with his hands. He put them flat on the blanket.

"I told him I was finished for tonight." Her finger stayed under his chin a moment longer. "I didn't say anything about you."

Then she laughed, softly, at whatever his face was doing, and stepped back to give him room.

He loosened the boot, keeping his eyes on his hands. But when he tried to pull it past his heel, pain ran up his leg and he stopped. He sat holding the heel of it, and Rhose waited.

"Could you hold my ankle still?"

"Since you asked nicely."

She knelt and supported his foot with one hand. When she eased the boot past his heel, he gripped the mattress.

"Hurts there?"

He nodded.

She worked it off more slowly. The swelling showed as soon as the boot was gone.

Rhose put a folded blanket beneath his foot. "Keep it up for a while."

Alaric sat back and let out the breath he had been holding.

He pulled the other boot off himself.

Rhose poured water from the jug into the basin and set it on the stool beside him, with a cloth over the edge.

"There's water there, and a cloth. Do what you can tonight. Marta's bringing something to eat."

He leaned towards the basin. The water was cold, and he scrubbed at his face until it stung. When he lifted his head, the water had gone grey with the road, and he stopped with the cloth still in his hand.

There was a face in it, looking up at him.

Rhose was gathering his boots.

"What's wrong?"

"I don't remember looking like this."

She came a little closer, then looked from the water to him.

"You don't remember your own face?"

"I don't remember anything from before yesterday." He touched the corner of his mouth. The face in the water did the same. "I thought I'd recognise it."

She stood quietly beside him. He waited for her to ask how anyone could forget that. Instead, she put the boots down by the door and moved the candle closer to the basin.

"I'll leave that here."

Alaric kept looking at the water.

"Thank you."

When he looked up again, she had stepped into the passage and was pulling the door gently shut.

He sat there until the water was still again. The face didn't look like anyone in particular. He turned his head, and it turned, and he still couldn't make it his. His clothes fitted him, and he couldn't remember ever putting them on.

Marta came up a while later with a bowl of stew and half a loaf, and a second bowl for Wena, which she put on the floor. She set his on the end of the bed. Her eyes went to Gerolt's sword lying beside him, and stayed there. Then she went out without a word.

Wena ate hers before Alaric had picked up the spoon. He ate all of his. Halfway through, he had to stop and sit with the spoon in his hand until he could go on.

When he had finished, he got up on his good foot and opened the shutter. It looked down on the lane beside the inn. The gap between the storehouse and its yard wall, where they had waited, was empty, and so was the lane in both directions. He stood there until the cold made him close it.

He slid the bolt across the door. Then he took off Silas's cloak, laid it over the foot of the bed, pinched out the candle and lay down.

*The elves had him when the report came in.* He and Silas had left Gerolt in the leaves, and the elves had gone back for him.

*She can't even bury him.*

Below him, two women were talking in low voices. He knew Marta's, and he thought the other was Rhose's. He couldn't make out the words. Once Rhose said something sharper, and after that they were both quiet for a long time.

For tonight, he didn't have to go anywhere. Marta had said he could stay.

He reached down until his hand found Wena's ear.

He didn't hear the voices stop.

---

Alaric opened his eyes.

He lay still for a moment, listening. Something had woken him, but he could not remember a sound. The room was dark, and the sheet had twisted round one of his legs.

Then he saw the blood.

It was seeping beneath the door, gathering in the gaps between the floorboards. As he watched, a narrow thread reached the foot of the bed.

He pulled his feet away from it.

*What happened?*

He got out on the other side and crossed the room, keeping close to the wall. His hand shook so badly that he missed the bolt before he found it.

The door caught on something when he opened it.

An arm.

The woman lay outside his room, her head turned towards the stairs. He knew her before he saw what had been done to her throat. The cut had opened it beneath the jaw, and the blood spreading from her neck had reached his doorway.

Rhose.

He backed into the doorframe. His stomach cramped, and he bent over, retching onto the boards. When he tried to straighten, he saw her again and could not keep the next breath down.

*Marta.*

He needed Marta.

Alaric stepped round her body and went towards the stairs. Blood covered the top step. He held the rail and lowered himself one step at a time, trying not to look at where his feet landed.

The common room was lit orange. Through the windows he could see flames, and beyond the glass someone was screaming.

Marta lay beside the bar.

He stopped halfway down the stairs.

Her dress was torn open across her stomach. Blood had soaked the cloth and spread beneath her, reaching the legs of the nearest table. One hand rested palm-up on the floor. Her eyes were open.

Wena stood beside her, pushing her nose against that hand.

"Marta?"

He went to her slowly. The blood was warm beneath his bare feet. He crouched and reached for her shoulder, but stopped before he touched it.

"Marta, please…"

Wena whined. She pushed at Marta's hand again, then looked up at him.

Alaric tried to call for someone. He got no further than opening his mouth before his stomach clenched again. He bent over beside the table, coughing until his eyes watered.

Outside, a crash shook the windows.

He had to find someone. Someone who could help them.

He got up, using the table, and crossed to the front door.

Heat struck him as he opened it.

The buildings opposite were burning from the ground floor up. Fire came through the windows and ran beneath the edges of the roofs. A beam fell into the street, scattering sparks across bodies lying on the stones.

He recognised the market stalls they had passed that evening. Their cloth covers were gone. Broken carts lay overturned among the wreckage, their wheels burning, and the people beside them were blackened and still.

A man lay close to the inn's step with one leg missing below the knee. Alaric could see the white end of the bone. Blood filled the cracks between the stones beneath him.

Further along, someone was trying to pull another person out from under a fallen wall. They dragged at an arm, slipped and tried again. A roof collapsed behind them, and both disappeared beneath the falling timber.

Alaric took a step towards them.

There was no way through.

The street was blocked by burning wood, fallen masonry and bodies. He turned towards the next one and found the same thing. Everywhere he could see, windows blazed above wrecked doorways. Flames rose beyond the shattered roofs, across the town, until he could no longer tell one burning building from the next.

People were still screaming somewhere inside them. He tried to follow one voice, then another, but the crashes and the fire swallowed the words. He could not find where any of them were.

Alaric stood on the bloodied stones, unable to get a full breath.

A figure stood further down the street.

His back was towards Alaric. He wore the black clothes Alaric had woken in on Gerolt's field, and he was facing the fires. A burning roof gave way in front of him. He did not move.

Then he began to turn.

Slowly, his shoulders came round, and his face followed.

Alaric saw his own eyes. His own mouth. The face he had seen in the water before he went to bed.

"This is who you are, Alaric."

The voice was his too.

*No. I didn't—*

Rhose's torn throat.

Marta's open eyes.

Blood beneath his door.

Alaric woke with his hands twisted in the sheet.

He dragged in a breath and could not stop taking more. His shirt clung to him. Pain drove through his ankle when he moved, and his head hurt so badly that he pressed both hands against it.

There were footsteps outside.

"Alaric, keep it down, will you? I've only just got back to bed."

He lifted his head.

It was Rhose's voice.

*She's alive.*

The footsteps went away. A door opened further along the passage, then closed.

Alaric stared at his own door. Nothing moved beneath it.

Wena stood beside the bed, her head raised. He reached down, and she pushed her nose into his hand.

He stayed sitting, one hand on her head, until his breathing grew quieter.

The pain behind his eyes did not ease.

*I'm fine.*
