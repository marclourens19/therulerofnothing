# The Past Within Reach

The officer lowered the lamp.

Its light sank to his knees. He turned towards the king without leaving the machine, and Alaric watched the ground between them, waiting for someone to cross it.

Nobody did.

The king stayed behind the drawn swords. One guard moved closer to him, and the king stepped aside when the man tried to put himself between him and the wreck. He kept it in sight. He didn't go any nearer.

Alaric's fingers were pressing Gerolt's token into his thigh. The edge had started to hurt through the borrowed cloth, and he couldn't remember tightening his hand.

"What do you think he found?" he whispered.

Silas didn't take his eyes off the field. "I couldn't really see."

"He seemed to know where to look, though."

Silas glanced at him. Whatever he might have said went unsaid, because green light had opened inside the black crystal behind the king.

The attendant held the silver frame away from his chest. A second line appeared under the first. Then both broke into unfamiliar characters that ran across the dark surface without anyone touching it.

He hurried towards the escort. A guard stepped into his way, but the king had already seen the light and came forward before the man could send the attendant back.

Alaric lifted his head a little, careful to stay under the thorn. The attendant turned the crystal towards the king. The green characters shifted into narrow rows and went still.

Nothing else on the field stopped. A wagon rolled between the tents. Beside the machine, the officer waited until the lamp's weight made him change hands.

The king read the glowing rows. They moved. He leaned closer and read whatever replaced them.

Alaric couldn't make out a single mark from where he lay.

The king demanded something. The attendant shook his head quickly. The king shoved the frame back into his hands and turned for the landing craft so suddenly that the nearest guard had to jump out of his way.

The officer called after him. This time Alaric heard the voice, but the warship swallowed every word.

The king stopped. He pointed at the dead, then swept the same arm across the machines. The officer started to answer. The king was already walking.

Nobody bowed this time. They ran to catch him up.

At the corner of the first tent, the king turned back once towards the wreck. His escort went on another pace before closing round him again.

"Looks like he's leaving," Redd said.

It was barely more than breath. Alaric looked across Silas's shoulder and found him staring at the royal standard, dirt still stuck to his cheek.

Alaric had wanted the king to stay. Long enough to explain what he'd seen. The thought was so foolish he'd never have said it aloud. There was no way to ask. No way to stand in front of those guards and tell them he'd woken up among their dead.

Below, the king reached the stair and climbed it without waiting for anyone. One soldier followed as far as the bottom step before a guard put an arm across his path. The king disappeared inside, and the door stayed open only long enough for his escort.

---

The first fire started while the stair was still folding.

Orange spread under a low heap beyond the tents. Alaric had taken it for a pile of discarded cloth, until an elven soldier pulled a pole free and an arm fell out of the side.

Another soldier pushed it back in.

The flame climbed through a sleeve, brightened under a breastplate, and caught the dry wood laid between the bodies. Smoke rolled low along the ground before the air stirred up by the ships caught it.

"They're burning them," Alaric whispered.

Silas's mouth tightened. He said nothing.

The carts were coming.

They came along the cut roads one after another, each drawn by a single horse and led by a soldier who didn't look at what he was pulling. The pile on each was stacked crossways, the way it had been stacked in the field. At the fire, two men with hooks waited to unload them.

Alaric knew which cart it was before it reached the light. He couldn't have said how. Wena knew too. She went rigid under his arm again, and this time she didn't make the sound. She pushed her face into the front of his coat and kept it there, as if she'd decided she wasn't going to watch.

He watched for both of them.

The soldiers didn't unload it one body at a time. That would have taken too long. They knocked the pins out of the cart's back, set their shoulders to the side and tipped it. The whole pile slid off the boards into the fire together, and sparks went up in a great bright rush, and for a heartbeat Alaric could see a dark shape near the top that was heavier at one end.

Then the flames closed over it.

He couldn't say anything. He didn't know what people said. Gerolt had never told him. Gerolt had meant to tell him a great many things, and there hadn't been time.

The token's edge had cut through the bandage on his palm. He could feel it, warm and wet. He didn't loosen his grip.

The soldiers led the empty cart away. The man at the horse's head was already talking to the next one about something else.

Against his chest, Wena was shaking.

"I'm sorry," Alaric whispered into her fur, because it was the only thing he knew how to say, and because nobody else there was going to say it.

Beyond the fire, soldiers had laid out chains beside a wagon, in lengths too heavy for one man to carry. Further back, another party was rolling thick timbers off a cart and stacking them beside the machine the king had touched.

Alaric watched until he understood.

They were getting ready to move it.

A second fire opened closer to the torn ground.

"Silas. We need to get down there."

"Not yet." Silas turned to him. "Let me see where those soldiers are going."

"They're getting ready to take the machines. Look at the chains."

"I can see them, Alaric. They haven't moved anything yet."

"That doesn't mean we've got time."

Silas reached across him and pushed a low branch away before it could scrape against his coat.

"We said we'd come to look. You could barely get out of bed this afternoon. I can't take you down there and just hope your leg holds."

Alaric looked back at the chains. Darcy had drawn two circles on a sheet. An orb small enough to carry, behind the thickest armour, near the middle. He could still see the torn place where her quill had pressed too hard.

"I didn't know they were going to clear it all away."

"Neither did I. That doesn't make it safe."

"But we can't just lie here while they —"

"Alaric. Keep your voice down."

It hit harder because Silas didn't raise his own.

Alaric shut his mouth. Wena lifted her head under his arm, and he kept his hand on her neck until she settled again.

The warship's hum filled the space between them. He tried once more.

"If they put them on a ship, how am I supposed to find them?"

Silas looked at the wrecks. Another pair of soldiers lifted a chain.

"I don't know how we'd follow them."

"That's what I'm trying to tell you." His voice was shaking now, though he kept it low. "They're burning everything that ever knew me. I don't know where else to look. Darcy gave me something to find. If it's inside one of those machines, I can't watch them take that too."

"She couldn't promise it would tell you anything."

"I know what she said, Silas."

Silas's eyes came back to him. Heat rushed into Alaric's face. He hadn't meant to sound angry at the one person who might help him.

"I know it might not tell me who I am," he said, quieter. "But what if she's right? What if it could help me, and I never even tried to find it?"

Below them, one length of chain dropped against another.

**KLANG.**

Alaric flinched. The soldiers bent to lift it again.

He'd been asking people for help since the first moment he could remember, sometimes before he'd understood what helping him would cost them. He could see Silas's bandaged right hand held clear of the soil, and the words he'd been about to say changed in his mouth.

"I don't want you getting killed because of me."

Silas didn't move.

"I just don't know how to walk away from it."

Silas looked at him for a long time. Alaric wanted to turn back to the field, but he made himself stay under that look.

At last Silas shifted towards the gap in the branches. "All right. Let me have a look."

Beyond the tents, the king's landing craft rose and turned towards the great opening under the warship. Silas didn't watch it. His eyes moved along the lower edge of the ruin, past a wagon, a length of broken metal, and the bent limbs of another machine.

"We might be able to reach that one."

The wreck lay further to their right, well away from the seamless one the king had touched. Its body had fallen between jointed limbs. One limb stretched towards the bank and ended in a broad plate half buried in the earth. Two work lamps stood beyond it. A separate plate lay nearer the bank, with a cut path between it and the wreck.

"What about the soldiers next to it?"

"I know. There'll be more behind it." Silas let the branch settle against his sleeve. "But see how the ground dips on this side? We can get below the bank and follow it round to that broken plate. I can watch them from there before we try to cross."

"You'll help me get it?"

"I'll try to get you close enough to look inside. That's all I can promise. If I tell you we have to turn back, you listen to me."

Alaric nodded too fast. Silas waited until he held still.

"I mean it. If you spot something, you tell me. You don't go running off while I'm looking the other way."

"I won't. I'll stay with you."

"And if your foot gets worse, you say so. You don't wait until you fall."

Alaric looked down the slope. Without the stick, the first drop would hurt. He'd known it would hurt before he asked.

"All right. I'll tell you."

Silas studied him for one more breath, then eased back from the crest.

---

Redd started sliding down towards the sack.

"Leave it, Redd," Silas said. "I need you to stay here."

Redd stopped with one hand above the strap. He looked at Alaric, then back at Silas. "You'll need a hand with him down there. Look at his foot."

"And I need someone up here with Wena."

"How are you going to help him if he falls? You've only got one good hand."

Silas's face hardened. "I know. And I need to be able to help him without wondering whether you're about to draw your sword again."

Redd pushed himself up on one elbow. For a moment Alaric thought he'd argue loudly enough to carry past the bank.

Instead, Redd looked at the place where his sword had scraped through the grass.

"I'm not going to do that again, Silas."

"I heard you. I'm still not going to risk it."

Redd rubbed at the dirt on his cheek with the heel of his hand. It only spread.

"I know I frightened Alaric."

"You frightened all of us."

Redd's hand dropped. Wena shifted against Alaric's side and turned her head towards him.

"Yes," Redd said at last. "I know I did."

He drew his feet further down the slope and sat back against the roots, the sheathed sword beside his leg.

"I should've left it alone. I know that. But he was *right there*, and everyone just kept getting out of his way —"

His voice stopped.

Alaric remembered the ridge of earth rising under Redd's hand. He couldn't look at those fingers without seeing it.

Redd followed his gaze down to his own hands, then looked up.

"I'm sorry, Alaric."

Alaric wanted to tell him it was all right. Redd had spent two days looking after him. Leaving the apology hanging felt cruel.

But it hadn't been all right.

"I thought we were all going to have to fight," he said. "I didn't know how we were supposed to stop you."

Redd's face changed. Silas looked back at the field.

"I won't put you through that again," Redd said. "I promise."

Alaric nodded. He didn't know if the promise was enough yet. But he'd heard what it cost to make.

Above the trees, the warship began to move. Its light slid across the branches, turning every leaf pale before it passed. The landing craft had vanished into the great hull. Below, soldiers drifted back to the strip where the king had come down and started dragging a wagon into the space he'd left.

The hum drew away slowly. Now Alaric could hear the fires. He hadn't realised how loud they were.

Silas waited until the last sweep of armour had cleared the nearer trees. Then he turned back to Redd.

"Keep Wena down here with you, and have the sack ready. If you see anyone coming up the bank, take her into the trees. Don't wait for us."

Redd's eyes went to Alaric. "What about you two?"

"We'll see them coming up the slope. If you have to go, we'll know which way."

Redd looked at Wena's rope, still tied to the low alder, and ran his fingers over the knot without loosening it.

"All right. I'll keep her here."

Silas nodded once and moved off along the inside of the bank to find a way down.

Alaric sat back carefully. It pulled at his ribs and brought the swollen foot under him. He kept his weight on the other leg until Redd offered an arm. This time he took it.

Redd helped him into the hollow beside the provisions and passed him the water bottle. Alaric drank a little and handed it back. He could hear someone below calling for another lamp.

His ash stick lay where he'd left it.

"Will you keep that here for me?"

Redd glanced at the slope. "You're sure you can manage without it?"

"I think so, if we go slowly."

"Then make him wait if you need to. You've barely got back on your feet."

There was no joke in it at all.

Alaric crouched beside Wena. She pushed her head under his chin and tried to stand when his hand moved towards the ground.

"You stay here with Redd, all right, girl?"

Her paws shifted. He rubbed the rough fur behind her ears. He didn't try to explain where he was going. The warmth under his hand made it harder to stand up and leave her.

Redd settled beside her and laid one hand against her chest. "I've got her. Go on."

Alaric looked at him. Redd met his eyes without reaching for another apology.

"I'll look after her," he said, more quietly.

Alaric let go.

---

They went along the back of the bank before going down. Silas kept low under the thorn, lifting branches with his good hand and holding them until Alaric was past. His right arm stayed close to his body. Where the brush thickened, he turned sideways so the dressing wouldn't catch.

The ground dropped between two exposed roots. Alaric sat on the upper one, lowered his good foot until it found earth, then brought the other down carefully. The bad ankle turned a little under his weight.

He stopped. Silas was below him with one hand raised. Alaric caught the forearm, and Silas braced his boots against the slope and held him until he could stand.

Neither of them spoke. With the warship gone, Alaric could hear his own coat brushing the leaves. That frightened him more than the great ship had. The ship had been beyond his control. This sound was his.

They followed the foot of the bank to the right. Above them, the thorn hid the place where they'd been lying. Ahead, pale light reached across a shallow hollow and stopped against the broken plate Silas had pointed out.

Up close, it was taller than Alaric at its highest edge. The broken metal had folded in on itself. Soil filled its lower curve, and a torn branch lay across one side.

Silas stopped in its shadow. Alaric sank down beside him, breathing through his mouth until the pressure under his ribs eased. He couldn't see Wena or Redd anymore.

Past the plate, a cut path. On its far side, the jointed wreck filled the space between two lamps. One limb had folded backwards under the body, lifting that side clear of the ground. The nearer limb lay stretched out towards them, its broad end resting beside the path.

Soldiers worked on the far side. Their shoulders showed above the metal whenever they stood. A length of chain passed from one pair of hands to another and disappeared behind the wreck.

Smoke drifted into the hollow. Alaric's first breath tasted of wet wood.

The next didn't.

He pulled the coat's collar over his mouth. His stomach clenched, and for several breaths he couldn't do anything but keep the air moving through his nose. He knew whose smoke it was. He knew it and he couldn't stop breathing it.

Silas touched his left elbow. Alaric started shaking his head before the question came, then made himself stop.

"Give me a moment," he whispered.

Silas settled back. Nobody told him the soldiers were working quickly. Nobody mentioned the chains. Alaric waited until he could draw a breath without swallowing against it.

"All right. I can go on."

Silas looked at him until he was sure, then turned back to the path.

A soldier crossed it with a bundle of long wooden wedges, passed the nearer limb and went round the machine. Another followed with a lamp and hung it on a jutting piece of the far side. Its light reached under the wreck, over ground Alaric had thought they'd use.

Silas drew back at once.

For a while they stayed behind the plate while the soldiers worked. A hammer struck wood on the far side, unevenly, stopping whenever someone shouted.

Alaric watched the nearest limb. Its broad end would hide them from the lamp. Between the path and that cover lay only a few paces of trampled stalks.

A few paces.

He looked down at the boot he couldn't fasten properly.

Silas shifted beside him. "Come after me when I go. Keep to this side of the foot."

Alaric nodded. Silas glanced at the soldiers once more, then crossed the path in one low, quick movement and vanished behind the metal.

Alaric followed. His first step landed cleanly. The second sank into a rut hidden under the crushed wheat. Pain shot up his leg, but the ankle held. He shortened the next step instead of trying to match Silas's stride.

Silas's hand came round the edge of the broad foot, caught Alaric's sleeve and pulled him into the dark behind the metal. Alaric went down with one knee on the earth. Every breath pulled under his right shoulder.

Across the path, the broken plate stood empty. He tried not to look at the ground he'd have to cross to get back to it.

Silas leaned close enough that Alaric felt the words against his ear. "Stay here a moment."

Alaric nodded. He looked down and found his wrapped hand resting beside a man's open fingers.

The arm came out from under the machine. Cloth had bunched above the dead wrist, its colour lost under the dirt. The fingertips had pressed shallow marks into the earth, as if he'd been trying to pull himself out.

Nobody had come for this one yet.

Alaric drew his hand back slowly. He kept his eyes on the fingers until Silas touched his sleeve and pointed at the path.

---

The chain came first.

Two soldiers carried its near end between them. Another pair held the middle, and the rest of it dragged behind them from a wagon.

They turned towards the limb hiding Alaric.

Silas pushed him lower.

Alaric pressed his good side into the inner curve of the broad foot, where its edge rose between him and the path. A broken length of the jointed leg slanted above them towards the machine's body. There was room to crouch under it, but not to straighten.

Silas settled behind him. His shoulder touched Alaric's back, then shifted as he found room for the sword at his hip.

The soldiers stopped on the other side of the foot.

"Get it under that joint," someone said. "It'll slip if you put it round the end."

A lamp was set on the outer edge of the foot. Its light reached the bent leg but stopped short of the hollow beneath.

One of the soldiers dropped the chain. The links hit the plate, so loud that Alaric's teeth closed on the inside of his cheek.

**KRRRANK.**

It shivered through the metal against his shoulder.

"Hold it there."

Boots came into the narrow space between the foot and the path. Alaric could see one through the gap by his knee. Mud had dried across the toe. A loose strip of leather hung from the fastening.

The soldier crouched and reached a hooked pole under the joint. Alaric watched the iron point pass through the strip of light beyond his boot. It caught a link on the ground and dragged it closer.

The chain came with it.

Silas's hand closed round Alaric's forearm and pulled him backwards, and Alaric understood before Silas pulled harder: his foot. He had to move his foot.

He drew the bad foot back and held it a finger's width above the earth. Pain tightened behind the ankle. The chain scraped through the place where his boot had been and snagged on a ridge under the broken joint.

The soldier pulled harder. Metal jerked beside Alaric's leg, and his foot hit the ground.

Pain drove up through the ankle so suddenly that his mouth fell open.

No sound came out.

Silas's grip tightened. He held Alaric's arm against his own body while the first spasm passed.

Alaric couldn't get enough air.

The soldier outside shifted closer. His knee appeared where the boot had been. Alaric stared at the worn cloth over it. One loose thread moved every time the leg shifted.

He could have reached out and touched it.

The thought drove his heartbeat up into his head.

**THOOM.**

He waited for the soldier to turn the lamp.

**THOOM.**

The hook pulled again. The trapped link came free.

"There. Got it."

The chain slid under the joint and rose towards the hands waiting above, links passing the gap one after another, drawing a trembling line of light across the ground.

The knee withdrew. Alaric kept waiting.

"Wait, don't pull yet," the soldier said. "My hand's still in there."

A pause. Then the chain moved again, slower.

The soldier stood. The lamp lifted off the edge of the foot, and its light followed his boots round the end of the limb.

Darkness came back under the joint. Silas didn't let go.

On the far side, a fastening snapped shut. The soldiers tested it twice, then carried the loose end back towards the wagon.

"Bring those timbers over here! We need this side up before we can get them underneath."

An answer came from beyond the wreck. Alaric listened to the boots go, following the sound as far as the path, then towards the stacked wood.

Only when the nearest voices had moved off did Silas loosen his hand.

Alaric's fingers were still knotted in Silas's sleeve. He made them open.

"Was that your ankle?" Silas breathed.

Alaric nodded.

"Did it make it worse?"

He couldn't tell. The pain filled too much of his leg to separate one part from another.

"I can't tell yet."

Silas looked at the path. "We can still turn back."

Alaric shut his eyes. His first answer would have been a lie. He'd have said it was nothing, that he could walk perfectly well, that Silas shouldn't have asked.

Silas waited.

"Just give me a moment," Alaric whispered. "Please. I'm not ready to go back."

Silas settled his shoulder against the metal. Alaric let himself breathe.

The chain lay slack beneath the joint beside them now. He could see where its last few links turned towards the soldiers getting ready to haul on it.

Any one of them could come back to check it.

---

Silas checked the far side before he helped Alaric up.

There wasn't room to stand under the broken leg. They backed out a little way, still behind the broad foot, and Silas braced himself while Alaric put his weight on the good leg. The other boot touched down. Alaric waited until he could bear it, then nodded.

They moved along the inside of the fallen limb towards the machine's body.

The metal changed as they got closer. What had looked smooth from the bank was dented in places, gouged in others. Dirt filled long scratches, but the silver beneath them held the light without a speck of rust.

The limb ran into a great rounded joint. Beyond it, the lower edge of the body had split open.

Alaric stopped.

A flap of outer armour had been driven inwards. It rested against a second curved surface inside the wreck, leaving a gap where the two met. The work lamp on the far side shone through another break above it, and something curved caught the light inside.

He leaned in before Silas could touch him.

"Wait," Silas whispered.

"I think there's something in there."

"All right. Let me get a look before you go any closer."

The firmness in it made Alaric draw back. He'd promised.

Silas lowered himself beside the split armour. Alaric knelt on one knee by his left shoulder with the swollen foot stretched out behind him.

Inside the broken shell sat a smaller enclosure, held between thick metal braces. Its front had a narrow slot, barely wide enough for two fingers together.

Beyond the slot, light rested on a clear, curved surface.

A dark centre hung inside it.

Alaric forgot the soldiers moving timber on the far side. He saw Darcy's quill darkening the smaller circle. He could hear the scrape as its point tore the paper.

"There," he whispered. "Can you see it?"

Silas followed his look. "Is that the orb she meant?"

Alaric nodded. His throat had closed too tight for anything else.

Only part of it showed through the slot. The curve he could see was smooth, and the dark centre was solid, not crumbled into powder. He couldn't see the back.

It looked big enough to fill both hands. He looked down at the cloth round his own, and the dirt pressed into its folds.

For the first time, he could imagine carrying it.

Silas laid his good hand on the enclosure. He felt along the edge of the slot, then lower, where the bent outer plate pressed against a jutting fastening.

Nothing moved.

"Can you see a way to get it out?"

"Give me a moment. I can't see a way in yet." Silas moved closer to the metal, his burned hand held against his chest.

Alaric studied the surface above the slot. There were joins, but no handle. A narrow line ran down one side and disappeared under the folded armour. Near its lower end, a small round head stuck out from a bent strip of metal.

He pointed. Silas pressed it, then tried to turn it. It didn't move.

Alaric leaned towards the slot again. He was close enough now to see how thick the clear shell was. No light came from its centre. Nothing stirred inside it. It was only an object, held inside something neither of them understood.

Someone called out on the far side of the machine. Silas froze.

Alaric held his breath until the footsteps faded towards the wagon. A timber hit the ground. Then another.

Silas waited through the second thud before easing the stolen sword out of its sheath with his left hand. He set its point against the narrow join and worked it in gently. The blade went only a little way before it met metal beneath. He tried another angle, then drew it out.

"Do you think there's a lock somewhere?" Alaric whispered.

"Could be. Or we're trying to open the wrong part." Silas pointed at the enclosure with the tip of the blade. "The whole thing might lift out, but I can't see what's holding it at the back."

"Could we break that edge off?"

"Doubt it. Let me see if I can shift it."

Silas laid the sword by his knee and pressed his left palm under the jutting edge. He set his weight carefully, keeping the burned hand clear, and lifted.

The enclosure didn't budge. Silas stopped before the effort could turn into noise.

Alaric had been watching the orb the whole time. Every time Silas moved, a reflection slid across its shell: white cloth, a dark sleeve, a narrow sliver of the lamp beyond the wreck. Nothing on its surface told him whether he'd chosen the right machine.

He hadn't expected the not-knowing to still be there once he'd found it.

"What if it's cracked at the back?"

Silas looked at him.

"We can't see that side," Alaric said. "What if we've come all this way and it's no good?"

"We won't know until we get it out where we can see it."

"But *can* we get it out?"

Silas's jaw tightened. He looked at the bent fastening, then at the place where the outer armour had folded against the enclosure.

"I don't know how to open it, Alaric. I'm trying to work it out."

Alaric stared at him, then back at the slot. He had no better idea.

Outside, the chain scraped across the earth. They both went still.

A few links rose beyond the broken limb, then dropped again as a soldier shouted towards the wagon. They were straightening it for the pull.

Alaric looked back through the slot.

They'd reached it. He'd done what Darcy asked. He'd remembered the shape. He'd followed Silas. He'd held still while a soldier knelt close enough to hear him breathe. He'd lain on a bank and watched Gerolt tipped into a fire and hadn't made a sound.

And now a plate of metal stood between his hands and the thing he'd come for.

"Please don't tell me we have to leave it here." His voice had gone thin. He hated how young it sounded.

Silas picked up the sword. "I haven't given up on it yet."

"Is there anything I can do?"

Silas studied the place where the bent plate met the housing. His mouth had drawn tight under the scar.

"Give me a little room. I want to try this side."

Alaric moved back as Silas shifted towards the damaged edge.

A hammer struck wood beyond the machine.

Alaric rested his left hand beside the slot. The light caught the bandage and laid its pale reflection over the orb, and in the middle of the white cloth there was a small dark stain where the token had cut through.

He could see his fingers there.

He couldn't put them round it.
