# The Voices We Follow

Redd knocked the heel of bread against the wall. A small piece came away from the stone.

He looked at both.

"I'm going to need a knife."

"You've got one," Silas said.

"I was hoping yours was sharper."

Silas passed it over without looking up from the water skins. They were sitting under a window that had melted into one dark fold of glass, several hours west of Foramen. The sun had reached the far wall but not their patch of shade yet. Alaric could still smell burnt reeds in his cloak.

His sword lay against his pack. He'd cleaned it in Foramen, and again at their first stop, with sand and a scrap of cloth, until Silas showed him how to keep the grit out of the sheath. Even with the blade covered, he'd sat down where the hilt wouldn't touch his leg.

Redd sawed through the bread and held out the bigger piece.

"Take that before he decides we've finished resting."

"I'm the one who told you to eat," Silas said.

"Yes, but you've been ready to leave since we sat down."

Silas stopped arranging the skins. He leaned back against the wall, stretched his legs out and closed his eyes.

Redd nodded, satisfied. "There. You look almost bearable."

Alaric bit into the bread. It was better inside than the crust had promised.

Wena left Redd and came to sit in front of him.

"You've had yours," Alaric told her.

She laid her chin on his knee.

Redd cut himself another piece. "She doesn't believe you."

"You fed her."

"I know. I wouldn't believe either of us."

Alaric broke off a little bread. Wena took it carefully, then leaned in harder. He rubbed the place under her ear until her weight started pushing his leg sideways.

Silas opened one eye. "Don't give her the rest. She'll have you walking on an empty stomach."

"I wasn't going to."

"Keep telling yourself that."

Redd put his own piece down to pour water into the tin dish from his sack. By the time he'd put the skin down, the bread was gone.

"Oh, you *rotten* girl."

She chewed with her head low, watching him.

Alaric laughed. It surprised him so much that he tried to stop. Redd pointed the knife at him.

"You were meant to be holding her."

"You took the rope."

"I feed her, and this is the thanks I get."

Silas split his own bread and tossed half into Redd's lap.

"Eat it this time."

Redd brushed the grit off the crust and held it up against his chest while Wena drank.

"When we get to Favale, I'm ordering something that needs a plate. A proper one. None of this eating off my trousers."

"With what money?" Silas asked.

"We'll have saved a fortune on inns by then."

"I'll have saved it."

"Well, I'm glad we've sorted out who's paying." Redd turned to Alaric before Silas could answer. "There was a stall in Kelmend that sold little pies. Meat and onion, edges folded over so you could carry them. You had to bite a hole and let the steam out first, or you'd take the skin off your mouth."

"The one behind the stables?" Silas asked.

"The woman with the red awning."

"She watered the filling."

"I didn't say they were good pies. I said I remembered them. Favale's bound to have better ones."

Alaric tried to picture the folded pastry. "Did you buy them often?"

"When I had coin. When I didn't, I'd walk down a different street so I wouldn't smell them." Redd picked a seed out of his teeth. "Usually went past anyway. What would you have, Al?"

Alaric had been about to finish the last of his bread.

"I don't know."

"You can have more than one thing."

"No, I mean I don't know what I like."

Redd studied him. Then he reached over and took the last piece of bread back before Alaric could give it to Wena.

"Well, don't judge anything by this."

"I liked the food at Marta's."

"Then you'll be all right," Silas said. "Most places sell something you'll recognise. Ask what's in it before Redd orders for you."

"Why?"

"Because he's already spent money he hasn't got."

Redd finished the stolen piece. "You could help by telling us where to eat."

"There's a place near the western market. Depends whether it's still there."

"Has it got chairs?"

"It had chairs."

Redd settled himself more comfortably against the wall. "I'm going to sit in one until somebody complains."

They stayed until the sunlight reached Silas's boots, while the other two argued about whether the woman under the red awning had owned a dog or an extremely ugly cat. Redd was sure it had bitten him. Silas remembered feeding it fish.

When they got up, Alaric buckled the sword where Silas had taught him to wear it. Silas looked back along their tracks, east, the way he'd done at every stop since Foramen. Then he turned west.

Beyond the wall, a pale street ran on between windows sealed with glass.

---

The buildings thinned after midday. Long stretches of sand lay between one standing wall and the next, and the line Kurdag had described became something Silas had to stop and search for.

Silas went first, Redd behind him with Wena, and Alaric kept watch on the ground they'd already crossed. At every turn, one of them waited until the others could see which way to go.

They passed a broken tower. Its top half had fallen through an arch, and a wedge of masonry hung jammed between two columns. Redd refused to walk under it, so they went round through the remains of several smaller rooms.

Alaric caught himself guessing what they'd been for. A shallow shelf might have held cups. The round hollow under it might have been a hearth. Even the gaps where the doors had hung were empty.

"Watch where you're putting your feet," Silas said.

Alaric stepped over a crack a second before Wena put her nose into it. Below lay another floor, so far down the light barely reached it.

He took her rope from Redd until they were past it.

"Have you been this way before?" he asked Silas.

"No. I kept further out." Silas pointed across the pale ground. "Last time I crossed, there were farms within a day's walk. You could turn back if you had to."

"And here?"

"Here I'm following Kurdag."

Redd wiped the back of his neck. "I'd like to follow him somewhere with trees next."

Silas stopped on the edge of an old foundation. Through the dust ahead, two dark heights stood up above everything else. Alaric took them for cliffs, though their tops were too blurred to make out.

"Those might be the figures," Silas said. "We'll check from higher ground. And if you hear anyone, tell me before you answer. Even if you think it's one of us."

Redd stopped wiping his neck.

"You could've mentioned that over breakfast."

"You've asked me twice whether there are people ahead."

"I heard talking."

"That's why I'm mentioning it now."

Alaric listened. Wind dragged grains of sand across the bare foundations. Somewhere to their left, a thin sound rose and fell, like someone trying to speak through a closed door. It came apart when he turned towards it.

"I can hear something," he said.

Silas waited while he tried to describe it.

"I can't make out any words."

"Good. Don't go looking for them."

They went on along the darker sand. When Alaric looked back for the crack in the floor, he couldn't tell it from any of the other broken rooms.

An hour later, Redd caught Silas's sleeve.

"We've gone round."

The tower stood ahead of them.

Its broken crown still hung inside the arch, above the smaller rooms they'd walked through.

Silas turned slowly, checking the sun and the buildings behind them.

"We might have curved south."

"We went past that side," Redd said. "I remember the crack. Wena nearly put her head in it."

Alaric had Wena beside him now. He could remember taking the rope. He could remember every place they'd turned, and none of them had been big enough to bring them back here.

"I don't think we went round," he said.

"Then how's it there?" Redd asked.

Alaric didn't have an answer.

Grit shifted under Silas's boot.

He stepped back at once and put an arm across Redd. A narrow line had opened in the sand in front of them. It widened, filling itself as fast as it opened, and the dark grains started moving sideways.

**Krrrrrrm.**

The sound came from under the road. Alaric pulled Wena up onto the foundation as the wall opposite them shuddered. Sand piled against its base, gathered, and began carrying it towards the tower.

The wall stayed upright.

One window slid past another. Alaric watched a corner turn through ground he'd almost stepped on. There was no wind strong enough to move it. The dust was even drifting the other way.

Redd crouched beside Wena.

"Silas."

"Stay on the stone."

"I *am* on the fucking stone."

The current widened until it filled the street. Chunks of paving rolled through it, vanished, and turned up further along. One struck the foundation under Alaric with a noise like a hammer on a door.

They waited. The moving wall passed the tower and slowly turned away. By the time the sand settled, the foundation's shadow had stretched across their boots.

Silas tested the ground with a loose stone before he stepped down. Redd followed him, then stopped.

"Wait. Where's —"

He looked back at Alaric.

Wena pulled towards him. Redd counted them under his breath, a finger for each man, then lowered the last one towards the dog.

"What is it?" Alaric asked.

"I thought someone was behind you."

"There isn't anyone."

"I can see that." Redd looked past him again. "I know. Come on."

He took three steps before he looked back one more time.

---

By late afternoon, the tower had gone behind a curtain of blowing sand. Silas didn't let more than a few paces open between them any more.

They found the dark ground again along a row of sealed windows. It led to a low building with most of its roof still on. Silas was checking the entrance when sand spilled over the sill from inside.

He backed off. A pale mound rose through the doorway, folding over itself until the lower half of the opening had gone.

Redd watched it fill.

"We're not sleeping in that."

"No," said Silas.

Further along, broken stairs climbed the face of a taller building to a doorway cut high in a windowless wall.

Redd had already seen it. "Up there. The floor's above the sand."

The stair had lost its top section. From the last landing, a gap nearly two paces wide separated them from the doorway, over a drop taller than Silas. Underneath the landing lay two planks joined with wooden crosspieces, dragged in out of the weather. The cut ends still showed fresh grain. Whoever had left them hadn't built this city.

Silas pulled them out and put his weight on the middle. "People have used this."

He and Redd slid the far end onto the sill. Silas crossed first, testing every step, and disappeared inside.

"Pass the packs over. One at a time."

Wena hated the crossing. She planted her front feet on the landing while Redd knelt in the doorway calling her. Alaric put one foot on the boards, saw the ground a long way down through the split between them, and understood her.

"Come on," he said quietly. "I'm coming with you."

Halfway over, Wena decided reaching Redd was better than staying where she was. Her claws scrabbled on the wood, and Alaric stumbled after her and caught the doorframe with his free hand.

Redd steadied him by the elbow. "She's been waiting all day to do that."

The room inside was wider than Redd's cottage had been, with a stone bench along one wall. A few blackened sticks and an empty cloth wrapper marked where someone had camped before.

Silas checked the ceiling while Alaric went to a window at the back, low enough to step through. It looked out over a roof that had fallen against the building and sloped down to the sand. Several holes broke the slope, but a strip along the wall was still whole.

"We could get down this way," he said.

Silas came and tested the stone under the window with his heel. "If we need to. Mind those gaps."

Outside, the light was turning brown.

They pulled the planks inside. A fallen length of stone shelf lay near the entrance, and Redd and Silas levered it close enough to the frame to push across the bottom of the doorway if they needed to.

Alaric spread a blanket by the bench and looped Wena's rope round a stone support, where she could reach their bedding but not either opening. He kept the satchel across his body.

They ate without a fire while sand rasped against the wall below.

Redd took his cup but didn't drink.

"Yes?" he said, towards the doorway.

Silas lowered the skin. "What did you hear?"

Redd looked at him as though he'd interrupted another conversation. "I thought you said something."

"I didn't."

"All right."

"What did it say?"

Redd rubbed the heel of his boot against the floor. "Something about coming in. I couldn't hear it properly."

Alaric listened. A murmur reached him from the window behind them. He thought it might have been his name, but when he tried to pick it out of the wind there was only sand scraping over stone.

"I'm hearing things too," he told Redd.

"I'm tired. That's all it is." Redd drank too fast and coughed into his sleeve. "I haven't slept since we got to that damned city."

Silas took the cup off him. "Lie down for a bit. I'll watch the front."

"What about you?"

"Alaric can take over later."

Redd lay down beside Wena. For a while he scratched behind her ears with the slow movement that usually put her to sleep. Then his hand stopped, and she lifted her head to push it back underneath.

Alaric sat by the rear window. Beyond the sloping roof, a courtyard was filling slowly with shadow.

The courtyard seemed further down than it had been.

He leaned back and looked at the sill instead. His boots were where they'd been. He kept his eyes on the stone and waited until the falling feeling passed.

"All right?" Silas asked.

"I think so." Alaric told him what had happened.

"Sit down to watch. Don't lean out."

He sat. Across the room, Redd had turned his face away from Wena and towards the door.

The last of the daylight left the ceiling.

---

The first figure passed under the broken stair without raising its head.

Silas beckoned from the doorway, and Alaric came far enough from the rear window to see down into the street.

For a few steps it looked like a person bent under a heavy load. Its back curved forward, and one arm dragged almost to the ground. Then it turned under what was left of the light, and Alaric saw there wasn't any load. Its shoulders had grown up round the side of its head. Plates of pale, ridged stuff overlapped across its back, with dark skin stretched tight between them.

One foot came down heel first. The other folded up underneath it and unfolded again with the next step.

Alaric drew back from the opening. "What is that?"

"I don't know," Silas whispered. "Stay back."

A second one appeared beyond the first, moving on three limbs with the fourth pressed to its chest. Neither came towards the stair. Alaric watched them go behind a fallen wall and went back to his window.

Wena was on her feet. Her nose was working towards the doorway, and a low growl went through her chest.

Alaric knelt by her. "Easy. Stay here."

He rested a hand on her back. The growl went on, quieter, while her ears followed something he couldn't hear.

Behind him, wood scraped on stone.

Redd was lifting the planks.

"What are you doing?" Alaric asked.

"He can't find the way up." Redd was pushing the far end towards the gap beside the shelf. "Help me get this out."

Silas turned from the door. "Put it down."

"Only for a moment. He's down there."

"There's nobody down there you know."

Redd's face sharpened with impatience. "I heard him."

Alaric grabbed the nearer crosspiece before Redd could slide the planks any further. They were heavier than he'd expected. He had to brace them against his thigh.

"Who did you hear?"

"My father."

Redd said it plainly, still trying to shift the wood. Alaric nearly looked outside for the man before he remembered the cottage, the sword, and what Redd had said in Foramen about his village.

"Redd, you said —"

"I know what I said. I can hear him now." Redd dragged the planks sideways. "He's telling me to come in. He always calls when it gets dark."

Silas reached across and took hold of the far end. "Listen to me. I need you away from that door."

"He doesn't know where I am."

"Redd."

"Let me answer him."

He pulled. The planks struck the stone shelf with a sharp crack, and Wena barked.

Below them, something hit the bottom of the stair.

Silas let go of the wood and drew his sword. "Get him back."

Alaric set his end down rather than let it drop. Redd twisted past him and pushed into the gap beside the shelf, leaning out over the sill, looking from one patch of dark to the next.

"I'm up here!" he called. "Da, I'm up here!"

Alaric caught hold of his coat. "You can't go out there."

"I'm not going anywhere. I'm *showing* him."

"Please come back."

Redd tried to prise Alaric's hand off. He kept leaning out, frustrated by the cloth holding him back from the edge.

"He's waiting for me. He'll think I've gone down to the stream again."

A pale hand came up out of the dark and closed over the sill.

The fingers were long enough to curl over the stone and reach the shelf. They found Redd's arm on the way and dragged across it, and Redd made a sound Alaric had never heard from him, high and surprised, like a boy's. Under the fingers, a forearm was lengthening out of the dark, its skin pulled tight between the ridges.

Silas struck.

The blade bit into the arm, but the fingers held on. He put one boot against the shelf and brought the sword down again.

Alaric hauled on Redd's coat. Redd turned towards the noise, his heel caught the planks, and both of them lurched sideways into the doorframe. Alaric hit it shoulder first. His right foot slid over the sill into nothing.

**THOOM.**

His heartbeat hammered inside his skull. The street was under his boot. He could feel the satchel dragging at him, the cold weight at his side, and Redd's coat starting to slide out of his fingers.

"Redd. Help me."

Redd looked down.

Alaric couldn't find the floor with his foot.

"*Please.*"

Redd grabbed the front of his coat with his bleeding arm and threw himself backwards, pulling so hard that Alaric's knee cracked against the sill. Alaric caught the shelf and dragged himself back through the gap, and they went down together on top of the planks.

Fire flashed over them.

Silas had pressed his free palm to the gripping fingers. The stink of burning skin filled the room. The fingers opened, and he kicked the arm clear of the sill. Something hit the lower landing with a heavy, lopsided thud.

"The stone," Silas said. "Help me."

Alaric got up. He and Silas shoved the shelf across the gap while Redd dragged the planks out from under their feet. Stone scraped on stone. The slab settled across the bottom of the doorway, leaving only the opening above it.

Silas stood behind it with his sword ready. "Back to the window, Alaric."

Wena was fighting the rope. Alaric caught her collar, made himself check the knot, and crossed to the rear opening. The sloping roof was empty. He stared at it until he could make out its broken edges again.

Behind him, Redd was sitting on the floor, holding his arm against his chest.

"He's still there."

Alaric turned. Redd was looking past Silas, at the doorway.

"I know it can't be him. I know that." His voice cracked. "So why does it sound like him?"

Silas didn't lower his sword. "Sit by Wena. Where I can see you."

Redd went on his hands and knees to the blanket and sat against the bench with his father's sword lying crooked across his legs. His sleeve had torn open from elbow to wrist. Under it were four dark lines, close together, filling faster than Alaric liked.

"Silas," Alaric said.

Silas looked, once. "Tear a strip off the blanket. The clean end."

Alaric tore it. Silas kept the doorway and talked him through the rest without turning round: a little water on the cloth, and no more than that; press, don't wipe; bind it firm, but not so tight the hand goes cold. Redd sat and let Alaric do it. He didn't make a single joke.

When it was done, Redd pulled what was left of his sleeve down over the bandage and held it there.

Outside, claws scratched at the wall under the sill.

They didn't stop for a long time.

Once the scraping climbed almost to the doorway, and Silas drove his sword into the dark above the shelf. A fragment of pale stuff fell inside. He hooked it on the point and flicked it back out.

Alaric kept his own sword across his knees at the rear window. Every time the scratching stopped, he expected something to move on the roof below.

Wena lay against Redd's legs, watching the front. When he stopped stroking her, she pushed her nose under his good wrist until he started again.

Alaric's knee throbbed where it had hit the sill. He could bend it. He left it alone.

A footstep sounded behind him.

He turned towards the bench.

"What?" Silas asked at once.

"I thought someone moved."

Redd and Wena were still on the blanket. Nothing stood between them and the wall.

Silas was watching the same empty space.

"I keep hearing it too," he said.

"From there?"

"Sometimes. Sometimes right behind me." He shifted until one shoulder rested against the doorframe. "If you hear it, check. Don't follow it."

"Were there voices when you crossed before?"

"Once. I got out before they got this clear."

Redd lifted his head. "Could we leave now?"

Silas glanced at Alaric's window. Alaric looked out again. Past the roof he couldn't make out the dark sand or the sealed windows, or anything else.

"I can't see where we'd go."

"Then we stay," Silas said. "Until we can."

Redd pressed his forehead against Wena's head. She put up with it for a little while, then shifted until he was leaning on her neck instead.

---

Later, when the scratching had moved away, Silas gave Alaric the front and took the rear window himself. Redd dragged his blanket over towards him, bringing Wena as far as the rope allowed. Alaric heard water being poured and Redd refusing it.

"Have a little," Silas said.

"I'm not thirsty."

"Drink it anyway."

Redd took the cup. Afterwards he held it between his knees and forgot to give it back.

"Can you hear what he's saying?" he asked.

Silas waited before he answered. "No."

"It's the same thing. Every time."

Alaric looked back from the door. Redd had his head turned a little, as if he was trying to hear through someone else's conversation.

"Redd," Silas said. "Do you want me to keep talking?"

Redd nodded.

Silas started telling him about a leak he remembered over Marta's kitchen.

Alaric heard him choose it. There was the smallest pause first, the kind a man makes when he's picking which door to open. Then Silas went through it and kept his voice exactly the same.

Redd interrupted to say the leak was still there. Marta had paid a man to mend it twice, but the water only came through a different cupboard. Silas knew the man. He asked whether he still brought his youngest boy along to hold the ladder.

"His youngest boy's got children now," Redd whispered. "It's the grandson holds it."

"Gods. I've been away a while."

"She puts a bowl under it," said Redd. "Then another one. Then she swears at the ceiling."

"She'll have run out of bowls by now," Silas said.

He talked about her as if they'd see her next week. Her bowls. Her swearing. The cupboard nobody was allowed to open without asking. He didn't say anything about what had been done to her. Alaric stood at the door with Liluth's voice in his head, *your innkeeper didn't make a sound*, and listened to Silas keep Marta whole for Redd, one cupboard at a time.

Now and then Redd asked him to say something again, though Alaric had heard it clearly from the door.

Wena fell asleep across both their feet.

Alaric let their voices carry him. The picture of Marta's kitchen was easy to hold. It stayed with him when the murmur outside the door tried to turn itself into words.

Then, under the murmur, there was another voice.

A woman's.

It wasn't saying anything he could hear as words. It had the shape of something said close to him, quietly, the way you'd speak to someone in the same room. He knew it. He didn't know how. It belonged somewhere with lamplight in it.

It frightened him more than the claws had.

"What?" Silas said from the window. He must have heard Alaric's breath change.

"A woman."

"Anyone you know?"

Alaric opened his mouth to say no.

"Don't answer her," Silas said.

"I wasn't going to."

It was true. It cost him more than it should have, and he couldn't have said why.

Later, Redd took the rear window and Silas went back to the front. Redd called out whenever he wasn't sure of what he was hearing, but he stayed beside Wena. Alaric slept in short, cramped stretches until it was his turn again. Every time he woke, both openings were still being watched.

---

Redd tried to make a joke while they packed.

"Well. Next time we pay for a room, I'll…"

He folded the blanket again, though he'd already folded it. Alaric waited for the rest. Redd pushed it into his sack without finishing.

Daylight lay across the outer landing. The claw marks under the doorway were deep enough to hold thin lines of shadow. None of the creatures were in sight, but Redd still turned towards the street every time the wind dropped. His sleeve stayed pulled down to the knuckles.

Silas shared out the last of the bread. He put Redd's portion beside his sack and went on sorting the water.

"Eat before we go."

"I'm not going to do it again."

"I'm asking you to eat."

Redd picked up the bread.

"And let me see the arm."

"It's fine."

"Then it'll be quick."

Redd held it out. Silas unwound the cloth, looked at the four lines, which had closed dark and angry overnight, smelled them, and bound them up again with the other end of the strip. He tied it off with a double knot, the way Marta tied everything.

Alaric went to untie Wena from the support. She'd pulled the loop tight in the night, and he had to work at it. Redd watched him struggle for a while, then leaned over.

"Push that bit back through first."

Alaric tried. The knot loosened a little.

"Like this?"

Redd took it off him and showed him. Their fingers were clumsy with cold. Wena waited until they'd finished, then shook sand over both of them.

"Thanks," Alaric said, wiping his mouth on his sleeve.

Redd coiled the rope. He didn't offer to hand it back straight away.

Silas passed him the smallest of the full skins and took the rest himself.

"Tell us when you hear it. Don't wait till you're sure."

"I was sure."

"I know."

They left by the rear window. A faint line of footprints crossed the sand beyond the roof, too far off to tell what had made them. Wena needed lifting over the sill, but once she was on the slope she picked her way down more steadily than any of them.

They left the planks inside for whoever came next.

For the first hour, Redd flinched at sounds Alaric barely noticed. Once he stopped and screwed his eyes shut so hard his whole face creased. Silas waited beside him. When Redd could look ahead again, they went on more slowly.

Alaric started saying which building he was heading for before he moved. It felt stupid to name a wall everyone could see, until Silas asked him to point out a doorway again and they realised they'd been looking at two different places. After that he walked between them. He still needed Silas to choose the way. When the sand changed colour under a shadow, Alaric would have followed the wrong strip if Silas hadn't stopped him.

By midday, the ruins had opened out into low foundations spread far apart. The wind blew freely across them. It had been a while since Redd had stopped to answer anything the others couldn't hear.

They rested under an overhanging wall. Silas pulled his boots off to empty them, and Wena lay on her side with her paws stretched into the shade.

Redd sat down next to Alaric.

"He used to call twice," he said.

Alaric waited.

"My father. The first time meant he wanted me home. The second time meant he'd already come looking." Redd rubbed at a patch of dust on his good sleeve. "I'd go down to the stream after he'd told me not to. Come back with my boots full of mud and swear I'd been somewhere else."

"Did he believe you?"

"Course he didn't. He washed the boots."

A little air came out of Redd that was nearly a laugh. He looked across the open ground.

"Last night I kept thinking he hadn't called the second time yet. That I could still get out before he started worrying."

"Are you still hearing him?"

"Sometimes." Redd hesitated. "Not right now."

Silas pulled his boot back on. "We'll rest a bit longer."

Redd started to argue, then leaned back against the wall instead. Wena opened one eye when his boot touched her paw. He moved it, and she went back to sleep.

Alaric watched the road while the other two rested. The distant heights came and went through the drifting dust. However long he looked, he couldn't make them into people.

---

They reached the first stones under the heights late that afternoon, at the end of their second day out of Foramen.

At first Alaric thought the wind had uncovered the face of a cliff. Dark bands crossed it in long folds that ran down into the sand. Broken houses huddled under the lowest ridge, their empty windows hardly bigger than the marks on its surface.

He followed the folds up.

A hand hung above them.

Three fingers were left. The shortest was longer than the room where they'd spent the night. Their tips rested against a carved thigh, and higher still an arm joined a shoulder so broad a fallen tower could have lain along it.

Alaric stopped walking.

The head came out of the thinning dust slowly. Most of the face had broken away. One side kept a cheek, the hollow under a brow, and an ear worn almost smooth. It faced west, towards the sinking sun.

Across the avenue, the second figure faced east.

That one had lost both arms. A deep split ran from its shoulder down through the carved clothing and into the rock under its feet. Its head was whole enough for Alaric to see it had once worn something on its brow. Whatever it had been had gone, and left a rough ring of broken stone.

Neither figure stood on a pedestal. Alaric could follow one unbroken seam from a stone beside his boot all the way into the nearer figure's heel.

Someone had carved people out of the land itself.

Redd stood with his sack hanging off one shoulder.

"He could've said they were this big."

Silas shaded his eyes and looked up. "Would you have believed him?"

Redd considered the surviving hand. "Probably not."

Alaric tried to picture someone working up there. A person on a rope, a small tool tapping at the side of a finger. How many times would they have climbed up? How much of a life could vanish into carving something that size?

"Do you think they finished them?" he asked.

Silas studied the broken head. "Can't tell."

"I'd like to have seen them before." Alaric pointed at the missing face, and then found he meant the whole avenue. "All of it."

Redd came and stood beside him. "I'd like to know how they got up to the nose."

"You'd fall off," Silas said.

"I didn't say I wanted to go."

They walked between the figures, through shade that reached right across the avenue. The sand had left pieces of the street showing: the edge of a wide step, a stretch of paving worn hollow by feet that had stopped walking here a long time ago.

Wena sniffed the stone and sneezed.

Alaric laughed under his breath and followed her south.

Past the avenue, the ground looked level. Silas kept them on a dark strip curving round its edge. Halfway along, Redd dropped a small stone onto the paler surface. It went through a thin crust and vanished without hitting anything Alaric could hear.

They gave the edge more room.

A low ridge took them past the heel of the eastern figure. Alaric was still looking up at the two backs when Silas raised a hand.

"Listen."

At first there was only the wind.

Redd turned his head away from the ruins. Alaric watched him, but Silas had knelt beside a line of flat stones.

Under the wind came a thinner sound. It hurried, broke, and hurried again.

Running water.

"There," said Silas. "Under that end."

They followed the sound along the stones. Redd crouched where some of the slabs had sunk and pushed his fingers into the grit.

"The corner's broken. Help me clear it."

Alaric knelt beside him, turning the satchel round behind him, and they scooped the sand away with their hands. Redd worked a jammed piece loose with his knife, one-handed, and Silas lifted it clear.

Water was moving under the opening, no further down than Alaric could reach with his forearm. It ran south along a stone channel, clear over a dark bottom.

Alaric reached for an empty skin.

"Let me check it first," Silas said.

He filled the tin dish, held it to the light and smelled it. He took a small taste, set the dish down, and waited while the other two finished clearing the hole. Alaric watched him more closely than he watched the water.

"Does it seem all right?"

"It seems all right." Silas took a longer drink. "Same channel Kurdag told us about. We fill up here."

Redd sat back on his heels and let his head drop forward.

"Thank fuck."

Wena pushed in between them.

She'd waited through the digging. She'd waited through the tasting. Now she put one front paw on Alaric's leg and tried to reach the hole over his knee.

"All right. Move back a bit."

She didn't.

Redd filled the dish again and carried it into the shade. Wena went after it so fast that Alaric had to catch himself on the slab.

They filled every bottle and skin. Alaric drank slowly at first, then had to make himself stop and breathe. Cold water ran down his chin. He wiped it away and found he was smiling.

When he lifted his share, the weight surprised him.

"Three dry days from here," Silas reminded them. "Keep the full skins out of the sun. We carry what we can and go slow."

Redd tightened the ties on his sack. "I'm not complaining about heavy water."

"Give it an hour."

They rested on a ridge of bare rock in sight of the channel. Nobody suggested leaving straight away. Alaric checked the satchel's fastening, found the core still wrapped, cold and quiet, and leaned back on his pack.

Above the ruins, the two stone figures watched their separate horizons. A strip of evening light found the broken cheek of the one facing west.

Redd sat close enough that Wena could stretch out across the space between them. He'd taken his boots off. Silas watched him tip a small heap of sand out of each one.

"You could've done that when we stopped earlier."

"I was busy imagining chairs."

Alaric looked over at him.

"I think I want one of those pies."

Redd lowered the boot. "The meat ones?"

"Yes. With the folded edges."

"You have to let them cool first. I wasn't joking about that."

"You said to bite a hole first."

Redd nodded. "That's right."

Wena rolled onto her side and pushed her paws against Alaric's leg. He pulled his cloak out from under her, careful not to disturb the satchel.

"You'll have to show me," he said.

"I can do that." Redd looked across at Silas. "Will your place have them?"

"If it doesn't, we'll find somewhere that does."

"With chairs," said Redd.

Silas settled his pack against the stone and sat down properly.

"Yes. With chairs."
