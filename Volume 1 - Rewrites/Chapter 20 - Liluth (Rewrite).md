# Liluth

The blade came free without a sound.

Alaric couldn't see the people of Foramen anymore. He could hear them. A door closing near the cistern. Bare feet crossing sand. The scrape of a metal cover dragged over the last cooking pit.

Five horses came on down the western street.

Their tack clicked softly between the ruined buildings. None of the riders carried a lamp. Moonlight found the pale edges of their armour whenever they passed under a gap in the old channels, then lost them again.

Silas reached Alaric before the riders reached the next arch.

"Keep that point down. Half the people round you can't see where you've put it."

Alaric lowered the sword.

Redd came from the other side with Wena hauling on the rope. "She isn't going inside."

Wena barked at the riders.

"Make her," Silas said.

"You're welcome to explain it to her."

Orieth came back without her daughter. She took the rope up short by Wena's collar and crouched beside her.

"The little one's frightened," she told the dog. "You can frighten everyone else once she's stopped."

Wena went on straining towards the street.

Orieth pulled once. "Come."

The dog held out until another horse set its hoof on loose stone. At the sound, she turned and let Orieth lead her to the council building.

Redd followed as far as the doorway. Orieth shut Wena inside and came back to the threshold before the riders had stopped.

"Stay there," Silas told Redd.

"If you need me —"

"I'll call. Watch the other end of the street."

Kurdag had reached the middle of the western street. He stood alone with his staff resting on one shoulder, and the riders slowed in front of him.

The horse in their centre stopped first.

Alaric knew the woman on it the moment she turned her good side to the moon.

A dark covering lay across the place where Gerolt's sword had taken her left eye. The scar beneath it pulled from her brow across the bridge of her nose and down into her cheek. Pale hair hid some of the damage before it vanished under the raised collar of her armour.

Her left arm stayed close to her body. Metal covered it from shoulder to wrist, newer and less marked than the rest of her armour. The hand hung stiffly by her thigh and never came up to join the other on the reins.

Alaric saw Gerolt's blade go into that bracer again. Heard the bone go under it.

Her one eye found Kurdag.

"Move out of the road."

Kurdag shifted the foot of his staff from stone to sand. "The road ends behind me."

"We didn't come for your settlement."

"Then you can leave it."

The two riders beside her drew apart. One stayed near the broken arch. The other guided his horse towards the row of brown-walled homes along the north side of the street.

Silas watched them without lifting his sword. "They're spreading out."

Alaric saw it now. They weren't surrounding Kurdag. They were putting distance between each other.

The woman's voice came across the dark.

"A human male came into these ruins today. Young. Dark hair. He travels with an older human, another man, and a farm dog."

Redd stepped away from the council doorway.

Silas put an arm across his path. "Not yet."

"You were the one who wanted me inside."

"I still do."

Kurdag didn't turn round. "What has he done?"

"He survived a site under Natharul investigation and fled lawful recovery."

"That isn't a crime."

"It isn't your concern."

"You brought it into my street." Kurdag pointed his staff back towards the outer arch. "That made it my concern."

The rider to the north stopped beside a roof thatched with dry reeds. His horse turned side-on. One of his hands slid under his cloak.

"The survivor is required alive," the woman said. "Give him to us, and nobody here needs to make themselves his accomplice."

No promise came after it.

Her eye moved to Orieth in the council doorway.

"You. Come away from them."

Orieth stayed where she was. "No."

"You'd stand between humans and Natharul?"

"I'm standing between you and my daughter."

"Then you can be judged with the rest of them."

Alaric looked at the roofs round her. One flame would cross three homes before it reached the old stone hall. Past those walls were the garden, the cistern, and every store Foramen needed to stay alive.

The rider by the reeds drew his hand back out of his cloak.

A faint orange glow sat in his palm.

Silas saw it in the same breath. "Kurdag."

The beastman's ears swung towards him.

Alaric stepped past Silas's arm.

"I'm here."

Every mounted head turned.

The orange light vanished into the rider's fist.

Silas caught the back of Alaric's coat and hauled him up short of the open street. "What are you doing?"

"If they search, they'll burn the roofs."

"Walking into the road won't stop that."

"She'll follow me." Alaric kept his voice low. "I only have to keep her away from the houses."

Silas looked at the covering over the woman's left eye.

"And when she reaches you?"

"Gerolt left her a side she can't see."

"That's not enough."

"It's what I have."

Silas didn't let go straight away. The woman nudged her horse forward.

"If the ground moves," Silas said, "don't watch it. Watch her."

His grip opened.

Alaric walked out into the street.

The woman stopped ten paces past Kurdag. Her horse pulled at the reins, unsettled by the smell of so many people hiding close by.

She looked Alaric over, from his boots to the sword in his hands.

"That isn't his blade."

Alaric didn't need to ask whose she meant.

"No."

"Where's the farmer?"

For a heartbeat Gerolt stood in the burning doorway again, sword dragging behind him, while Alaric ran through the wheat.

"He's dead."

The horse sidestepped. She checked it with her right hand.

"Did you see him die?"

"I saw him stay behind."

"After he put a sword through my partner's throat." Her voice stayed level, but the layers in it had stopped moving together. "He struck before a single question had been answered."

Alaric remembered the pale man framed in Gerolt's broken doorway. The sword under his jaw. The wet clicking that came after.

"You tore his door off its hinges," he said.

"We gave him the chance to answer."

"You threatened everyone in the village when he didn't."

She leaned forward a little. Moonlight caught the gold lines on her armour.

"You called his name."

Alaric's stomach knotted.

"You warned him," she said. "My partner would be alive if you'd kept quiet."

"Gerolt would have been dead."

"He *is* dead."

Alaric shifted his grip on the sword. "Then killing everyone here won't change what happened."

"Come with us, and nobody else needs to be involved."

Behind her, the Fire rider opened his hand again.

This time the flame climbed up between his fingers.

Alaric faced him. "He's going to burn the roof."

The rider's arm drew back.

Kurdag struck his staff on the street.

Earth reared up under the horse's front legs. The animal screamed. Its chest hit the sudden ridge and threw its rider across the sand as the flame left his hand.

**WHUMM.**

Fire rolled along the roof.

Water hit it in six narrow streams from six different windows. None of them reached far enough alone. The streams crossed and spread and darkened the reeds, while a wind from somewhere pulled the smoke away from the rooms underneath.

The western end of the roof kept burning.

Foramen answered from every side. Spears came out between the walls. A horn sounded once near the cistern. People ran clay jars up from the covered channel, while others dragged soaked cloth across the nearest doors.

The riders moved.

Wind crossed the street before Alaric saw the elf shaping it. The pressure caught Kurdag across the shoulder and tore the hood off his head. A second stroke followed low, throwing sand in a line towards his legs.

Kurdag drove his staff into the ground. Stone folded up between him and the attack. The wind split against it and hit both sides of the street hard enough to burst shutters out of their frames.

Silas ran past Alaric.

Fire wrapped his right hand and ran along the flat of his sword. He didn't try to meet the next pressure stroke head-on. He threw a short flare at the rider's face, forcing the elf to let go of the spell before it reached the people carrying water.

The Wind rider's horse reared from the flame. He left the saddle before it could throw him and landed hard on one leg, but his sword was up when Silas reached him.

The woman on the centre horse pointed at Alaric.

"Take him."

Water lifted out of two of the clay jars. It crossed the street in a twisting rope and closed round Alaric's sword arm. Cold locked from his wrist to his elbow, and the pull wrenched him sideways.

He planted his feet the way Silas had shown him.

The water dragged both boots through the sand.

Redd struck at it with the flat of his father's sword. The blade went through without any resistance at all. The water closed round the metal and pulled him forward too.

"I *dislike* magic," Redd said through his teeth.

The rider shaping it turned one hand, and the water climbed towards Alaric's throat.

Steel rang under the old channel. Silas forced the Wind rider back onto his bad leg and flicked a thin flare off his sword while the elf recovered. The heat passed close enough to tighten the skin on Alaric's neck. It touched the length of water stretched between him and the rider. Steam burst from the severed line, and the binding fell away.

"*Move!*" Silas shouted.

Alaric moved.

He ran for the open ground beside the old cooking pits. The mounted woman came after him, as he'd hoped she would.

Her horse covered half the distance before he'd taken four strides.

The earth ahead of him split.

He didn't look down.

He watched her.

Her shoulder shifted before the stone rose. Alaric threw himself left. A narrow point of rock burst up out of the street where his chest had been and caught the side of his coat instead. Cloth tore. The force spun him and put him down on one knee.

The horse bore down on him.

Kurdag swept his staff sideways.

The ground under the animal went soft. Both front hooves sank to the joint. The horse pitched forward, and the woman was out of the saddle before its head struck the street.

She landed on her feet.

Kurdag had already turned elsewhere. The other Earth rider had driven a rolling break towards the cistern, splitting the fitted stones in a line. Kurdag laid his staff across it. The crack stopped close enough to slop water over the channel's rim.

He could hold that line, or he could hold the ground under the woman.

Not both.

She drew her sword.

Alaric got up and brought his point up between them.

The first blow nearly took it out of his hands.

He'd seen her move. He'd raised his guard. Neither of those things mattered. Her blade struck his, slid down its length and stopped against the guard, close enough for the edge to touch his knuckles.

She turned her wrist.

His sword went aside.

Her shoulder drove into his chest. He hit the ground on his back, still holding the grip only because both hands had locked round it.

She stepped over him.

"Thousands died around you," she said. "And you stood up and walked away."

Alaric rolled as her sword went into the sand beside his ribs.

He cut at her leg and didn't come near it. She wrenched the blade free and kicked his shoulder, and sent him skidding across the street.

There was no exchange to win here. No movement slow enough to study.

Only distance.

He found his feet and ran further from the houses.

Behind him, wood cracked.

He looked back, despite everything Silas had told him.

The wall beside the council building had pulled away from its supports. A dark gap was widening under the roof, while people crowded through the passage beneath it.

Orieth stood under the failing edge, pushing two children towards the inner room. Past them, Wena stood across the inner doorway with Orieth's daughter behind her, and every bark hit the walls hard enough to reach the street.

Redd got to her.

"The west brace!" Orieth shouted.

"Which one's that?"

"The wall falling on us!"

Redd dropped his sword and pressed one palm towards the ground.

Earth rose under the wall.

It wasn't the small, stubborn support Alaric had watched him fail at on the road. A broad shelf of it broke up through the floor and lifted the whole lower course of brown blocks. The roof lurched upward. Dust poured over Redd's head and shoulders.

He stared at what he'd done.

Orieth grabbed his coat. "Keep it *there*!"

Redd braced with both hands. His elbows shook, but the stone held while the last of the people cleared the passage.

The woman's sword passed through the space where Alaric's head had been.

Someone shouted her name from the western arch.

"Liluth!"

So that was her name.

The Fire rider was back on his feet. Three of Foramen's people held spears between him and the burning house. Blood ran down one side of his forehead, and flame was gathering round both his hands.

"They have children in there!" the Water rider shouted.

Liluth didn't turn.

"Then they should have surrendered him."

The Fire rider let go with both hands.

Kurdag twisted his staff where it lay across the crack by the cistern. Stone closed round the elf's ankle. His flame climbed higher as he fought to tear free. A woman leaned out from the wet roof and emptied a clay jar over his head. Steam swallowed him. The three spears went in through it.

One of them went in under his arm.

The fire went out.

Liluth came at him again.

He met the blade because there was nowhere left to go. Steel struck. His wrists gave before the rest of him understood the weight behind it. His sword was knocked back across his own shoulder, and her edge opened his sleeve above the elbow.

The cut burned. It wasn't deep.

Liluth reversed her grip.

Alaric stepped back. His heel caught the rim of a dead cooking pit, and he went down beside it.

By the western arch, Silas broke away from the Wind rider by throwing fire across the narrow street. The elf's next pressure stroke tore the flames open, but Silas had already turned towards Liluth.

She raised one hand.

The covering over her left eye was facing Silas.

He came at her from that side before she could turn.

His sword hit the new armour on her damaged arm. Fire flashed along the point of contact. The metal blackened but held. Liluth turned too late and caught his second cut on the lower edge of her bracer.

Her arm buckled.

She knew him then. Alaric saw it in her face.

"You." Her eye went over him. "The one in the stolen armour."

Silas didn't answer. He raised his sword.

"Your innkeeper didn't make a sound," Liluth said. "Not one. I had to ask a peat-cutter instead."

For a heartbeat, Silas didn't move at all.

Then his face changed into something Alaric had never seen on it, and he came at her.

Liluth drove her boot into the ground.

A slab of stone rose under Silas and flung him across the cooking pit. He twisted before he landed, shoulder first, sword still in his fist, and he was up again before Alaric had got his feet under him. He didn't say anything. He was already coming back.

"Alaric," he said, very quietly. "Get up."

Liluth turned to Alaric. When she spoke, the layers in her voice had come apart entirely.

"He died because you spoke."

She raised her sword in both hands. The ruined arm shook inside its armour.

"You should have died with him."

Alaric pushed himself upright.

"I didn't."

Liluth came forward.

Kurdag wrenched his staff free of the crack by the cistern. The Earth rider lunged after him. Kurdag caught the spear under one iron band, turned it aside, and drove the heel of his hand into the elf's breastplate. The armour folded inwards. The rider left the ground and hit the broken channel behind him.

Kurdag didn't watch him fall. He was already pointing his staff at Liluth.

The street moved under her.

Not enough to break it. One fitted stone sank as the next one rose under the outside of her boot.

Her weight went towards her damaged side.

She threw out her left arm to stay on her feet.

The blackened bracer pulled away from the plate at her shoulder. A narrow gap opened under her arm, with nothing to protect it but the dark cloth drawn across her ribs.

Silas reached her from the right.

He struck once, at her sword and not her body. The blades met. Liluth turned towards him, her good eye following the greater threat.

The side she couldn't see was facing Alaric.

Her boot scraped for purchase. Silas was holding her sword aside, but the blades were already turning back towards him.

Alaric put his left foot forward.

He chose what the next movement was for.

Then he finished it.

The point went in under Liluth's raised arm.

Cloth resisted first. Then something harder. Alaric drove with both hands and felt the blade force its way between her ribs.

Her body turned round the steel.

Her sword fell off Silas's blade. The point struck stone.

Alaric kept going until his guard met her armour.

Then it was done.

For a moment she stayed upright between him and Silas. Her good eye found Alaric.

She didn't say anything.

The fingers of her ruined hand opened once, beside his shoulder. Then her weight came down the blade and pulled his arms down with it.

He drew the sword out.

The blood came after it.

Liluth dropped onto one knee. Her good hand pressed against the wound, then slipped. The earth under her palm trembled and didn't rise.

She fell on her left side.

Alaric knew what he'd done before she stopped moving.

He'd known before the sword went in.

A pressure stroke split the rim of the cooking pit beside him.

"Alaric!" Silas shouted. "*Move!*"

The fight hadn't ended with her.

Alaric ran.

Silas met the Wind rider near the western arch. Their blades struck inside a whirl of sand, the elf favouring the leg he'd landed on.

The rider looked at Liluth's body.

Silas didn't.

The elf dragged the air back for another cutting stroke. Silas stepped inside it before it could form, caught the rider's blade on his guard, and drove fire-wrapped steel up under his chin.

The wind collapsed outwards. Alaric threw an arm over his face as the sand hit him.

By the cistern, Kurdag lowered the Earth rider down from the broken channel. The armour was still folded in. The body inside it didn't move.

The Water rider stood alone beside her horse.

A ring of shallow water turned round her wrists. Four of Foramen's spears pointed at her from four different streets. Orieth stood behind the nearest one with dust in her cropped hair.

"Drop it," she said.

The rider looked at Liluth.

Her water fell to the road.

She drew her sword with two fingers round the grip and let it drop beside it.

"I surrender."

Nobody lowered a spear.

---

The fire gave up last.

It had got into the reeds above two homes and into one of the old wooden supports before enough water reached it. After the flames were out, the smoke stayed under the open roofs, catching in every throat and turning the covered lamps into pale circles.

Two of the riders' horses had bolted into the dead streets. Three were still in Foramen, shivering while people cut away their broken tack. Liluth's horse stood where Kurdag had let its legs out of the softened road, blood darkening one knee.

Alaric left his sword on the council table and joined the line passing jars. He kept going until someone took the empty one out of his hands and told him the cistern couldn't spare any more.

Only then did he see the musician.

The man lay by the passage to the inner rooms. A wind stroke had opened his coat, and the body underneath it, in one long diagonal line. One of his arms was still curved round a child who wasn't there anymore.

His three-stringed instrument lay under the broken channel. Its wooden face had split near the bottom edge. Two strings were still drawn across it. The third had curled up against the sand.

Alaric stood close enough to see the dark stain spreading under the man's side.

He'd never learned his name.

Someone called for another bandage near the burned house. Alaric picked up the instrument and carried it into the council building.

Wena was waiting just inside the door. Orieth's daughter sat against her chest with both arms buried in the fur round her neck. Wena watched every person who came in. When Alaric crossed the threshold, her tail struck the floor once.

He laid the instrument on the low table.

Redd stood by the lifted wall while two of the residents wedged timber under the shelf of earth he'd raised. The dust had turned his hair grey. He kept looking down at his hands whenever he thought nobody was watching.

Orieth came in from the street with blood on one sleeve that didn't seem to be hers.

"You can let it go now," she told him.

Redd lowered his palms.

The broad shelf stayed where it was.

He looked at her. "Was it supposed to do that?"

"You're asking *me*?"

"You sounded confident."

"I was hoping you knew." She went to her daughter and checked the child's face and arms. "At least it's still up."

Redd picked his father's sword up off the floor. He wiped the dust from the hilt and said nothing more about the earth.

Silas found Alaric beside the table.

His coat had been slit across one shoulder. The skin underneath was unbroken. Blood darkened the whole length of his sword, but none of it seemed to be his.

"Your arm," he said.

Alaric looked at the slit in his sleeve. The cut beneath it had already stopped bleeding.

"It's shallow."

"Let someone with bandages decide that."

"Are you hurt?"

Silas moved his shoulder once. "No."

Alaric looked out through the doorway. Liluth lay where she'd fallen. Someone had covered her face and laid a length of brown cloth over the rest of her.

He picked his sword up off the table. A dark line ran almost to the guard. He held it well away from Wena and the child.

"I meant to kill her," he said.

Silas followed his eyes into the street.

"I know."

"At the fort, I didn't know until the knife was already in him."

"This wasn't the fort."

Alaric waited for more.

Silas took a folded cloth from the medicine supplies and held it out. "Clean the blade before it dries."

Alaric took it.

"What she said," he said. "About Marta."

Silas's hand stayed where it was after the cloth had left it.

"She held," he said. "That's what it means. She didn't say a word, so they found someone who would."

He turned away before Alaric could see his face.

Alaric cleaned the sword himself.

---

The surviving rider sat under the old arch with her ankles bound and her hands tied in front of her. Someone had taken the fitted armour off her forearms. Without it, she looked younger than Alaric had first thought.

Six people stood round her. More listened from the roofs and doorways close by.

Kurdag stayed outside the circle. A split ran up the wood of his staff near its lowest band, and he'd wrapped one hand round it to hold the damage closed.

The frightened man who'd met them with a spear that evening was holding the rider's sword now.

"Ask her again," he said.

Orieth crouched in front of the prisoner. "Did Liluth send word before you came into the city?"

"I don't know."

"You rode with her from Kelmend."

"She left us twice after dark. She said she was checking the trail." The rider's eyes went to the sword in the man's hand. "She could have sent a bird. She could have done nothing. She didn't tell us."

"She's lying," the man said to Kurdag.

"I'm not."

"You expect us to believe that?"

"No," said the rider. "I don't expect you to."

Silas stood beside Alaric at the edge of the crowd.

"What were your orders?" he asked.

She turned towards his voice. "Take the survivor alive and bring him back to Kelmend."

"Whose orders?"

"Field command."

"Did field command send you into the Expanse?"

She hesitated.

"A bird came at the marker. Liluth read it. She had it burned before any of us saw it." She swallowed. "She said command wanted us to press on. She said we had to take him before the relief party reached the marker and took him off her."

"What relief party?" Kurdag asked.

"Forty riders. Coming behind us from Kelmend." She looked round at the faces. "I don't know how far behind. Days. She never said."

The circle went quiet.

"Forty," someone on a roof said.

"To the marker," the rider said. "Not here. Nobody told them about here."

"Unless she did."

"I said I don't know what she did." It came out louder than anything else she'd said, and dropped again when the nearest spear moved. "She told us almost nothing. We were supposed to follow her, take the human, and get back before the others arrived."

The man with her sword stepped closer. "Then nobody knows we're here but her. And she's dead."

"You don't know that either."

"Forty riders at the marker," a woman by the burned house said. "What happens when these five don't come back to meet them?"

"They come looking," someone answered.

"Then we kill her and bury all five past the dead streets," said the man with the sword. "And when the forty come looking, there's nothing to find."

Everyone started talking at once.

One agreed with him. Another told him to put the sword down. A woman asked what killing the prisoner would change if a message had already gone. Someone else said letting her go would make certain one did.

The rider pulled against the rope as the sword came closer.

Orieth stood up between them. "Put that down."

"Your daughter was under that roof."

"I know where my daughter was."

"Then move."

"No."

Kurdag stepped into the circle. He took the sword out of the man's hand before the grip could turn into a struggle. The man let go of it reluctantly and didn't step away from the prisoner.

"We decide together," Kurdag said.

"While we argue, forty riders are getting closer."

"Then we get ready for them." Kurdag passed the sword to a woman behind him. "Move the western watches out past the dead arch. Wake everyone who knows the northern paths. Count the water, and find out what the fire cost us."

The man looked at the prisoner. "And her?"

"She stays bound until the council's finished."

"How long?"

Kurdag looked round at all the people waiting for him to decide on his own.

"I don't know."

The spears round the prisoner stayed up.

Alaric stepped away before the argument could start again.

Beyond the arch, four of Foramen's people were carrying the dead riders out towards the dead streets on doors taken off their hinges. The last one was Liluth. They'd left the brown cloth over her face. Her good hand hung off the edge of the door, still in its glove, swinging a little with each step.

Nobody took the gloves off her. Nobody searched her. They carried her out past the last repaired wall and into the dark, and whatever she'd been carrying went into the ground with her.

---

Kurdag followed him to the council building.

"They came for me," Alaric said.

"They chose what they did after they got here."

"If forty more come, they'll be looking for the same person."

Kurdag studied him. "What are you asking?"

"Which way avoids the middle?"

Silas had followed them in. "It adds two days, at least."

"Then it adds two days."

"You wanted the quick way when we got here."

"That was before this." Alaric looked out at the people carrying burnt reeds down off the nearest roof. "I don't want to lose anyone else. If it takes longer, it takes longer."

Redd came up with his travelling sack already over one shoulder. "I do appreciate being consulted after the decision."

Alaric turned to him. "Do you want to stay?"

Redd glanced at Orieth's daughter inside the council building, then at the prisoner under the arch.

"No," he said. "But I don't enjoy leaving people to prop up a wall I helped heave out of the ground."

"It would have crushed them if you hadn't."

"It can be useful and inconvenient at the same time."

Silas took the sack off him and tightened the loose shoulder strap. "Pack the water. Complain while you do it."

"That was the plan."

Redd went to the covered cistern.

Kurdag led Alaric and Silas inside. He cleared a space on the low table without moving the broken instrument, and drew three lines in the dust.

"The direct route runs here." He touched the deepest mark. "It brings you within half a day of the central crater. Don't use it."

His claw traced a wider path to the west.

"Follow the buildings whose windows have melted shut. When you reach two stone figures facing away from each other, turn south. The ground between them looks level. It isn't. Keep to the darker sand."

Silas studied it. "Water?"

"A buried channel crosses the southern line. You'll hear it before you see the opening. Fill everything there. After that, you have three dry days, if the paths stay where they were."

"And if they don't?" Alaric asked.

Kurdag tapped the western line. "Go back to the figures. And don't follow your own tracks if they start curving back towards you."

Redd came back in time to hear it.

"I was worried the safe road might be dull."

"You'll be disappointed," Kurdag said.

He wiped out the direct route and left the western line in the dust.

They gathered up the blankets, food and water they'd never unpacked. Alaric checked the satchel under his cloak. The wrapped orb was cold and still inside it. Redd fastened Wena's rope while she pushed her nose against Orieth's daughter through the doorway.

Orieth came to collect the child. She checked the knot Redd had tied and pulled the loose end through a second time.

"That would've come open before the outer arch."

Redd inspected the new knot. "I was leaving room for improvement."

"You found it."

He took the end of the rope from her. "If the wall shifts again —"

"We know where to brace it now."

He nodded.

She picked up her daughter. The girl kept one hand in Wena's fur until the distance pulled it free.

Orieth carried her away towards the injured without saying goodbye.

Kurdag waited at the start of the western street. Behind him, Foramen was still awake. Lookouts were slipping out through the northern ruins. Wet reeds were coming down off the burned roofs. The prisoner had been moved inside the council building, and the argument had followed her in.

Kurdag pointed down the road with his staff.

"The first figures are two days from here, if you don't lose the dark sand."

"We won't," Alaric said.

"You might." Kurdag looked at Silas. "He knows enough to check."

"We'll check," Silas said.

Alaric rested a hand on Wena's back as they passed him.

"I'm sorry about the musician."

"So am I," Kurdag said.

He turned back to his people.

They left before the sky changed.

The outer streets took them one at a time. Silas went first. Redd followed with Wena, and Alaric walked at the back until the last repaired wall of Foramen vanished behind the ancient stone.

Through the first dead street, only their own footsteps moved through the ruins.

Then a string sounded behind them.

The note was thin and unsure of itself.

A second followed it.

Alaric knew the start of the tune from the fire.

He listened for the third.

It never came.
