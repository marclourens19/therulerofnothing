# Chapter 11 — Dialogue Round 1: Day 3

**10 October 2026. Two options for each principal exchange.** These are proposed passages for choosing the voices, not a continuous chapter. Neither option is approved merely because it appears here. Small recollections and incidental staging are also proposals.

The three latest decisions are incorporated: Alaric watches for approaching guards and gives Silas a useful warning; the twins are sold separately and he leaves during the second sale; the silent battle has brought Brand to Kelmend, and Brand notices neither man.

The carter remains a proposed information source. The particular warning, the twins' ages and genders, Brand's appearance and the first lesson's location are not settled by the earlier answers. These passages avoid assigning them unnecessarily.

Clothing, Wena's care and privacy must be staged in the full chapter. Silas stays outside Marta's house throughout Day 3. The guards' promised drinks still occur that day; these selected passages do not replace that visit. Darcy's transfer, Empty and the fort agreement belong to the next dialogue round.

## 1. Breakfast: Alaric asks Marta where to begin

**What they want:** Alaric wants a person worth asking about the machines. Marta wants to feed him and get through the morning's work without promising an answer she cannot give.

### 1A — Marta postpones the conversation, but takes the request seriously

“Do you know anyone who might recognise those machines?” Alaric asked. “The ones I told you about, on Gerolt's field?”

Marta put a bowl down in front of him. Someone at the far table called for her, and she raised a hand without turning.

“Give me till tonight, lad. I need a moment to think, and this lot won't give me one.”

“I could ask them myself, if you told me who—”

“And half the men in here would tell ye they built the bloody things if it got them a free drink. Let me find ye someone worth asking.” She pushed the bowl closer. “Eat that first, will ye?”

He sat, but left the spoon beside the bowl.

“What about Wena? I can't leave her shut in that room all day.”

“I'll ask Rhose to take her out the back. Keep her off the street, mind. I don't need those guards coming through here again.”

“Thank you. I wasn't trying to make more work for you.”

“Ye can thank me by eating before it goes cold.”

### 1B — Marta notices that he has hardly slept

“Marta, could I speak to you about the field? I want to find someone who knows what those machines were.”

She stopped with a bowl in each hand and looked at him properly.

“Did ye get any sleep?”

“Some.” He looked past her at the tables. “I know you're busy. I only wanted to ask where I should start.”

Marta set one bowl in front of him and passed the other to a woman going towards the common room.

“Start with that. Come and find me tonight, when I can hear what ye're saying.”

“I don't want to sit here doing nothing.”

“Then eat, and give me room to work. Ye're not doing nothing by getting a bit of strength back.”

He picked up the spoon. “And Wena? Could someone let her out?”

“I'll see to her. Ye leave Gerolt's sword upstairs, and don't take that dog out where every guard in Kelmend can see her.”

**Recommendation: 1A.** It recognises that he has chosen an investigation and gives Marta an active response without producing a convenient expert at breakfast. The final exchange lets her care remain practical.

## 2. Outside: Silas, the marriage and the outing

**What they want:** Alaric wants to understand the man helping him and find a way to investigate the field. Silas wants his cloak back, his private history left alone and the day's work begun.

### 2A — Silas closes the personal question, then answers the practical one

Silas took the folded cloak from him and shook it out.

“She let you stay, then?”

“Yes. She's going to speak to me tonight.” Alaric hesitated. “I didn't know you were married.”

Silas settled the cloak over his shoulders.

“Well, now you do.”

“She was so angry with you. Did you think she'd let you in when we came?”

“I brought you to the door, Alaric. I didn't come to argue with my wife.” He tugged the cloth clear of his bound hand. “Leave it there.”

Alaric looked down the lane. Silas started towards its mouth, and he went after him.

“Have you heard anything else about Gerolt's field?”

“More guards going out. Seen them myself. The elves are still here, too.”

“Does anyone know what happened?”

“Everyone's got a story. I'm trying to find out which bastard's actually been there.”

“Let me come with you. I saw it. I can tell you if what they're saying is wrong.”

Silas stopped and looked him over.

“You stay with me. And if I tell you to keep your mouth shut, keep it shut. I won't have you telling a stranger where you woke up.”

“I won't. But tell me what you're doing.”

“I will. Come on.”

### 2B — Alaric presses harder, and Silas gives him work to do

“How did it go?” Silas asked.

“She let us stay. Wena's upstairs.” Alaric handed him the cloak. “Why didn't you tell me she was your wife?”

“Would it have made the door open any quicker?”

“No. But I wouldn't have had to hear it from someone else.”

Silas drew the cloak round himself. For a moment he kept looking at the fastening.

“You know now. I'm not discussing it out here.”

Alaric waited, then said, “I'm going to find out about the machines. Someone must know where they came from.”

“And where were you planning to start?”

“Marta said she'd think about it tonight. I thought you might know somebody.”

“I might. I'm going to ask a few questions myself.”

“Can I come?”

“If you listen. I've no use for you wandering off the moment something catches your eye.”

Alaric's face warmed. “I stayed when you told me to, at the stream.”

“Aye. You did.” Silas glanced towards the street. “Let's see you keep doing it.”

**Recommendation: 2A.** It keeps the marriage boundary, gives Silas an immediate purpose and lets Alaric offer something relevant. Option B gives Alaric more resistance and pays off the stream, but risks making Silas sound as though yesterday's agreement is still untested.

## 3. The task: Alaric watches for guards

**Staging:** choose a meeting place where an approaching patrol gives them time to end the conversation normally. Silas wants to avoid drawing official attention to his source. The warning must change what he does.

### 3A — A plain instruction and a direct warning

“See the way in from the street?” Silas asked. “Keep an eye on it while I speak to him. If guards turn this way, come and tell me.”

“Are they looking for him?”

“No. And I'd like to keep it that way. He has to work here after we've gone.”

Alaric looked from the entrance to the man beside the cart.

“What are you asking him?”

“What they're taking out to the field, and what they're bringing back. He's a better chance of knowing than someone shouting about it over a drink.”

Later, Alaric saw two guards turn towards the entrance. He went to Silas without calling across the yard.

“There are two coming in. They were on the other side of the street, but they've crossed.”

Silas looked past him, then turned back to the carter.

“We'll leave it there. You've got work to finish.”

They moved away before the guards reached the cart. Once they were clear, Silas glanced at Alaric.

“You saw them soon enough. Good.”

### 3B — An ordinary question serves as the warning

“I need you watching the entrance,” Silas said. “Guards come this way, walk over and ask me whether we're going soon. Don't stand there pointing at them.”

Alaric looked towards the street. “Even if they're only passing?”

“No. If they turn in. I'm not ending a conversation every time a man in a helmet goes past.”

“All right. And you're asking about the farm?”

“About the wagons. What he's seen them carrying. I want you listening as well, when you can.”

When the patrol turned towards the entrance, Alaric went across to him.

“Are we going soon?”

Silas looked at him. Alaric glanced towards the street, then back.

“Aye. He's got enough to do without us standing about.”

He ended the conversation, and Alaric went with him. He waited until they had turned out of sight before speaking.

“That was right, wasn't it? They were coming in.”

“It was right. You don't need to ask me again.”

**Recommendation: 3A.** His competence is simple and readable. B introduces a useful precaution, but the full scene must justify why they need a concealed signal rather than a quiet warning.

The carter's actual answer must be designed separately. It cannot become an explanation of the ancient battle. Any claim about what was moved must come from something he handled or witnessed.

## 4. The auction: anger becomes a request to learn

**Staging:** the first child has been sold and is being taken away. The second remains on the platform. Alaric understands what he is seeing. No second explanation of slavery is needed.

### 4A — Alaric asks how the buyer can bear doing it

“He could see they were holding on to each other.” Alaric kept looking after the buyer. “How can he just take one and leave the other?”

“He can see,” Silas said. “He doesn't give a shit.”

“They're children.”

“Aye. He knows that, too.”

The auctioneer called again. Alaric turned towards the platform, then looked at the guards beside the crowd.

“I don't want to keep standing here while this happens.”

Silas watched him for a moment. “What do you want me to do here, Alaric?”

“Teach me to fight. Please. I don't expect to learn everything today. I just need to start.”

Silas shifted the cloth round his burned hand.

“I can start you. But you learn what I give you, and you do it again when it's boring and your hands hurt. You don't get to skip it because you've decided you're ready for someone bigger.”

“I won't.”

“We'll see how you feel after you've done some work.”

### 4B — Alaric admits how little he can do

Alaric watched the first child being led through the crowd.

“I know what you're going to say. There are guards, and I can't fight, and we'd get caught.” He turned towards Silas. “But how can everyone else watch it? Some of them are laughing.”

“Some of those bastards will have children of their own at home. They know what they're doing.”

“Then I need to be able to do something.” The words came out too loudly. Alaric lowered his voice. “Will you teach me? Properly?”

Silas glanced towards the nearest guard before answering.

“I'll teach you to use a sword. Don't go promising those two you'll save them because you've had a lesson.”

“I'm not promising them anything.” Alaric looked back towards the platform. “That's what I hate. I haven't got anything I can offer.”

Silas followed his look.

“Then you start learning. And you listen when I tell you what you can and can't do.”

“I will. I'm asking you to help me.”

**Recommendation: 4B, with care over Silas's warning.** Alaric's frustration is specific and his request is personal. Silas must sound concerned about a reckless promise, not contemptuous of the wish to help. A is less confrontational and avoids another account of the guards and danger.

Brand's arrival interrupts this conversation or follows closely enough that their departure happens during the second sale. Neither option implies that the second child has been sold before they leave.

## 5. Brand passes: a dangerous battle interests him

**Staging:** Alaric catches part of a conversation already in progress. Brand and the guards continue their own journey. Brand neither sees the fugitives nor addresses them. Reports remain reports.

### 5A — Curiosity about a survivor

“And none of the farms heard it?” Brand asked.

“That's what they're saying. We haven't had a proper account yet.”

Brand glanced at the man walking beside him.

“Has anyone come back alive from it?”

“Not that they've told us.”

“I hope someone did.” He smiled. “I'd like to meet them.”

Silas caught Alaric's sleeve.

“Keep walking. Come with me.”

“What—”

“Now, Alaric. And don't look back at him.”

### 5B — The guards want the danger gone; Brand wants it found

“Whatever did it, I'd rather it was well away from here,” one of the guards said.

Brand gave a quiet laugh.

“Would you? I've come all this way hoping it's still nearby.”

The guard looked up at him. “We don't know what happened out there.”

“Then I suppose you'll have to find out. I'd be disappointed to miss it.”

Silas moved close beside Alaric.

“We're going back to the inn.”

“Already? You said—”

“I know what I said. Walk.”

**Recommendation: 5B.** It conveys Brand's appetite without demonstrating his power or giving him knowledge nobody has. A is quieter, but “a survivor” can sound like he is pursuing a witness out of concern; the performance would need to make his interest in a worthy opponent clear.

Brand's exact manner should remain his own. He can be sociable and interested without borrowing another character's wording or becoming a speech about battle. His deeper willingness to abandon Mydea is not proved by this exchange.

As Silas leads Alaric away, the auctioneer continues calling the second child's price. No sentence closes that child's life for the reader.

## 6. Away from Brand: Silas gives a real answer

**Staging:** Silas checks the route back and speaks once they have privacy. Alaric has followed him, so the reply does not reprimand him for disobeying. Do not assert that Silas witnessed the killings unless that history is selected.

### 6A — Alaric has seen Silas afraid

“Who was he?” Alaric asked.

Silas looked back towards the mouth of the lane before answering.

“Brand. The Ruler of Fire.”

“You were afraid of him.”

“Aye. And if you'd known who he was, you would have been too.” He loosened his hold on Alaric's sleeve. “He killed men I've fought beside. Men who knew what they were doing.”

Alaric looked towards the street.

“Does he know you?”

“I don't know. I'd rather keep my face out of his way than find out.”

“He was laughing with them.”

“I saw.” Silas drew the cloth back over his palm. “Men I knew are dead, and there he is, having a pleasant fucking morning.”

### 6B — Silas challenges the reassurance of Brand's appearance

“He didn't even look at us,” Alaric said.

“Good.” Silas watched a passer-by reach the end of the lane. “That's how I want it to stay.”

“Who is he?”

“Brand. Ruler of Fire. Mydea calls on him when it wants something fought.”

“He can't be much older than me.”

Silas turned back to him.

“He killed men I knew, Alaric. I'd have trusted those men with my life. His age won't do us any good if he decides he wants a fight.”

Alaric's eyes went to the bandage on Silas's hand.

“Are you all right?”

“I'll be a good deal better once I know no one's followed us. Wait here a moment. I'll have a look.”

**Recommendation: 6A.** Silas's admission is personal and answers the fear Alaric actually saw. B makes the public affiliation more explicit, but the last instruction needs a properly staged return so it does not repeat the Chapter 6 problem of unexplained disappearances.

A further question about what the title means may be needed in the full conversation. Answer only what Alaric asks; neither passage supplies an unapproved lecture on every Ruler.

## 7. A first lesson actually happens

**Staging proposal:** after Silas has checked that they are safe, a brief exercise in a quiet place outside Marta's house. A piece of wood permits practice without displaying Gerolt's sword in the street. This particular prop and location remain proposals. The full scene needs simple, usable physical blocking.

### 7A — Alaric pushes back on an unclear instruction

“Loosen your hand,” Silas said. “You're holding it hard enough to hurt yourself.”

Alaric loosened his fingers. The wood shifted in his grip.

“Not that much. You'll drop it.”

“You told me to loosen it.”

“And you did. Too much.” Silas took it with his good hand and showed him. “Here. Try it again.”

Alaric watched, then took it back.

“Like that?”

“Better. Keep your hand there.”

He held it for a moment, then glanced towards the inn.

“Are we doing this again tomorrow?”

Silas looked at him. “If you're here, and you still want it.”

“I do.”

“Then watch what I'm showing you.”

### 7B — His attention keeps returning to the children

“You're looking down the lane again,” Silas said.

Alaric turned back. “Sorry.”

“You wanted me to teach you. Give me your attention.”

“I keep thinking about them.”

Silas waited until Alaric looked at him.

“I know. But I'm standing here showing you, and you're missing it.”

Alaric adjusted his grip.

“Can you show me again?”

“Aye. And this time look.”

He demonstrated with his good hand. Alaric followed the movement, then tried it himself.

“Will we do more tomorrow?”

“We'll do more now. You've not got this yet.”

**Recommendation: 7A.** The disagreement is about a real instruction. Silas improves his explanation instead of producing a maxim about discipline. B keeps the children present, but should be used only if Alaric's distraction has not already been repeated elsewhere.

This exercise does not establish that he has learned to fight, reveal Empty early or reverse the scale of Brand's threat.

## 8. Evening with Marta: an ordinary memory of Gerolt

**What they want:** Marta wants something of the man before the riders came. Alaric wants to tell her something true that is not another account of the death. Later in the full conversation he can return to his investigation. Neither option settles that second part.

### 8A — Wena brings a small recollection from Marta

Wena pushed her nose towards the edge of the table. Marta moved her bowl away.

“Ye've eaten. Don't start on mine.”

Alaric watched her settle beneath the table.

“She was hiding under Gerolt's bed when I woke up. I thought he was alone until she made a noise.”

Marta looked down at the dog.

“She wasn't coming out?”

“Only her nose. He brought me food, and there she was, trying to get close enough to smell it.”

Marta gave a small laugh and rubbed at her eyes.

“He used to tell me I spoiled her. Then he'd sit right there feeding her off his own plate, like I couldn't bloody see him.”

“He said she barked at everything.”

“Aye. He'd leave her outside when he came, and then complain when she wanted in.” Marta rested her hand below the table. Wena came to it. “I'd let her in. He could moan all he liked.”

Alaric let her sit with her hand in the dog's fur.

### 8B — His back, and the week he expected to have

“Was his back hurting him?” Marta asked.

Alaric looked up from his bowl.

“His back was. He carried me to the house. I couldn't stay awake.”

Marta pressed her lips together, then nodded for him to continue.

“He kept putting his fist against it. He said carrying me as far as the bed had nearly finished him. That he'd be feeling it for a week.”

“A week.” Marta looked towards the hearth. “He'd have been telling me about it the whole of that week.”

“I didn't want him to have to carry me.”

“I know ye didn't.” She wiped her nose with the back of her hand. “Don't apologise for that, lad.”

He put the spoon down.

“He stayed by the bed. Even when I wasn't speaking. I think he was tired, but he didn't go anywhere.”

Marta kept looking towards the hearth. She picked up her spoon, then put it down again.

**Recommendation: 8A.** Marta gives Alaric a new, small memory. Wena participates as a dog, not a symbol explained by the narration. B rests on verified details from Chapter 1 and lets the unspent week hurt without a speech about it. Neither passage ends by resolving their grief for them.

The recollection of feeding Wena and Marta's remarks about past visits are proposed new character details. They do not introduce a hidden history or change Gerolt's last day. The evening conversation should also allow pauses, a disagreement or a practical question; it is not a complete tribute scene.

## Selection guide

My current starting set is **1A, 2A, 3A, 4B, 5B, 6A, 7A and 8A**. These are recommendations, not recorded author selections. Mixing individual lines is preferable to retaining an option whose wording feels wrong.

The strongest candidates to test carefully are the auction request, Brand's casual exchange and Silas's answer afterwards. Their replies must sound like people responding to one another under the circumstances, rather than declarations of what the novel means.

For the 100/100 ambition, this day must change what Alaric and Silas can expect from each other. The useful warning, the prompt departure and the actual lesson supply that change through actions. Marta's evening then gives Alaric a relationship beyond needing a bed or an answer. None of these scenes guarantees a score; they make the chapter's later undertaking more credible.
