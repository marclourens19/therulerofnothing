# Chapter 11 – Selected Dialogue (Day 3): Claude's Notes

10 October 2026. Notes on the selected and revised Day 3 passages, kept word for word in `Chapter 11 - Selected Dialogue (Day 3).md`. Your selections are recorded in `Decisions.md` and aren't reopened here. These notes only check the new wording against Chapters 1–10 and the working design.

## In short

The revisions are better than the options they came from:

- Silas's "Because getting you through that door was enough trouble." answers the practical side of a personal question, which is exactly how he works.
- Marta's "That wasn't meant for you." catches Alaric flinching, so we see him without being told.
- Brand's "Thousands, and no one heard a thing" is grinning, specific and frightening.
- The 8A evening is lovely. When Marta says "Ye probably were." about the bowl, she doesn't know he did drop it, after the vision in Chapter 1, but Alaric and the reader do.

Six things need attention before drafting. Two of them are gaps, not wording.

## The two gaps

### 1. The outing never pays off for Alaric

He goes out to find where they've taken a machine ("If they've taken one of those machines into town, I want to know where it is."). The carter's conversation happens out of his hearing, the guards cut it short, and nobody tells him what was said. The chapter needs him to ask, "What did he say?", and Silas to give a limited, truthful answer. Otherwise the motive the author's revision gave him is dropped.

What the carter says is still to design. One candidate, your call: the machines are too heavy to haul, so they're still on the farm, and the fort has had bodies and weapons. That keeps Darcy's later lead intact ("recover a surviving war-machine core from Gerolt's field"). If the carter says a machine has gone into the fort, the core lead has to account for it.

### 2. Silas's loss has gone from 6A

The working design's Day 3 has Alaric see "someone whose immense power has brought Silas personal loss". In the revised 6A, Silas gives only a general warning, and "He killed men I've fought beside" is gone. That line doesn't name Brand or reveal his domain, so it can stay inside the new rule. I'd restore it after "You haven't the faintest idea what people like him can do." If you'd rather hold Silas's loss back for later, the working design's line needs changing instead.

## Four wording points

### 3. Silas and "Aye", again

The revisions give Silas "Aye. And now you've heard it…" (2A) and "Aye, well, ask me again…" (4A). Marta says "Aye, well, ye can spare me reheating that bowl" in 1A, so the same phrase now opens a line in two mouths in one chapter. In Chapters 1–10, "Aye" is only ever Gerolt's or Marta's; Silas says "Yes". This was question 5 last round, and it's still open.

### 4. 4A: "Aye, well, ask me again when we're clear of this place. And there's time."

"And there's time." reads as a fragment. Try: "Ask me again when we're clear of this place and there's time for it."

Also, "Alaric caught his sleeve." sits alone between two of Alaric's own lines. Join it to the speech that follows: "Alaric caught his sleeve. 'I can't stand here…'"

### 5. 6A: "He didn't look that dangerous."

He has just heard the young man grin about thousands dead and say "I'd have liked to see that." As it stands, he doesn't react to that at all, and it was Gerolt's farm, where he woke under a dead man. Silas's reply ("You haven't the faintest idea what people like him can do") answers a remark about how dangerous Brand looks, so keep that remark. But let Alaric register what he heard first. My suggestion, new, your call:

> "He said he'd have liked to see it." Alaric looked back the way they had come. "But he can't be much older than me. He didn't look that dangerous."

### 6. 3A repeats 2A

In 2A Silas has already said "A man who hauls for the fort. He might know what they've been bringing in." In 3A Alaric asks "What does he know?" and gets the same answer. Cut the 3A pair, so that "Are they looking for him, too?" follows the instruction directly.

## For the draft

- **"Later, two guards crossed from the other side…"** needs showing the way he sees it, step by step, as he watches the entrance. In 3A he says "They stopped on the other side first", so the reader should see them stop.
- **The clothes are still unsolved.** He hands back the cloak before going out, and he still has the clothes he woke in. Silas told him in Chapter 8 that "nobody in Kelmend dresses like you", and the working design says the strange clothes "can still expose him".
- **The pasted text uses curly quotes.** House style is straight quotes. The draft will use straight ones.
