# Chapter 11: Design

Started 6 October 2026. **Status:** Claude's proposal and round 1 of questions. Nothing in this file is decided until the author answers. New material is marked **Your call**.

**The target:** 4,000–5,000 words, and longer if the story needs it. Claude's estimate for the shape below is **4,800–6,000 words**. Per-scene budgets have run about a quarter high on every chapter so far, so read it as a range.

## Where it starts

- **Chapter 9 ended in the small hours of Day 3.** It closed on the false awakening, Rhose's voice through the door ("Alaric, keep it down, will you?"), Wena's nose in his hand, a headache that didn't ease, and *I'm fine.*
- **Chapter 10 came between.** Seralune and Nereth came out of the mountain before dawn on Day 3, and a bell was ringing in the port below. Chapter 11 picks up in Kelmend later that same morning. Nothing on the page needs to say so.
- **The state they're in:**
  - **Alaric** is in the room at the end of the upstairs passage, with the door bolted and the shutter looking onto the lane. His ankle is swollen (the ogre, Chapter 6), and his head still hurts. Gerolt's sword is beside the bed and Silas's cloak is over its foot. He's still in the black clothes he woke in on the field, the same clothes the double wore.
  - **Wena** is beside the bed. She hasn't been outside since the lane.
  - **Marta's** uncle is dead, and "the elves had him when the report came in". She asked the guards "Can I see him?" and got "I don't know, Marta." She has promised them a drink: "Come back tomorrow when ye've finished work."
  - **Rhose:** "But we're talking about this again when they've gone." Alaric heard the two of them talking under his floor, and once Rhose said something sharper.
  - **Silas** is somewhere in Kelmend, paying his own way. His palm is still burned raw. Marta told Alaric: "Ye can give it back tomorrow" (the cloak).
  - **The search** is for a boy and a big dog, brought in alive. It has no name and no face. The small craft came down by the gatehouse ("Elves, I'm telling you, three of them", the crowd said), and the regional destroyer passed over on Day 2.

## What Alaric knows, and what he doesn't

- **He knows:**
  - Marta is Gerolt's niece and Silas's wife ("Aye. Me." / "Leave it at that, Alaric.").
  - The guards' version: Gerolt was found in the woods by the river, and the elves have him.
  - The search wants him alive, and the guards weren't told why.
  - "Only 『Faint』 can be sold" (Silas, Chapter 8). He thought then: *Gerolt hadn't known what they would do with someone who had none.*
  - Rhose objected to him, then hid him anyway.
  - He has a face now, and he still can't make it his.
- **He believes** Gerolt died because of him, and the double has just told him "This is who you are, Alaric."
- **He hoped,** on the road (Chapter 6), that once he reached Marta "there would be somebody who knew what to do".
- **He doesn't know** why Silas and Marta are apart, or about the gorge, Darcy, the fort, what the elves want with him, the king of Natharul, or Seralune. He doesn't know the double is anything but himself.
- **Nobody in Kelmend but Alaric knows he's Empty.** Silas has never been told.

The next opportunity requires haste; its mechanism will be designed in the relevant chapter. Alaric acknowledges that he cannot manage the task alone, and Marta directs him to Silas." (item 9)

**Deferred to this chapter** (Chapter 9's round 3 and working design):

- **Silas's lodging,** and how Alaric reaches him: "a simple means of contact or an agreed meeting; its exact arrangement can arise naturally".
- **The Darcy source and opportunity.** The working design sets the tests:
  - the lead "should arise from fresh information, newly completed verification or a newly available contact";
  - "Marta should not hold a known urgent lead for two days and then demand haste";
  - "a transfer should not silently bring the Volume 2 rescue into this chapter";
  - "identify what the customer could actually know, what Marta verifies, what Darcy could tell Alaric and whether the goal is contact rather than the planned later rescue".
- **Redd and Freya's chapter number,** which "should remain open until the recovery and Darcy opportunity are shaped".
- **The father line,** and why Marta and Silas are still married.
- **Marta's 『Affinity』.**

**Character rules that bear on it:**
- **Silas** answers practical questions and evades personal ones. Marta's rule holds: he isn't let in, and "Marta's later recommendation of his help need not mean forgiveness."
- **Marta** has Baderon's voice: light dialect, and keep the swearing. She's firm in her rules, used to being obeyed, and a hub of information. The women of the house choose their customers, and Marta pays separately for what they hear.
- **Alaric** is kind, smart and caring, and his humour survives. When he's frightened he has Subaru's mouth inside his head. "He likes to think a lot and keep emotions to himself."
  - Asking Silas for help "is a small step towards trusting others. It does not complete the Volume 1 arc."
  - "If the double has already urged withdrawal, asking for help can also be an early act of resistance."
- **The double:** later encounters "can be dreams or waking, chosen one by one". Only Alaric sees it, and it drives his hunger for his past.
- **Darcy** is "regarded as the greatest mind in Lazaran", and "may know something about what happened on Gerolt's farm". Her reputation "doesn't mean she knows anything about Alaric's erased past".
  - Fort Kelmend and Darcy stay in Volume 1.
  - Her rescue is Volume 2's mission, by Alaric with his friends.
  - Gilmot's threat against the Faint quarter is part of why she can't simply be taken.
- **Wena** attaches to Alaric, "but often Freya more than anyone else".
- **Volume 1, for Alaric:** "he's so locked in on chasing the past… that he doesn't realise he's living right now." Belonging is the centre.
- **The guard for the whole volume:** until Alaric and Seralune meet, their two stories touch through cause and effect, never through near-misses.

**House rules for the draft:** no counting, no "the dark", no horses' smell and no buzz words. Plain beats clever, thoughts reason, sentences are joined, and there's one pair of eyes.

## The old version (raw material, not canon)

The old *Refuge* (`Archive/Volume 1 - Rewrites/Chapter 7 - Refuge (Rewrite).md`) did both of this chapter's jobs in one evening.

- **Empty.**

  - Silas said "I thought he was spent." / "That wasn't the question." / "No. I felt nothing."
  - Then Marta: "My uncle was right. You really are Empty." Gerolt had said it like a question; Marta said it like a verdict. "No one can know."
- **Darcy.**
  - She was Marta's childhood friend.
  - At her Water test, with a marked silver cup, she made three drops, below the lowest line, and the examiner made her try three times while every child in the queue watched.
  - The garrison held her as property, and officers put their seals on her plans. "They value what she gives them. They don't value her."
  - Marta: "I held the water for her. She apologised for spilling it."
  - She was to be moved at dawn to the frontier, and Marta wanted Alaric to carry an offer to her: "You carry my offer. You don't fight your way to her." And: "You will not drag her away from one lot of armed men just because you think where you're taking her is kinder."

**What no longer fits:**
- Gerolt never called him Empty. It's Alaric's own word.
- **Nobody senses mana on the page.** Gerolt was Eminent too, and he couldn't tell; he had the boy feel for it himself. Marta sensing it would make her better at it than her uncle, with no reason given (question 4).
- Silas was let into the inn. Now he isn't.
- The token is now the sword.
- The dawn transfer. The working design keeps a transfer as only a proposal, and warns against bringing the Volume 2 rescue forward.

**Worth keeping, as proposals:** the cup, told short in Marta's voice; "They value what she gives them"; the water and the apology; "You don't fight your way to her."

## The chapter's job (proposed)

- **Its question:** *Where does he go when the two days are up?* Under it is the double's question: *who was he?*
- **What Alaric wants:** to stop being a danger to the house, and to find out who he was. The double has told him, and he half believes it.
- **The choice the reader sees, twice:**
  1. He tells Marta and Silas the truth about Empty when a lie would be easier, because they're the ones taking the risk. In the same chapter, he keeps the double to himself.
  2. At the end, he admits he can't reach Darcy on his own, and goes to ask Silas.
- **What's different by the end:**
  - Three people know he's Empty, and it has a rule attached: never say it.
  - He has a road towards his past: a woman in the fort who has been studying what came off Gerolt's field.
  - He has chosen to ask for help instead of going alone, which in Chapter 6 he didn't.
  - Marta has carried him for two days, and he has hardly noticed the life going on round him. The reader has.
- **Small answers it pays:**
  - Gerolt's surname, from someone who knew him (**Your call**, question 8).
  - What's happening to the field.
  - A little more of Marta and Silas, shown at a door rather than told.
- **Big answers it withholds:** who he is, what the field was, why the search wants him alive, and why the elves are leaving (if question 11 is yes).
- **The hook:** Silas's door, and Fort Kelmend behind it.

## A proposed shape (seven scenes, two days)

All in Alaric's eyes, from the late morning of Day 3 to the night of Day 4.

### Day 3

**1. What the house agreed** (600–800 words)
- He wakes late, with daylight round the shutter and the inn's noise under the floor. The headache has dulled but hasn't gone.
- He doesn't look in the basin. *(Proposal: the plain action, with no comment.)*
- Wena wants out, and he can't take her: a boy and a big dog is the whole description.
- Marta brings food, and the terms.
  - **Your call (question 2):** last night's talk with Rhose ended in a limit. He stays until the ankle can carry him, out of the common room, and Wena stays out of sight.
  - "Two days" gives the chapter its clock from the first page.
- She asks how his head is. "I'm fine." It's the lie he told himself at the end of Chapter 9, now told to her face.

**2. The back door** (1,200–1,600)
- Silas is in the lane. He's come for his cloak, and because he has news. He can't come in, and doesn't try.
- **What he brings (Your call, question 5):**
  - plain clothes for Alaric, bought with his own money, because "nobody in Kelmend dresses like you" (Chapter 8);
  - the town's news: the elves have been carting things off the field into the fort since morning, and the search is still a boy and a dog;
  - **Your call:** "The elf king's out at the field himself."
- **The threshold.** Marta stands in the doorway, Silas in the lane, and Alaric on the step between them. Her rule holds the whole time: Silas never crosses it.
- **Empty (questions 3 and 4).** Marta is working out how to hide him. If a guard catches him in her house, what does he say he is?
  - He could lie. Instead he tells them he has none, in his own word. Silas hears it for the first time.
  - Nobody senses it. They have his word, and Silas has his memory of the ogre fight.
  - **It becomes a danger, not a sorrow.** Marta makes it a rule: never say that word where anyone can hear.
  - What would they do with him? Gerolt didn't know. Silas answers a practical question practically. *(Your rough lines wanted in the dialogue round.)*
  - Marta says she'll ask around. That's her investigation starting, on the page.
- **Silas's hand (Your call, question 5).** Marta puts a pot of salve and clean cloth in Alaric's hands, not Silas's, and goes in. Alaric dresses Silas's hand in the lane. She won't touch him, and she won't leave him untreated either.
- **Silas tells Alaric where to find him.** Scene 7 needs it.

**3. The kitchen** (900–1,200)
- It's the quiet hour before the inn opens. He asks for work, and gets something he can do sitting down. *(Your call: what. Peeling something, say, which he's bad at; his humour survives.)*
- Marta asks about Gerolt. Not his death; his last day. "Did he feed ye?" *(Proposal: question 7.)*
- **The centre of the scene.** Alaric's whole memory is three days long, and the first of them is Gerolt's:
  - the stew;
  - the flame in Gerolt's palm;
  - the shutters that didn't sit right;
  - "the worst horse I ever had".

  He gives it to her, and it's everything he has.
- **What she gives back (Your call):** one thing about Gerolt, from someone who knew him. The surname, Warde, which Decisions keep for exactly this. Her version is yours.
- **"Heh." (Your call).** If she can bear the end now, Alaric tells her what he saw when Silas came: Gerolt looked at him a long time, made a sound, and his fire went out. Alaric reports it; he doesn't explain it. Marta knew her uncle, so she might know that sound. What it does to her is yours.

**4. The drink** (800–1,100)
- It's evening, and the promise from Chapter 9 is paid. The two guards come back after their shift, and Marta has their drink waiting.
- **Alaric isn't hiding this time.**
  - He's in the kitchen washing cups, and hears it through the passage.
  - Chapter 9 was fear behind a door; this is him watching Marta work.
  - The drink is the price of their talk, and the women at the tables are part of the same trade.
- **What they let slip (question 9):**
  - The elves still have Gerolt, and she can't have him back.
  - The carts from the field go into the fort's storerooms, and the commander has put "his 『Faint』 woman" to work on them.
  - *(Proposal:* the loud guard, who hates elves giving orders and said "Good for him" about Gerolt, raises his cup to Gerolt.)
- **Marta's hand stops** on the jug, for a moment. Alaric sees it, and doesn't know what it means.

### Day 4

**5. The house by day** (600–900)
- **The lightest scene in the chapter, on purpose.** The women, Wena, the kitchen, and someone teasing him. Wena has made friends with half the house. *(Your call: which women, and whether any are named.)*
- **Marta is out,** checking what the guards said. That's her investigation; she isn't sitting on a lead.
- **Your call (question 11): the ship.**
  - In the afternoon, a long, dark ship with blue-green light along its underside goes back over Kelmend, fast, towards the sea. It isn't the red one from Chapter 6.
  - The common room fills with talk: the elf king's going home, and he hadn't finished at the field.
  - Nobody in Kelmend knows why. The reader does: his daughter is gone (Chapters 7 and 10), and the news has reached him.
- Alaric asks about the woman at the fort, and gets no answer yet.
- **The thing he doesn't notice.** He has had two days of being fed, teased and kept, and he's been watching the street for answers. The page shows both and says nothing about it.

**6. Darcy** (1,000–1,300)
- **After closing,** a woman of the house comes back from a customer at the fort with what Marta asked her to listen for. *(Your call, question 10: who. If it's Rhose, she's helping him and getting him out of her house at the same time, and the page doesn't say which.)*
- **The lead.** The 『Faint』 woman has been brought up from below to a room where the field's things are laid out. She'll be there only as long as the fort has them. The elves are taking everything when they go, and since the ship went over, they're going sooner.
- **Marta tells him who she is (question 12).**
  - Her name is Darcy. *(Your call:* she's Marta's childhood friend, and the cup is told short.)
  - The commander, Gilmot, puts his name on her work.
  - She's the one person in Kelmend who has had her hands on what came off Gerolt's field.
- **For Alaric, it's the first door towards who he was.** It's also a way out of this house before his two days are up.
- **Marta's price (question 13):** a word for Darcy that only Alaric can carry.
- **He wants to go now.** "Into Fort Kelmend? On that ankle? On yer own?" He starts to say he can manage, and stops. **"I can't do this on my own."** *(Your rough line wanted.)*
- **Marta has to say the name herself.** There's one man in Kelmend who could get him in and out again. It costs her to say it, and it isn't forgiveness.

**7. Silas's door** (300–500)
- He goes without Wena, with his hood up, in the clothes Silas bought him (question 14).
- **The chapter ends where Chapter 8 ended, the other way round.** In Chapter 8 he knocked on Marta's door because Silas couldn't. Now he knocks on Silas's door for himself.
- **The last line (Your call):** Silas opens the door and Alaric asks. Or it ends on the knock, before the door opens.

## Three threads through the whole chapter

1. **The clock.** "Until ye can walk on it" comes on the first page, and the lead arrives as the two days run out. Nothing has to say so.
2. **The double, without the double (question 15).**
   - There's no appearance: only the headache in the morning, the basin he doesn't look in, and *This is who you are* once in his own head, at the moment the house's risk is said out loud.
   - Its job here is the hunger it leaves. Darcy matters this much to him because something wearing his face has called him a killer.
3. **The life he's living.** In every scene someone does something ordinary for him: food, terms, clothes, salve, work, a joke, a lead. He's looking past all of it. That's the Volume 1 picture, and it's shown, never said.

## What Alaric can and can't know

- He sees the threshold scene, but not what passed between Marta and Silas before it. When Marta's face changes, he sees it change.
- He hears the guards through the passage, and can't see their faces unless he looks round the door.
- He doesn't know what Darcy is to Marta until Marta tells him, or what the fort is like inside.
- If the ship goes over, he sees a ship and hears what the common room says. He doesn't know who the king is to anyone, or why he's going.
- He can't know whether Rhose's motive (or anyone's) is mixed. The page shows what she does.
- Darcy is "the 『Faint』 woman" in the guards' mouths, and "Darcy" only once Marta says the name.

## Length

The seven scenes are budgeted at 5,400–7,400 words. Past chapters came in about a quarter under budget, so the real figure is likely **4,800–6,000**.

If it runs long, scene 5 is the one to compress. Its jobs (the ship, the life he doesn't notice, Marta out checking) can move into the edges of scenes 4 and 6.

## What could make this chapter 100/100?

1. **A rest chapter needs a spine, or it's a summary.**
   - Two days of recovery is the chapter most likely to go flat in the whole volume.
   - The answer here is the clock from Rhose's objection: from the first page, he has two days and nowhere to go. Every scene then asks "and after?", and the lead answers it.
   - **Test in the draft:** cut any scene that doesn't move the clock, Empty or the lead.
2. **Empty has to be a choice, not a reveal.**
   - If Marta senses it or forces it, it's something done to him. If he could lie and doesn't, it's his.
   - The strongest version has him consider the lie in his head, then tell the truth because they're the ones taking the risk.
   - In the same chapter he hides the double. So the reader sees where he draws the line: he tells what endangers them, and keeps what is only his. That line is the start of his Volume 1 flaw.
3. **The threshold.** One image carries the marriage without a word of backstory: Marta inside, Silas outside, and Alaric on the step. She hands the salve to the boy, not the husband. Nothing more is needed this chapter.
4. **The kitchen scene is the chapter's heart, and could be the best scene in Kelmend.**
   - A boy whose whole memory is three days long gives a grieving woman the last day of her uncle's life, which was also the first day of his.
   - **The risk** is that it turns sentimental. It works if it's practical: Marta asks practical questions ("Did he feed ye?"), and the feeling comes through what she does with her hands.
   - Principle 5 applies at full strength.
5. **The lead must pass the working design's tests, and this chain does.**
   - The guards' gossip is fresh (Day 3).
   - Marta checks it (Day 4).
   - The woman is newly reachable (above ground, for now).
   - The clock is real (the elves are leaving).
   - Marta never sits on it.
6. **The ship, if you take it, is the volume's guard in action.**
   - Seralune's escape changes Alaric's chapter through cause and effect: her father goes home, the elves pack up, and Darcy's window shrinks. Nobody on the page knows.
   - **The risk:** it's the third ship over Kelmend in three Alaric chapters. Keep it to one paragraph and the talk it causes. If that's still too much, the elves can leave on their own timetable instead.
7. **"I can't do this on my own" has to cost him.**
   - He offered to go twice in Chapter 9 ("I can leave.", "I can go."), and he's someone who goes alone (Chapter 6).
   - The line lands only if we watch him start to say he can manage.
   - And Marta saying Silas's name has to cost her, too.
8. **Don't let Darcy arrive as a lecture.** Marta is the one telling him about her, so the danger is a talk about the fort and the law. Keep it to what Marta knows personally: the cup, the water, Gilmot's name on her work. The rest waits for the fort, where Alaric sees it.
9. **Humour.** After Chapter 9's vision and Chapter 10's horror, readers need room to breathe.
   - Two chances come out of the situation: Alaric at kitchen work, and the women teasing him.
   - There's no reflexive guilt after a laugh (Chapter 6's rule).
10. **What I'd challenge in my own shape:** seven scenes means a lot of entrances and exits.
    - If scenes 1 and 2 became one morning (Silas arrives while Marta is giving the terms), the chapter would be tighter.
    - I've kept them apart so the terms land before Silas complicates them.
