# Chapter 11 — Working Design

9 October 2026. **POV: Alaric. Title: undecided.**

This consolidates the author's current direction and confirmed decisions. The sequence below is the working proposal, not a finished chapter. Recommendations and unresolved details remain marked; requesting this design does not approve continuous drafting.

## Purpose and movement

Alaric wakes fearing that his dream showed him who he really is. During the day, he experiences people living ordinary lives, witnesses another sale he cannot stop, and sees someone whose immense power has brought Silas personal loss. He begins asking how he can become capable of helping.

The news about Darcy gives that wish an immediate object. He wants to help Marta, repay kindness and reach someone whose knowledge might explain the field—and himself. These motives coexist.

The chapter builds attachment to the inn and develops Alaric and Silas through time spent together. It begins the movement towards an attempt to free Darcy in Volume 1. Her eventual successful rescue remains in Volume 2.

**Time:** morning of Day 3 through Day 4, after Chapter 9's first night at the inn. Chapter 10 takes place in Seralune's separate thread.

## Confirmed decisions

- Marta shelters Alaric with clear household restrictions. There is no fixed two-day departure deadline.
- The large, rough-looking but kind guest is another guest, not Redd.
- Brand is the Ruler of Fire.
- Silas knows Brand's position through his years with Avarice. Brand has killed some of his comrades.
- When Brand appears, Silas takes Alaric away from the street and back to Marta's inn. They do not leave Kelmend.
- Marta has long wanted and planned to free Darcy, but has never managed it.
- Both fort security and danger to other people defeated those earlier plans.
- Marta believes the transfer may be her last chance. This is her judgement, not a guarantee that future rescue is impossible.
- Marta hints at Darcy's intelligence, especially her knowledge of history and war tactics. Alaric hopes she might help him understand himself.
- The attempt begins in Volume 1; Darcy's eventual rescue remains in Volume 2.

## Day 3 — The inn, the town and Brand

### 1. Morning after the vision

Alaric wakes to the sound of guests and work downstairs. The inn is alive again.

The dream returns through a few particular things: the door, the passage, or a familiar voice. Do not replay the entire destruction. He knows what he saw, but not whether it was merely a dream, a memory or something else.

His question is immediate: *Who am I really?* He has seen his own face and heard his own voice making the accusation.

The headache and injured ankle remain. Wena is alive in the room. He leaves her there while he goes to find Marta or breakfast.

**Recommendation:** he continues concealing the vision. Ordinary responses from the people he saw dead make that concealment uncomfortable.

### 2. The large guest

A huge, rough-looking man passes Alaric in the upstairs passage and accidentally bumps him aside.

Alaric expects anger. The man turns back, apologises earnestly and takes his hands too firmly. His attempt to be gentle is clumsy; if Alaric winces, he loosens his grip.

The man goes on his way. Alaric laughs a little.

This is a short human encounter. The guest does not need a secret identity, a plot assignment or a connection to Redd.

### 3. The common room in daylight

Downstairs, Alaric sees the inn working: guests, food, business, flirtation, complaints and conversations moving between tables.

The adult women live and work here. Paid companionship, kisses and physical familiarity can be part of the scene. Their established terms remain: they choose customers, can refuse and can leave. Marta pays separately for information they choose to pass on.

Give the women different behaviour and concerns. Information travels through relationships, but every interaction does not need to provide a clue.

Marta is busy and accustomed to being obeyed. Her authority appears through the way she handles the work and the people around her. Her grief has not disappeared because the room is bustling.

Follow Alaric's attention rather than presenting an inventory of the inn's activities.

### 4. Marta feeds him

Alaric approaches Marta, wanting to talk. She cannot give him a long conversation now.

She gives him food and promises they will speak that evening. He asks about Wena; Marta arranges her care.

The shelter has practical conditions, not an expiry date.

**Implementation still to confirm:** ordinary clothes, where Gerolt's sword is kept, Marta's knowledge of the outing, and which household member helps with Wena. The search identifies a boy with a large dog; keeping them apart helps, but does not make Alaric safe.

### 5. Meeting Silas

Alaric meets Silas outside. Returning Silas's cloak gives them an existing reason to make contact.

Silas asks how things went. Alaric tells him about being allowed to stay and asks about Marta and the marriage. Silas declines to discuss it; the refusal should respond to the actual question.

Silas reports guards travelling towards Gerolt's farm and elves in Kelmend. Distinguish what he has seen from what he has heard.

Alaric wants to understand the place and what he should do next. He asks to accompany Silas or asks him to teach him about the town.

**Recommendation:** Silas reluctantly accepts because supervised company is safer than Alaric wandering alone. He already has work to do.

### 6. Silas gathers information

The author's proposed purpose for the outing is Silas gathering information for Avarice about Kelmend and the farm investigation.

Choose a real source: a contact, someone handling supplies or another person with a reason to know about the soldiers' movements. Silas's questions should follow that person's knowledge.

Alaric observes the contacts and asks questions between stops. Whether Silas names Avarice to him remains open. The narration cannot reveal Silas's private assignment as fact unless Alaric learns it.

Keep the route limited. Alaric's ankle requires pauses and may slow them. Silas's burned hand remains relevant. This is not a day of effortless sightseeing.

Give room to ordinary exchanges as well as danger. Kelmend contains people doing business and living their lives while the search continues.

### 7. The twin children at auction

Alaric encounters another sale, this time of twin children.

He recognises what is happening. Chapter 8 already explained the auction; this scene concerns his inability to help despite understanding it.

**Recommendation:** buyers bid for the children separately. One keeps hold of the other while the sale treats them as individual possessions. Give them different observable responses and an immediate wish of their own.

Only Faint people can legally be auctioned. Both children's status and ages must fit the established testing rule at ten. Their exact ages, genders and the sale's outcome remain open.

Alaric does not intervene. He questions Silas about how people accept this and expresses his desire to become strong enough to protect people.

Let leaving hurt. His intention to learn does not undo what happens to the children.

### 8. The request to learn

Alaric's distress becomes a request for Silas to teach him to fight.

The request grows from what he has just witnessed. His conviction can be sincere without becoming a settled philosophy that strength will prevent every injustice.

Silas has seen his courage and poor judgement during the ogre fight. He has reasons both to train him and to distrust what the boy might do with a little confidence.

Brand's arrival interrupts or cuts short this conversation. The first practical lesson can follow later, if retained.

### 9. Brand appears

Brand walks with a couple of guards, smiling and laughing. The commotion in Kelmend interests him.

His exchange with the guards needs a conversational cause. He can ask about conflicting reports or respond to something they have said; avoid a speech announcing his personality.

Silas recognises him and reacts immediately. Alaric sees the change in the man who fought the ogre and the elves.

**Confirmed cause:** Brand is a Ruler and has killed Silas's Avarice comrades.

**Recommendations:** Brand does not notice Alaric yet. Some members of the public respond comfortably or respectfully to him, creating a difference between their response and Silas's. Neither recommendation is approved yet.

Brand's earlier violence does not establish that he knows Silas by sight. That remains open. His first appearance does not need a display of white fire or an invented magical fear aura.

### 10. Withdrawal to the inn

Silas takes Alaric away from the street and back to Marta's inn.

They leave promptly, keeping their departure ordinary enough to avoid drawing attention. Establish whether anyone follows; do not assume safety merely because they have turned a corner.

Once away, Alaric asks who Brand was and why Silas reacted that way. Silas can reveal the title and the personal loss without explaining an entire past operation.

Alaric has now seen great power beside an auction that continues unchanged. Let that experience affect him without having Silas explain its thematic meaning.

His desire to learn persists.

**Optional first lesson:** a limited exercise or instruction, suited to both injuries. The location must respect Marta's refusal to admit Silas. A full training session is unnecessary.

### 11. The evening conversation with Marta

Marta keeps her promise.

Alaric can tell her about the day and what he wants to learn. Their conversation has room for Gerolt's ordinary behaviour as well as his death. Marta contributes memories and reactions; Alaric does not recite Chapter 1 to her.

Her grief and work coexist. Allow warmth, irritation and humour without implying that she has recovered from the news.

**Empty remains to be placed:** a practical question about Alaric's abilities can provoke voluntary disclosure. Recommend a private exchange in which Marta and Silas both learn the truth before agreeing on his part in the attempt. The staging is unresolved.

Do not automatically introduce a sensing ability or a universal rule that nobody can sense mana.

**Continuity:** Marta promised the guards a drink the day after Chapter 9. Under this calendar that belongs on Day 3 after their shift. It may be brief, but the Day 4 report must be distinguished from it.

## Day 4 — Darcy's transfer and the decision to act

### 12. Some ordinary life before the news

Give Alaric a little more time at the inn. He begins recognising people, helping with something within his physical limits or having a conversation that does not concern his past.

Wena is cared for. His injuries continue to influence what he can do.

This passage gives the previous day's experiences time to remain with him. It also makes the shelter something he can feel himself beginning to belong to.

The exact activity is a drafting choice, not a required new subplot.

### 13. The guard's report

A guard reveals that Darcy is being moved towards the frontier facing Kozmagar.

He needs a credible source: escort duty, preparations, supplies or orders he has handled. Marta can draw out the information through questions.

Current geography places a narrow sea between Mydea and Kozmagar. A Mydean coastal fort, harbour or island is a possible destination. The exact destination, purpose and departure time remain open.

The transfer must change something practical for Marta: access, the reach of her contacts or the viability of her plans.

Marta believes she is losing her last chance. The narration does not have to confirm that belief as an absolute fact.

### 14. Marta summons Silas

Marta tells Alaric to bring Silas.

Her longstanding intention now becomes an immediate demand. She wants Darcy freed, despite the fort security and danger to other people that defeated earlier plans.

Calling on Silas is not forgiveness. She is asking for abilities she knows he possesses while their marriage remains wounded.

The meeting's location requires a choice. If she allows him inside, that is a meaningful exception. Otherwise establish a genuinely private place to speak.

We still need to choose whether the transfer creates a new practical opening or makes Marta willing to attempt a previously unacceptable risk.

**Recommendation, not approved:** Marta understands the possibility of retaliation but does not know the full extent of Darcy's reasons for staying. Her plans may contain safeguards and still fail to address what Darcy needs protected.

### 15. Darcy's reputation and Alaric's hope

During the discussion, Marta hints at Darcy's knowledge of history and war tactics.

Her confidence should have a personal or practical basis. The relationship between the women remains to be settled; childhood friendship is an old option, not a confirmed decision.

Alaric connects Darcy's knowledge with the armies, machines and destruction on Gerolt's land. He hopes she could help him understand who he is.

Marta wants Darcy freed whether or not she can answer his questions. Alaric's inference does not establish that Darcy knows his erased past.

### 16. Alaric asks to join

Alaric volunteers. He wants to help Marta, repay Gerolt's kindness, protect someone being used and pursue his own questions.

Silas refuses because the boy is injured, wanted and untrained. Those objections remain credible.

Alaric persists, but insistence alone should not reverse the refusal. He must offer something useful or accept a defined, limited task.

**Recommended initial objective:** contact and assessment before extraction. Alaric's firsthand account of the field gives him a reason to speak with Darcy; the specific route must explain whether that makes his presence useful.

His contribution and Silas's conditions remain open. Participation is not payment for shelter.

**Recommended ending:** finish on a concrete next step or changed agreement that starts the attempt. The actual rescue is not completed in Chapter 11.

## Continuity and later consequences

- Maintain close Alaric POV. Silas's history, Marta's plans and Brand's intentions need observable evidence or conversation.
- The boy's face and name are not established search identifiers. Wena, the strange clothes and the sword can still expose him.
- Marta and Silas are already known to Alaric as married. Do not repeat the disclosure.
- Keep the adult workers' individual choices and household concerns established in Chapter 9.
- Do not repurpose the large guest as Redd. Redd and Freya's introductions remain separate design work.
- Reports travel physically. No instant magical communication is needed for this chapter.
- Brand's younger age should be respected if dates are later attached to the earlier killings.
- A new appearance of the double is not proposed here. The aftermath of the first vision is enough.
- The Volume 1 attempt must eventually change something, even though the successful rescue remains in Volume 2. Its obstacle and consequence require design before that encounter is drafted.

## Main unresolved choices

1. Search precautions, clothing, Wena's care and the location of the sword.
2. Silas's information source and how much Alaric learns about Avarice.
3. The twins' ages, individual behaviour, separate or joint sale, and outcome.
4. Brand's reason for being in Kelmend, recognition of Silas, public reception and whether he notices Alaric.
5. The first lesson's placement, physical limits and location.
6. The Empty disclosure and how both listeners learn it.
7. The Day 3 drink and the source and timing of the Day 4 report.
8. Darcy's destination, departure time and the practical change caused by transfer.
9. Marta's relationship with Darcy, the people endangered and what she knows of Darcy's reasons for staying.
10. The meeting with Silas, Alaric's useful role and the final agreement.

The exact guest name, food and title can be chosen later.

## Pacing and the 100/100 ambition

An initial estimate is roughly 5,500–7,000 words, governed by what the conversations and scenes require. Do not expand to meet the estimate or compress ordinary life solely to reach the mission.

The test is whether the chapter becomes indispensable:

- The inn's people matter before the plot needs them.
- Alaric and Silas learn something about each other during the outing.
- The second auction changes Alaric's response rather than repeating an explanation.
- Brand is memorable through behaviour and Silas's personal reaction.
- Marta's request has a history and costs her something.
- Alaric's final request grows from both kindness and his search for himself.
- The chapter pays off its conversations and begins a concrete next movement.

The chapter starts with Alaric doubting who he is. It ends with him asking for a part in what happens next.
