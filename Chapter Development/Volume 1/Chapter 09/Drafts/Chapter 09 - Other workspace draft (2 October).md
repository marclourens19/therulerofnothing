*Chapter 9*

"The old fool sent me."

Alaric said it to the shut door. He had meant to say it when Marta first opened it, before she saw Silas, before any of the shouting. Now the words were out, and he stood with Gerolt's sword hanging from his hand, waiting to find out whether she had heard.

The footsteps inside stopped.

Wena put her nose back to the gap beneath the door. Silas said nothing. Then the bolt scraped, and Marta opened it again.

"Where'd ye hear that?"

There was more light behind her this time. Alaric could see her properly, and for a moment, despite everything, he understood why the men in the yard had talked about her. Then her eyes dropped to the sword, and he remembered what he had come to tell her.

"Gerolt. He told me to show you this." He lifted it a little. "But before—"

She saw Silas still beside him.

"Are ye waiting for me to come out and drag ye away myself?"

"Please," Alaric said. "There's something I have to tell you."

"Aye, and I'll hear ye once he's—"

"There are guards coming here!"

Marta turned back to him. His voice had been too loud. He glanced down the lane, towards the corner they had watched for so long, and lowered it.

"We heard two of them. They're finishing their round, then they're coming here to ask you about Gerolt. And they're looking for a boy with a big dog."

Her eyes went to Wena.

"They're looking for *you*? I don't want trouble in this house, lad. Ye'll have to leave."

Her hand shifted on the door. Alaric stepped forward before he could stop himself, then stopped with his bad foot barely touching the ground.

"Please. I don't have anywhere else to go." His voice shook. "Gerolt sent me to you."

Marta looked down at the sword again. Wena had come forward to sniff her fingers, her tail moving against Silas's leg. Alaric waited while Marta stood with her hand on the door and the dog pressed close enough to touch her.

She let out a long breath and stepped back.

"Oh, get in, then. And keep yer voice down."

Her eyes returned to Silas.

"That means the boy. Don't ye use me uncle to talk yer way back into this house."

"The message isn't mine, Marta. He had your name before I ever met him."

"Then let *him* speak. I don't want another bloody word out of you."

Silas handed Alaric Wena's lead. Their fingers met over the cloth, and Alaric saw the brown and black binding on his other hand. He wanted to ask whether Silas would still be there when he came back. Marta was waiting for him to move, and the guards could turn the corner at any moment.

He took Wena inside.

Marta let them pass, then stood in the doorway again.

"Ye aren't setting foot under me roof."

"I'll stay outside."

"Aye. Ye fucking will."

She shut the door before he could say anything else.

Alaric stood where she had left him, with Wena's lead in one hand and the sword in the other. The room beyond the passage was much bigger than Gerolt's cabin. Tables filled it, some with chairs still standing round them and others with chairs already turned upside down on top. There were mugs waiting to be washed near the bar. A damp cloth lay across the nearest table, and a lamp burned beside it.

Someone moved upstairs. Water splashed somewhere behind the bar, and a woman's voice complained about a jug that had been put in the wrong place.

The inn had gone on working while they waited outside.

"That foot," Marta said. "Can ye put weight on it?"

"Some."

"Then come over here before ye drop that blade on it."

She pulled a chair out at the table beneath the lamp. Alaric sat down and laid the sword across his knees. Wena came straight under the table, though she kept her head turned towards the passage.

Marta did not sit.

"Why've ye got that? Where's Gerolt?"

"He gave it to me."

"Well, the old fool can come and tell me himself. Where is he?"

Alaric had tried to think of a way to say it while they waited in the lane. He had got as far as Gerolt giving him the sword, and the arrows, and Silas coming through the trees. None of it made the next words easier.

"He can't. Not anymore. He—he's dead."

Marta's face went still. Her lips were parted, but she said nothing.

Alaric lowered his eyes. The lamp shone on the blade across his knees. There were dark marks near the grip that he had not been able to wipe away with his sleeve.

Neither of them spoke.

"Look at me, lad."

He did.

"Tell me what happened. Don't just tell me he's dead."

"I think they wanted to find out what happened on his farm, but they broke the door in, and he started fighting them. I still don't understand why. We ran, but they followed us, and there were arrows…" His fingers closed round the hilt. "He told me to run. I didn't. I couldn't. I couldn't leave him behind."

Marta kept staring at him. He waited for her to say something, but her expression did not change.

"He stood there fighting them while ye—"

"I know." His face twisted. "I know I should have done something. I had his sword right here, and I couldn't even—"

The words stopped. He could still see Gerolt kneeling, still waiting for Alaric to take Wena and move. He had been shouting at him. Gerolt had arrows in him, and Alaric had been shouting because he was afraid of being left alone.

Marta pulled out the chair beside him and missed the seat with her first attempt to sit.

Alaric reached towards her. She caught the edge of the table instead and lowered herself into the chair, keeping hold of it until she was down.

"Give me that," she said, looking at the sword. "Careful. Let me hold it a moment."

He laid it in her hands.

Marta ran her thumb over the worn grip. She turned it a little towards the lamp, then back again. Alaric moved his hands into his lap. Without the sword, he could see how dirty they were.

"He only met ye yesterday?"

"That morning."

She nodded once, still looking down at it. When she tried to speak again, nothing came.

Wena pushed her head out from beneath the table. Marta put one hand down, and Wena pressed into it at once. Her thumb moved over the dog's forehead while her other hand stayed on the sword.

"He brought me to his house," Alaric said. "I couldn't walk. He carried me, and he gave me something to eat. I didn't know there was anywhere else I could go."

Marta shut her eyes, her fingers tightening on the grip. Alaric stopped.

Footsteps came towards them from behind the bar.

A woman stood at the end of the table with a stack of clean mugs in her hands. She looked at Alaric, then at the sword. Her gaze stayed on Marta.

"What's happened?"

Marta opened her eyes, but did not turn towards her.

"Gerolt's dead."

The woman lowered the mugs to the table. She set them down one at a time, though they had all been stacked together.

"Oh, Marta."

Marta shook her head. The woman left the mugs where they were and stayed beside her chair.

Alaric looked towards the back passage. He could not hear Silas through the door. He wondered whether Marta could, whether that was why she had turned her head the same way.

"And Silas?" she asked. "Where was he while this was happening?"

"He came after Gerolt was hit. He killed the last of them, then dragged me away."

"After." Marta looked down at the sword. "Aye."

Alaric shifted in his chair.

"There were more coming. I could hear them. He got us across the river, and he—"

"And brought ye to me door."

"Gerolt sent me. Silas helped me get here."

She looked at him then. Alaric held her gaze, though he wanted to look away.

"I'm not saying ye owe him nothing," Marta said. "I'm saying I know the man standing outside me house."

She moved her hand off Wena's head and wiped her eyes with the back of it. Alaric looked down before she had finished.

"It was my fault," he said.

Marta's hand stopped on the table.

"They wanted something from me. If he hadn't found me—"

"Don't."

He shut his mouth.

"I can't hear that just now." She swallowed. "I can't, lad."

The woman beside her touched her shoulder. Marta covered that hand for a moment, then let go.

"Ye said guards were coming."

Alaric nodded.

"To ask about me uncle?"

"And a boy with a big dog. They said to bring me in alive."

The woman's hand fell from Marta's shoulder.

"Here?"

"After they finish their round," Alaric said. "We heard them in a yard near the wall."

She looked towards the front of the inn, then back at Marta.

"Marta, they're coming here looking for him. We've got guests upstairs. And the rest of us live here too. You can't just decide this and expect us to—"

"Gerolt sent him here. I'm not turning him out."

"Don't look at me like that. I'm not asking you to hand him over. I'm asking what happens to us if they find him."

Marta looked away from her, towards the passage.

"I haven't got an answer ye'll like."

"Then don't talk as if it's settled just because you've said so."

Alaric began to stand.

"I can leave. I didn't mean to bring all this here."

The woman put a hand out to stop him.

"Stay where you are a moment. You'll fall over before you reach the door."

He sat back down. Wena came out from under the table, ready to follow him.

The woman watched her, then looked back at Marta.

"He can use my room while you talk to them. It's empty, and it's better than having him sitting here when they come knocking."

"Ye don't have to do that."

"I know. I'm offering." She took Wena's lead from his hand. "But we're talking about this again when they've gone."

Marta nodded.

The woman turned towards the stairs. Another woman had come down halfway and was standing with a folded cloth over her arm.

"He's asking for you," she said.

"Tell him I'm finished for tonight."

"Do you want me to—"

"Yes. Send him on. I'll speak to you in a minute."

The other woman went back up. Alaric got up more carefully this time.

Marta held the sword out, grip first. He took it carefully. She let go without looking at him.

"Thank you. I'm… Alaric."

"Rhose." She put his free hand on her arm. "Come on, then, Alaric. Lean on me if you need to."

He did. There was nowhere else to put his weight without his ankle giving, and she stood still until he had found his balance.

"Keep the point down," she said. "And take your time. I don't want you falling on both of us."

They went up together, Wena ahead of them until the lead brought her back. At the turn in the stairs Alaric looked down. Marta still sat at the table beneath the lamp, with her empty hands resting where the sword had been.

Rhose stopped at a door close to the top of the stairs. She let him lean against the wall while she opened it, then brought him inside.

There was a bed, a chest beneath the window and a small table with a brush and a few pins scattered across it. A dress hung over the back of a chair. Rhose moved it onto the bed and put the chair near the door.

"Sit here for now."

Alaric sat, resting the sword across his thighs. Rhose drew the curtains together, then came back and opened the door just enough to listen.

Wena stood beside his knee, her ears raised. He rubbed the side of her neck and kept his hand on her collar.

"Do you think they'll come upstairs?" he whispered.

"I don't know." Rhose looked back at him. "Don't call out if they do. Let Marta talk."

Alaric nodded. From where he sat, he could see only a narrow strip of the passage and the wall beyond it. Anyone turning towards the room would disappear from view before they reached the door.

He moved the sword off his knees and laid it beside the chair. He could not keep it still across his legs.

Rhose watched him do it.

"How long since you slept?"

"This morning. A few hours."

"You look it."

He wanted to ask whether she was afraid. She had asked Marta what would happen to them, and Marta had not been able to answer. Now Rhose stood at her own door with a boy the guards wanted sitting among her things.

He looked at the dress on her bed and the pins on the table. He had not known this room existed when he knocked downstairs.

*If they find me here—*

Voices sounded outside. Rhose raised one hand, and Alaric stopped moving.

The front door opened below.

"We've shut the bar, lads."

"Not here for a drink."

Alaric knew that voice. The man in the yard, angry about the elves, had sounded the same even when he tried to speak quietly.

"That makes a change," Marta said. "What can I do for ye?"

"Gerolt's your uncle, isn't he?"

"Aye. What's this about?"

"When was he last here?"

"His last delivery. If ye need the day, I'll check the book. Why are ye asking?"

"They found him in the woods by the river. He's dead, Marta. I'm sorry."

Alaric pressed his hand against his mouth.

He had known. He had watched Gerolt's fire go out, and Wena trying to get her head beneath his hand, and the trees moving between them as Silas dragged him away. He had just told Marta himself.

Someone had found him. Someone else had seen him lying there and gone to tell these men.

Downstairs, a chair scraped across the floor.

"Where've they taken him?"

"The elves had him when the report came in. Nobody's told us where they've taken him since."

"The elves?"

"That's all we know."

"Can I see him?"

"I don't know, Marta. Nobody's told us where he is."

Alaric lowered his hand. Wena's head had turned towards the stairs. He put his fingers beneath her collar again, though she had made no sound.

Rhose kept her eyes on the gap in the door. Her hand was still raised, but lower now, close to the latch.

There was no answer from Marta. After a while, the other man spoke. Alaric remembered him telling his companion to keep his voice down in the yard.

"Has anyone come from the farm tonight? A boy, especially. With a big dog."

"No."

"Anyone asking after Gerolt?"

"Not before you two. What d'ye want this boy for?"

"Orders are to bring him in alive. We haven't been told why."

"Haven't even given us a fucking face," the first guard said.

"Will you leave it?"

"I'm just saying."

"Well, ye can say it quieter," Marta told him. "There's people sleeping above yer head."

"Sorry."

Alaric looked towards Rhose. She had pressed her lips together, listening. Wena shifted against his leg, and the lead slid across the floor. He gathered it before the loose end reached the door.

"If he turns up, send someone for us."

"Aye. Come back tomorrow when ye've finished work. I'll put a drink aside for ye."

"A drink for me?"

"Listen to him," the other man said. "She smiles at him once and he forgets I'm standing here."

"Fuck off. I was asking."

"Both of ye," Marta said. "And don't come arguing on me doorstep. I've enough of that without paying ye in drink for it."

Alaric heard her laugh. He looked down at his hands. A few minutes ago she had been sitting beside him, trying to speak and unable to. He could not understand how she had made that sound come out of her now.

Boots moved towards the front door. It opened, and one of the men said goodnight. Marta answered him. Then it shut.

Rhose stayed where she was until the footsteps outside had gone. Even then she waited, her head turned towards the stairs.

At last she opened the door wider.

"All right. Come on."

Alaric picked up the sword. His hand had cramped from holding Wena's collar, and he had to straighten his fingers before he could grip it properly.

Rhose looked down at the dog.

"You were quiet, at least."

Wena stood and followed her out. Alaric used the back of the chair to get himself up, then went after them.

Marta was on her feet when they reached the common room. The sword had left a mark on the table where it had rested. She kept wiping that place with the cloth, though there was nothing loose left to wipe away.

Alaric stopped beside the chair.

"They know he's dead."

"Aye."

"They've taken him."

"I heard."

He sat down. He had no idea what else to say. He wanted to ask whether she would go to find Gerolt, but the guards had not known where he was, and he could not bear to make her tell him that again.

Marta put the cloth down and went behind the bar. She came back with a mug of water.

"Drink."

He took it in both hands. Rhose drew out the other chair, but remained standing behind it.

"I meant what I said," Alaric told them. "I can go. You let me in before you knew everything. If I stay, they'll keep coming here."

Marta watched him over the table.

"And where would ye go?"

He glanced towards the back passage.

"Silas might still be outside."

"He might. Ye'd still be leaving with a bad foot and a dog they've been told to look for."

"I don't want them hurting you because I came here."

Marta rested both hands on the back of the empty chair.

"Ye warned me before ye came in. I heard ye. Ye can stay tonight."

Alaric looked at Rhose. She had not sat down. He remembered her saying that the rest of them lived here too, and he waited.

"Upstairs," she said. "You stay upstairs. If you need something, ask one of us. Don't come down just because you hear people talking."

He nodded.

"And if they come back?"

"Then we hear them before they see you." She glanced at Marta. "But we still need to talk."

"We will." Marta took up the cloth again. "Take him up when he's finished that. I'll bring him something to eat."

Alaric drank. The water tasted of the mug, faintly, and he did not mind. He had not noticed how dry his mouth was until he started.

"What about Silas?"

Marta kept wiping the table.

"What about him?"

"Will you let him in now they've gone? His hand's hurt. He burnt it getting me away from—"

"I heard ye, lad. He isn't staying here."

Alaric pulled at the edge of the cloak.

"This is his. I should give it back if he's still outside."

"Ye can give it back tomorrow. Silas has coin. Let him pay for a bed somewhere his wife doesn't have to look at him."

Alaric's hand stopped on the cloak.

"His wife?"

Marta set the cloth down.

"Aye. Me."

She sighed and picked it up again.

"Leave it at that, Alaric."

He nodded. He had meant to ask where Silas would sleep. Now he could not think of anything to say without making it worse.

Rhose touched the back of his chair. He finished the water and set the mug down, taking care not to put it in the place Marta had been wiping.

"Come back down when he's settled," Marta told her. "We'll talk about it."

Rhose nodded, then held out her arm to Alaric again.

He took it.

They went past Rhose's door this time. She opened another a little further along the passage and reached inside to light the candle on the table.

"This one's yours. You can leave your things here now."

Alaric followed her in with the sword and Wena. The bed was made. A basin waited on the table, with a folded cloth beside it. There was a chair against the wall and enough space at the foot of the bed for Wena to lie down without anyone stepping over her.

He stood in the doorway until Rhose turned back towards him.

"Thank you. For helping me, and letting me stay."

"That's all right. Come on, sit down. Let me help you with that boot."

He sat on the bed and laid Gerolt's sword beside him. Silas's cloak slipped from his shoulders, and he pulled it off and folded it across the end of the mattress.

Rhose bent towards his boot. His eyes dropped to the cleavage above the low neckline of her dress, and he turned away at once, his face burning.

"W-wait. I can do it myself, please."

She gave a quiet laugh.

"It's only your boot, Alaric."

She stood beside him and took his arm, leaning close enough that her chest pressed against his shoulder.

"There's nothing to be embarrassed about."

He looked up at her, then away again. Even his ears felt hot.

"I wasn't trying to stare."

"Now I've had a proper look at you…" She leaned closer, smiling. "You're a handsome one, you know."

"Thank you."

She laughed and let go of his arm. Then she moved back and left him room to bend towards his foot.

He loosened the boot, keeping his eyes on his hands. When he tried to pull it past his heel, pain ran up his leg and he stopped, still holding it.

Rhose waited beside him. He could see her feet on the boards and the hem of her dress, but he was too embarrassed to look up.

"Could you hold my ankle still?"

"Of course."

She knelt again and supported his foot with one hand. When she eased the leather past his heel, he gripped the mattress.

"Hurts there?"

He nodded.

She worked it off more slowly. The swelling showed as soon as the boot was gone. He had kept walking on it all day. Looking at it now, in a room where he did not have to get up and follow anyone, he could feel every place it hurt.

Rhose put a folded blanket beneath his foot.

"Keep it up for a while."

He sat back and let out the breath he had been holding. Then he pulled the other boot off himself.

"There's water there, and a cloth," she said. "Do what you can tonight. Marta's bringing something to eat."

He reached for the basin. It was close enough that he could wash without getting up. The water darkened when he put his hands in it, and dirt came away from the lines in his palms. He rubbed at his fingers until Rhose put the cloth within his reach.

"Use that."

He wiped his hands, then his face. There was dried dirt at his temple, near the place that had hurt when Gerolt found him. He had to hold the wet cloth against it before it loosened.

When he lowered the cloth, the water was moving. He waited for it to settle.

A face looked back at him.

Alaric leaned closer. The face came closer too. He parted his lips, and its mouth moved. When he touched the corner of his own mouth, the same hand rose in the basin.

He stayed bent over it.

Rhose was gathering his boots.

"What's wrong?"

"I don't remember looking like this."

She put the boots down and came a little closer, looking from the water to him.

"You don't remember your own face?"

"I don't remember anything from before yesterday." He touched his cheek. "I thought I'd recognise it."

The room was quiet. He could hear Wena turning at the foot of the bed, settling herself on the floor.

Rhose stood beside him. He waited for another question, for her to ask how he could have forgotten, or what he had done. Instead she moved the candle closer to the basin.

"I'll leave that here."

His face grew clearer in the water. There was dirt he had missed beside one eye. He wiped it away, then looked again.

"Thank you."

When he looked up, Rhose had stepped into the passage and was pulling the door gently shut.

Alaric stayed over the basin until a drop fell from his chin and broke the reflection. Then he sat back, with the cloth resting between his hands.

*That's me.*

He looked at Gerolt's sword beside him, then at the folded cloak. Both had belonged to someone before he knew their names. His own clothes fitted him perfectly, and he could not remember putting them on.

There was a knock.

He straightened too quickly. Water tipped over the edge of the basin onto the table.

"Alaric?" Marta's voice. "I've brought ye something."

"Yes. Come in."

She carried a plate in one hand and a mug in the other. He reached for the sword to move it, but she had already put the plate on the table, clear of the water.

Bread, and something warm in a small bowl. He could smell it before he looked properly.

Marta set the mug down.

"Eat what ye can. Don't leave it till it's cold."

"What about Wena?"

"I'll fetch her something."

Wena had got up when Marta entered. She came close and waited beside her skirt. Marta bent to touch her head, and stayed there a moment longer than Alaric expected.

He looked down at the plate.

"Will you be able to find out where they've taken him?"

Marta straightened.

"I'll ask."

He nodded. He wanted to go with her. He could hardly stand, and the men downstairs had been told to bring him in alive. He left the question where it was.

Marta looked at the basin, then at the cloth in his hands.

"Ye can stay put tomorrow. Get some rest. We'll see what can be found out when there's people awake to ask."

"About the farm?"

"About what's happened. I can't give ye an answer tonight."

"I know."

She went to the door, then stopped with her hand on it.

"Gerolt brought ye into his house?"

"Yes."

She nodded, looking towards the floor.

"Eat, lad."

She went out.

Alaric picked up the bread. His hands had stopped shaking enough to hold it. He ate slowly at first, then found himself tearing off the next piece before he had finished the one in his mouth.

Marta came back with a bowl for Wena and set it near the door. Wena followed her hand down and began eating at once. Marta left them with the candle still burning.

The inn made small sounds round him. A door opening, someone crossing the passage, voices downstairs too low for him to make out. Once he thought he heard Rhose's voice, but he could not catch the words. He hoped Marta was listening to her.

When he had finished, he put the plate on the table and set Gerolt's sword beside the bed, with the grip within reach. Silas's cloak lay folded at the foot of the bed. Wena had settled close to the mattress, with her head on her paws.

He lay down.

Alaric watched the candle for a while.

Tomorrow he would not have to get up and walk straight away. He could stay in this room while the streets filled outside. Someone would know he was here. Someone had said he could stay.

He closed his eyes before the thought could go any further.

* * *

Alaric opened his eyes.

He lay still for a moment, listening. Something had woken him, but he could not remember a sound. The room was dark, and the sheet had twisted round one of his legs.

Then he saw the blood.

It was seeping beneath the door, gathering in the gaps between the floorboards. As he watched, a narrow thread reached the foot of the bed.

He pulled his feet away from it.

*What happened?*

He got out on the other side and crossed the room, keeping close to the wall. His hand shook so badly that he missed the bolt before he found it.

The door caught on something when he opened it.

An arm.

Rhose lay outside his room, her head turned towards the stairs. He knew her before he saw what had been done to her throat. The cut had opened it beneath the jaw, and the blood spreading from her neck had reached his doorway.

He backed into the doorframe. His stomach cramped, and he bent over, retching onto the boards. When he tried to straighten, he saw her again and could not keep the next breath down.

*Marta.*

He needed Marta.

Alaric stepped round Rhose's body and went towards the stairs. Blood covered the top step. He held the rail and lowered himself one step at a time, trying not to look at where his feet landed.

The common room was lit orange. Through the windows he could see flames, and beyond the glass someone was screaming.

Marta lay beside the bar.

He stopped halfway down the last flight.

Her dress was torn open across her stomach. Blood had soaked the cloth and spread beneath her, reaching the legs of the nearest table. One hand rested palm-up on the floor. Her eyes were open.

Wena stood beside her, pushing her nose against that hand.

"Marta?"

He went to her slowly. The blood was warm beneath his bare feet. He crouched and reached for her shoulder, but stopped before he touched it.

"Marta, please…"

Wena whined. She pushed at Marta's hand again, then looked up at him.

Alaric tried to call for someone. He got no further than opening his mouth before his stomach clenched again. He bent over beside the table, coughing until his eyes watered.

Outside, a crash shook the windows.

He had to find someone. Someone who could help them.

He got up, using the table, and crossed to the front door.

Heat struck him as he opened it.

The buildings opposite were burning from the ground floor to the roofs. Fire came through the windows and spread beneath the edges of the roofs. A beam fell into the street, scattering sparks across bodies lying on the stones.

He recognised the market stalls they had passed that evening. Their cloth covers were gone. Broken carts lay overturned among the wreckage, their wheels burning, and the people beside them were blackened and still.

A man lay close to the inn's step with one leg missing below the knee. Alaric could see the white end of the bone. Blood filled the cracks between the stones beneath him.

Further along, someone was trying to pull another person out from under a fallen wall. They dragged at an arm, slipped and tried again. A roof collapsed behind them, and both disappeared beneath the falling timber.

Alaric took a step towards them.

There was no way through.

The street was blocked by burning wood, fallen masonry and bodies. He turned towards the next one and found the same thing. Everywhere he could see, windows blazed above wrecked doorways. Flames rose beyond the shattered roofs, across the town, until he could no longer tell one burning building from the next.

People were still screaming somewhere inside them. He tried to follow one voice, then another, but the crashes and the fire swallowed the words. He could not find where any of them were.

Alaric stood on the bloodied stones, unable to get a full breath.

A figure stood further down the street.

His back was towards Alaric. He wore the black clothes Alaric had woken in on Gerolt's field, and he was facing the fires. A burning roof gave way in front of him. He did not move.

Then he began to turn.

Slowly, his shoulders came round, and his face followed.

Alaric saw his own eyes. His own mouth. The face he had seen in the water before he went to bed.

"This is who you are, Alaric."

The voice was his too.

*No. I didn't—*

Rhose's torn throat.

Marta's open eyes.

Blood beneath his door.

Alaric woke with his hands twisted in the sheet.

He dragged in a breath and could not stop taking more. His shirt clung to him. Pain drove through his ankle when he moved, and his head hurt so badly that he pressed both hands against it.

There were footsteps outside.

"Alaric, keep it down, will you? I've only just got back to bed."

He lifted his head.

It was Rhose's voice.

*She's alive.*

The footsteps went away. A door opened further along the passage, then closed.

Alaric stared at his own door. Nothing moved beneath it.
