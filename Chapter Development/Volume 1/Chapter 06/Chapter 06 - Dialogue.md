# Chapter 6 — Dialogue refinement

Working dialogue pass, 30 September 2026. The chapter title remains open. These excerpts follow the agreed sequence; they are proposed wording for review, not a replacement for the full chapter.

## D1 — Waking

Silas nudged his boot with the toe of his own.

“Up, boy. You've had a few hours. We need to get moving.”

Alaric eased his arm out from under Wena and sat up. There was daylight at the entrance now.

*We. So he meant it.*

“You're coming all the way to Kelmend?”

“I'm not sending you up to those guards on your own. Get your things.”

Silas bent to pick up the sack. He still hadn't asked Alaric's name.

## D2 — Wena and the food

“Your dog's a thief.”

Alaric looked down. Wena had her nose inside the sack.

“She's hungry.”

“So am I. Get her head out of my breakfast.”

He lifted the sack as Alaric caught her collar. Wena came away with a piece of bread between her teeth.

“Wena, don't—”

She swallowed it.

Silas looked into the sack. “Oh, for fuck's sake.”

Alaric laughed, still holding her collar. Wena was trying to follow the sack again.

“Here.” Silas broke an end off the next piece and held it out to Alaric. “Give her that before she takes the rest. And eat yours, will you? That's what I got it out for.”

## D3 — The smoke

“Boy.”

Silas had stopped further along the path. Alaric kept looking across the river.

“Just a moment.”

Silas didn't answer. His footsteps didn't start again either.

Wena leaned against Alaric's leg. He lowered his hand to her neck and stood with her a little longer.

Then he turned away.

“Come on, Wena.”

## D4 — Distance and the road

“How far is Kelmend?”

“Far enough that you'll want to walk faster than that.”

“Could you just tell me?”

Silas glanced back. “Keep moving, and we'll get there before dark.”

*Only the rest of the day, then. Lovely.*

Alaric stepped over the root Silas had gone round. “Wouldn't the road be quicker?”

“It would. It also takes you straight to the gate, and you saw who those guards were keeping company with.”

“So how do we get in?”

“From the south, if the way's clear. I'll have a look when we get closer.”

## D5 — The warning

Silas stopped beside the remains of the deer. He turned one of its legs with his boot.

“Ogre.”

“What's an ogre?”

“Big ugly bastard. Eats anything it can get hold of. Don't let your dog go pushing into the brush.”

Alaric looked between the trees. “Is it still here?”

“That one's had its fill, at least. It'll be lying up somewhere, I'd say.” Silas stepped past the carcass. “Come on. Keep her with you.”

## D6 — The neighbours

The woman held the fence rail while the man worked at its end.

“Still smoking over at Gerolt's,” she said.

“Burning off that back field again, I suppose. Told the old fool he'd smoke us out one day.”

“Someone ought to go over. See if he's all right.”

The man looked towards the river. “Not past those riders. We'll leave it till they've gone.”

Alaric looked at Silas.

“Do you know them?”

Silas's eyes stayed on the woman.

“Silas?”

He turned away from the fence.

“Move.”

## D7 — Gerolt and Marta

“You knew him, didn't you?”

“Watch where you're putting your feet.”

“Silas, I asked you last night. You never answered.”

“I heard you just fine. Leave it.”

Alaric followed him over the fallen trunk.

“You knew his name. And when I said Marta—”

“She keeps an inn. Half of Kelmend knows her.”

“Then tell me about her. Is there a reason I shouldn't go there? Because if you know one, you could just tell me.”

Silas stopped and looked back at him.

“All right. Listen. Here's what we're doing when we reach Kelmend.”

## D8 — The sword and the arrangement

“You wait outside the wall with the dog. I'll take the sword to Marta and find out whether it's safe.”

Alaric looked down at the hilt in his hand.

“He told *me* to show it to her.”

“And if they're watching the inn? You walk in, they take you, and they take her along with you.”

“Then we wait till it's dark. You check first. I'm not asking you to walk straight up to the guards.” Alaric looked at him again. “But I'm going in with you, and I'm showing her the sword.”

Silas looked down the slope.

“We'll see.”

“Silas—”

“Stay here a minute. I need a look at what's below us.”

He was already moving between the trees.

## D9 — Alaric decides to leave

*We'll see.*

Alaric watched until he could no longer make out Silas's cloak.

*So he takes the sword to Marta, and I sit outside with Wena until he decides what happens next. What if she won't let him in? Am I supposed to go somewhere else because he says so?*

He shifted the sword in his hand. Gerolt had pressed that hilt into his fingers and told him where to go.

*I can keep off the road. The town's beyond that rise—he pointed it out himself. I'll find the south gate, and then the inn.*

Wena was looking in the direction Silas had gone. Alaric touched her shoulder.

“Come on, girl. This way.”

She didn't move at first.

*And that ogre's eaten already. Silas said it'd be lying up somewhere. I just have to get through here.*

He went on, and after a few steps she followed.

## D10 — The ogre's words

It lifted him closer to its face.

“Good eating.”

The words were clear. It sounded pleased, as though someone had put a bowl in front of it.

*It talks?*

“Put me down!” Alaric twisted towards the ground. “Put me down!”

“Put me down,” it repeated.

Then its mouth opened.

## D11 — After the kill

“Would it kill you to listen, boy? Just once?”

*Nearly.*

Alaric tried to stand. His ankle gave, and he caught himself against the tree.

“I'm sorry. I thought I could get there.”

“You nearly got both of you eaten.” Silas came over. “Sit down. I want a look at that foot before you try walking on it.”

## D12 — Gerolt and the new agreement

Alaric watched him pull the loosened cloth away from his hand.

“How long had you known Gerolt?”

Silas stopped pulling.

“I knew him for years.” He looked down at the cloth again. “Can we leave it for now?”

Alaric nodded. After a while, he said, “I wasn't trying to make things harder. I was trying to get to her.”

“And I was trying to get you there without bringing those bastards down on her inn.”

“Then tell me that. You wouldn't tell me anything about her, and then you said you were taking the sword and I had to wait outside.”

“I wasn't going to leave you sitting there all night.”

“How was I meant to know that? You said, ‘We'll see.’”

Silas let the cloth rest across his knee.

“There's a way along the south wall. That's what I was checking. When we get there, I'll have a look at the inn. If it's clear, we both go in, and you show her the sword yourself. If there are riders, we wait. All right?”

“If you tell me what you're checking. Don't just tell me to sit somewhere and disappear.”

“Fine. But no more wandering off.”

“I won't.”

## D13 — The name and the bandage

Silas tried to catch the loose end of the binding. It slipped out of his fingers.

“Here.” Alaric reached for it. “Let me hold that.”

Silas handed him the end and began drawing the cloth round his palm.

“It's Alaric, by the way.”

Silas looked up.

“My name. You never asked.”

“All right, Alaric.” He drew the cloth across again. “Keep hold of that while I tie it.”

Alaric held it steady. Silas finished the knot, then opened his hand a little and shut it again.

“That'll do. Give me the sack. We're not staying here.”

## D14 — The ship

The shape overhead kept growing. Alaric found the bow, then the dark red plating behind it, and the spines beneath. Blue-green light ran along its underside.

“What *is* that?”

Silas had stopped beside him.

“Natharul's.”

A bell began ringing in Kelmend. Alaric looked towards the town, then back up at the ship.

“Is it coming here?”

“No. It's going past us.”

Silas watched it turn out over the river. Then he looked at Alaric, and at the sword hanging from his hand.

“All those bodies. You said they were on Gerolt's land?”

“Yes. I told you last night.”

Silas looked towards the river again. Alaric waited for another question.

Instead, Silas stepped back towards the trees.

“Alaric. Get Wena into the trees.”

## Notes for the full draft

- The ogre restraint should last a short, strenuous interval: its momentum and Silas's leverage put it against the trunk, and he cannot safely release the weapon while it is reaching for Alaric and Wena. The blade does not magically immobilise the whole creature simply by entering its ribs.
- “Size first” means the enormous ship registers before secondary details. Omitting smaller craft is a workable choice, but was not required by that description.
- The statement about the fed ogre resting is Silas's judgment, which Alaric uses to reassure himself. The narration must not establish it as a reliable rule or guarantee that the forest is safe.
- D7 deliberately removes the claim that Silas knew Marta's name before Alaric finished speaking: in Chapter 3, his reaction follows Alaric's full explanation.
- The new question in D14 lets Silas begin taking the battlefield account seriously through conduct. It neither confirms the vessel's mission nor gives him knowledge of who is aboard.
- D10's repetition of Alaric's words demonstrates mimicry. Its timing and delivery should remain unsettling rather than become a comic exchange.
- Revisit “What could make this chapter 100/100?” against the complete scenes. The dialogue should retain interruptions, practical work, imperfect understanding and emotional variation; these excerpts are not a set of lines to insert mechanically.
