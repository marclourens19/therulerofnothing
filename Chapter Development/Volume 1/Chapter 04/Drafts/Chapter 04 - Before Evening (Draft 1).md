# Chapter 4 – Before Evening

Her mana moved.

In all of Seralune's life it had never once left her. It had only ever sat inside her, more of it than anyone had found the end of, and done nothing at all. Now it was pouring out of her, all at once, through her chest and her hands and the backs of her eyes, towards somewhere so far away that she couldn't have said where.

It found something there, and took hold.

Everything around her cracked. The sound went through her teeth and into her bones, and someone was screaming, and it was her, and the whole world went white.

Then it was black, and she fell, and hit stone.

Her mana came back into her as fast as it had gone. It settled where it always settled, heavy and full, and was still.

Stone pressed against her cheek. The cold of it had got into that whole side of her face and into the fingers curled under her chin, and she couldn't tell whether her eyes were open or shut. She lifted a hand and touched them, and her lashes brushed her fingertips.

They were open.

*Am I dead?*

*No. Don't be ridiculous. Surely the dead don't get cold, and I'm freezing.*

*So I'm alive, and lying on a stone floor I don't know, somewhere so black I can't see my own hand. Very well. Before I do anything else, I need to find out where I am.*

She got an elbow under herself and pushed. Her arm shook and the floor seemed to tip, and she had to put her cheek back down on the stone and wait for it to stop.

The second time, she made it to sitting. Her hair had come loose and was everywhere, across her face and down her back and caught under her hand, and when she moved her knee, something small and hard skittered away across the stone, ringing as it went. She held still and listened until it stopped.

"Hello?"

Her voice went out a long way before it came back to her. The room was big, then, bigger than any of hers, and there was nobody in it to answer.

There was no other sound at all. She held her breath to be sure, and heard only her own heart.

*I can't hear the falls.*

She had never been anywhere in the palace where she couldn't hear the falls. They were there under everything, day and night, so constant that she hardly ever noticed them.

*Then I'm not in the palace. Or I'm somewhere very deep in it.*

She tried to stand, and her knees folded before she was halfway up, and she came down hard on both hands. Her legs had never done that to her before, not once, and it frightened her more than not being able to see.

*Then I'll crawl. It's hardly dignified, but at least no one can see me.*

The floor was gritty under her palms. The small hard pieces were everywhere, some no bigger than her fingernail and some as long as her finger, all of them smooth, with edges that caught at her skin, and she had only crawled a little way when her fingers met something that wasn't the floor.

It rose straight up in front of her, smooth and cold, far colder than the stone. She put both hands flat on it and followed it up as she got to her knees, and then to her feet, leaning on it, and it kept going past the highest she could reach. When she tapped it with a fingernail, it rang, thin and high, somewhere far over her head.

*Glass? No. It's too big, and too cold, and glass doesn't ring like that.*

*Crystal, then. A great deal of crystal.*

She walked with her palms on it. It curved away from her and went on curving, and her bare feet went through more of the little pieces, and she had walked a long way before the smooth surface came to an edge.

She didn't know how sharp the edge was until it had opened her right palm.

She snatched her hand back and pressed it into her dress. It stung, and then it was warm and wet, and she felt the blood run down to her wrist.

She found the edge again, carefully this time, with her left hand. It ran up past her head and down to the floor, and on the other side of it the crystal simply stopped. There was an opening into it, taller than she was.

She reached inside.

The inside was smooth and curved, and it went back further than she could reach, and her mana moved again.

This time it didn't rush out of her. It was taken. Something in the crystal had hold of it and was drawing it out of her through her fingers, slow and steady and cold, and it didn't stop.

She tore her arm free and threw herself backwards. Her legs gave, and she scrambled away on her hands and heels through the little pieces until her back hit a wall.

She sat against it with her cut hand held to her chest. Her mana had crowded up under her ribs and behind her eyes the way it always did when she was frightened, so full she could hardly breathe around it, and as always, it was no help at all.

*What is that thing? What was it* doing*?*

She didn't want to know. She pulled her knees up and stayed where she was, as far from it as the wall let her be.

*Think. Think properly.*

*Mother was in my room this morning and we fought, though I can't even remember what about, and Thaer was there too, promising he'd be back before evening. And then… what? I must have done something after he left. I must have gone somewhere. So why can't I remember going anywhere at all?*

She went back to the beginning and tried again.

There had been sun on her floor this morning; she remembered that. She remembered being angry with her mother, and her mother being angry back, and how hot her own face had been. She couldn't remember a single word either of them had said.

She remembered Thaer's two knocks, and the door opening before she'd said he could come in, because her brother had never once in his life waited to be let in, and she remembered him turning to go.

Every time she tried to think past him going, she was back here, on the stone, in the cold.

Somewhere across the room, a piece of crystal came loose and fell. It rang on the floor, and she flinched so hard that her head knocked against the wall.

*It's still breaking.*

*Whatever I said to Mother this morning, I'll take it back. I don't even care who was right.*

*Someone will come. Someone always comes.*

She got up, using the wall, and followed it with her good hand, feeling for a door.

The wall was stone, so smooth she couldn't find a single join in it, and it was a long way to the first corner. She passed that, and another, and at the third she had to stop and rest. A few steps after that, her fingers slid into a seam.

It ran straight up from the floor, higher than she could reach, and a few steps further on there was another running beside it: the two edges of a door. She felt all over the stone between them. There was no handle, no latch and no keyhole. On her side, there was nothing at all.

"Hello?" She put her mouth to the seam. "Is anyone there? I need someone to open this door."

Nobody answered.

There were always people outside her door. Always. There was a guard at the end of her passage day and night, and someone to bring her water, and someone else to take the cup away again, and she couldn't remember a single morning in her life when she had called out and nobody had come.

"Thaer!"

Her voice filled the room and came back to her in pieces.

"*Thaer!* Mother!"

She called until her throat was raw, and then she pressed her ear to the seam and listened. There was no sound on the other side at all.

*He'll come. He'll knock twice and walk straight in without waiting, the way he always does, and I'll tell him off for it.*

She hit the door with the flat of her good hand. The sound was small and dull and went nowhere. She hit it again, harder, and then with her fist, and then she put her shoulder into it, and it didn't so much as shift.

"Open this door!" She was shouting now. "Whoever is out there, open it! Do you hear me? I'm telling you to open it!"

She hit it until her good hand hurt as much as the cut one, and then she stood there with both of them throbbing and her breath coming hard.

*Well. That was clever.*

*But I'm not going to sit on the floor and wait. I won't.*

She tried to work her fingernails into the seams instead. They were too fine even for that. She tried both of them from the floor to as high as she could reach, and there wasn't one place anywhere where they gave.

She stood with her hand flat against the door.

*A door with no handle on the inside. Nobody builds a door like that for getting out of a room. It's for keeping someone in.*

*Keeping who in?*

*Me?*

Her breath caught. She took another, and that one caught too, higher up, and then she couldn't get one all the way in at all. Her mana pressed up under her skin everywhere at once, and there was no air anywhere, however hard she pulled at it, and she slid down the door until she was sitting with her back against it.

*Stop it. Stop it. Panicking won't open that door, and it won't bring anyone.*

*Breathe. You know how to breathe. You've been doing it all your life.*

She managed one breath. It was a bad one, and it hurt, but it went all the way down, and the next was easier. She sat against the door with her cut hand in her lap and made herself take them, one after another, until her mana had sunk back down and lay still.

After that, she lost track.

She walked the walls again, because walking was something to do. She sat when her legs shook, and stood up again when sitting was worse. She called for Thaer and for her mother until her voice gave out, and then she whispered at the seam, because whispering was all she had left. Once, she said out loud, "Someone will come," just to hear a voice, and it sounded so small in all that stone that she didn't say it again.

Her mouth was so dry that her tongue stuck to the roof of it. She had been thirsty before, but never like this, with no one to ask for water.

The blood on her wrist dried stiff. The cut stopped stinging and started to throb in time with her heart. Her bare feet were so cold she could hardly feel her toes, so she drew them up under her dress and wrapped her arms round her knees.

*It must be evening by now. He said he'd be back before evening.*

She was facing the crystal. She couldn't see it, but she knew exactly where it was, and she couldn't stop thinking about the opening in its side, and the hollow inside it that she hadn't been able to reach the back of.

*Was I in there?*

*No. That's absurd. People don't sleep inside crystal.*

She wanted her mother. She wanted her the way she hadn't since she was very small, with her whole body, and she was so angry at herself for it that she refused to cry.

*I won't. I will not sit in a hole and cry.*

Her eyes stung anyway. She squeezed them shut until they stopped.

Then her cheek was on the stone again.

She didn't remember lying down. She didn't know whether she had slept, or for how long, or whether it was still the same day, and it was exactly the way she had woken the first time, with the cold in her face and no idea at all how she had got there.

*How long have I been lying here?*

*Maybe this is what being dead is: a room, and nobody coming, for ever.*

Her hand throbbed.

*No.*

She got her good hand under her.

*No. I am not going to die on the floor of a room I don't even know.*

It took her three tries to stand, and when she did, she had to lean her whole weight on the wall until her legs stopped shaking. Then she felt her way back along it to the door, and put both palms flat against it, the cut one too, and rested her forehead on the stone between them.

"I'm here," she said. It came out much quieter than she'd meant it to. "I'm here, Thaer."

She felt it before she heard it: a trembling in the door, under her hands, so faint she thought she'd imagined it.

It came again.

Then there were footsteps, far off and hurried, and getting louder. Someone was running.

"Here!" She pounded on the door with both hands, and didn't care what it did to the cut. "I'm in here! *I'm in here!*"

The footsteps didn't slow. They came on and on, and then they stopped, right on the other side of the door.

She stepped back from it and waited for two knocks.

They didn't come.

A line of light appeared down the middle of the door, from the top of it to the floor, straight and fine, and the two halves of the door fell apart along it and crashed down, and light poured in through the gap.

She cried out and threw her arm over her eyes.

After so long without it, the light hurt. It forced tears out under her eyelids and glowed red through them.

Someone was standing in it.

She squinted at him past her arm. He was tall and very still, and a drawn blade hung from one hand with its point towards the floor. She couldn't make out his face yet. But the light caught the narrow point of one ear through his hair, and the line of his shoulder, and she knew that shoulder. She knew him before her eyes could bear to look any higher.

"Thaer."

It came out of her with all the breath she had.

She dropped her arm and ran to him. Her knees nearly buckled on the last step, and she caught herself against his chest and threw her arms round him and held on.

The plates of his armour were cold against her cheek. He smelled of horses, and under the armour his breathing was going far too fast.

But he was here. Whatever this place was, she wasn't alone in it any more.

She pressed her face into him and waited for his arms to close around her.

They didn't.

She tipped her head back to look at him.

He wasn't looking at her. His arms hung at his sides, all the colour had gone out of his face, and his eyes were fixed on something behind her. She didn't need to turn round to know what it was.

"Thaer?"

He didn't answer. He didn't even blink.

And for the first time in her whole life, Seralune saw fear in her brother's eyes.
