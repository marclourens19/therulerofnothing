## The changes

### The run and the bridge (draft lines 3–57)

#### 1. Silas grounds him

*Draft lines 31–35 → revised lines 31–33*

**Before**

> "Stop drowning in self-pity, boy, and move."
>
> Alaric stared at him.
>
> "You want to die? Fine. Stay here and let them have you." He let go. "Then Gerolt died for nothing."

**After**

> "Move, boy, now, or Gerolt died for nothing!"
>
> He let go and went on.

**Why.** The handoff, §3, lists this as accepted dialogue. It keeps the author's intent ("snap out of it … for Gerolt's sake", "Make Gerolt's death mean he died for nothing") and gives Silas a practical reason to move. It replaces the author's own rough line (Chapter 3 design, line 1: "stop drowning in self-pity … If you want to die, fine, stay"). **Round 2 (29 September):** "The man let go and went on" became "He let go and went on", to bring "the man" back down; he's the only other person there. **Round 2, the line itself (29 September):** Alaric is already standing, so "Get up" went. The author's line: "Move boy, now, or Gerolt died for nothing!" (the comma after "Move" is the house style for calling someone by name, as in Chapter 2's "Get up, boy, or we're both dead!").

**Your decision.** Accepted in the line pass of 28–29 September. The handoff (§3) quotes this line as accepted. Round 2's "He let go": the author, "Agreed". Round 2's line is the author's (29 September).

#### 5. His name, and why Silas was there

*Draft lines 139–141 → revised lines 137–145*

**Before**

> He didn't care to ask for Alaric's.
>
> Silas pulled a rag out of the sack and began wiping down his blade. "So how did the old man know you?"

**After**

> Alaric waited for Silas to ask his name. Instead, Silas pulled a rag out of the sack and began wiping down his blade.
>
> "How did you know Gerolt?"
>
> Silas kept his eyes on the steel. "I heard something from across the river. Sounded like fighting, and by the time I got close enough to see what was happening, it was already too late."
>
> "That isn't what I asked."
>
> "No, it isn't." The rag moved along the blade. "How did you know him?"

**Why.** The handoff, §3: "Chapter 3 was the weakest of the four before its focused revision because Silas withheld both his history and the ordinary reason he had been near the farm. His history should remain hidden; his arrival needed a causal answer." And: "Alaric waits for Silas to ask his name. Silas does not. He begins cleaning his blade instead. This is meant to be observed rather than explained." It replaces "He didn't care to ask for Alaric's" (agreed in round 3), which told the reader what to conclude.

**Your decision.** Accepted in the line pass of 28–29 September. The handoff (§3) quotes this line as accepted.
