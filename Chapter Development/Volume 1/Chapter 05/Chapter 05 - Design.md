## Where the story stands

- **Chapter 4** ends with Thaer at the cut door. Seralune runs to him and his arms don't close round her; he's staring past her at the crystal, and for the first time in her life she sees fear in his eyes.
- **It's night on Day 1.** Chapter 5 runs to first light on Day 2, the same first light that ends Chapter 3, where Alaric cries into Wena's fur. Nothing on the page needs to say so.
- **What she knows:** her mana left her and found something far away; she woke in a crystal that split open, which pulls at her mana; the door had no handle on her side; her last memory is fighting with her mother "this morning", and Thaer promising to be back before evening.

## Fixed for this chapter

- **Her eyes only.** Nothing goes on the page that she couldn't see, hear or know.
- **Her voice:** Alisaie with everyone, reasoning her way forward and turning to the next practical step. With Thaer it starts closer to Shoko, and turns Alisaie as he shuts her out. She says sorry.
- **Nobody tells her** "sealed" or "a thousand years" to her face. She puts the pieces together herself, and the number and her mother come from Cyrandor in Chapter 7.
- **Her mana can't be used,** and she never tries. It acts on its own. It never flares in this chapter.
- **Thaer gives orders instead of answers,** because "he wants to get her away from prying eyes, and in a place he thinks is safe, so he can do other things". He regrets the sealing and hopes their father will think differently (the handoff).
- **Leorin** is her cousin and looks about seventy now. Thaer looks almost the same; her father only a little older.
- **The palace:** massive, on a hillside, "like a kingdom in FF16 and the elven kingdom of Rivendell in LOTR", beside a holy waterfall "bigger than a massive building", with a great forest below and the city inside it, "like Oriflamme". The royal tree stands at the top of the falls, far bigger than she remembers. The royal warship is leaving for Mydea as she comes up.
- **Nereth** is composed on duty, Ram-like, and answers only what she's asked. Cyrandor secretly told her to watch over Seralune. Her first slip is in Chapter 7, not here.
- **Cyrandor** appears once, bows and smiles a little, and is gone.
- **No wardwrights and no silver chain.**
- **When she's angry with him, she calls him Thaeroval** (the author, 29 September). Everywhere else he's Thaer.

## Part 1: What happened to the world she remembers?

From the chamber to her room. Each beat gives her a piece, and nobody hands it to her.

1. **The embrace, which hurts.** Thaer asks his own questions ("How long have you been awake?") and answers none of hers.
2. **The court comes.** Leorin is old now. He calls her "Your Highness", and the guards are afraid of her. She says: "I'm standing right here, you know. You can just ask me."
3. **The walk up.** Servants press themselves to the walls. One old servant bows and smiles a little. *She's never seen him before, and he's the first person who has smiled at her.* Then he's gone.
4. **Outside.** The sound of the falls first, then the waterfall, the forest and the city, and something enormous moving off over the trees, with blue-green light along its underside, that she has no word for. Then the royal tree, far bigger than this morning. *Piece: it has been a very long time.*
**Ends:** on the tree: *How long was I in that room?* (round 1).

## Part 2: What is her family preparing to do to her?

From her room to first light.

5. **Her room,** ready for her. Thaer leaves her there with an order to rest. Nereth stands in the corner by the door, composed, and answers only what she's asked, which is next to nothing. "Where's Elowen?" "I've never heard of her." *Piece: long enough for everyone she knew to be gone.* Getting nowhere, Seralune sighs and asks the maid her name (round 1).
6. **Pacing.** She walks back and forth, frustrated that nobody will tell her anything, and worried, because she has never seen her brother act like this. *Why is he so on edge? He's never been like this, not once.*
7. **The door.** She hears voices outside and goes to listen. Nereth stays in the corner by the door and does nothing, in silence (round 1). It's mumbled and she can't make it out: *Is that Thaer speaking? And who else?* Leorin's voice, and one word breaks through: "…sealed…". Then Thaer's, angry, in pieces: "…wait until… my father… returned… answers…". She backs away. *Who? Me? Sealed? No.* Then: *Is that what that room was?*
8. **Thaer comes back.** She doesn't tell him what she heard. She asks what they're going to do with her, and gives him the chance to tell her the truth. He tells her to rest. Then her anger ("You told me to follow you, and I did…"), and the first time she calls him Thaeroval, his "Seralune, get some rest. I'll be back in the morning.", and her "You said you'd be back before evening, too." His hand stops on the door. He goes out, and the lock turns.
9. **The night.** Blows come up through the floor. *Piece: they're making it again, and it's for her.*
10. **She snaps at Nereth.** "Of course. No one can say anything." Then she breaks: "*Why can't anyone just say what is going on?*" The guards come in with their swords out. Nereth steps between them: "Her Highness is tired. She needs to rest." Once they've gone, Seralune's sorry.
11. **First light.** Sounds in the corridor. Two knocks, and then whoever it is waits, where Thaer always walked straight in. She's been waiting for him all night. "Thaer?" The lock turns.

**Ends:** on the knock, with Cyrandor unseen.

### Why she says "I'm sorry" (the author's question on call 1)

**Why I wrote it.** Her brother, who has never once been afraid in her whole life, is staring past her in fear, and he hasn't put his arms round her. She has just come out of a place she doesn't understand. So her first instinct is that she's done something, and she apologises before she even knows what for. That's the side of her we took from Shoko, who says sorry for things that aren't her fault. Chapter 4 also has her ready to apologise all day (*Whatever I said to Mother this morning, I'll take it back*).

**The honest problem.** None of that is on the page. A reader sees an apology with no visible cause, and you asked why, so it isn't working as written.

- **A (recommended):** "What's wrong?" she whispered. It's what anyone says to someone they love who looks frightened, and it's her care for him before herself. His eyes come down to her, and the embrace follows. Her "sorry" still comes in Part 2, to Nereth, where its reason is on the page.
- **B:** keep "I'm sorry", and give it a reason she can think: one line before it, such as *What did I do?* The risk is that it repeats Part 2's "Did I do something wrong?"
- **C:** she says nothing, and the chapter opens on his eyes coming down to her at last.
