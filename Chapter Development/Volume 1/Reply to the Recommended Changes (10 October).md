# Reply to "Recommended Changes After the Volume Review"

10 October 2026. My answer to the other workspace's response to my volume review. The response is kept word for word in `Recommended Changes After the Volume Review (other workspace, 10 October).md`. I checked each proposed edit against the chapters and against your own rules.

## In short

It's a careful, fair response. It's right about several things my review overstated. Its main advice matches mine: keep Chapters 1–10 substantially as they are, and fix Alaric's drive in Chapter 11. Its edits are small: about 135 words in Chapter 8, about 50 in Chapter 10 and a few lines elsewhere.

I'd take the Chapter 9 fixes and the Chapter 10 trims, with one wording change, and leave Chapters 1, 5 and 8 as written.

## Where it corrects my review

1. **"Alaric has nothing driving him" was too absolute.** He makes choices throughout: the sword, Wena, insisting on showing Marta the sword himself, leaving Silas, knocking. What's missing is a search for his past that he chooses himself. Its wording is better than mine.
2. **Distance isn't pace, and my claim about Chapter 8 was wrong.** I said most of its 5,300 words were waiting. The two waits (behind the handcart, and in the lane by the inn) come to about 680 words. I'd move its mark from 70 to 75. What felt slow to me is a run of small obstacles that cost nothing (the guard on the lane, the busy yard, the patrol). Under your new rule that the world keeps moving (10 October), that texture is part of the design, not a fault.
3. **"Nobody has told him he's nothing" missed Silas in Chapter 3:** "A boy like you means nothing to them." What hasn't happened is anyone responding to his having no Affinity. That's the gap, and Chapter 11 is where it closes.
4. **"The women's freedom to choose isn't on the page" was wrong.** Rhose's "Tell him I'm finished for tonight" is on the page.
5. **Nereth's "I cannot hold one for hours" doesn't contradict the blast.** Keeping a flame going and one great burst are different limits. The real questions are where the power came from and whether its scale keeps mattering.
6. **"Mark time by what she can see and feel, not by 'a long time'" could be read as replacing all nine.** It shouldn't be. The trims belong on the travel lines. The pauses that carry fear stay: Nereth looking at their hands, and the wait for her breath after the blast.
7. **"One answer that costs something in every chapter" was too rigid as a rule.** Some chapters need rest or ordinary pleasure. As a question each design asks, it still helps: is this holding back earned, and could this overheard news come some other way?

One point needs no correction. The response warns against making Seralune's compassion look foolish in Chapter 7. My review didn't suggest that; it called turning back for Nereth the clearest choice either lead makes. We agree that her flaw is taking control of other people's choices, not kindness itself: she frees the lizard over Nereth's "Leave it", and she takes the lamp by order.

### 1. Chapter 1: "that morning". Leave it.

It isn't an error. Gerolt was up and out from the morning ("Found her pressed against my door this morning"; "I go to bed with a field… and I wake up to—this."). He had searched the field before he found the boy at midday: "Have you found anyone else alive?" / "I found you." So "that morning" is accurate, and more exact than "that day". The response itself calls it optional.

### 2. Chapter 5: "Don't you dare send me to bed like a child!" Leave it.

- **It's her voice.** The Chapter 7 design uses this line as the model of Seralune breaking ("she breaks when she breaks").
- **It's her side of the volume's question in one line:** other people deciding her life for her own good, as if she were a child.
- **It names Thaer's pattern.** He puts her off with rest three times: "Try to rest", "When you've slept—" and "get some rest". Her line is where she calls it out. Cut the protest and the pattern stays, unanswered.
- **The cut leaves a gap.** Thaer's eyes come up to hers "at last", and then he walks out, with nothing in between to send him.

### 3. Chapter 8: the wait behind the handcart. Leave it.

- It saves about 70 words.
- The new version ends in four short sentences in a row: "The lights in the houses went out one by one. People called goodnight in the lane and shut their doors. Wena slept on. Alaric was cold despite the cloak…" That's the list-like rhythm you've rejected as robotic.
- It cuts the dishes and the window shutting. Those are the household sounds the response itself says to keep.
- It also cuts "he had stopped trying to work out which set of footsteps might be the last". That line is what the wait is doing to him.

If you want a trim anyway, take only "The yard stayed busy." (the next sentence says it) and the man counting sacks.

### 4. Chapter 8: the wait in the lane by the inn. Leave it, except one line.

- **The thought.** It turns a thought that reasons into one that only asks. "*After the round, they said. How long does a round take? They were complaining about it, so it must be a long one. Unless that was only them complaining.*" becomes "*How long does a round take?*" That's the fault named in your review of Chapter 5: a thought reasons; it doesn't just ask.
- **The voices.** "Alaric listened for the loud voice and the tired one from the yard" becomes "Alaric listened for the two guards' voices". That loses what Chapter 9 pays off: "It was the loud one from the yard."
- **The danger.** It cuts the boots and the shout towards the gate, which are the only danger in the wait.

The one line I'd happily lose is "After that, they waited."

### 5–7. Chapter 9: the midday answer, "any more" and the double's line. Take them.

"At midday." matches Chapter 1 ("The sun stood directly overhead"; "You've been senseless since midday"). "Any more" is house style.

Your Word file has "Alaric. This is who you are." and no "*No. I didn't—*". The repo still has "This is who you are, Alaric." and the thought. Bringing the repo in line is a sync, not a new change.

### 8. Chapter 9: Rhose. Your decision, recorded.

The response reports that you've confirmed her advances are intentional, part of her directness. That was your call to make; I gave my reasons and you've decided. It's recorded in `Decisions.md`. The response is also right that "Since you asked nicely" ties her teasing to her care, and that checking the joins into and out of the scene is all that's left to do.

### 9–13. Chapter 10: the five trims. Take all five, with one wording change.

- **9. "They walked for a long time."** Cut it. The sea conversation carries the time.
- **10. The first return.** Take it. It's also a point-of-view fix. "They came to the pillar again a long time later. Seralune didn't know it at first." tells the reader before she knows. In the new version she sees their prints first, then looks up at the pillar. The three returns still build: she knows the pillar after the prints, then before the lamp is lifted, then at the edge of the light.
- **11. The shaking lamp.** Take it.
- **12. The walk before "Say it to my face".** Take it, but keep "didn't let herself think". That's Seralune holding herself in; "trying not to think" is weaker and could be anyone:

  > She didn't offer again, or say anything else, because it seemed safest not to. She walked behind the light and didn't let herself think about the water or the pillar or how far down they might have come.
  >
  > Nereth stopped ahead of her.

- **13. "There was more light than the lamp's."** Take it.

That leaves five of the nine time phrases in the narration, and they're mostly the ones that carry fear.
