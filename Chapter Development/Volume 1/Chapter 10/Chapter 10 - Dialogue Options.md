# Chapter 10 — Dialogue and Key Passages

Third version, 2 October 2026. The voices are recast after the author's note: "Seralune should sound like Alisae when she thinks and speaks and Nereth like Ram from rezero and when she breaks down she gets aggrisive". The earlier versions are in `Drafts/`.

**Seralune (Alisaie).** She's quick, sharp-tongued and sure of herself, and she takes charge as soon as she can see a reason to. Under pressure she's sarcastic ("Well. That was clever." in Chapter 4). She scolds others, and herself hardest of all. She never begs. She says sorry only when she knows she's wrong, and briefly. Her thoughts give orders and draw conclusions: *Up. Sitting here helps no one.*

**Nereth (Ram).** She's composed, deadpan and politely superior. "Your Highness" goes on the front of a cutting truth, the way "That's a wall, Your Highness" and "No, Your Highness. You shouldn't have" do in Chapters 5 and 7. She uses few words and never shows feeling; if she shows anything, it's contempt. **When she breaks, she turns aggressive:** she shouts, swears, shoves, hits, and turns the title into a weapon or drops it.

The rest stays as in the second version:
- Seralune's mana crowds up when she's frightened, and each time Nereth gets worse.
- Nereth's habits break one by one.
- The falls can't be heard.
- The descent follows the author's guide.

Choose by section and letter, mix lines, or rewrite anything.

## 1. Into the channels

### A. The falls

The arch was behind them before Seralune had properly decided to go through it. After that there was the ledge, and the black water lying beside it, and Nereth's lamp making a small room of light that moved with them and never got any bigger.

She kept listening for the falls. She had listened for them all her life without knowing she did it, the way she breathed, and now she couldn't stop doing it on purpose. There was nothing: their feet on the stone, water dripping somewhere, Nereth's breathing. Under all of it, where the falls should have been, there was nothing at all.

*Don't cry. The falls never stop, and I'll never stop looking for you.*

They did stop, though. You only had to go far enough down.

*Stop it. You're not six, and you're not lost on a step. Walk.*

Nereth hadn't said a word since the arch. She walked half a step ahead with the lamp held out and her other hand in Seralune's, and every so often she looked back over her shoulder, the way she had at every turning since the iron door. There was never anyone there.

"Nereth. About Cyrandor—"

"Not now, Your Highness." Nereth didn't turn her head. "If Your Highness would like me to sit down in the water and stay there, that would be the quickest way to manage it."

*Later, then. But you will tell me.*

The channel split round a pillar. Nereth lifted the lamp to one side and then the other, and neither way went anywhere the light could follow.

"This is as far as they sent us," she said. "From here on, Your Highness is following a laundry maid who has never been past this pillar. I'd keep my hopes modest."

"I followed my brother up a staircase last night and woke up a prisoner. You can hardly do worse." Seralune looked at the two black openings. "Which way?"

"The water came from the left, when we cleared it."

"Then left. And if you're wrong, I'll tell you."

"I don't doubt it for a moment, Your Highness."

### B. What she had done

*I turned round.*

She had been free in the wash passage. The guard hadn't so much as glanced at her, and Cyrandor was already going back for Nereth, and in a few more steps the crowd would have closed behind her.

*Of course I turned round. I'd do it again, so there's no use whining about it.*

It didn't help. Cyrandor was on the other side of an iron door with its key at the bottom of a drain, and she was down here.

"How far do these go, Nereth?"

"I couldn't say, Your Highness."

"You said they sent you down once."

"To the first turning." The lamp swung as Nereth looked back over her shoulder. "Someone stood at the arch and bellowed our names every so often, so that we'd know which way was out. It was very undignified."

Nobody was standing at the arch now.

"When we're out," Seralune said, "you're going to tell me about him. All of it."

"When we're out, Your Highness may ask whatever she likes." Nereth held the lamp up to the first turning, to one black opening and then the other. "Down here, I'd rather keep my mind on where I put my feet."

Seralune watched her try to decide, and watched her try much harder to look as if she knew.

"Left," Nereth said.

## 2. The circles

### A. "Five?"

They walked for a long time.

The ledge was too narrow for two, so Nereth went first with the lamp, and Seralune came after her with one hand in Nereth's and the other on the wall. The wall was cold, and slick in places where something had grown over the stone. The water beside them didn't move. In the palace everything ran: the falls, the channels in the courts, the fountain Elowen used to let her trail her fingers in when nobody was looking. This just lay there.

Her feet hurt in the borrowed shoes. Twice she found she had stopped walking and didn't remember stopping, and both times Nereth's hand pulled her on.

Then she saw the prints.

They were in the silt where the ledge widened: soft soles, and harder ones beside them, going the same way they were going.

"Nereth. Stop."

Nereth brought the lamp down.

"Those are ours." Seralune put her foot beside the nearest soft print, and it fitted exactly. "We've walked in a circle."

"We have not, Your Highness." It was the even voice, the one Nereth had used to the guards and their swords. "I've counted every turning."

"How many?"

"Six." Nereth looked back the way they had come, and her lips moved. "Seven." Then, so quietly that she might have been asking herself, "Five?"

Nereth didn't ask herself things. Nereth knew.

*Wonderful. Lost in a drain, with a guide who can't count.*

It didn't come out funny, even in her head. Her mana crowded up under her ribs, the way it always did when she was frightened, so full she could hardly breathe round it, and as always, it was no help at all.

The hand in hers had started to shake. Last night Nereth had stood a step from two drawn swords, and afterwards she had cleaned Seralune's cut and bound it, and her hands hadn't shaken then.

"Back to the pillar," Seralune said. "We'll try the other way."

"Yes, Your Highness. The other way." Nereth was still looking at the prints. "Obviously."

They went back to the pillar, and they tried the other way. A long time later the prints were there again, more of them now, trodden one over another, and Nereth walked straight across them without looking down.

### B. Her mark

At the third turning, while Nereth held the lamp up to choose, Seralune picked a chip of stone out of the silt and scratched a line on the pillar beside her, as high as she could reach. It took three tries before it showed.

Nereth watched her. "Is Your Highness decorating?"

"I'm marking it. So we'll know if we see it again."

"We won't."

She said it so certainly that Seralune almost believed her, and was annoyed with herself for how much she wanted to.

The ledge narrowed, and widened, and narrowed again. Seralune's heels were rubbed raw in the borrowed shoes, and then she stopped feeling them, and then she stopped feeling her feet at all.

Then the lamp found a pillar, and on the pillar, as high as she could reach, a pale scratch.

She put her finger in it. It was hers. It had taken three tries.

"Nereth. We've come back."

"That isn't yours." Nereth didn't even look at it. "Yours was neater."

"It's mine. It took me three tries, and you stood there and watched every one of them."

"Then someone else down here is as clumsy as Your Highness." Nereth held the lamp up to the left-hand way. "This way."

It was the way they had taken last time.

Seralune didn't move. Her mana had come up, cold and enormous under her ribs, and Nereth, who hadn't looked round once, stopped dead.

"Don't," Nereth said, without turning. "Whatever you're doing back there. Don't."

"I'm not doing anything."

Nereth stood a moment longer with her back to her. Then she went on, and Seralune, because the lamp was going and the black was coming in behind it, went after her.

## 3. What Nereth hears

### A. Her name

It was Nereth who stopped this time, so suddenly that Seralune walked into her back.

"Be quiet, Your Highness."

"I wasn't saying anything."

"You're breathing. Be quiet." Nereth had her head on one side. "Someone's talking."

Seralune held her breath until her chest hurt. There was the water dripping somewhere ahead, one drop and then a long wait and then another, and under it there was nothing at all.

"There's no one down here but us."

"They're saying my name." Nereth said it as flatly as if she were reporting that the soup had gone cold. "Over and over. How rude of them."

Seralune's mana came up again, under her ribs and behind her eyes, and Nereth flinched and pressed her free hand hard over her ear.

"Louder," she said through her teeth. "They've got louder. Stop it."

"Stop what? Nereth, I'm not—"

"I don't know. Whatever it is. *Stop it.*"

### B. The pattern

Nereth had stopped walking. She was staring at the wall, where roots had begun to come through between the stones, and she had her free hand pressed against her chest as if something in it hurt.

"Don't look at the wall, Your Highness. You'll only make a fool of yourself."

"Why? What's on it?"

"The pattern." Her voice was very quiet. "It keeps going in. Every time I look at it, it goes further in, and it wants me to follow it."

Seralune looked. There were roots and wet stone, and the lamplight sliding on both. There was no pattern anywhere.

"There's nothing there, Nereth."

"I know there isn't." Nereth didn't take her eyes off it. "I'm not stupid. I can still see it."

Then she shut her eyes, and walked on with them shut, and let Seralune lead her by the hand past the place where it was.

## 4. The kindness

### A. The shawl

Nereth was shivering. Seralune could feel it through their joined hands, a fine, steady shaking that didn't stop.

She pulled the shawl off her own head. This morning Nereth had put it over her hair and tucked the ends in, then stood back to look, and tucked them in again. Seralune put it round Nereth's shoulders now and tried to do the same.

"You're freezing. Put this on."

"I'm quite all right, Your Highness."

It was what she had said last night, sitting on the very edge of the chair by the hearth with her hands folded in her lap, in the same voice. But she was shaking so hard now that the shawl wouldn't stay on.

"You're not, and I'm not asking. Hold still."

"*Don't fuss over me!*" Nereth dragged the shawl off and threw it at her. "You've been awake for a day. You can't find your way down a stair, you can't stand up without someone holding you, and you want to look after *me*?"

Seralune caught the shawl against her chest. Her face had gone hot.

"Say that again."

For a moment Nereth looked as though she would. Then something went smooth in her face, as if a hand had wiped it.

"I beg your pardon, Your Highness," she said evenly. "Shall we go on?"

Seralune's mana sat high and hot under her ribs all the way to the next turning, and Nereth didn't once look back.

### B. "Sit down"

Nereth's foot went off the edge of the ledge. She caught herself on the wall before she went into the water, and then she stayed there, leaning on the stone with her eyes shut, breathing as if she'd been running.

"That's enough. Sit down, Nereth."

"I'm quite all right, Your Highness."

"You said that last night too, after you'd been standing in that corner for hours. Sit *down*."

"Stop *ordering* me about!"

It went off down the channel ahead of them and came back from behind, as if the whole place had shouted it with her.

"I'm not a child." Nereth was off the wall now, right in front of her. "And you're not my mother. Your mother didn't even stay to be yours."

Seralune's hand came up before she'd decided anything. It stopped a finger's width from Nereth's face.

Nereth looked at it, and smiled.

"Go on, Your Highness," she said. "It's the first useful thing you'll have done all day."

Seralune put her hand down. It took everything she had.

## 5. Seralune takes the lead

### A. "That's an order"

When the pillar came round for the fourth time, Seralune stopped.

She knew it by the crack that ran up one side. She had known it the last time, and the time before, and both times she had said nothing: because Nereth was so sure, and because Nereth had been down here and she hadn't, and because she was so tired that being led was easier than thinking. She had let everyone lead her since she woke. Thaer had taken her up the stairs, and Cyrandor had taken her down them, and Nereth had taken her into this.

*Enough. If we're going to be lost, I'll at least choose where.*

"Give me the lamp. I'm going first."

"Your Highness doesn't know the way."

"Neither do you! We've passed this pillar four times!"

It went off down every channel at once and came back at them from all sides. Nereth's hand clamped on hers so hard that the bones ground together. Seralune didn't make a sound. She pulled her hand free and held it out.

"The lamp."

Nereth held on to it.

"Give me the lamp, Nereth. That's an order."

This morning she had told Nereth she was sorry for giving her one, and asked her properly, and been glad she had. It had taken her half a day to forget.

"Yes, Your Highness," Nereth said, and put the lamp into her hand as if it were something dirty.

Seralune held it up. Their prints went left, trodden over and over, and they went straight on. To the right, low down, roots hung over an opening she hadn't seen before, and the silt in front of it was smooth.

*Left we've done, and straight on, and we've never once been through there. Then that's where we're going.*

She pushed the roots aside. They were cold, and softer than roots should be, and after she let go, they went on moving for a while.

"Go on," Nereth said, close behind her. She was so close that Seralune could feel her breath on the back of her neck, quick and warm. "Go on, Your Highness. Go on. *Go on.*"

She had never heard Nereth want anything before.

### B. Nereth admits it

Nereth stopped at the pillar and didn't go on.

She stood with the lamp held out in front of her, looking at the three ways, and she didn't seem to see any of them.

"Your Highness," she said at last. "I'm going to say something I've never said in my life, and I'd thank you never to repeat it." Her voice was as level as ever. Her hand, round the lamp, was white. "I don't know where we are."

Seralune took the lamp out of her hand. Nereth let her.

"Then I'll lead," Seralune said. "I can hardly do worse."

"That," said Nereth, "remains to be seen."

There were prints going left and prints going straight on. To the right, low down behind a hanging mat of roots, there was an opening nobody's feet had touched. Seralune held the roots aside and went through first.

Behind her, very softly, Nereth laughed, the way someone laughs at a thing that has pleased them.

## 6. Deeper

### A. The rat, and the servant in grey

The way went down. The lamp found roots on the walls, then roots over the walls, so thick that the stone only showed in patches. After a while there was a light that didn't come from the lamp: a pale glow in the roots themselves, so faint that she only saw it when she held the lamp behind her.

Small white flowers grew out of the roots. When she brought the lamp past, they turned on their stems to follow it. When she held it still, they went on turning, slowly, until they were facing her.

Something came along the ledge towards them.

She took it for a rat until the light found it. It had a rat's shape and a rat's tail, but roots had come out through its back and along its sides, with the little white flowers growing among them, and where its eyes should have been there were more flowers, shut tight.

It came on without seeming to know they were there. It went past Seralune's feet without stopping. At Nereth's, it stopped.

It lifted its head, and the flowers where its eyes should have been opened, one after the other.

Nereth kicked it into the channel. It went under without a sound, and the flowers stayed open all the way down.

"Nereth!"

"It was looking at me." Nereth wiped her shoe on the ledge. "I don't like being looked at."

The first of the dead was sitting a little further on, against the wall, with its knees drawn up as if it had stopped to rest and never got up again. Roots had gone in at its mouth and come out between its ribs, where the cloth had rotted away. What was left of the cloth was grey.

Seralune looked down at her own sleeve.

"They sent people down here," she said. "To clear the channels. You told me."

"Some of them didn't come back." Nereth stepped over its legs without looking down. "We were told they'd run off. I always thought that was generous of them."

There were more of them after that. One lay face down in the water with the roots holding it under. Two sat against the wall together, one with its head on the other's shoulder, the way people sit when they've been waiting for someone a long time.

Nereth stepped over all of them.

### B. Someone Nereth knew

The lamp found a shoe first, on its side on the ledge. Then the foot it belonged to, and then the rest of her, half grown into the wall, in grey.

Nereth stopped.

Seralune waited for her to cry out, or turn away, or reach for her hand. Nereth did none of those things. She stood and looked down at the woman with her head a little on one side.

"She did the ironing," she said. "She sang while she worked. The same song, all day, until somebody threw something at her to make her stop." She considered the roots coming out of the woman's mouth. "That was me. They told us she'd run off with a soldier. I never believed it. No soldier would have had her."

"Nereth! She's *dead*."

"Yes, Your Highness." Nereth sounded puzzled. "It's only her."

She stepped over the woman's legs and went on. After a little while, very quietly, she began to hum. Seralune didn't know the song. She didn't have to ask whose it was.

All round them, in the roots, the white flowers turned to listen.

### C. "I am watching her"

Nereth had begun to talk.

At first Seralune thought it was to her. Then she saw that Nereth's head was turned back the way they had come, as though someone were walking there, just out of the light.

"I am," Nereth was saying, in the voice she used for answering to her betters. "I am watching her. I haven't let go of her once. She's very tiresome, but I haven't let go."

"Nereth. Who are you talking to?"

"He said even if she seems—" Nereth stopped. She turned back, and looked at Seralune as if Seralune were the one who had been talking to nobody. "No one, Your Highness. Do try to keep up."

Seralune didn't answer. She was looking past Nereth's shoulder, to where the lamp didn't reach, and she couldn't make herself stop.

## 7. The lash-out

Nereth breaks here, and every version is aggressive.

### A. The iron door

Nereth let go of her hand.

She went to the wall, took hold of a root as thick as a rope, and pulled on it with both hands, the way she had pulled on the iron ring.

"Cyrandor, open it." She set her foot against the stone and hauled. "Open the door. *Open the door!*"

"Nereth, stop it!" Seralune caught her arm. "He isn't here. There's no door. It's a wall!"

Nereth went still. She looked down at Seralune's hand on her arm.

"Get your hand off me."

"Not until you—"

"Every time you touch me, they get louder!" She tore her arm free so hard that Seralune staggered. "What are you *doing* to me?"

"I'm not doing anything!"

Seralune's mana came up so hard that the lamp shook in her hand, and Nereth flinched as if she had been struck. When she lifted her head, it was a face Seralune had never seen her wear.

"You turned round."

"What?"

"In the wash passage." Nereth came at her a step at a time. "He was already coming back for me. You only had to keep walking, and he'd have got us both through that door. But you turned round, so he stayed. Do you understand that, Your fucking Highness? He stayed because of *you*!"

She had thought it herself, all the way down from the arch. It was worse to hear someone else say it.

"I wasn't going to leave you to them!"

"No. You weren't. So he had to."

Nereth's arm came across her throat and slammed her back against the wall. The lamp went out of Seralune's hand. She heard it break on the ledge, and the flame went out.

In the pale glow from the roots, Nereth's face was a hand's width from hers. All along the wall behind her, the white flowers had turned on their stems towards Seralune, every one of them.

"Do you know what he told me," Nereth said, "before I'd ever set eyes on you? 'Watch over her. Even if she seems dangerous, she's important.'" She laughed, and it wasn't her laugh. "*Important.* Look at you. I could burn this whole place down round you, and you'd still be standing there saying sorry."

*Dangerous? Me? I've never had magic in my life.*

Seralune got both hands on Nereth's arm and shoved, and it didn't move. Her mana crowded up and up, behind her eyes, under her ribs, into her throat where the arm was, and Nereth's face twisted, and she leaned in with all her weight.

### B. "He's dead"

Nereth let go of her hand. She went to the wall, took hold of a root as thick as a rope, and pulled on it with both hands, the way she had pulled on the iron ring.

"Cyrandor, open it. Open the door!"

"He isn't here, Nereth! There's no door!"

Nereth stopped pulling. She stood with both hands still on the root, and her shoulders began to shake.

"He's dead," she said. "They've killed him."

"You don't know that. Thaer sent for him himself, Thaer wouldn't—"

"*Thaer!*" Nereth spun round and shoved her, two hands flat on her chest, so hard that Seralune's back hit the wall. "Your precious brother let them mend that thing for you all night and called it keeping you safe! And you turned round!" She was crying now, without seeming to notice, and her hands were knotted in the front of Seralune's dress. "He was coming back for me. You only had to keep walking!"

"Let go of me!"

"He's dead, and you're alive, and I'm down here holding your *hand*!"

She shook her, once, hard, and Seralune's head struck the stone. The lamp went out of her hand and broke on the ledge, and then there was only the pale glow from the roots.

Nereth's voice dropped, all at once, into something almost fond.

"I could burn this whole place down round you. I can feel it, Your Highness. It's right here." She let go with one hand and pressed it flat against her own chest. "And you'd still be standing there saying sorry."

### C. The slap

Nereth let go of the root and turned round.

"You turned round in the wash passage." Her voice was rising with every word. "He was coming back for me anyway. You only had to keep walking, but you can't, can you? You can't leave anything alone. You have to go to it. You have to *touch* it!"

"Nereth, listen to me—"

Nereth hit her.

It was an open hand, across the face, hard enough to turn her head and fill her eyes. The lamp swung wildly and nearly went into the water. For a heartbeat Seralune couldn't hear anything but a high thin ringing.

Then she hit her back.

She had never struck anyone in her life. Her palm stung, and Nereth's head went round with it, and Seralune stood there shaking, appalled, already opening her mouth to say she was sorry.

Nereth turned her face back slowly. She was smiling.

"*There* she is," she said.

She knocked the lamp out of Seralune's hand. It broke on the ledge, and the flame went out, and Nereth's hands closed round her throat.

## 8. "Help me"

### A. The voice

"Help me."

It came from the wall at Seralune's shoulder.

The dead servant sitting there hadn't moved, but its jaw hung open round the roots, and the voice had come out of it: a girl's voice, hoarse and cracked, as if she had been calling for a very long time and nobody had come.

Seralune knew that voice. She had made it herself, against a door with no handle.

Nereth's grip went slack.

"Help me." It came again from further along, out of another of the dead, then from ahead, and from the roots overhead, all in the same voice. "Help me. Please. Please, somebody help me."

"Someone's alive," Seralune said. "Someone's down here. Come on!"

Nereth was staring at the open mouth. "They're dead, Your Highness. Even you can see that." It was nearly her own voice again. "All of them."

"Then who's calling?"

She ducked under Nereth's arm and went towards it. She heard Nereth swear and come after her. Then Nereth's hand found hers in the half-dark and closed on it, tight, and didn't let go.

### B. Her own voice

"Help me."

It was a girl's voice, young and worn thin, and it came out of the dead servant beside them, out of the open mouth with the roots in it.

"Please. I can't find the way back. I keep coming back to the same place."

Beside her, Nereth had gone completely still.

"Where are you?" Seralune called. "I can hear you! Keep talking!"

For a moment there was nothing. Then, from somewhere ahead, out of another of the dead, came a voice that was hers, exactly hers, sharp in exactly the same places:

"*Where are you? I can hear you! Keep talking!*"

Seralune's hand went to her mouth.

"Someone's lost down here," she whispered. "Someone else is lost down here."

"Yes," said Nereth. "Us."

Seralune went after the voices anyway. She didn't know how not to. Nereth came after her, and caught her hand, and didn't let go.

## 9. The lizard and the bite

### A. "Frightened things still bite"

It was in a hollow at the very end, caught in the roots: a lizard, no longer than her hand. Roots had grown through it and out of it, and small white flowers had opened all along its back. Its sides went in and out, quick and shallow, the way a hurt thing breathes, and every time they did, the voice came out of the dead behind them.

"Help me."

"It's caught," Seralune said. "It's caught in them, and it's hurt."

"It's watching your hand." Nereth's breath was against her ear. "Not your face."

"It's frightened."

"Frightened things still bite, Your Highness. Even you must know that."

It was Nereth's own voice, dry and exact, the voice that had told her the linen stair was a wall. Seralune heard it, and knew it was right, and reached out anyway.

Nereth wrenched her arm back.

The lizard came off the roots at Nereth's face.

She screamed until she had no breath left to do it. Then she went quiet, and that was worse. She knelt in the channel with both hands over the left side of her face, rocking, and the lizard was gone, and Seralune hadn't seen where.

"Nereth. Let me see. Nereth, *let me see*—"

Nereth lifted her head. Black-purple lines were spreading out from under her hands, into the skin round her eye, up towards her temple and into her hair.

She opened her mouth, and the voice that came out of it wasn't hers.

"Help me."

It was the voice from the walls.

"No," Seralune said. "No, you don't. Look at me. *Look at me*, Nereth—"

Nereth's hand found her wrist and held on hard enough to hurt. For one breath, the voice was her own again.

"Seralune—"

It was the first time she had ever said her name.

### B. Laughing

It was in a hollow at the very end, caught in the roots: a lizard, no longer than her hand, with roots grown through it and small white flowers open all along its back.

When Seralune came close, the voices stopped. They stopped all round them at once, in every open mouth in every wall, and the quiet afterwards was the loudest thing she had heard since the arch.

The lizard turned its head, slowly, and looked past her at Nereth.

"Your Highness," Nereth said, very quietly. "Come away from it. Now."

"It's hurt."

"*I said come away from it!*"

Seralune reached for it, and Nereth wrenched her arm back, and the lizard came off the roots at Nereth's face. It hung there by its teeth, under her left eye, until she tore it off and flung it away down the channel.

She didn't scream. She laughed.

Black-purple lines were spreading out from the bite, under the skin. Nereth stood with her hand pressed to them and laughed until her legs went, and she knelt in the channel, still laughing. Between her fingers, a light had started: a thin flame along her palm.

"Put it out. Nereth, put it out!"

"Make me." She looked up at Seralune, smiling, and her eye on the bitten side was all black. "Go on, Your Highness. Order me."

## 10. The fire

### A. Light, then nothing, then the falls

The light came first.

It filled the channel from wall to wall, white at the heart and purple at the edges, and for an instant Seralune saw everything at once, every root and every one of the dead, as clearly as if it were noon. Then she couldn't see anything at all.

There was no sound.

Then there was nothing but sound, bigger than the falls had ever been under her window, so big that it stopped being a sound and became a weight, and the floor went out from under her.

The channel heaved. Somewhere behind them, stone came down and went on coming down. Ahead of them, the fire went away down the channel faster than anything she had ever seen move. Everywhere it went, the roots went up white, and the dead went up, and the walls lit from inside, chamber after chamber, deeper and deeper into the mountain, further than she had known there was any mountain to go through.

Then it came back.

It filled the channel from side to side, and it came back up it towards them. Seralune had time to pull Nereth against her and nothing else.

It reached them, and went round them.

It went past on both sides, so close that she could have touched it. Her hair didn't stir. Her skin didn't burn. She felt no heat from it at all, and in the middle of everything, her mana moved, the way it had in the black, and settled again.

*It went round us.*

Nereth hung against her, heavy and quite still.

### B. The breath before

Before the fire, the air went.

It went out of the channel all at once, towards Nereth, and took Seralune's breath with it. Every white flower on the walls bent towards Nereth's open hand. Seralune's ears hurt. Then the pale light in the roots went out, all of it at once, as if the walls had shut their eyes.

Nereth's hand opened.

Seralune never saw the fire leave it. There was white, and a weight that came down on her whole body at once, and a sound she felt in her teeth and the soles of her feet before she heard it. Then she heard it, and it didn't stop.

The mountain answered. Far off down the channel, then nearer, then under her, stone broke and went on breaking, chamber after chamber, further than she could count. The ledge dropped under her feet and caught again. Water that had lain still in its channel for longer than anyone could remember stood up out of it and went past her as steam.

Then the fire came back.

She held on to Nereth, because there was nothing else in the world to hold, and turned her face into Nereth's hair, and it went round them.

## 11. The hole

### A. Stars

When it had gone, the roots were gone with it, and so were the dead.

The channel ran on ahead of her, black and bare and smoking, and the stone ticked as it cooled. A long way off, at the end of it, there was a ragged hole, and in it, stars.

Nereth lay across her knees and didn't move. Seralune put her ear to her mouth, and for a long, terrible time there was nothing, and then there was a breath.

*Good. Then get up, Seralune. You are not going to die in a hole, and neither is she.*

She got Nereth onto her back somehow, with Nereth's arms over her shoulders and her own hands locked under Nereth's knees. Her legs shook so badly that she thought they would fold, the way they had in the black when she tried to stand.

*Then they can shake. Walk.*

She walked towards the stars.

The hole was taller than the palace gates. The stone round its edges had melted and run like candle wax and set again, and below it, where the side of the mountain had been, a long black scar ran down the slope until she lost it, still burning in places.

All her life, people had said there was nobody in Natharul like Thaer. She had never seen him do anything like this.

### B. The ocean below

She came out onto the lip of the hole and had to stop, because there was no more mountain in front of her.

The fire had gone straight through it. Below her, the slope fell away in a long burnt furrow, wider than the court she had stood in last night, with fires still burning along its edges and smoke going up into the stars. At the bottom of it, a long way down, something pale moved and caught the starlight, and moved again. It was a while before she understood that it was the ocean.

The wind came up the furrow into her face. It was cold, and it smelled of salt and burning, and it was the first air she had breathed since the arch that didn't smell of rot.

All her life, people had said there was nobody in Natharul like Thaer. She had never seen him do anything like this.

On her back, Nereth's head moved against her shoulder, and was still.

## 12. Fresh air, and down the mountain

### A. "It felt like me"

They were a long way down the furrow when Nereth's arms tightened round her neck.

"Your Highness."

Seralune nearly dropped her. Nereth's voice was hoarse and very weak, but it was her own again, and so was the way she said it.

"Put me down. I'm perfectly capable of walking."

"You're perfectly capable of falling on your face. I've watched you nearly do it all night."

"Then Your Highness should put me down before she drops me. You aren't built for carrying things."

"I'm built for whatever I decide." Seralune hitched her higher. "Be quiet."

They went on. The stars began to go out over the water, one by one.

"Leave me here, then," Nereth said, after a long time.

"Don't be absurd."

"You didn't even stop to think about it."

"I thought about it under the mountain."

"Under the mountain, I had my hands round your throat."

"I noticed."

Nereth was quiet for so long that Seralune thought she had fallen asleep.

"It felt like me," she said.

Seralune walked for a while before she answered.

"Then you were angry with me. Fine. Be angry with me. You're still not staying on this mountain."

The sky was greying over the ocean when Nereth spoke again, into her hair.

"If this is how you rescue everyone, Your Highness, I understand your brother's concern."

It was so exactly Nereth, so dry and so tired, that Seralune laughed out loud, and then found she was crying, and was furious with herself for it, and couldn't stop.

"When we get to the bottom," she said, when she could, "I'll find you a bed. You can spend the whole day telling me everything I did wrong."

"Only a day?"

### B. Sorry, and the ocean

They were halfway down the furrow when Nereth's arms tightened round her neck.

"Your Highness." A long pause. "I'm… sorry. Don't make me say it twice."

It was her own voice. Seralune had to stop walking.

"For what?"

"Don't be obtuse, Your Highness. It doesn't suit you." Nereth's breath caught against her neck. "I remember all of it. The wall, and what I said, and your throat—"

Seralune hitched her higher on her back and went on.

"Sorry won't get us down this mountain," she said.

There was a long silence. Then, against her neck, she felt Nereth smile.

The light came up out of the water before the sun did. First the sky went grey over it, then the grey went pale, and then the ocean was there below them, wider than anything Seralune had ever seen from her window, going on and on until it met the sky.

She felt Nereth lift her head to look at it.

*I've wanted to see what's past the falls since I was small.*

Neither of them said anything. Seralune went on down the mountain with Nereth on her back, and Nereth looked at the ocean the whole way, and didn't put her head down again.
