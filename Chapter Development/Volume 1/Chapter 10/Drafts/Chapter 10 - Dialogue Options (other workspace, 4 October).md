## Dialogue options rebuilt from Chapters 7–9

Prepared 4 October 2026 after the author rejected the previous options for lacking emotional and individual voice.

These are twelve moments with two alternatives each. They are passages for comparison, not the continuous chapter. The wording of the opening is also reopened under the current request; its established circumstances remain.

The companion document, **Chapter 10 - Dialogue Diagnosis Against Chapters 7-9 - 2026-10-04.md**, explains the failures in the earlier proposal with evidence from the manuscript.

Seralune remains mentally clear throughout. Nereth's corruption affects what she hears, remembers, believes and tries to make Seralune do. Neither woman knows its cause. The copied voices from the bodies are real environmental sounds, separate from Nereth's private misperceptions.

The alternatives incorporate the author's latest chapter shape: the initial route brings them round in circles; Seralune proposes the right-hand tunnel; Nereth becomes sluggish and stops answering until the creature encounter. Seralune helps the creature, Nereth tackles and attempts to strangle her there, and the creature's bite lets Seralune break free. ## 1. Leaving the arch; asking about Cyrandor

### A — Seralune wants reassurance that Nereth cannot give

The passage narrowed until Nereth had to go ahead. Seralune kept her bound right hand against her chest, away from the wall, and held on to Nereth with her left.

There was water falling somewhere beyond them. She could hear the drops quite clearly.

*Surely I should be able to hear the falls here.*

"Do you think he will be all right?"

Nereth slowed.

"Cyrandor?"

"Thaer sent someone. He knows where to find him." Seralune waited for an answer. "You heard him, didn't you?"

"Yes, Your Highness."

They went a little further. Nereth was looking down at the ledge, and Seralune tried to leave it there.

"My brother wouldn't let Leorin hurt him. Not after I asked."

Nereth stopped.

"I hope he won't."

It was the answer Seralune had been asking for, almost. She looked at Nereth's face beyond the lamp.

"You don't believe me."

"I want to."

Nereth looked back the way they had come. The arch was no longer visible.

"He was standing right there. I thought he was coming through after us."

"So did I."

Nereth turned away. For a moment she did not move at all.

"Forgive me," Seralune said. "I shouldn't keep making you talk about him."

"You needn't apologise, Your Highness. Just give me a moment."

Seralune waited. When Nereth lifted the lamp again, she followed without asking anything else.

### B — Nereth tries to keep working because she cannot bear to stop

"Keep your hand close, Your Highness. The wall's rough here."

Seralune drew her injured hand further against her chest. Nereth went ahead with the lamp in her left hand and Seralune's good hand in her right.

They had passed beyond the last sound of the falls. Water dripped further in, and Seralune kept listening beneath it for the noise she knew.

"Nereth. Do you think Cyrandor will be all right?"

"I don't know."

The answer came quickly. Nereth had not turned.

"I was only thinking that Thaer—"

"I heard what he said."

Seralune fell quiet. Nereth took two more steps, then stopped.

"I'm sorry, Your Highness."

"You haven't done anything."

"You asked me a question."

Nereth brought the lamp closer to the wall. Her mouth was pressed tight, and Seralune could not tell what she was looking at.

"He told me to mind your hand. That was the last thing he said to me before we went downstairs."

Seralune remembered it.

"And mind yourself."

Nereth drew a breath through her nose.

"Yes."

Seralune wanted to put her arm round her. There was scarcely room for them to stand beside one another.

"We can stop here, if you need to."

"Not yet, please."

Nereth looked ahead again.

"Let me get you over this broken stretch first."

She held Seralune's hand more firmly. Seralune went when she did.

## 2. The first turning

### A — Seralune admits what she has been hoping

Nereth stopped beside the pillar.

"This is as far as we came."

Seralune looked into the left passage, then the right. Neither went anywhere she could see.

"Nothing else you recognise?"

"We worked from the left edge."

"Then we can go that way."

"We can try."

Seralune looked at her.

"I know you said you hadn't been further. I kept hoping you'd recognise something when we got here."

"I wish I did, Your Highness."

Nereth held the lamp into the left opening.

"The ledge is broken just inside. I can see enough for us to get over that. After that, I would be guessing."

"Then guess. Please. I can't choose between them by looking."

Nereth was quiet for a moment.

"The left, then. Stay close while we cross."

"And if we have to come back?"

Seralune heard the question leave her mouth and thought of the stairs, the guards, the room.

Nereth turned towards her.

"To this pillar. We don't have to go back upstairs."

Seralune nodded. She had needed someone to say it.

"All right. Show me the broken part."

### B — Nereth lets Seralune choose without pretending she knows

"Wait here, Your Highness."

Nereth went to the left opening and raised the lamp. Seralune stood beside the pillar until she returned.

"There's room to walk. I can't see round the next bend."

"What about the other?"

Nereth checked that one too.

"Much the same."

Seralune looked at the dark water between them.

"There ought to be something telling people where to go."

"Perhaps there was."

Nereth moved the lamp across the stones. Roots filled the joints; there was no mark Seralune could recognise.

"Which would you take?"

"The left. That's where we worked."

Seralune looked back once.

"We'll try it."

"Stay behind me until we're past the broken ledge."

"I can manage a ledge."

Nereth held her hand out anyway.

Seralune took it.

"I only meant you needn't keep worrying about me."

"I need to see where you're putting your feet. Come a little closer, please."

Seralune moved closer. It was easier than continuing to insist she did not need the help.

## 3. The first return, then the marked return

### A — Nereth's shame grows into refusal

"We've come back."

Nereth stopped beside the pillar. She brought the lamp down to the footprints and stood looking at them.

"Oh."

Seralune was too tired to be angry yet.

"That branch must join this one. We can take the other."

"I should have noticed."

"Neither of us noticed."

Nereth wiped her face with her sleeve.

"Give me a moment."

While she stood there, Seralune let go of her hand and scratched a line across the pillar with a loose stone.

"There. We won't mistake it for another."

Nereth watched her do it.

"The other branch, then," she said. "Keep hold of me."

They walked until Seralune's feet hurt through the soft soles. They stopped once, but the cold stone was no place to sit for long.

When the pillar appeared again, Nereth kept walking.

"Nereth."

"I know. Past the pillar, then left."

"No. The mark."

Nereth turned.

"That isn't yours."

Seralune held her breath, then let it out.

"It is. I made it beside that broken corner."

"Yours was lower."

"Come and look."

"I was standing beside you. I know where you put it."

Seralune looked at the corner again. She remembered reaching across it; the scratch began exactly where she had put the stone.

"Then let me make another. We can both watch this time."

"And spend another hour walking back to admire it?"

Seralune's face warmed.

"I don't want to come back either."

Nereth turned towards the left opening.

"Then come."

"I need to sit down."

"We just sat."

"My feet hurt."

Nereth looked back. For a moment Seralune thought she would offer her arm.

"They'll still hurt when you get up."

### B — Seralune stops following before she understands why Nereth has changed

On the first return, Nereth found their footprints before Seralune did.

"We've gone round."

She lowered the lamp and traced the way they had come with her free hand.

"It must have been the branch after the low arch. I turned right."

"Then left this time."

"Yes."

Seralune scratched a line on the pillar before they went. Nereth waited while she did it, and helped her over the broken ledge again.

The second time, Seralune saw the line before Nereth raised the lamp towards it.

She let go of Nereth's hand.

"I'm not going through there again."

"We have to go somewhere."

"We've already been somewhere. We've been walking for hours, and we're still here."

Nereth was staring at the mark.

"I took the left."

"I know."

"I took the left."

"Nereth, I was behind you. I'm not saying you didn't."

Nereth turned on her.

"Come round the pillar. I'll show you."

Seralune stepped back.

"Show me from here."

Nereth lifted the lamp too quickly. Oil ran over its rim.

"You can't see from there."

Seralune looked at the lamp, then at her face.

"Then bring the lamp back. I need to sit down."

"We can't sit here all night."

"I can't walk round again either."

Nereth started towards the passage.

Seralune stayed beside the pillar.

"Leave the lamp, then. I'm not sitting in the dark."

## 4. Nereth hears or sees an accusation Seralune has not made

### A — The lamp exposes how much Nereth is struggling

Nereth missed the place she meant to put her foot.

Seralune reached for her arm. Nereth caught the wall first, striking the lamp against it.

"Give it to me," Seralune said. "You can use both hands."

"I'm quite all right, Your Highness."

She tried to move on. The flame was shaking so badly that the wall seemed to shift with it.

"Nereth, wait."

"What is it now?"

Seralune held her hand out.

Nereth looked at it.

"If I give it to you, will you stop looking at me like that?"

"Like what?"

"You've been doing it since the pillar."

Seralune drew her hand back.

"I thought you might fall."

"And you were waiting to see?"

"I reached for you."

Nereth looked at the arm Seralune had touched.

"Every time the light swings, I think you're going into the water," Seralune said. "Give it to me for a little while."

Nereth did not answer.

She drew her sleeve down over the hand holding the lamp and went on.

Seralune lowered her own hand. She walked closer to the wall.

### B — Nereth's certainty makes Seralune afraid to speak

"Say it properly."

Seralune looked up.

"What?"

Nereth had stopped a little way ahead.

"You can laugh when we're out. I haven't got us there yet."

Seralune looked at her for a moment.

"I couldn't laugh if I tried."

"I heard you."

"I've been counting the bends. I keep losing count, and I thought if I could remember one of them—"

"Poor Nereth."

The words came out in a high, ugly imitation. Seralune stopped.

"Stop doing my voice."

Nereth turned away with the lamp.

Seralune stood still as the light moved along the ledge.

"Nereth, wait."

Nereth stopped without looking round.

"I can't see where to put my foot."

The lamp turned towards her.

Seralune crossed the gap. She had been silent before Nereth stopped; she could not think where the words had come from.

## 5. Sitting down; an ordinary offer becomes difficult

### A — Seralune asks for company, and Nereth cannot accept it

At a wider patch of ledge, Seralune sat without asking. Her legs had begun to shake.

Nereth stood beside her.

"We shouldn't stay long."

"Sit with me a moment."

"I'm quite all right."

"You said that upstairs, and you hadn't sat down all night."

Nereth looked towards the channel.

Seralune moved along the stone to leave room.

"Please. I don't want you standing over me while I'm sitting here."

Nereth sat at the other end.

Seralune lifted one side of the shawl.

"Come closer. This covered my feet before. It will go round us both."

"Put it back on."

"You're cold too."

Nereth gave a small, impatient breath.

"And then you'll have me up again."

Seralune reached far enough to lay the edge over Nereth's knees.

Nereth moved it back.

"I'll sit here."

Seralune put the shawl over her own shoulders. She watched the water.

Nereth set the lamp down and took hold of the loose edge of Seralune's sleeve.

"Your binding's caught."

She freed the cloth without pulling on the cut.

Seralune did not know whether to thank her or ask again.

### B — Seralune's own fear comes out before her offer

"We need to stop."

"We stopped at the pillar."

"Then we'll stop again."

Seralune sat down. She could not keep walking while Nereth was looking at her like that.

Nereth stood with the lamp.

"Stay there, then. I'll see where this goes."

She turned towards the passage.

"Don't leave me here."

Nereth stopped.

"I'm not leaving you. I'm looking ahead."

"You're taking the light."

Seralune could hear how young she sounded. She hated it and could not help it.

"Will you sit here first? Just for a little while. I don't want to listen to you going away."

Nereth remained standing.

"You've had me beside you since we came in."

"I know."

Seralune had to swallow before she could go on.

"I don't know why you've started looking at me like that. I keep trying to ask you, and everything I say makes it worse. So please just sit here. We needn't talk."

Nereth set the lamp down and sat.

For a while Seralune listened to her breathing. Then she laid part of the shawl over Nereth's knees.

Nereth left it there.

Seralune was careful not to thank her for sitting down.

## 6. Seralune proposes the right-hand tunnel

### A — She asks Nereth to let her try

The mark was still there beside the pillar.

Seralune held her hand against it. She had made it herself. There was no longer any reason to pretend they had reached another turning.

"We haven't tried the right."

Nereth kept the lamp facing left.

"That goes further in."

"And this brings us back here."

Nereth said nothing.

Seralune turned towards her.

"Let me take the lamp. You needn't keep finding the way for both of us."

The handle remained against Nereth's skirt.

"I was trying."

"I know you were. Please, Nereth. Let me try this one."

For a moment she thought Nereth would refuse. Then the lamp came towards her, and she took it in her good left hand.

Nereth moved to follow. Her shoe dragged across the stone.

Seralune waited until she was beside her before entering the right-hand passage.

### B — Seralune stops waiting for agreement

Nereth had begun towards the left opening again.

"Wait."

She stopped.

"The mark's here. We came through that one."

Seralune pointed with her bound hand, then lowered it. Nereth had not looked where she was pointing.

"Nereth. Give me the lamp."

"Then take it."

The handle struck her open left palm. She caught it before the lamp could swing into the pillar.

"We'll try the right. Stay behind me."

Nereth looked at her.

"Now you know the way."

"No." Seralune raised the light. "But we haven't been down there. I want to look."

She entered slowly, leaving enough room for Nereth to follow.

At the first bend she turned. Nereth was still beside the pillar.

"Come with me."

Nereth began walking. Seralune waited.

## 7. The right tunnel: Nereth stops answering

### A — Seralune waits for an answer, then changes how she walks

The footsteps behind her had grown louder.

Seralune turned. Nereth's shoe caught against a stone, and she dragged it free without looking down. Her breathing carried along the passage.

"Shall we stop?"

Nereth came towards her.

"There should be room just past this bend. We could sit a little."

Nereth stopped beside her. One hand stayed against the wall.

Seralune lowered the lamp to her face.

"Nereth?"

Her eyes moved towards the light. She said nothing.

Seralune waited. The breathing did not grow quieter.

"Let me know if you need to sit. I'll go more slowly."

She turned, took two steps and looked back.

Nereth followed.

At the next bend Seralune did not ask another question. She held the lamp low and waited for the heavy footsteps to catch up.

### B — A familiar act of care no longer happens

The binding had loosened against Seralune's sleeve. She could feel the wet edge catching each time she moved her right hand.

"Nereth, could you—"

She turned.

Nereth was several steps behind, head lowered, breathing through her open mouth. One foot scraped along the ledge before she brought the other forward.

Seralune stopped. Nereth took another step before stopping too.

"Never mind. I'll do it."

She set the lamp down and tucked the loose edge beneath the binding with her left hand. It hurt more than she expected.

Nereth stood beside her.

"Are you feeling ill?"

Nothing.

Seralune looked up.

"Please look at me."

Nereth's face remained turned towards the water.

Seralune tightened the cloth as best she could and picked up the lamp. She waited before moving on.

After a while Nereth came with her.

## 8. The rooted dead; Nereth remains silent

### A — Seralune tries to make Nereth see what she sees

The lamp found a shoe beneath the roots.

Seralune raised it. A grey skirt hung against the wall; above it, an open mouth held pale flowers between the teeth.

She backed into Nereth.

"Don't come any closer."

Nereth stopped. Seralune could hear her breathing beside her ear.

The roots had gone through the body's chest. One hand was held against the wall with its fingers spread, as though the person had been trying to push away.

"Nereth, look."

No answer came.

Seralune raised the lamp further. Another grey sleeve lay behind the first.

She began to ask whether servants had been sent down here, then looked at Nereth's face and stopped.

"We'll keep close to the water. There's room to get past."

She wanted Nereth to tell her that was a bad idea. She would have listened.

Nereth followed when she moved.

### B — Seralune cannot keep Nereth from drifting towards the wall

Something scraped beyond the lamp.

Seralune stopped and lifted the light. A small shape moved behind the roots, too quickly for her to see what it was.

Nereth continued past her.

"Wait."

Her shoulder brushed a grey sleeve hanging from the wall.

Seralune caught the back of her dress and pulled her away. The sleeve did not move with them. There was an arm inside it, held fast beneath the roots.

"Stay here."

She moved the light.

The head above the arm had fallen against one shoulder. Flowers grew out of its mouth. Another body lay lower down, beneath Nereth's feet.

Seralune could not make herself look at the face for long.

She loosened her grip on the dress.

"I'm sorry. I didn't want you touching them."

Nereth stood where she had put her.

Seralune waited for her to look down, or pull her skirt free, or say anything at all.

Then she raised the lamp and found a place for them to pass.

## 9. The copied plea interrupts the silence

### A — Seralune looks for someone living

"Help me."

Seralune raised the lamp.

A mouth beside her opened. Pale flowers shifted against its teeth.

She stepped back so quickly that her shoulder struck Nereth's.

"Can you hear me?"

The chest did not move. A root had gone through it beneath the torn grey cloth.

Further along the wall, another jaw opened.

"Help me."

Seralune looked towards Nereth. She was staring ahead.

"There's someone calling."

Nereth did not answer.

"Can you hear me?" Seralune called again. "Where are you?"

The plea came from further down.

She took a step towards it, then turned back and held the lamp so Nereth could see the ledge.

"Come. Stay beside me."

Nereth followed.

Each time Seralune called, another mouth opened. None gave her a name.

### B — The cry is the only answer Seralune receives

"Help me."

Seralune stopped.

"Nereth?"

Nereth's mouth was closed.

The words came again. A jaw moved among the roots behind her.

Seralune looked at it, then at the empty place beneath its ribs.

"Who are you?"

"Help me."

This time the voice came from further along the channel.

Seralune raised the lamp towards it.

"We're here. Tell me where you are."

The words did not change.

She moved towards the next mouth, slowly, until she could see that body too. It was no more alive than the first.

A cry came from beyond it.

Behind her, Nereth's foot scraped against the stone.

Seralune turned and waited for her before going on. She could not leave the voice calling, and she could not leave Nereth behind.

## 10. Seralune helps the creature; Nereth attacks; the bite breaks her grip

Both alternatives follow the author's new proposal. The creature is made from Seralune's mana and intervenes on her behalf as author knowledge. Seralune observes the movement and the bite; she cannot yet know why it acts.

### A — Nereth will no longer let Seralune choose to stop

The cries ended at a hollow beneath an old arch. The ledge widened there, leaving a strip of bare stone between the wall and the channel.

A lizard lay in the hollow. Flowers grew along its neck, and roots had gone through the skin of its back. When it tried to move, a hind leg pulled against a thick strand laid across it.

Its mouth opened.

"Help me."

Behind them, the words came from the dead.

Seralune set the lamp down. Nereth sank against the wall with one hand pressed to her forehead.

"Come away from it."

Seralune looked back at once.

"It's caught."

"It can still bite you. Leave it there."

Seralune moved her fingers back from its head.

"I can lift that root. Just that one."

She found a loose stone and worked it beneath the strand with her good hand. The lizard strained again. Its claws scraped against the hollow.

"We're going," Nereth said. "Get up."

Seralune glanced back.

"I heard you. Give me a moment."

The root lifted. The hind leg slipped beneath it, and the lizard scrambled out so abruptly that Seralune drew back. Its claws caught her sleeve. She could feel it climbing towards her shoulder.

Nereth got to her feet.

"Look at me when I'm speaking to you."

Seralune turned, keeping her arm still.

"Nereth, what's—"

Nereth threw herself at her. Her shoulder hit Seralune's chest, and they fell sideways onto the wider stone. The lamp broke beneath Nereth's knee.

Before she could get her elbow under her, Nereth was over her.

"Why won't you listen to me?"

Fingers closed beneath Seralune's jaw.

"Nereth!"

The cry broke as the hands tightened. Seralune caught both wrists and tried to wrench them away. She could not get her fingers beneath them. Nereth leaned over her, pressing harder, and Seralune's next breath would not come.

She clawed up Nereth's forearms. Her nails caught skin; her right palm burned beneath its binding. She pushed at Nereth's chest, struck her shoulder, then grabbed for the wrists again.

Her shoes scraped across the stone. She planted one foot and tried to throw Nereth off, but the dress caught beneath her hip. She kicked until one heel struck Nereth's thigh. Nereth jolted, and for a moment Seralune thought she would let go.

The fingers tightened.

Seralune tried to scream. A broken sound scraped out of her, scarcely louder than the water.

*Let go. Please, let go.*

She pulled harder. The cut in her palm hurt so badly that her grip slipped, and she caught Nereth's sleeve instead. Her chest kept heaving against the weight above her. She needed to breathe. She opened her mouth wider, trying again, but nothing came.

Nereth was looking straight at her.

Seralune struck at her face. Her hand glanced off the cheek and fell against the stone. She dragged it up again.

The edges of her sight were going dark. She blinked, trying to keep Nereth's face clear, but could no longer make out her mouth. Her legs were still moving. She could hear her shoes against the floor; she could hardly feel them.

*It's me. Nereth, it's me.*

She tried to lift her head. Pain drove through the back of it, and she fell flat again. One hand was still caught in Nereth's sleeve. She could not make it pull.

Something moved against her upper arm.

Pale flowers brushed her cheek.

Nereth screamed. Her face turned sharply, and one hand left Seralune's throat.

Seralune seized the remaining wrist. She pulled with both hands and twisted beneath it until the grip slipped from her neck.

She rolled onto her side. The first breath came with a harsh, choking sound; she tried to take another and coughed so hard that she could not stop. Her throat hurt with every attempt.

Nereth was screaming beside her.

Seralune got one elbow beneath her. When she could look up, she saw the lizard hanging from Nereth's cheek, its teeth fastened beneath the left eye.

Nereth tore it away and flung it towards the channel. She stumbled after the motion.

Seralune pushed herself up against the wall and reached before she could think. Her left arm caught Nereth round the waist; the other braced against the stone as she pulled her back from the channel.

"Sit."

She scarcely recognised the sound of her voice.

Nereth was striking at the wound.

"Get it off!"

Seralune stayed beyond the reach of that hand.

"It's gone."

"Get it off me!"

Blood ran between Nereth's fingers. Beneath them, a dark line spread towards the corner of her eye.

Seralune swallowed and could not stop herself touching her throat.

Nereth reached for her.

"Your Highness—"

Seralune drew back.

Nereth's arm fell. She folded against the wall, still trying to scrape something from her face.

### B — A warning turns into the demand that Seralune obey her

The small creature lay beneath the arch, caught by one hind leg. Roots grew from its back. There was something terribly wrong with the way its mouth opened: each cry came from the bodies behind them as well.

Seralune put the lamp on the wider ledge.

Nereth sat against the wall.

"Don't touch it."

"I won't touch its mouth."

"Don't touch any of it."

Seralune crouched beside the hollow. A strand pressed the little leg flat against the stone. She found a loose piece of rock and eased its edge beneath the root.

"I only want to get its leg free."

Nereth kept her eyes shut.

"Get away from it. I told you to get away."

The creature pulled against the growth. Its foot slid a little further.

"I'm almost finished."

"Your Highness."

Seralune looked round.

Nereth was watching her.

"If it bites you, I can't carry you. I can barely keep myself on my feet."

Seralune looked back at the leg. The root was beginning to lift.

"We can rest here. Let me finish, and then we'll both sit."

The lizard came free. It scrambled onto her sleeve, and she held her arm still while its claws worked up the cloth.

"I told you not to touch it."

Nereth stood.

"I didn't pick it up. It climbed—"

"Then put it down."

Seralune brought her arm towards the wall.

Nereth came at her before she could lower it. Her shoulder drove Seralune sideways onto the stone. Glass broke beside her head.

Seralune tried to get up. Nereth pushed her flat and put both hands round her throat.

"Stop. Just stop."

Seralune pulled at her wrists. She tried to bend one knee beneath her, but Nereth's weight held her down. Her head struck the stone when she turned it, and the air she had meant to take would not come.

She opened her mouth.

Nothing.

Nereth bent closer. Seralune could see the pale growth reflected in her eyes. She was waiting for Nereth to recognise her.

The lizard moved beside her shoulder.

Nereth cried out. Flowers shook against her cheek, and Seralune saw the little jaw clamped beneath her left eye.

One hand lifted. Seralune caught the other, shoved it away with all the strength she could find and rolled out from under her.

She lay on her side, gasping.

Nereth tore the creature off. Blood struck the wall when she flung it away.

"It's burning."

Seralune pushed herself up. She could not go any closer yet.

Nereth staggered towards the water, one hand held out before her. Seralune caught her skirt and pulled. Nereth fell against her, and she lowered them both onto the wider stone.

"Stay here."

Nereth stared at the blood on her hand.

"Why won't it stop?"

Seralune looked at the dark marks spreading from the wound. She could not make herself answer.

Nereth caught her sleeve.

"Please."

Seralune flinched. Nereth kept hold of the cloth, but her other hand stayed against her own face.

"Tell me what to do," Seralune said.

## 11. The fire

### A — Seralune is asked for something she cannot do

Nereth would not stop touching the wound.

Seralune caught her left wrist and brought it down.

"You'll tear it open."

"Then get it out."

"There's nothing I can pull out."

Nereth looked at her. Blood was running beneath the spreading marks on her cheek.

"Don't tell me that."

Seralune tried to hold the wrist still.

"I don't want to hurt you more."

"You can feel it, can't you? You must be able to feel it."

Nereth pushed at the floor with her free hand, trying to stand. Seralune caught her as her legs gave way and drew her back under the arch.

She sat against the thick stone support with Nereth held across her lap.

"Stay here."

Nereth pulled on her sleeve.

"Please."

Seralune looked at the hand. She had been trying to get those fingers off her throat only a little while ago.

"Tell me what to do."

Nereth's right hand reached down the channel.

Flame showed between the fingers.

"Not that."

Seralune leaned towards her face.

"Nereth. Look at me."

Nereth's mouth moved.

"Help me."

It was the voice from the dead.

Seralune forgot what she had meant to say.

The light spread across the wall. She could see the roots throw shadows further and further down the channel, and then she could not see them at all.

She pulled Nereth's head against her chest.

"Wait—"

The mountain shook beneath them.

Her back struck the support. Something hit her shoulder; she felt it, then felt nothing but the sound forcing itself through her body.

She shut her eyes. The light was still there.

Stone kept striking the ledge beside her feet. She bent over Nereth and tried to keep her head beneath the surviving arch.

When she could look again, the passage ahead had gone.

Fire filled the space where its walls had stood. It poured through the breach and rolled back beneath the broken roof.

Seralune tried to get up.

Nereth's weight dragged her down. She fell against the stone and pulled her closer instead.

The fire came into the recess.

Seralune waited for her hair to catch, for her skin to burn, for the pain that would make her let go.

The flames passed round them.

She saw fire stream past Nereth's open hand without touching it. Her sleeve stayed whole. Seralune could feel the injured palm beneath its binding, and the stone at her back, and Nereth's face against her chest.

She could not understand why they were still there.

She kept holding on.

Long after the first shock, rock was still falling down the channel. Nereth's hand hung empty; there was no more fire between the fingers.

Seralune bent close to her mouth.

There was breath.

"Nereth?"

She listened again.

"Please answer me."

Nothing came, but the breath was there.

When the falling stone had slowed enough for her to raise her head, she could see stars.

The opening was enormous. The far side lay beyond the light of the fires still burning in it. Below, a broad strip of mountain had been torn open towards the coast, with trees uprooted along its edges and broken rock falling into the darkness.

She had heard people speak about Thaer's power all her life. She had never imagined this.

Nereth had done it.

Seralune looked down at the hand that had finally gone still and held her closer, listening for the next breath.

### B — Nereth tries to send her away

"Go."

Seralune had an arm beneath Nereth's shoulders.

"You can't stand."

Nereth tried to push her away.

"Your Highness, go."

Seralune flinched at the hand near her face. Then she saw where Nereth's other hand was pointing.

Light had begun between its fingers.

"Put it down."

Nereth stared down the channel.

"Nereth. Put your hand down."

The light grew.

Seralune drew her under the old arch and lowered them both against its thick support.

"You can't make me leave you here."

Nereth turned towards her.

"Help me."

The voice was the one from the corpses.

Seralune stared at her mouth.

Then the passage became white.

She shut her eyes and pressed her face into Nereth's hair. The ledge jolted beneath them hard enough to lift her foot from the stone.

She found the ground again and pushed against it, trying to keep them under the arch. The support shook at her back. A piece of stone struck her arm; another broke beside her shoe.

She had opened her mouth, but there was too much sound to hear herself.

The breaking travelled down the channel. She felt each shock through the stone and could do nothing about it.

Nereth's arm dropped across her knees.

Seralune opened her eyes.

The roof ahead was gone. Fire filled the broken passage and came back towards them, climbing over the fallen stone.

She looked for somewhere to move. There was nowhere she could reach with Nereth in her arms.

*Don't let go.*

The fire rushed into the recess.

It divided beside her face.

Seralune stared at it. Flames passed her elbow and ran beneath Nereth's slack hand. Her dress should have burned. Nereth's hair should have burned.

Neither did.

Stone was still falling. Seralune lowered her head over Nereth's and stayed beneath the support until the impacts grew further apart.

Only then did she try to speak.

"It's passed."

Her voice was scarcely there.

"Nereth. It's passed. You can look at me now."

Nereth's head fell back.

Seralune caught it and leaned close, waiting for breath against her cheek.

It came.

She stayed like that until she could make herself lift her head.

Through the breach, the coastal slope lay open far beyond the reach of the firelight. Whole stretches of the aqueduct had disappeared. The broken edge on the other side was so far away that she could not see where it ended.

She had never seen destruction like it. People had spoken about her brother as though such things belonged only to him.

Seralune looked down at Nereth's face.

She had wanted to see past the falls.

There was sky above them now, and Seralune did not know whether she would wake to see it.

## 12. Dragging Nereth out; seeing the port below

### A — A destination becomes visible before either has strength to reach it

Seralune got one of Nereth's arms over her shoulder and tried to stand.

Her right hand failed when she tried to hold the weight. She sank back against the stone and lowered Nereth as carefully as she could.

"I can't. Not like that."

Nereth's head fell against her shoulder.

Seralune listened for breath. Then she worked the shawl beneath Nereth's upper back and gathered its edges beside her shoulders.

"I'm going to move you. I need to get you out."

She pulled until Nereth's head was clear of the broken stone, then dragged her a short distance. Her throat hurt each time she drew breath. She stopped, lowered her and found another place to put her feet.

At the next pull, cold air reached her face.

Seralune kept going towards it.

The first open ground was not level. She hauled Nereth clear of the breach and settled her against a broad stone before looking down.

The mountain had been torn open. Broken channels ended above empty space, and a strip of shattered rock ran down the slope, with fires still burning along its edges.

Beyond it, off to one side, lights lay beside the water.

As the sky grew paler, Seralune could make out roofs, then the harbour and the dark shapes of vessels beside it.

"Nereth. There's a town."

She bent closer. Nereth's eyes moved towards her face.

"Can you hear me?"

Nereth tried to lift her head. Seralune put a hand beneath it before it fell back.

"Your Highness?"

Seralune shut her eyes for a moment.

"Yes."

Nereth looked towards the lights. She could not get her arm beneath her to sit up.

Seralune stood and examined the ground below them. There was a way between the larger stones, but she could not see all of it from here.

"We'll go towards that. Slowly."

She crouched beside Nereth again and worked an arm beneath her shoulders. Getting her upright took longer than Seralune wanted it to.

The lights remained far below them when they began to move.

### B — Seralune hears the familiar voice and cannot stop to answer it

Seralune dragged Nereth towards the cold air, stopping whenever the shawl caught against broken stone.

Once it caught beneath Nereth's shoulder. She had to go back, lift her clear and pull the cloth free with her good hand. For a while she could do nothing but sit beside her and breathe.

Then she tried again.

Outside, she lowered Nereth beside a rock and stayed crouched with one hand against the ground.

The side of the mountain had gone. She could see pieces of aqueduct along the broken edge, and fires running down the long scar below it. She had never seen anything like this. People had spoken about Thaer's power all her life, but those words had never given her a picture she could believe.

Further to the side, a town stood beside the harbour.

Seralune stared at it. There were lights in the houses. There were people down there who might hear her if she could get close enough.

Something brushed her sleeve.

She flinched before she saw Nereth's hand.

"Your Highness."

Seralune looked down.

Nereth was looking at her throat.

"I—"

"Not now."

Nereth's hand withdrew.

Seralune wanted to hear what she had been about to say. She could not sit here and hear it.

"There's a town below us. I need to get you there."

Nereth tried to rise and could not. Seralune caught her head as it fell back.

"Don't try yet."

She looked at the slope, then at Nereth, and moved the shawl clear of her face.

"I'll find somewhere for us to stop. Just a little further down."

The descent began with another difficult lift. Seralune kept close to the rock and looked towards the harbour lights whenever she had to put Nereth down.

## What changes between the alternatives

The options offer different pressures, not a "more emotional" and "less emotional" setting:

- **1A** makes Seralune ask for reassurance about Thaer. **1B** lets Nereth's work carry the grief she cannot discuss.
- **2A** exposes Seralune's dependence on Nereth's knowledge. **2B** lets the ordinary work of checking the openings steady them.
- **3A** starts with shame and ends with refusal. **3B** lets Seralune stop following before she understands what has happened to Nereth.
- **4A** makes usefulness the sensitive point. **4B** turns an imagined insult into something Seralune cannot persuade her she did not say. Both precede the sustained silence.
- **5A** has an offer of shared warmth refused. **5B** has Seralune ask Nereth to remain beside her without demanding an emotional answer. Both precede the right-hand route.
- **6A** uses an appeal before Seralune takes the lamp and tries the right. **6B** uses her authority and leaves the discomfort of that choice present.
- **7A** lets Seralune change her pace when questions fail. **7B** makes a missing act of care show how unreachable Nereth has become.
- **8A** makes Seralune try to share what she sees. **8B** makes her physically keep Nereth away from the rooted dead. Nereth stays silent in both.
- **9A** gives Seralune a cautious search for someone living. **9B** makes the repeated plea the only answer her questions receive. Nereth stays silent in both.
- **10A** lets a demand for Seralune's attention become the attack. **10B** begins with a warning about the creature, then turns it into enforced obedience. Both break the long silence here and place the strangling at the creature.
- **11A** makes Seralune ask for instructions she cannot obtain. **11B** lets Nereth try to send her away before the discharge.
- **12A** gives Seralune relief at a coherent response and a visible destination. **12B** lets the familiar voice return while the attack remains difficult to discuss. Both begin with dragging and end with a slow descent towards the port.

My preferred assembly under the revised shape is **1B, 2A, 3A, 4A, 5B, 6A, 7B, 8A, 9A, 10A, 11B and 12A**. This is a recommendation, not an author selection. The separate moments do not oblige the chapter to include twelve discrete conversations; several now deliberately contain no reply from Nereth.

## Joining the passages into the chapter

Give the successful parts of their journey time to exist. Nereth should help them over ledges, identify sound footing and make decisions that work before her directions fail. Their walking, a failed rest, diminishing lamp oil and sore feet should make the passage from Day 2 towards predawn on Day 3 feel prolonged.

Seralune's early attempt to ease the silence by speaking about Cyrandor need not succeed. It is a painful topic for Nereth. Once Seralune understands this, practical questions and offers of help can provoke the later irritation instead of having her repeatedly reopen the same loss.

The corruption can amplify real fears without making every sharp reply proof of corruption. The opening reluctance to discuss Cyrandor is understandable grief. The first wrong turn is understandable uncertainty. The marked return, changed interpretation, sustained silence and physical attack establish the deterioration cumulatively.

The initial route is left. The repeated mark verifies its failure, and the right-hand route remains untried. Seralune's choice of the right is reasonable; she cannot know it leads into stronger contamination. Do not retain the former low opening as an additional route change.

After the right turn, Nereth's speech stops. Keep that silence through the bodies and the copied pleas. Her first command at the creature should register as a change; do not insert a prolonged Cyrandor argument in between.

The lamp begins in Nereth's left hand. Seralune takes it in her good left hand. It stays intact through the silent stretch and the copied pleas. She sets it down to free the creature's leg; it breaks during Nereth's tackle. The roots and flowers already give off pale light.

The freed creature climbs Seralune's sleeve before the tackle. Nereth brings her face close while bearing down over her on the wide ledge. The bite takes one hand off Seralune's throat; she pulls away the other and rolls free. Her escape remains a struggle she participates in.

The creature's origin and defensive response are author knowledge. Seralune cannot conclude that she created it, recognise it as a servant or acquire command over other corrupted creatures. Its intervention injures Nereth and creates a second emergency; it does not make the attack deserved or settle their relationship.

The wider ledge and arch in **10** prepare the position for **11**. Nereth's right hand points down the channel; Seralune holds her before the discharge. The fire returns and passes round them through contact. Stone, pressure and other ordinary physical harms remain dangerous.

Both fire options retain the copied voice emerging from Nereth's mouth after the bite. It is an uncanny observation, not an explanation of the mana or evidence of a newly established deity.

The immense discharge spends Nereth's mana. Seralune drags her towards the breach in short stages; no effortless carry is assumed. Outside, clearer thought returns gradually, while the wound, marks, exhaustion, Seralune's sore throat and memory of the attack remain.

The destruction opens an uninhabited coastal slope. The port below is visible off to one side of the scar, outside the blast's path. Preserve the prior decision that there are no living casualties aboveground. Seralune cannot survey every distant street or know the full political consequences from the breach.

End as they start the descent towards the port. They have escaped the tunnels and found a destination; reaching help and resolving what happened between them still require time.
