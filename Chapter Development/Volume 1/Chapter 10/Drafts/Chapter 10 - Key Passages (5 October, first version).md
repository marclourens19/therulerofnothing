# Chapter 10 — Key Passages

5 October 2026. These replace the 4 October options, which the author rejected: "they don't sound like the characters… flat text on a page with no emotion… I don't feel the horror, nor do I feel Nereth's descent into madness… it just feels like words and then a weird explosion and then done."

**This isn't the chapter.** It's the chapter's key passages, in order, in **one version each** instead of two. Two short options per moment can't build anything: a descent into madness only works if you can feel each step land on the one before it. So read it straight through. Then keep, cut or rewrite any passage or line, by number.

It follows the 4 October shape: the left first, the circles, the right-hand tunnel, Nereth stops answering, the dead, "Help me", the creature, one strangling, the bite, the fire, dragging her out and the port. Passage 1 is your own opening from 2 October, unchanged. Everything else is new and is listed at the end for your yes or no.

## Why the 4 October options went flat

1. **The voices you set on 2 October were lost.** You'd asked for Seralune as Alisaie and Nereth as Ram, turning aggressive when she breaks. That instruction sat on a branch that was never merged, so the other workspace never had it. Its Nereth says "I wish I did, Your Highness", "I want to", "Not yet, please" and "I'm sorry, Your Highness": a soft, sad maid. Its Seralune says "Forgive me", "Please" and "I'm sorry" again and again. Swap their names and most lines still work.
2. **Your guide to mana corruption was dropped.** None of it is on the page: the paranoia, the whispers, grandiosity, rage, obsession, losing all fear, the second voice, slips in time, losing empathy. After the right tunnel she only drags her feet and breathes. Silence takes Nereth away, and with her gone the reader sees an exhausted woman, not a mad one. Madness needs her there, and wrong.
3. **The cause was dropped.** It's decided that Seralune's fear makes Nereth worse. No passage showed it, so the decline had nothing driving it that a reader could watch.
4. **Every frightening thing was soothed at once.** A corpse appears, Seralune "could not make herself look at the face for long", and she finds a way past. Nothing in the place follows a rule, nothing confirms a fear, and nothing gets worse because of something she chose.
5. **The fire came from nowhere.** Nereth's Fire never appeared before the blast, and nothing in her head made her fire it. That's the weird explosion.
6. **The prose was chopped into report lines:** "Nereth said nothing." "Seralune waited." "No answer came." "Nereth followed." That's the list rhythm you've called robotic since Chapter 2. The narration also slipped into "did not" and "could not", where Chapter 7 says "didn't" and "couldn't".

## What these passages do instead

- **The voices.** Seralune gives orders, scolds herself, reasons and hits back. She says sorry only when she's wrong. Nereth is dry and politely superior, with "Your Highness" on the front of a cutting line.
- **Nereth's speech is the gauge.** On duty she speaks formally, with no contractions ("Your Highness did not ask"). They come when she's close to Seralune (the sea) and when she's angry (the lamp). After the right tunnel she never speaks to Seralune, but she isn't silent: she counts, talks to Cyrandor, whispers to her flame and mouths the dead's words. Her first words to Seralune are "Leave it." When she's herself again, the formality comes back, and that's how the reader knows.
- **One new kind of wrong at each step**, from your guide (table below). She does something the reader has already seen her do well, and does it wrong.
- **The cause is on the page.** Each time Seralune's mana crowds up, Nereth gets worse in the same moment. The reader can see the pattern; Seralune never puts it together.
- **The place has rules she half understands.** The flowers turn to her, not to the lamp. The dead repeat what they've heard, including her.
- **The fire is built rung by rung.** It goes from a flame bigger than Nereth meant, to a blistered thumb, to a burning sleeve she doesn't feel, to the blast. She fires it to make the voices stop.

## 1. Into the channels

*Yours, 2 October (1A revised), unchanged. It already sets up the right-hand way: "we can always turn back and try the right".*

The arch was behind them before Seralune had properly decided to go through it. After that there was the ledge, and the black water lying beside it, and Nereth's lamp making a small room of light that moved with them and never got any bigger.

She kept listening for the falls. She had listened for them all her life without knowing she did it, the way she breathed, and now she couldn't stop doing it on purpose. There was nothing: their feet on the stone, water dripping somewhere, Nereth's breathing. Under all of it, where the falls should have been, there was nothing at all.

*There's nothing down here to fear, so stop this at once. You're no child of six. You can bear this. Walk.*

Nereth hadn't said a word since the arch. She walked half a step ahead with the lamp held out and her other hand in Seralune's, and every so often she looked back over her shoulder, the way she had at every turning since the iron door. There was never anyone there.

"Nereth. About Cyrandor—"

"Not now, Your Highness." Nereth didn't turn her head. "We may speak of Cyrandor once we are out of these tunnels."

*Later, then. But you will tell me.*

The channel split round a pillar. Nereth lifted the lamp to one side and then the other, and neither way went anywhere the light could follow.

"This is as far as I have ever come," she said. "Beyond it, I would keep our hopes modest, Your Highness. Modest at best. Stay close to me, and we shall find our way out together."

"Which way, then?"

"When last they sent us down, the water ran from the left." Nereth held the lamp towards it. "What would Your Highness have us do?"

*And if it isn't the left, we can always turn back and try the right.*

"The left, then," Seralune said. "Lead on, Nereth."

## 2. The ledge, and the first flame

*Nereth at her most capable, so there's something to lose. Her Fire appears for the first time, and it comes out bigger than she meant.*

The left-hand way was narrower. The ledge ran along under the wall with the water a step below it, and every so often the edge had broken away, so that Nereth had to stop and hold the lamp low and find where it began again.

Seralune put her right hand out to the wall.

"Your hand, Your Highness."

She took it back and held it against her chest. "It's only a wall."

"It is a wet wall, and a filthy one, and that cut has opened twice since last night. If it festers down here, I shall have to explain to Cyrandor how I let—" Nereth stopped. She lifted the lamp again. "Keep it close, please."

Seralune had heard Cyrandor at the door. *Mind her hand.* She kept it close, and didn't ask Nereth to finish.

Further on, a whole length of the ledge had gone. Nereth crossed first on what was left of it, with her shoulder to the wall, then set the lamp down on the far side and turned and held out both hands.

"Step where I stepped, Your Highness. There, and then there. Not near the edge."

Seralune stepped where she had stepped. On the second stone her borrowed shoe slid on something soft, and before she'd even felt herself start to go, Nereth had her by the forearm, well above the binding, and pulled her across.

"There."

"You might have told me it was slippery."

"I did tell Your Highness. Your Highness was looking at the water."

She had been. It lay beside the ledge, black and so still that the lamp sat in it without a ripple. Once, a long way out, something touched the surface from underneath. The rings went out from the place slowly, until they reached the ledge and lapped against it, and nothing came up.

*Whatever that is, it's down there and you're up here. Walk.*

They had gone a long way past it when the lamp went out.

A drop fell from the roof straight onto the wick. The flame hissed and shrank to a blue bead, and then there was no light at all, anywhere, and Seralune couldn't have said which side of her the water was on.

Her mana crowded up under her ribs, the way it always did when she was frightened, so full she could hardly breathe round it.

"Hold still, Your Highness." Nereth's voice was perfectly level. "Don't move your feet."

There was a small sound, like a breath let out, and a flame stood up on the end of Nereth's finger.

It lit her face from below. She touched it to the wick, and the flame jumped, up past the top of the glass, so high and white that Seralune had to shut her eyes. Nereth shook her hand out sharply. When Seralune looked again, there was only the lamp, burning small and yellow as before.

Nereth was looking at her fingers.

"That was larger than I meant," she said.

"You have Fire."

"Yes, Your Highness."

"You might have mentioned it before we came down here with one lamp between us."

"Your Highness did not ask." Nereth picked the lamp up. "And I can make a flame, but I cannot hold one for hours. The oil will last longer than I would."

Before she started walking, she rubbed the tips of her fingers together, as though they were still warm.

## 3. Past the falls

*New. Seralune's attempt at closeness, after Cyrandor is closed to her (the 4 October beat 2). It's the last good moment between them, and the ending pays it off.*

They walked for a long time.

Mostly to hear something besides the water and their own feet, Seralune said, "What did you think was past the falls? When you were small."

Nereth didn't answer straight away, and Seralune thought she wasn't going to.

"The sea, Your Highness."

"The sea?"

"The laundry women used to say that if you went far enough past the falls, you came to water so big you couldn't see the other side of it, and it was salt all the way through, so you couldn't drink a drop." Her voice didn't change, but she walked a little slower. "I didn't believe a word of it. I thought they were making a fool of me. I wanted to go and see it for myself, so I could come back and tell them so."

"And now?"

"Now I am in a drain, Your Highness, with a princess." Nereth lifted the lamp over a fallen stone. "They would never believe that either."

Seralune laughed before she knew it was coming. The sound went off along the water ahead of them, and a little after she had stopped, it came back to them from somewhere further on.

Nereth hadn't laughed. But when Seralune glanced at her, the corner of her mouth had moved, and it stayed that way for a few steps.

## 4. The circles, and the mark

*The first return is innocent; the second isn't. Nereth hears her name in the dripping and starts counting from the wrong number. When Seralune's mana rises, Nereth stops dead.*

They came to the pillar again a long time later.

Seralune didn't know it at first. She only knew that she was tired and that her heels had rubbed raw in the borrowed shoes, and then Nereth stopped and lowered the lamp, and there in the silt at the foot of the pillar were their own prints: the soft soles of Seralune's shoes and Nereth's harder ones beside them, going away down the left-hand opening.

"Ah," Nereth said.

She studied them.

"The branch after the broken ledge. It must have curved round and brought us back. That was my fault, Your Highness. I should have seen it."

"Neither of us saw it." Seralune felt about in the silt with her good hand until she found a chip of stone. "Hold the lamp up."

It was harder with her left hand than she'd thought. The first line she scratched on the pillar didn't show at all, and the second skidded away sideways. The third was a pale, crooked mark, as high as she could reach.

Nereth watched all three tries.

"Is Your Highness decorating?"

"I'm marking it, so we'll know it if we see it again."

"We shall not see it again."

She said it so flatly that Seralune almost believed her, and was annoyed with herself for how much she wanted to.

They took the left again, and at the branch after the broken ledge, Nereth took the other way.

She began counting the turnings. She did it under her breath, so quietly that at first Seralune thought she was praying: "Four," at one opening, and "Five," at the next, with her lips still moving in between, as if she were going back over the ones before.

At the seventh, she stopped.

"Did Your Highness call me?"

"No."

Nereth stood with her head on one side. Somewhere ahead, water was dripping: one drop, then a long wait, then another.

"Someone did."

Seralune held her breath until her chest hurt. There was a drop, and a long wait, and another drop.

"It's the water, Nereth."

"The water does not know my name, Your Highness."

She went on. After a while she started counting again, from four.

The second time, Seralune knew the pillar before Nereth lifted the lamp to it. She knew the crack that ran up one side of it and the chip out of its foot, and then the light came up, and there was her mark, pale and crooked, as high as she could reach.

She put her finger in it. It had taken her three tries.

"Nereth. We've come back."

Nereth didn't look at the pillar. She looked at Seralune.

"That isn't yours."

"It's mine. It took me three tries, and you stood there and watched every one."

"Yours was neater."

"Mine was a mess. Look at it!"

"Then someone else down here is as clumsy as Your Highness." Nereth turned the lamp to the left-hand opening. "This way."

It was the way they had gone both times before.

Seralune didn't move. Her mana had come up, cold and enormous, under her ribs, and Nereth, with her back to her, stopped as if she had walked into a wall.

"Don't," Nereth said, without turning round. "Whatever you are doing back there. Don't."

"I'm not doing anything!"

Nereth stood a moment longer with the lamp held out. Then she went into the left-hand opening, and the light went with her. Seralune went after it, because the only other choice was to stand by herself, with no light at all, beside a pillar she had now seen three times.

*Wonderful. Lost under the palace with a guide who can't count, and I'm the one who said "Lead on".*

It didn't sound funny, even in her head.

## 5. The offer, and "Poor Nereth"

*Your "she lashes out at Seralune's small kindnesses". Her care survives the anger for one more passage. Then she hears Seralune say something Seralune never said.*

The lamp had begun to shake.

Seralune watched it from behind for a long time before she said anything: the flame trembling in its glass, and the light trembling on the wall with it, so that the stones seemed to shift whenever she looked at them. Last night, with two swords a step away from her, Nereth's hands hadn't shaken at all.

"Let me carry that for a while."

"I am quite all right, Your Highness."

"You're shaking. Give it to me, and you can have both hands for the wall."

"I am not *shaking*." Nereth turned round so fast that the flame lay down sideways in its glass. "I'm cold, and I'm tired, and I've had Your Highness hanging off my arm since the arch, and I'd thank you to stop *looking* at me as though I'm about to fall in!"

Her voice went off along the water, and came back to them late.

Then something went smooth in her face, as if a hand had wiped it, and she was looking at Seralune's sleeve.

"I beg Your Highness's pardon. Your binding has come loose."

She set the lamp down, took Seralune's right hand, tucked the loose end of the linen back under and drew it firm, all without hurting her once. Her fingers were as quick and careful as they had been last night. They were also shaking so badly that it took her three tries.

Seralune let her do it. She had opened her mouth to say *I won't be shouted at*, and she shut it again.

*She's lost Cyrandor, and she hasn't slept, and she's held you up every step since the arch. Let her shout. You'd shout too.*

She didn't offer again.

By then she was counting her own steps, to have something to think about besides the water and the pillar and how far down they might have come. She had got to two hundred and something when Nereth stopped ahead of her.

"Say it to my face, Your Highness."

Seralune looked up. "Say what?"

"You can laugh at me once we're out. I haven't got us there yet." Nereth didn't turn round. "But if you've something to say about me, say it to my face."

"I haven't said a word."

"'Poor Nereth.'" It came out high and sweet, not in Nereth's own voice at all, and Seralune went cold, because it was a little like hers. "I heard you."

"I didn't say that. I didn't say anything. I was counting."

Nereth turned her head, just far enough to look at her over her shoulder. In the lamplight her eyes were very steady, and quite sure.

"I heard you," she said again, and went on.

*I didn't say it. I know I didn't.*

Seralune went after the light.

## 6. The failed rest

*Their one stop, to give the night its length. Nereth looks after her in the worst way, and her Fire takes the next step.*

Where the ledge widened under a low arch, Seralune sat down. She didn't ask. Her legs stopped being something she could stand on, and she put her back to the wall and slid down it.

"We shouldn't stop," Nereth said.

"Then go on, and I'll catch you up."

It was meant as a joke. Nereth didn't take it as one. She stood with the lamp and looked down the passage, and for a long, horrible moment Seralune thought she might really go.

Then she put the lamp down and stood beside it.

"Sit, Nereth."

"I'm quite all right."

Seralune hadn't the strength to argue. She pulled the shawl down off her hair and round her shoulders, drew her feet up under her skirt, and shut her eyes, only to rest them.

When she opened them, Nereth's face was a hand's width from hers.

She was crouched over her with the lamp held close to Seralune's cheek. The oil had burned so low that the lamp's flame was hardly bigger than a seed, and Nereth was holding a second flame on the end of her thumb beside it, to see by. The skin round her thumbnail had blistered white. She didn't seem to know.

"You stopped breathing, Your Highness," Nereth said.

Seralune couldn't move.

"I was waiting to see whether you'd start again."

"And if I hadn't?"

Nereth looked at her. She seemed to give the question real thought. Then the flame on her thumb went out, and she stood up and took the lamp with her.

"We should go."

Seralune didn't know how long she had slept. The lamp had been half full when she sat down. It took her two tries to get up, and when she did, Nereth was already walking.

## 7. The right-hand way

*Seralune takes charge, the way she always does, and it costs her something to do it. The tunnel welcomes Nereth. After this she stops answering.*

The pillar came round a fourth time.

Seralune knew it by its crack before the light reached it. When the light did reach it, there was her mark, and below it their prints going left, trodden over and over, one on top of another, until the silt was churned to mud.

*Enough.*

She had let everyone lead her since she woke. Thaer had taken her up the stairs and Cyrandor had taken her down them, and she had let Nereth take her round this pillar three times, because Nereth had been down here once and she hadn't, and because being led was easier than thinking.

*If we're going to be lost, I'll at least choose where.*

"Give me the lamp, Nereth. I'm going first."

"Your Highness doesn't know the way."

"*Neither do you!* We've passed this pillar four times!"

It went off down every opening at once and came back to them out of all of them, a little late, in pieces. Nereth's fingers tightened on the handle.

"Give me the lamp. That's an order."

This morning she had told Nereth she was sorry for giving her an order, and asked her properly instead. She hadn't even lasted the day.

"As Your Highness commands."

Nereth held the lamp out by the very end of its handle, as if it were something dirty, and let go before Seralune's fingers had closed on it. Seralune caught it in her left hand and lifted it.

Left was the circle. Straight on ran back the way they'd come. To the right, the opening they had never tried went down in three shallow steps, and the silt in front of it was smooth, without a mark in it.

"That goes further in, Your Highness," Nereth said behind her. "Even you can see it goes further in."

"And the left goes round. I've seen quite enough of the left."

She went down the first step. The air was different there: warmer, and close, and it smelled of wet earth, and under that, of something sweet that had been left too long.

Behind her, Nereth let out her breath.

It was a long breath, slow, the kind you let out when you've put down something heavy. When Seralune turned with the lamp, Nereth was standing at the top of the steps with her eyes half shut and her face lifted a little towards the passage.

"Oh," she said softly. It wasn't to Seralune. "There you are."

"Nereth? Who are you talking to?"

Nereth opened her eyes. She looked at Seralune for a while, the way she might have looked at something someone had left on the steps. Then she came down them.

## 8. The right tunnel: no answer

*The 4 October silence, with Nereth still in it. She stops answering Seralune, but she isn't silent. Seralune works out that it's this place, and chooses to go on anyway.*

The steps gave onto a long slope, with the channel running down beside it, and the water in the channel had begun to move again, slowly, the same way they were going.

Seralune went first. With the lamp in her good hand, there was no hand left to hold, so she kept her cut one against her chest and listened behind her for Nereth's feet.

They came slower than before, and heavier, every step landing flat, and between them one shoe scraped along the stone before the other came down. Nereth was breathing through her mouth.

"Shall we stop for a while?"

The footsteps kept coming.

"There's room past this bend, I think. We could sit."

Nereth came round the bend and stopped beside her with one hand flat on the wall, though the ledge was wide there and she had no need of it. Seralune lowered the lamp to see her face.

"Nereth?"

Her eyes went to the light, the way anyone's would. They didn't come to Seralune.

Seralune waited. Somewhere ahead, the water went over something in a small hollow sound, again and again.

"I'll go more slowly," she said at last. "Tell me if you need to stop."

After that she asked less. She walked slower and looked back over her shoulder every few steps, and every time, Nereth was there, a few paces behind, with her head down, coming on.

She was talking.

At first Seralune took it for breathing. Then she caught a number, and another. Nereth was counting her steps, the way Seralune had counted hers, but she couldn't get past twenty. She'd reach nineteen, or seventeen, and lose it and start again from one, and her voice never rose or fell, or sounded the least bit troubled at losing her place.

"Nereth, what are you counting?"

"…six. Seven."

Further on, she stopped counting and began talking to someone else.

"I am," she said. She sounded the way she had in the bedroom, with her hands folded and her eyes on the floor, answering someone she had to answer. "I am minding her. I haven't let go of her once."

Seralune turned with the lamp.

Nereth's right hand was held a little out from her side, the way she had held it out at the arch for Seralune to take. The fingers were closed, carefully, round nothing.

*Mind her hand. And mind yourself.*

*She thinks he's behind us. She thinks she's still holding on to me.*

Seralune went back to her. She took the closed hand in her own cut one, because the lamp was in the other, and it hurt, and she held on anyway.

"I'm here. I'm right here, Nereth. Look at me."

Nereth looked down at their hands for a long time.

Then she took hers away, gently, as though Seralune's had been in its way, and closed it round nothing again.

Seralune stood with her own hand held out and empty.

*She was herself at the arch.* She made herself go back over it, the way she would have gone over a lesson. *At the pillar she was sharp. At the mark she was wrong, and by the lamp she was shouting at me, and since the steps she won't even look at me. It's this place. The longer she's in it, the less of her there is.*

She looked back up the slope. At the edge of the light she could just make out the bottom of the three steps.

*And back is the pillar, and the pillar is the circle, and the lamp won't last another time round.*

She turned and went on down.

Some time later there was more light than the lamp's.

Nereth had stopped, and was looking down into her own right hand. She had opened the fingers at last, and in her palm there was a flame the size of an egg, steady and yellow, and she was watching it with her head a little on one side and the corner of her mouth turned up, the way it had turned up over the sea.

"Go on," she whispered to it. "Bigger."

It grew while Seralune watched. It crept over the edge of her palm and up her wrist, and the cuff of her sleeve caught.

"Nereth!"

Nereth went on watching it.

Seralune set the lamp down, dragged the shawl off her shoulders and threw it over Nereth's arm, and beat at it with her good hand until the cloth stopped smoking. When she took the shawl away, the flame in Nereth's palm was gone, and the skin of her wrist was red and shining, with the burnt cuff stuck to it.

Nereth hadn't made a sound.

She lifted her head, and for the first time since the steps, she looked straight at Seralune. There was no anger in her face, and no pain, and Seralune couldn't find anything in it that knew her.

Seralune's mana came up so hard that she felt it behind her eyes.

Nereth flinched. Her burnt hand went to her ear and pressed there, hard, and she looked past Seralune down the passage and started walking again, with her hand still over her ear.

## 9. The roots, and the dead

*The place shows its rules: the flowers turn to Seralune, and the dead are servants, in her grey. Nereth tucks in a dead woman's collar exactly as she tucked in Seralune's shawl in Chapter 7.*

The roots had been coming for a while before Seralune paid them any mind: a few pale threads in the joints of the wall, then cords, then whole mats of them hanging from the roof, so low that she had to hold them aside with the lamp. They were cold, and softer than roots should be, and when she let them fall back they went on swaying after they ought to have stopped.

Then there was a light that wasn't the lamp's.

She only saw it when she held the lamp behind her: a faint, cold glow, the colour of milk, coming from the roots themselves, and from small white flowers growing all among them, hundreds of them, closed up tight.

When the lamp came near, they opened.

They turned on their stems to the light as she passed, the way any flower turns to the sun. Then the lamp went on past them, and they didn't turn back. They went on turning, slowly, every one of them, until they were facing her.

She stopped. She held the lamp out to her left, as far as her arm would reach.

The flowers didn't follow it.

*Not the lamp, then.*

She took a step, and all along the wall, slowly, they came round after her.

Behind her, Nereth's feet dragged and stopped, and dragged and stopped.

Things moved in the roots after that. She never saw one properly. There was a scuttle high up that stopped when she raised the lamp, and a tail drawn back into a gap in the stone, and once, at the very edge of the light, something small that sat up on the ledge and looked at her with two white flowers where its eyes should have been, and was gone before she'd finished seeing it.

Behind her, there was a small, wet crunch.

She turned. Nereth had stopped, and under her shoe something was moving, little legs working at the stone on either side of it. Nereth didn't look down. She lifted her foot and set it down further on, and came on, and the thing she had stood on went on moving where she'd left it.

The lamp found a shoe.

It lay on its side on the ledge, soft-soled, like the ones on Seralune's own feet, and she had stepped round it before she saw that there was a foot in it. Then the light went up a grey skirt, gone stiff and dark with damp, to where the roots had come out through the front of it, and up to a face.

It had been a woman. Her mouth was open, and the roots came out of it, and white flowers had opened along them, between her teeth.

Seralune looked down at her own skirt. It was the same grey.

*They sent servants down to clear the channels. Nereth told me so, at the bottom of the stair.*

She held the lamp further on. There was another of them, and another: sitting against the wall with their knees drawn up, or lying face down where the water had left them, and all of them in grey. Two sat side by side, one with its head on the other's shoulder.

*And some of them never went back up, and nobody came down for them.*

"Nereth." Her voice came out wrong, and she tried again. "Nereth, look at them."

Nereth looked. She looked at the woman by the shoe, and then she crouched beside her, slowly, with her burnt hand still pressed to her ear, and for a breath Seralune thought she had reached her at last.

Nereth reached out and straightened the dead woman's collar.

It had rucked up against the roots. She turned it down and smoothed it flat with her fingers. Then she sat back on her heels to look, and tucked it again.

Seralune had seen her do that once before, with a shawl, in a bedroom at the top of the palace.

Nereth got up, stepped over the dead woman's legs without looking down, and went on.

## 10. "Help me"

*The dead repeat what they've heard, and they repeat Seralune. Nereth's lips move with them. Seralune reasons her way to someone alive, and goes after it.*

They had passed a great many of the dead when one of them spoke.

"Help me."

Seralune stopped so hard that the lamp swung, and the light swung with it, across the wall and across a man sitting there in grey with his head tipped back against the stone and his jaw hanging open. The roots in his mouth moved.

"Help me."

It was a girl's voice, hoarse and worn thin, the way Seralune's own had been by the end of that day in the room with the crystal. It hadn't come from his chest. His chest had a root through it. It had come out of his mouth.

"Who's there?"

"Help me." Further along, another jaw had moved. "Help me."

"Where are you? I can hear you!" Seralune held the lamp as high as it would go. "Keep talking, I'm coming!"

For a while there was only the water.

Then, from a mouth somewhere ahead, beyond the edge of the light, came her own voice.

"*Where are you? I can hear you! Keep talking, I'm coming!*"

It was hers exactly: the same words, the same crack on *coming*, sharp in the same places.

Seralune's hand went to her mouth.

*"Poor Nereth." She heard me say it at the pillar, and I never said a word.*

She turned round.

Nereth was a pace behind her, looking past her down the passage, and her lips were moving. No sound came out of them. Seralune didn't need one. She watched the shape of the words go round and round, in time with the voice from the dead.

*Help me. Help me.*

"Help me," said the girl's voice, from further down.

Seralune made herself think.

*The dead can't call out. They only say what they've heard. Then something said it first, and it's still saying it, down there, and every mouth between here and there is only passing it on.*

*Someone down there is alive.*

She knew what Nereth would have said, the Nereth at the arch. She would have kept their hopes modest. Modest at best.

Seralune lifted the lamp and went towards the voice. After three steps she looked back. Nereth was coming.

## 11. The creature; "Leave it"; the bite

*The long silence breaks. Nereth's paranoia is half right, and that's what makes it frightening: every time Seralune's mana surges, the voices get louder and Nereth gets worse. One strangling. The creature bites.*

The voices led her to an old arch over the channel, where the ledge widened into a floor of bare stone, and there they stopped.

Under the arch, in a hollow at the foot of the wall, something was caught.

It was a lizard, no longer than her hand. Roots had grown through it, in at its sides and out along its back, and white flowers had opened all down its spine. One hind leg was pinned flat under a root as thick as Seralune's wrist. It was pulling against it, again and again, and every time its sides heaved, the voice came out of the dead behind her.

"Help me."

Its mouth opened with the words. No sound came out of it. The sound came from behind her, from all of them.

Seralune set the lamp down on the stone. Behind her, Nereth sat down against the wall all at once, with her legs out in front of her.

Seralune knelt by the hollow. She found a loose stone, a flat one, and began working its edge under the root with her good hand.

"Leave it."

Her heart jumped so hard that it hurt.

*She spoke. She spoke to me.*

She looked round. Nereth was sitting against the wall with her eyes on the lizard.

"Nereth—"

"Leave it there."

"It's only caught. Give me a moment, and I'll—"

"I said *leave it*."

The root shifted. Seralune leaned on the stone.

"Get up." Nereth's voice was climbing. "Get away from it. Get *up*."

"I've nearly done it!"

The root lifted. The leg came free all at once, and the lizard was out of the hollow and up onto her sleeve before she could draw her arm back, its claws pricking through the cloth. She held her arm still. It was lighter than she'd thought. It climbed past her elbow to her shoulder and stopped there, and turned its head, and looked at Nereth.

Nereth stood up.

"Look at me when I'm speaking to you."

It wasn't loud. It was the even voice she had used on the guards with their swords out. Seralune turned to her slowly, with her arm held still and the small weight on her shoulder.

"Nereth, it's all right. It isn't going to—"

"You can't leave anything alone, can you?" Nereth came a step closer. Her burnt hand hung at her side, and its fingers kept opening and closing. "You have to go to it. You have to *touch* it. You went back for me in the wash passage, and he stayed behind that door because of you—"

"I know he did! I know—"

"—and every time you go near anything, they get louder." Nereth's voice cracked straight down the middle, and what came out of the crack wasn't even any more. "Every time. Ever since the pillar. You're doing it. You're *doing* it, and you stand there with that face, and you won't *stop*—"

"Doing what? Nereth, I'm not doing anything!"

Seralune's mana rose under her ribs so fast that she nearly doubled over, and all down the channel, out of every mouth at once, the dead screamed it.

"*HELP ME.*"

Nereth came at her.

She came with her whole body, shoulder first, and drove Seralune sideways off her knees onto the stone. The lamp went over. Seralune heard the glass break, and the flame went out, and the only light left was the cold white glow of the flowers along the wall, every one of them turned towards her.

Before she could get an elbow under her, Nereth was on top of her, and Nereth's hands were round her throat.

Seralune got both hands on Nereth's wrists and pulled. They didn't move. She couldn't get her fingers under them. Nereth leaned down over her with all her weight on her hands, and the breath Seralune had started to take stopped halfway, in her throat, and wouldn't go either way.

"Make them stop." Nereth's face was a hand's width from hers, lit from below by the flowers, white and wet. "Make them stop. Make them *stop*—"

Seralune clawed at her arms. Her nails caught skin and dragged. The cut in her palm split under its binding, and the pain went up her arm so fiercely that she let go with that hand and hit at Nereth's face instead. It glanced off a cheek and fell back onto the stone.

She kicked. Her heels skidded. She got one foot flat and heaved, and the borrowed dress twisted round her legs and held her, and Nereth rocked and leaned in harder.

Her mana was everywhere: in her chest, behind her eyes, in her throat under Nereth's thumbs, crowding up and up with nowhere to go. With every heave of it the voices came louder, out of every mouth in every wall, *help me, help me, HELP ME*, until she couldn't hear the water any more.

"*Stop it!*" Nereth was screaming into her face. "*Stop doing it!*"

Her sight was going, in from the edges.

*No.* It came from somewhere a long way down. *I did not get out of that room to die on another floor.*

She got her hand up again, onto Nereth's face, and pushed, and it wasn't enough. Her sight had closed to a small grey place with Nereth's mouth in the middle of it, moving, and she couldn't hear what it said. Her legs were still kicking. She could hear her shoes on the stone, and she couldn't feel them.

Something cold moved across her neck, between Nereth's fingers.

Small claws went over her collarbone, quick and light, and on up Nereth's wrist and Nereth's arm, and white flowers brushed past Seralune's cheek.

Nereth screamed.

One hand left her throat. Seralune caught the other in both of hers and wrenched, and twisted out from under it, and rolled.

The first breath tore its way in. She choked on it and coughed, and couldn't stop coughing, curled on her side on the stone with her throat on fire, dragging in breath after breath, each one hurting worse than the last.

Nereth was still screaming.

When Seralune could lift her head, Nereth was on her knees by the channel with the lizard hanging from her face, its teeth in her below the left eye. Nereth tore it off and flung it. It struck the water and went under, and Nereth lurched after it, over the edge—

Seralune didn't decide to move. She was up against the wall, and then she had Nereth round the waist with her good arm and was hauling her back from the edge, and they both went down on the stone together.

"Get it out." Nereth had both hands over her face, and blood was coming between her fingers. "Get it out of me. Get it *out*—"

"It's gone. Nereth, it's gone, it's in the water—"

"*It's in me!*"

She took her hands away.

The bite was a small thing, a ragged crescent below her eye. But lines were spreading out from it under the skin, dark and branching, the way the roots had branched over the stones, up into the corner of her eye and down her cheek towards her mouth, and Seralune could see them grow.

She put her hand to her own throat. She couldn't help it.

Nereth's mouth opened.

"Help me."

It wasn't her voice. It was the girl's, hoarse and thin, the voice from the dead, and it had come out of Nereth's mouth.

Nereth heard it.

Seralune watched her hear it. Her eyes went wide, and her hand flew up and clamped over her own mouth, and above it her eyes came to Seralune's, and for one breath they were Nereth's eyes, and they were terrified.

Then all down the channel, from every one of the dead, the voice answered her.

"*Help me.*"

Nereth took her hand away from her mouth and turned her face towards them.

## 12. The fire

*She fires it to silence the voices. The blast comes in the order Seralune would feel it: the air pulled in, the light, a silence, then the weight of the sound, the mountain opening, the fire coming back. When it's over, the voices have stopped.*

"Shut up," Nereth said, in her own voice.

Her right hand came up off the stone towards the channel, towards all of them.

"Shut up. Shut up, shut up, *shut up*—"

There was a flame in her palm, and it wasn't small.

"Nereth, no—" Seralune got her arm round her from behind and pulled, and they went back together against the thick foot of the arch. "Put it down. Put your hand *down*—"

The air went.

It went past Seralune's face all at once, towards Nereth's hand, and dragged her hair forward over her eyes and the breath out of her chest. The water in the channel humped up and ran towards them. Every white flower on every wall bent on its stem towards that hand.

Then the light in the roots went out, all of it, from one end of the channel to the other, at once, and the only light left was in Nereth's palm, and it was white.

Seralune never saw it leave her hand.

There was white everywhere, and in it she could see everything at once: every root and every stone, every one of the dead along the walls with their faces and their open mouths, down the whole length of the channel and further, much further than she had known there was any channel to go, as clear as noon.

Then she couldn't see anything.

There was no sound at all. There was no water, and no breathing, hers or Nereth's. She couldn't hear the falls, and she couldn't hear anything in the place where the falls should have been, and she thought the white had taken every sound there was.

Then it came.

It didn't come as a sound. It came as a weight, all over her at once, through her teeth and into her bones, the way the cracking had in the moment before she fell onto the stone in the room with the crystal, and it didn't stop. The arch shook at her back. The floor dropped out from under her and came back up and struck her. Somewhere behind them the roof was coming down, and kept coming down, and she felt every piece of it land.

She held on to Nereth.

When she could see again, the fire was going away from them.

It filled the channel from wall to wall and went down it faster than she had ever seen anything move, and everywhere it went, the walls lit from inside. The roots went up white. The dead went up. The water stood up out of its channel and went past her as steam, screaming. Chamber after chamber lit and went black behind it, further and further into the mountain, until it was a single point of light so far off that she couldn't believe it was the same fire, and then it struck something.

The mountain answered. She felt it before she understood it: a long, deep shudder that began far off and came back up through the stone towards her. At the very end of the channel, where the point of light had been, there was a different light now. It was grey and very faint, and there were stars in it.

The wind came in.

It came up the channel from that far grey place, cold, and tasting of something she didn't know, and the fire came back with it.

It rolled up the channel towards them, filling it from side to side, and there was nowhere to go. There was the arch at her back and the wall beside her and Nereth in her arms, and that was all there was.

Seralune pulled Nereth's head in against her chest, put her own face down into Nereth's hair and shut her eyes.

*Don't let go.*

It reached them, and went round them.

She felt it go. It went down her left side and down her right, roaring, so close that it dragged at her skirt, and it poured over the top of the arch and down in front of her face, and divided, and went on. Her hair didn't catch. Her skin didn't burn. There was no heat in it at all. She opened her eyes on fire a finger's width away, going past and past and past, and none of it touched them.

A piece of the arch came down and struck her shoulder so hard that her whole arm went numb. She cried out. She didn't let go.

Then the fire had gone back down the channel the way it came, and it was quiet.

It was a quiet she hadn't heard since the arch. The voices had stopped. All of them.

Stone was still falling somewhere, in long rattling runs, then in single pieces, further and further apart. Water dripped onto hot stone and hissed. The wind went on coming up the channel, cold, into her face.

Nereth's arm came down across Seralune's knees, and lay there.

There was no flame in her hand any more. The fingers were open, and they didn't close.

Seralune bent her face to Nereth's mouth. For a long time there was no breath at all. Then there was one, very small, against her cheek.

"Nereth?"

The breath came again. That was all.

"Nereth. It's over. You can open your eyes."

## 13. Out

*The dragging, the breach, your Thaer line word for word (round 3, 1A), and the port. Nereth comes back in the fresh air with a line about the shawl. Then she sees Seralune's throat, and she sees the sea.*

She couldn't carry her.

She tried. She got Nereth's arm over her shoulders and her own good arm round Nereth's back and pushed up with her legs, and got halfway, and then her right hand gave under the weight and her throat closed on the breath she needed, and they both came down again. She lowered Nereth as carefully as she could and sat beside her until the spots had gone out of her eyes.

*Not like that, then.*

She worked the shawl in under Nereth's shoulders, gathered both ends in her good hand, and pulled.

Nereth moved about the length of an arm. Seralune stopped, and breathed, and moved her feet, and pulled again.

That was how they went: a pull and a stop, and a pull and a stop. Whenever the shawl caught on broken stone, she went back to lift Nereth's shoulders clear and work it loose, with her knee under Nereth's head to keep it off the rock, and when the spots came back, she rested. The channel was bare now, black, and the stone ticked as it cooled. Where the dead had been, there were only shapes on the walls, paler than the stone round them. She didn't look at them for long.

The wind was in her face the whole way.

She didn't know how long it took. It was long enough for the grey at the end of the channel to grow until it stopped being a far-off light and became a ragged hole she could see the sides of. Then there were no sides, and she was dragging Nereth out over broken stone into the open air.

She laid her against a rock, straightened up and looked.

The mountain had been opened. The hole they had come out of was taller than the palace gates, and its edges had run like candle wax and set again. Below it, where the side of the mountain should have been, a long black furrow ran away down the slope, wider than any road she had ever seen: broken rock and burnt earth and the stumps of trees, with fires still burning along both its edges and smoke going up from them, all the way down until she lost it in the grey.

All her life, people had said there was nobody in Natharul like Thaer. She had never seen him do anything like this.

She looked down at Nereth's hand, lying open on the stone, and couldn't make the two things fit.

Off to one side, well away from the furrow, there were lights.

They lay along the edge of the water at the bottom of the mountain, small and yellow, a great many of them. As the sky greyed, she began to make out roofs behind them, and a wall running out into the water, and dark shapes along it that she knew after a while for ships. Beyond them, the water went on, flat and grey, until she couldn't tell where it stopped and the sky began.

*A town. A town has doors, and water, and someone in it who'll know what to do for her face.*

*It's a long way down.*

Behind her, Nereth drew a breath, a deep one that went all the way down, as she hadn't since the steps.

Seralune knelt. Nereth's eyes were open. They were on the sky, where the last stars were going out, and then they moved, slowly, and found Seralune's face, and stayed there.

"Your Highness."

It was hoarse and very weak, and it was hers.

Seralune couldn't answer.

Nereth's eyes went down to the shawl bunched under her shoulders, scorched and torn and black with ash.

"Your Highness has ruined the shawl."

Seralune laughed. It hurt her throat so much that it came out as a sob, and then it was one, and she put her good hand over her face and couldn't stop, and was furious with herself for it, and still couldn't stop.

When she took her hand away, Nereth was looking at her throat.

Her face changed. Seralune watched her see the marks, and know them, and watched it go all the way through her.

"Did I—"

"Not now."

Nereth shut her mouth.

Seralune wiped her face with the back of her wrist. "There's a town at the bottom. I'm going to get you there, so don't try to walk."

Nereth didn't try. She turned her head against the rock, slowly, until she was looking out past the furrow, past the roofs and the ships, to the grey water going on to the edge of the sky. The wind came up off it into their faces.

"Salt," Nereth said, so quietly that Seralune almost didn't hear it.

She didn't look away from it again. Seralune got the shawl back under her shoulders and found the first place below them where the ground was flat enough to stop. It wasn't far. The next one would be further.

The lights were still a long way below them when they started down.
