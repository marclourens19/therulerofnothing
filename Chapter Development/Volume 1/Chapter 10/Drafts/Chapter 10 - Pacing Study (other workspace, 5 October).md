# Chapter 10 – Beneath Natharul

Pacing Study and Compression Proposals

5 October 2026 · Based on the Five Pass Revision · Proposals only; the chapter has not been changed

# 1. My honest conclusion

This chapter earns its slow build. I would keep its structure and all its major events. It begins with two people trying to preserve an ordinary relationship in an unfamiliar place, gradually removes their ability to rely on one another, breaks that relationship through violence, then makes Seralune carry the person who nearly killed her. The physical journey and the relationship deteriorate together. That is the chapter's strongest pacing decision.

There are local passages I would tighten. They are mostly sentences that announce an observation before showing it, explain an image after its meaning is already clear, or repeat a position the reader has just been given. The best compression would shorten the distance between an observation and its consequence. It would preserve the time spent fearing, resisting, recovering and choosing.

My earlier criticism of the carrying section was too broad. Measured in the full chapter, the carry towards the opening takes 815 words, about 9% of the text. Its failures are different: contact triggers the memory of strangling; a corrected hold hurts; she chooses a rest; she falls despite trying; getting up produces helpless anger. Those are developments. They should not be removed simply because they all happen while she is carrying Nereth.

I recommend four small changes, shown below, totalling 78 words. Four other alternatives demonstrate a more noticeable tightening, but each has a cost. Applying all eight would remove 235 words, about 2.6% of the chapter. I would not cut towards an arbitrary length or pretend these changes would transform the chapter. The useful finding is that most of its length already serves the experience you want.

A reader can understand an action and still need time to feel it. Conversely, a clear sentence can delay a scene by repeating work another sentence has already done. The proposals distinguish those cases.

# 3. Where the pacing works, and where it risks holding too long

## The opening: establish what deterioration will cost

The opening's 1,212 words occupy 13.5% of the chapter. That is a substantial allowance, but it establishes more than the tunnel. Nereth knows how to get Seralune across a damaged ledge, watches her injured hand, corrects her and can still make her answer back. Cyrandor remains a subject Nereth cannot comfortably discuss. The sea gives Nereth a private desire beyond her duties.

The lamp-lighting also introduces a warning before the loops: Nereth’s small flame jumps higher and whiter than she intends. She can still speak evenly and keep Seralune safe, but her control has already changed. This lets the opening carry both ordinary companionship and the beginning of the threat. Removing that overshoot would make the later fire feel less prepared.

Those details make later events hurt. Her practical care returns grotesquely when she adjusts a dead woman's collar. Her hand, once a source of safety, becomes a threat at Seralune's throat. The sea eventually appears when Nereth cannot share it. Cutting the ordinary interaction to reach the frightening material faster would remove the standard against which her deterioration is measured.

I would keep the opening conversation, the ledge assistance and the sea exchange. I would also keep the absence of the falls. It gives Seralune a familiar sound to seek before she knows what to fear. The missing sound matters again during the blast and changes meaning there. It is not merely a repeated description of quiet.

The opening could become too long if expanded with another unrelated conversation, another crossing with the same outcome, or a general explanation of the aqueducts. The present version does not need those additions. Its existing length is defensible.

## The loops: preserve three different failures

The first return exposes a navigation error. Seralune makes a mark; Nereth can still joke about it. The second return exposes a more dangerous failure: she rejects the mark she watched Seralune make. The third return ends the arrangement in which Seralune follows her.

Removing a return would change the progression. One mistake followed by immediate takeover would make Seralune seem briskly competent and Nereth abruptly broken. Three returns allow hope, doubt and finally a decision. The changing responses matter more than the repeated location.

The compressible part is the approach to the second recognition. The original recognizes the pillar, lists its identifying features, brings up the light, identifies the mark and touches it. A shorter version can bring the light onto the evidence, then reach Nereth's denial. Proposal 1 does this without removing a return or summarizing the argument.

## Between the pillar and the dead: the most exposed stretch

The argument, failed rest, third return and right passage together take 2,019 words. This is the stretch most likely to feel slow to a reader who wants the horror to become physical sooner. Its task is to sustain interest while the visible problem remains largely the same: Nereth is becoming less reliable.

It succeeds when each symptom changes their relationship. A shaking lamp matters because Nereth's hands did not shake in front of armed men. Misheard speech matters because silence no longer protects Seralune from accusation. Nereth walking beyond the light makes Seralune physically responsible for her safety. The phantom hand makes care impossible: Seralune offers her real hand, and Nereth prefers an absent one. The self-burning moves the danger into Nereth's own body.

These stages should remain. The risk comes in the sentences connecting them: watched, looked, checked, waited, received no answer, went on. When two adjacent sentences perform the same monitoring, the reader spends time without learning anything else about either woman. Proposals 2–5 address this stretch at different levels of intervention.

The attempted rest should stay. It gives exhaustion somewhere to settle and then denies the expected relief. Alongside the oil, repeated long intervals and eventual dawn, it also supports the duration of the journey. Cutting these intervals because they lack action would make the night pass implausibly quickly on the page.

Its decisive moment is Nereth watching to see whether Seralune will start breathing again. Seralune asks what she would have done if she had not; Nereth leaves the question unanswered. The woman who pulled her across the ledge is now close enough to help and may not do so. That is a changed danger, and it earns the pause.

## The fauna and the dead: discovery is action here

Seralune tests whether the flowers follow the lamp or her. That test should stay in full. It makes her participate in interpreting the place rather than receive a succession of frightening descriptions. The moving creatures, crushed creature and servants in familiar clothing then change scale and recognition.

Nereth straightening the dead woman's collar is especially important. It interrupts conventional exploration with an action that still belongs to her character. The repeated adjustment is compulsive, so its repetition conveys deterioration. Compressing it into “Nereth tended the corpse” would remove both the horror and the remnant of care.

The copied pleas also develop. A dead mouth seems to speak; a voice copies Seralune; a trapped creature's mouth moves while the dead produce its words; then the voice emerges from Nereth. Each change makes the same plea less reliable. I would preserve that sequence. Proposals 6 and 7 only test whether the explanation of a source can be stated once rather than twice.

## The creature and the strangling: do not hurry the choice into an attack

The creature sequence takes 768 words before the 878-word assault-and-bite movement. The time spent examining and trying to release it gives Seralune a reason to remain in danger. Nereth's warning grows from speech into desperation. This keeps her recognizable as a frightened person before she becomes violent.

A summary such as “She freed it despite Nereth's warning” would technically preserve the decision but erase its cost as it develops. Seralune needs time to believe she can help; Nereth needs time to stop accepting that belief. The fight then breaks an interaction the reader has been inside.

The strangling should remain sustained. Pulling at wrists, trying to kick, failing to get air, losing sight and registering claws by touch are different attempts and losses. The five-pass version also respects her narrowed perception: she does not suddenly see actions she could not witness near blackout. Compression must not restore an explanatory view from outside her body.

The aim is not to make every sentence brief. Longer connected sentences can hold the reader inside a failing effort. The isolated beats should be saved for changes such as a lost breath, a released grip or the wrong voice coming out of Nereth.

## The blast: the direction and scale need room

The casting, blast and return take 890 words. That is not excessive for the chapter's largest change in scale. The sequence has distinct stages: gathering, white light, missing sound, bodily impact, fire travelling away, distant impacts, a mountain-wide response, incoming wind, returning fire and a protected space that still admits physical injury.

The returning fire is essential. Without it, Seralune's contact with Nereth would not have the same immediate significance. The stone striking her shin is essential too: it keeps the protection from reading as freedom from every consequence. I would retain the apparent absence of heat and the fire passing on both sides. Those observations let the reader experience the boundary without receiving a magic-system explanation in the middle of the blast.

This section would drag if it added more adjectives of magnitude or an objective power ranking. It currently shows changing effects instead. The later comparison with Thaer remains Seralune's judgment, based on what she has heard and imagined. It should not become a narrator's declaration that Nereth has surpassed him.

## The carry and the ending: the cost must outlast the spectacle

The failed waking, carry and exterior account for 1,473 words, about 16.4% of the chapter. The major threat ends before the physical work does. That is appropriate: an explosion should not immediately turn two injured people into capable escapees.

The first failed carry is psychological as well as practical. Nereth's arms lie across Seralune's throat, and her body reacts before she can reason herself out of it. The successful lift changes the hold. Later she chooses a rest before her legs fail; later still she falls anyway and protects Nereth's head. Her rage after that fall gives the exhaustion an emotional consequence. These are not three versions of “she was tired.”

Proposal 8 removes only a repeated description of the successful hold. I would keep the abandoned shawl, the breathing at her ear, the chosen rest, the fall, the rage and the distant opening becoming daylight. The long approach to the opening conveys duration and ends in the collapse of her legs. Breaking it into a string of short announcements would likely weaken that experience.

The exterior is 524 words. That space lets the reader move from bodily confinement to a view large enough to understand the damage. The port appears off to one side; its lights imply a possible destination, not certainty that every person is safe. Nereth's breathing improves without her suddenly waking restored. The sea is the chapter's emotional return, and the renewed carry is its practical ending. I would keep both.

# 4. Before and after: eight controlled proposals

Every Before below is an exact, contiguous excerpt from the Five Pass Revision. After passages are alternatives for comparison, not updates. Paragraph locations refer to the current manuscript body, numbered from its first paragraph. Dialogue and italics are retained unless the proposal explicitly identifies a cut.

## 1. Second return: reach the evidence sooner

**Recommended · 63 → 33 words · 30 removed · Paragraphs 78–79**

**What I notice:** The evidence takes two introductory sentences to arrive. The reader already knows the mark was made awkwardly and high on the pillar. Its height does not need to be established again before the denial.

**Before**

The second time, Seralune knew the pillar before Nereth lifted the lamp to it. She knew the crack that ran up one side of it and the chip out of its foot, and then the light came up, and there was her mark, pale and crooked, as high as she could reach.

She put her finger in it, where the chip had skidded.

**After**

The second time, the light came up on the pillar's crack and chipped foot, and there was her mark, pale and crooked. Seralune put her finger in it where the chip had skidded.

**How it compresses:** Bring the light, identifying damage and mark into one movement; keep the finger finding the imperfect groove. This also places the visual recognition explicitly in the available light.

**What it preserves, and what it costs:** The crooked mark, the physical verification and all the following disagreement remain. The original has a slower recognition; the alternative reaches the contradiction sooner. I prefer the alternative here.

## 2. The shaking lamp: combine announcement and observation

**Recommended · 62 → 41 words · 21 removed · Paragraphs 98–99**

**What I notice:** “The lamp had begun to shake” announces what the next paragraph immediately shows. The flame trembling and the light trembling describe the same visible instability in succession.

**Before**

The lamp had begun to shake.

Seralune watched it from behind for a long time before she said anything: the flame trembling in its glass, and the light trembling on the wall with it, so that the stones seemed to shift whenever she looked at them. Last night, with two swords a step away from her, Nereth's hands hadn't shaken at all.

**After**

For a long time Seralune watched Nereth's lamp shake, its light making the stones on the wall seem to shift whenever she looked at them. Last night, with two swords a step away from her, Nereth's hands hadn't shaken at all.

**How it compresses:** Combine the announcement with Seralune watching. Retain the wavering wall and the comparison with Nereth standing before swords.

**What it preserves, and what it costs:** “For a long time” preserves the delay before Seralune speaks. The alternative loses the separate view of the flame inside its glass, but keeps the consequence Seralune experiences. This is a small, useful compression.

## 3. The silence: separate suppression from elapsed time

**Optional · 53 → 40 words · 13 removed · Paragraphs 110**

**What I notice:** The sentence covers not offering, not speaking, following, suppressing thoughts, elapsed time and Nereth stopping. Its repeated negatives are emotionally plausible, but the transition carries several jobs.

**Before**

She didn't offer again, or say anything else, because it seemed safest not to. She walked behind the light and didn't let herself think about the water or the pillar or how far down they might have come, and she had been quiet for a long time when Nereth stopped ahead of her.

**After**

She followed the light without another word, because it seemed safest not to, and didn't let herself think about the water, the pillar, how far down they might have come. A long time passed before Nereth stopped ahead of her.

**How it compresses:** Consolidate the refusal to speak, retain the forbidden thoughts, and give elapsed time its own sentence. This is rhythm editing as much as word removal.

**What it preserves, and what it costs:** Her silence still makes the next false accusation disturbing. The original keeps her suppression in one long breath; this version releases that breath earlier. I would compare them aloud before choosing. It is not a necessary fix.

## 4. The right passage: compress the supervision

**Optional · 280 → 163 words · 117 removed · Paragraphs 166–176**

**What I notice:** This is the largest local option. The walking is heard, then heard again as Nereth passes; the positions of the lamp, wall, feet and faces are tracked in detail; the final paragraph recaps supervision.

**Before**

Seralune went first. With the lamp in her good hand, there was no hand left to hold, so she kept her cut one against her chest and listened behind her for Nereth's feet.

They came slower than before, and heavier, every step landing flat, and between them one shoe scraped along the stone before the other came down. Nereth was breathing through her mouth.

"Shall we stop for a while?"

Seralune stopped, so that Nereth would too. The footsteps didn't. They came on, flat and heavy, up level with her and past her, and went on down the slope out of the light. Seralune caught her sleeve with her injured hand. The pull against the binding made her gasp. Nereth stopped with one foot at a broken section of ledge.

Seralune set the lamp down so she could take her arm properly, eased her backwards and picked it up again.

Nereth stood with one hand flat on the wall, looking down the slope to where the light didn't reach. Seralune lowered the lamp to see her face.

"Nereth?"

Her eyes went to the light, the way anyone's would. They didn't come to Seralune, and in a moment they had gone back down the slope.

"Stay behind the light," Seralune said. "Do you hear me? Behind it."

After that she walked slower and looked back over her shoulder every few steps, and every time, Nereth was there, a few paces behind the light, with her head down, coming on.

**After**

The steps gave onto a long slope, the channel running beside it with its water moving slowly downhill.

Seralune led with the lamp in her good hand and her cut one against her chest. Behind her, Nereth's steps landed heavily, one shoe scraping between them. Nereth was breathing through her mouth.

"Shall we stop for a while?"

Seralune stopped. Nereth passed her and went on out of the light. Seralune caught her sleeve with her injured hand and gasped as the binding pulled. Nereth stopped at a broken section of ledge.

Seralune set the lamp down, took Nereth's arm with her good hand and eased her back. Then she picked it up and held it towards Nereth's face.

Her eyes went to the lamp, then back down the slope. They never met Seralune's.

**How it compresses:** Keep the slope, changed water, heavy gait, mouth breathing, ignored stop, dangerous ledge, injured-hand catch, lamp placement and failure to meet Seralune’s eyes. Condense the movement between them.

**What it preserves, and what it costs:** The alternative preserves the practical sequence: Seralune sets the lamp down before using her good hand on Nereth, then retrieves it. However, it removes the slow approach of the footsteps and Nereth resting a hand on the wall. It makes this episode arrive and pass much faster. I would use it only if this stretch feels stalled when read continuously; I would keep the original for maximum oppressive duration.

# 5. What I would deliberately leave alone

## The voices should recur; their meaning should change

The recurring “Help me” is not a phrase to remove simply because it appears repeatedly. Its source becomes less trustworthy each time. What begins as apparent testimony from a person becomes a mechanism of attraction and finally an invasion of Nereth’s speech. That progression supplies its own pace.

I would not combine the pleas into “They heard voices calling for help” or add a paragraph explaining what the voices are doing. Both would make the reader understand faster while feeling less uncertainty. The small optional cuts above concern the narration identifying a source, not the sequence of sources itself.

## Nereth’s remaining personality is part of the suspense

Her dry correction at the ledge, reluctance to discuss Cyrandor, professional phrasing, phantom duty and care for the dead collar are what make the breakdown personal. Cutting most dialogue to hurry her corruption would turn her into a set of symptoms. Adding several new arguments would risk the opposite: repeating her aggravation without changing what Seralune can do.

I would preserve the current dialogue length in this pass. The largest proposed cut leaves its dialogue untouched. Silence is useful once Seralune can no longer obtain an answer, but it should follow the recognizable woman who spoke earlier.

## Seralune’s compassion needs both a choice and a consequence

Helping the creature, holding Nereth through the returning fire and carrying her afterwards are linked decisions. The chapter should not rush from compassion to punishment and then imply that Seralune has learned simply not to help. Her thought about being told to leave the creature remains guilt, not an authoritative verdict about what caused everything.

The aftermath must also retain fear and anger. Carrying Nereth is not proof that the assault no longer matters. The throat-triggered failed mount is the passage that stops compassion from becoming painless forgiveness. Removing it would damage more than pacing.

## The view should widen before the chapter closes

The mountain scar gives scale to something previously experienced from inside. The distant lights turn scale into a next task. The sea turns the escape into an interrupted shared hope. These are three different uses of the exterior, and all are needed. I would not end on the breach as a spectacle or cut straight from fire to the port.

# 6. Sentence rhythm: what compression must not do

Your preference for fuller, natural sentences matters here. Tightening should not mean turning every clause into an isolated line. The chapter already has moments where a short paragraph is earned by a new danger or a lost ability. If more of the walking becomes clipped, the assault and blast will have less contrast.

The repeated “and” structures have different functions. In the struggle and the long approach to daylight, they can keep actions joined because Seralune cannot step outside the experience to organize it neatly. During routine observation, they can make several small checks take equal weight. Proposal 4 changes some of those routine chains; I would not impose its rhythm on the assault or the exterior approach.

I would also retain a distinction between a physical stop and a narrative pause. Resting on the stone is a physical stop that changes whether Seralune can continue. The lamp shaking is a pause that changes her judgment of Nereth. A second summary of the already-corrected carrying hold changes neither. That is why the last is a cut and the first two are largely protected.

# 8. How I would judge the revised pace

After any chosen cuts, I would check the chapter continuously rather than grade its isolated paragraphs. These are the practical tests:

1. **Before the first return, do I know the Nereth I am about to lose?** Her competence, guarded grief and wish to see the sea should be present without feeling like a checklist.

2. **Does each return make a different demand on Seralune?** Error, rejected evidence and taking the lead should remain distinct.

3. **Does the middle change the relationship before the attack changes their bodies?** Watching Nereth must lead to new responsibility or a failed attempt at connection, not merely another observation of unease.

4. **At the creature, do I understand both why Seralune stays and why Nereth desperately wants her to stop?** Neither should feel like a person placed there to activate the next event.

5. **During the blast, can I follow the changing direction without feeling instructed?** Outbound fire, incoming wind, returning fire and physical debris must remain legible through Seralune’s perceptions.

6. **After the blast, am I still afraid of Nereth while wanting her to survive?** That tension is more valuable than finishing the escape quickly.
