# Chapter 10 – Beneath Natharul

The arch was behind them before Seralune stopped looking back at it. After that there was the ledge, and the black water lying beside it, and Nereth's lamp making a small room of light that moved with them and never got any bigger.

She kept listening for the falls. She had listened for them all her life without knowing she did it, the way she breathed, and now she couldn't stop doing it on purpose. There was nothing: their feet on the stone, water dripping somewhere, Nereth's breathing. Under all of it, where the falls should have been, there was nothing at all.

*There's nothing down here to fear, so stop this at once. You're no child of six. You can bear this. Walk.*

Nereth hadn't said a word since the arch. She walked half a step ahead with the lamp held out and her other hand in Seralune's, and every so often she looked back over her shoulder, the way she had at every turning since the iron door. There was never anyone there.

"Nereth. About Cyrandor—"

"Not now, Your Highness." Nereth didn't turn her head. "We may speak of Cyrandor once we are out of these tunnels."

Seralune wanted to tell her that Thaer had sent someone to find him. She wanted to say her brother wouldn't let Leorin hurt him, but she had already told herself a great many things about Thaer, and some had lasted less than a morning.

*Later, then.*

The channel split round a pillar, with one passage on either side and the way back rising behind them. Nereth lifted the lamp towards the left, then the right. On the right, shallow steps went down beyond its reach.

"This is as far as I have ever come," she said. "Beyond it, I would keep our hopes modest, Your Highness. Modest at best. Stay close to me, and we shall find our way out together."

"Which way, then?"

"When last they sent us down, the water ran from the left." Nereth held the lamp towards it. "What would Your Highness have us do?"

*And if it isn't the left, we can always turn back and try the right.*

"The left, then," Seralune said. "Lead on, Nereth."

The left-hand way was narrower. The ledge ran along under the wall with the water a step below it, and every so often the edge had broken away, so that Nereth had to stop and hold the lamp low and find where it began again.

Seralune put her right hand out to the wall.

"Your hand, Your Highness."

She took it back and held it against her chest. "It's only a wall."

"It is a wet wall, and a filthy one, and that cut keeps opening. If it festers down here, I shall have to explain to Cyrandor how I let—" Nereth stopped. She lifted the lamp again. "Keep it close, please."

Seralune had heard Cyrandor at the bedroom door this morning. *Mind her hand.* She kept it close, and didn't ask Nereth to finish.

Further on, a whole length of the ledge had gone. Nereth crossed first on what was left of it, with her shoulder to the wall, then set the lamp down on the far side and turned and held out both hands.

"It's slippery. Step where I stepped, Your Highness. There, and then there. Not near the edge."

Seralune stepped where Nereth had stepped. On the second stone her borrowed shoe slid on something soft, and before she'd even felt herself start to go, Nereth had her by the forearm, well above the binding, and pulled her across.

"There."

"You might have told me it was slippery."

"I did tell Your Highness. Your Highness was looking at the water."

"I was looking where I would fall."

"Then look where you mean to stand. It generally works better."

Seralune wanted to object, but Nereth was still holding her up. She got both feet beneath her and waited until her heart slowed before letting go.

The water lay beside the ledge, black and so still that the lamp sat in it without a ripple. Once, a long way out, something touched the surface from underneath. The rings went out slowly until they lapped against the ledge, and nothing came up.

*Whatever that is, it's down there and you're up here. Walk.*

They had gone a long way past it when the lamp went out.

Nereth had raised it to look for a dry place beneath a steady drip. Water splashed through the open top onto the wick. The flame hissed and shrank to a blue bead, and then there was no light at all, and Seralune couldn't have said which side of her the water was on.

Her mana crowded up under her ribs, the way it always did when she was frightened, so full she could hardly breathe round it.

"Hold still, Your Highness." Nereth's voice was perfectly level. "Do not move your feet."

There was a small sound, like a breath let out, and a flame stood up on the end of Nereth's right thumb.

It lit her face from below. She touched it to the wick, and the flame jumped, up past the top of the glass, so high and white that Seralune had to shut her eyes. When Seralune looked again, Nereth was shaking her hand out sharply. There was only the lamp, burning small and yellow as before.

Nereth was looking at her fingers.

"That was larger than I meant," she said.

"You have Fire."

"Yes, Your Highness."

"You might have mentioned it before we came down here with one lamp between us."

"Your Highness did not ask." Nereth lifted the lamp. "And I can make a flame, but I cannot hold one for hours. The oil will last longer than I would."

Before she started walking, she rubbed the tips of her fingers together, as though they were still warm.

They walked for a long time.

Mostly to hear something besides the water and their own feet, Seralune said, "What did you think was past the falls? When you were small."

Nereth didn't answer straight away, and Seralune thought she wasn't going to.

"The sea, Your Highness."

"The sea?"

"The laundry women used to say that if you went far enough past the falls, you came to water so big you couldn't see the other side of it, and it was salt all the way through, so you couldn't drink a drop." Her voice didn't change, but she walked a little slower. "I didn't believe a word of it. I thought they were making a fool of me, and I wanted to go and see it for myself, so I could come back and tell them so."

"And now?"

"Now I am in a drain, Your Highness, with a princess." Nereth lifted the lamp over a fallen stone. "They would never believe that either."

Seralune laughed before she knew it was coming. The sound went off along the water ahead of them, and a little after she had stopped, it came back to them from somewhere further on.

Nereth hadn't laughed. But when Seralune glanced at her, the corner of her mouth had moved, and it stayed that way for a few steps.

They came to the pillar again a long time later.

Seralune didn't know it at first. She only knew that she was tired and that her heels had rubbed raw in the borrowed shoes, and then Nereth stopped and lowered the lamp, and there in the silt at the foot of the pillar were their own prints: the soft soles of Seralune's shoes and Nereth's harder ones beside them, going away down the left-hand opening.

They had come through a smaller opening on the left of the pillar. The right-hand steps were still there, and beyond it the passage towards the arch rose into darkness.

"Ah," Nereth said.

She studied them.

"The branch after the broken ledge must bend round on itself. Whoever cut these channels had no sense of direction at all, Your Highness."

"Or neither of us was watching." Seralune felt about in the silt with her good hand until she found a chip of stone. "Hold the lamp up."

It was harder with her left hand than she'd thought. The chip skidded on the wet stone and wouldn't bite, and she had to go over the same place again and again before anything showed: a pale, crooked mark, as high as she could reach.

Nereth held the lamp up and watched.

"Is Your Highness decorating?"

"I'm marking it, so we'll know it if we see it again."

"We shall not see it again."

She said it so flatly that Seralune almost believed her, and was annoyed with herself for how much she wanted to.

They took the left again, and at the branch after the broken ledge, Nereth took the other way.

A little way past the branch, she stopped.

"Did Your Highness call me?"

"No."

Nereth stood with her head on one side. Somewhere ahead, water was dripping: one drop, then a long wait, then another.

"Someone did."

Seralune held her breath until her chest hurt. There was a drop, and a long wait, and another drop.

"It's the water, Nereth."

"The water does not know my name, Your Highness."

She went on, and she didn't say anything else until the pillar.

The second time, Seralune knew the pillar before Nereth lifted the lamp to it. She knew the crack that ran up one side of it and the chip out of its foot, and then the light came up, and there was her mark, pale and crooked, as high as she could reach.

She put her finger in it, where the chip had skidded.

"Nereth. We've come back."

Nereth didn't look at the pillar. She looked at Seralune.

"That is not yours."

"It's mine! You held the lamp up for me and asked if I was decorating!"

"Yours was neater."

"Mine was a mess. Look at it! And our prints are still here."

Nereth lowered the lamp and studied the silt. Then she lifted it towards the left-hand opening.

"This way."

It was the way they had gone both times before.

Seralune didn't move. Her mana had come up, cold and enormous, under her ribs, and Nereth, with her back to her, stopped as if she had walked into a wall.

"Whatever Your Highness is doing back there," Nereth said, without turning round, "stop it."

"I'm not doing anything!"

Nereth stood a moment longer with the lamp held out. Then she went into the left-hand opening, and the light went with her.

Seralune opened her mouth to call her back. Beyond that opening lay the broken ledge. If Nereth put a foot wrong, she wouldn't even be able to see where she had fallen.

"Nereth, wait."

She followed before the light could leave her.

*Wonderful. Lost under the palace with a guide who won't believe her own eyes, and I'm the one who said "Lead on".*

It didn't sound funny, even in her head.

The lamp had begun to shake.

Seralune watched it from behind for a long time before she said anything: the flame trembling in its glass, and the light trembling on the wall with it, so that the stones seemed to shift whenever she looked at them. Last night, with two swords a step away from her, Nereth's hands hadn't shaken at all.

"Let me carry that for a while."

"I am quite all right, Your Highness."

"You're shaking. Give it to me, and you can have both hands for the wall."

"I am not *shaking*." Nereth turned round so fast that the flame lay down sideways in its glass. "I'm cold, and I'm tired, and I've had Your Highness hanging off my arm since the arch and giving me orders since this morning, and I'd thank you to stop *looking* at me as though I'm about to fall in!"

Her voice went off along the water, and came back to them late.

Then her face went smooth, all at once, and she was looking at Seralune's sleeve.

"I beg Your Highness's pardon. Your binding has come loose."

She set the lamp down and took Seralune's right hand. Twice the loose end of the linen slipped away before she could tuck it in. On the third attempt she drew it firm and smoothed it with her thumb, carefully, without pressing the cut.

Seralune let her do it. She had opened her mouth to say *I won't be shouted at*, and she shut it again.

*She hasn't slept. She doesn't know what's happened to Cyrandor, and she's held you up every step since the arch. Let her shout. You'd shout too.*

She didn't offer again, or say anything else, because it seemed safest not to. She walked behind the light and didn't let herself think about the water or the pillar or how far down they might have come, and she had been quiet for a long time when Nereth stopped ahead of her.

"Say it to my face, Your Highness."

Seralune looked up. "Say what?"

"You can laugh at me once we're out. I haven't got us there yet." Nereth didn't turn round. "But if you've something to say about me, say it to my face."

"I haven't said a word."

"'Poor Nereth.'" It came out high and sweet, not in Nereth's own voice at all, and Seralune went cold, because it was a little like hers. "I heard you."

"I didn't say that. I haven't opened my mouth since you shouted at me."

Nereth turned her head, just far enough to look at her over her shoulder. In the lamplight her eyes were very steady, and quite sure.

"I heard you," she said again, and went on.

*I didn't say it. I know I didn't.*

Seralune went after the light.

Where the ledge widened under a low arch, Seralune sat down. She didn't ask. Her legs had begun shaking whenever she stopped, and she put her back to the wall and slid down it.

"We should not stop, Your Highness," Nereth said.

"Then go on, and I'll catch you up."

Nereth looked down the passage and took a step.

"Nereth."

The step stopped.

"I can't follow you without the lamp."

For a moment Nereth seemed to consider this. Then she came back and stood beside the recess, with her shoulder against the wall.

"Sit down. There's room out of the drip."

"And the moment I shut my eyes, Your Highness will have the lamp and be gone."

"Where would I go?"

Nereth looked at the lamp.

Seralune's anger went away so abruptly that she was left with nothing to say.

"I'm staying here. You can see me."

Nereth remained standing. She turned the wick down until the far edge of the channel disappeared. Seralune pulled the shawl round her shoulders and drew her feet beneath her skirt. One heel had left blood inside the shoe. She moved it off the sore place and kept her eyes open until the stones began to blur together. Then she closed them, only to rest them.

When she opened them, Nereth's face was a hand's width from hers.

She was crouched over her with the lamp held close to Seralune's cheek. Its flame was hardly bigger than a seed, and Nereth held a second one on the end of her right thumb beside it. The skin round her thumbnail had blistered white.

"You stopped breathing, Your Highness," Nereth said.

Seralune couldn't move.

"I was waiting to see whether you would start again."

"And if I hadn't?"

Nereth looked at her. She seemed to give the question real thought. Then the flame on her thumb went out, and she stood up and took the lamp with her.

"We should go."

Seralune didn't know how long she had slept. The lamp had been half full when she sat down, and it wasn't now. Her legs had gone stiff, and by the time she had got them under her, Nereth was already walking.

The pillar came round again.

When the light reached the pillar, Seralune knew it by its crack. There was her mark, and below it their prints going left, trodden over and over, one on top of another, until the silt was churned to mud.

*Enough.*

*If we're going to be lost, I'll at least choose where.*

"Give me the lamp, Nereth. I'm going first."

"Your Highness does not know the way."

"*Neither do you!* That's my mark, and we keep walking past it!"

It went off down every opening at once and came back to them out of all of them, a little late, in pieces. Nereth's fingers tightened on the handle until the flame shook in its glass.

"Give me the lamp. That's an order."

That morning she had asked Nereth to choose whether to come with her. Now she was standing in front of her with her hand out, waiting to be obeyed.

"As Your Highness commands."

Nereth held the lamp out. Seralune took it in her good left hand and lifted it.

Left was the circle. Behind her, the channel ran back to the arch and the palace. To the right, the opening they had never tried went down in three shallow steps, and the silt in front of it was smooth, without a mark in it.

"That goes further in, Your Highness," Nereth said behind her. "Even you can see it goes further in."

"And the left goes round. I've seen quite enough of the left."

She went down the first step. The air was different there: warmer, and close, and it smelled of wet earth, and under that, of something sweet that had been left too long.

Behind her, Nereth let out her breath.

It was a long, slow breath, and her shoulders came down with it. When Seralune turned with the lamp, Nereth was standing at the top of the steps with her eyes half shut and her face lifted a little towards the passage.

"Oh," she said softly. It wasn't to Seralune. "There you are."

"Nereth? Who are you talking to?"

Nereth opened her eyes. She looked at Seralune for a while, the way she might have looked at something someone had left on the steps. Then she came down them.

The steps gave onto a long slope, with the channel running down beside it, and the water in the channel had begun to move again, slowly, the same way they were going.

Seralune went first. With the lamp in her good hand, there was no hand left to hold, so she kept her cut one against her chest and listened behind her for Nereth's feet.

They came slower than before, and heavier, every step landing flat, and between them one shoe scraped along the stone before the other came down. Nereth was breathing through her mouth.

"Shall we stop for a while?"

Seralune stopped, so that Nereth would too. The footsteps didn't. They came on, flat and heavy, up level with her and past her, and went on down the slope out of the light. Seralune caught her sleeve with her injured hand. The pull against the binding made her gasp. Nereth stopped with one foot at a broken section of ledge.

Seralune set the lamp down so she could take her arm properly, eased her backwards and picked it up again.

Nereth stood with one hand flat on the wall, looking down the slope to where the light didn't reach. Seralune lowered the lamp to see her face.

"Nereth?"

Her eyes went to the light, the way anyone's would. They didn't come to Seralune, and in a moment they had gone back down the slope.

"Stay behind the light," Seralune said. "Do you hear me? Behind it."

After that she walked slower and looked back over her shoulder every few steps, and every time, Nereth was there, a few paces behind the light, with her head down, coming on.

She was talking.

It was so quiet that at first Seralune took it for breathing. Then she caught the words, and they weren't meant for her.

"I am," Nereth said. She sounded the way she had in the bedroom, with her hands folded and her eyes on the floor, answering someone she had to answer. "I am minding her. I haven't let go of her once."

Seralune turned with the lamp.

Nereth's right hand was held a little out from her side, the way it had been when Seralune's was in it. The fingers were closed, carefully, round nothing.

*Mind her hand. And mind yourself.*

*She thinks he's behind us. She thinks she's still holding on to me.*

Seralune went back to her. She took the closed hand in her own cut one, because the lamp was in the other, and it hurt, and she held on anyway.

"I'm here. I'm right here, Nereth. Look at me."

Nereth looked down at their hands for a long time.

Then she took hers away, gently, as though Seralune's had been in its way, and closed it round nothing again.

Seralune stood with her own hand held out and empty.

*She isn't listening to me any more. She's listening to someone else.*

She looked back up the slope. The light didn't even reach the bend now.

*Back to the pillar. Then the arch, the stairs, the room they had locked her in.*

*There has to be another way out.*

She turned and went on down.

Some time later there was more light than the lamp's.

Nereth had stopped, and was looking down into her own right hand. She had opened the fingers at last, and in her palm there was a flame the size of an egg, steady and yellow, and she was watching it with her head a little on one side and the corner of her mouth turned up, the way it had turned up over the sea.

"Go on," she whispered to it. "Bigger."

It grew while Seralune watched. It crept over the edge of her palm and up her wrist, and the cuff of her sleeve caught.

"Nereth!"

Nereth went on watching it.

Seralune set the lamp down, dragged the shawl off her shoulders and threw it over Nereth's arm, and beat at it with her good hand until the cloth stopped smoking. When she took the shawl away, the flame in Nereth's palm was gone, and the skin of her wrist was red and shining, with the burnt cuff stuck to it.

Nereth hadn't made a sound.

She lifted her head, and for the first time since the steps, she looked straight at Seralune. There was no anger in her face, and no pain, and Seralune couldn't find anything in it that knew her.

Seralune's mana came up so hard that she felt it behind her eyes.

Nereth flinched. Her burnt hand went to her ear and pressed there, hard, and she looked past Seralune down the passage and started walking again, with her hand still over her ear.

Seralune picked up the lamp and followed, with the scorched shawl over her arm.

The roots had been coming for a while before Seralune paid them any mind: a few pale threads in the joints of the wall, then cords, then whole mats of them hanging from the roof, so low that she had to hold them aside with the lamp. They were cold, and softer than roots should be, and when she let them fall back they went on swaying after they ought to have stopped.

The lamp was failing by then. The small room of light it made had drawn in round her until it hardly reached the water, and more than once the flame sank to a blue bead, and she stood quite still until it came up again.

Then there was a light that wasn't the lamp's.

She only saw it when she held the lamp behind her: a faint, cold glow, the colour of milk, coming from the roots themselves, and from small white flowers growing all among them, hundreds of them, closed up tight.

When the lamp came near, they opened.

They turned on their stems to the light as she passed, the way any flower turns to the sun. Then the lamp went on past them, and they didn't turn back. They went on turning, slowly, every one of them, until they were facing her.

She stopped. She held the lamp out to her left, as far as her arm would reach.

The flowers didn't follow it.

*Not the lamp, then.*

She took a step, and all along the wall, slowly, they came round after her.

Behind her, Nereth's feet dragged and stopped, and dragged and stopped.

Things moved in the roots after that. She never saw one properly. There was a scuttle high up that stopped when she raised the lamp, and a tail drawn back into a gap in the stone, and once, at the very edge of the light, something small that sat up on the ledge and looked at her with two white flowers where its eyes should have been, and was gone before she'd finished seeing it.

Behind her, there was a small, wet crunch.

She turned. Nereth had stopped, and under her shoe something was moving, little legs working at the stone on either side of it. Nereth didn't look down. She lifted her foot and set it down further on, and came on, and the thing she had stood on went on moving where she'd left it.

The lamp found a shoe.

It lay on its side on the ledge, soft-soled, like the ones on Seralune's own feet, and she had stepped round it before she saw the ankle above it. The woman sat against the wall with her legs twisted beneath her. The light went up a grey skirt, stiff and dark with damp, to where the roots had come through the front of it, and up to a face.

It had been a woman. Her mouth was open, and the roots came out of it, and white flowers had opened along them, between her teeth.

Seralune looked down at her own skirt. It was the same grey.

*They sent servants down here to clear the channels. Nereth was one of them.*

She held the lamp further on. There was another of them, and another: sitting against the wall with their knees drawn up, or lying face down where the water had left them, and all of them in grey. Two sat side by side, one with its head on the other's shoulder.

*How long have they been here? Did anyone look for them?*

"Nereth." Her voice came out wrong, and she tried again. "Nereth, look at them."

Nereth looked. She looked at the woman by the shoe, and then she crouched beside her, slowly, with her burnt hand still pressed to her ear, and for a breath Seralune thought she had reached her at last.

Nereth reached out and straightened the dead woman's collar.

It had rucked up against the roots. She turned it down and smoothed it flat with her fingers. Then she sat back on her heels to look, and tucked it again.

She had tucked Seralune's shawl in just like that this morning.

Nereth got up, stepped over the dead woman's legs without looking down, and went on.

They had passed a great many of the dead when one of them spoke.

"Help me."

Seralune stopped so hard that the lamp swung, and the light swung with it, across the wall and across a man sitting there in grey with his head tipped back against the stone and his jaw hanging open. The roots in his mouth moved.

"Help me."

It was a girl's voice, hoarse and worn thin, the way Seralune's own had been by the end of that day in the room with the crystal. It hadn't come from his chest. His chest had a root through it. It had come out of his mouth.

"Who's there?"

"Help me." Further along, another jaw had moved. "Help me."

"Where are you? I can hear you!" Seralune held the lamp as high as it would go. "Keep talking, I'm coming!"

For a while there was only the water.

Then, from a mouth somewhere ahead, beyond the edge of the light, came her own voice.

"*Where are you? I can hear you! Keep talking, I'm coming!*"

It was hers exactly: the same words, the same crack on *coming*, sharp in the same places.

Seralune's hand went to her mouth.

*"Poor Nereth." She heard me say it, and I never said a word.*

She turned round.

Nereth was a pace behind her, looking past her down the passage, and her lips were moving. No sound came out of them. Seralune didn't need one. She watched the shape of the words go round and round, in time with the voice from the dead.

*Help me. Help me.*

The girl's voice came again from further down, louder than it had been yet.

"Help me."

Seralune made herself think.

*Something must have said it first.*

*What if it's still down there?*

She knew what Nereth would have said, the Nereth at the arch. She would have kept their hopes modest. Modest at best.

Seralune lifted the lamp and went towards the voice. Before she had gone far, she looked back. Nereth was coming.

She wanted to take her away from the dead, cover her burnt arm properly and find someone who could make her answer. But the voice kept coming from ahead, and the passage was still the only way they had not tried.

The voices led her to an old arch over the channel, where the ledge widened into a floor of bare stone, and there they stopped.

Under the arch, in a hollow at the foot of the wall, something was caught.

It was a lizard, no longer than her hand. Roots had grown through it, in at its sides and out along its back, and white flowers had opened all down its spine. A separate root as thick as Seralune's wrist crossed the hollow and pinned one hind leg flat. It pulled against it again and again, and every time its sides heaved, the voice came out of the dead behind her.

"Help me."

Its mouth opened with the words. No sound came out of it. The sound came from behind her, from all of them.

Seralune set the lamp on the wide stone, well back from the water. Behind her, Nereth sat down against the wall all at once, with her legs out in front of her.

Seralune knelt by the hollow and reached towards the root. The flowers along the creature's back turned towards her face, and she stopped short of touching it.

*Don't put your hand in. You've seen what these things do.*

She found a loose stone, a flat one, and began working its edge under the root with her good hand.

"Leave it."

Her heart jumped so hard that it hurt.

*She spoke. She spoke to me.*

She looked round. Nereth was sitting where she had dropped, with her burnt hand still pressed to her ear and her eyes on the lizard, but the voice had been her own, the even one she had used on the guards with their swords out.

"Its leg's caught. I think it's making them call." Seralune was so glad to hear her that it came out as half a laugh. "If I can get it free, perhaps they'll stop."

She turned back and leaned on the stone until the root gave a little. The lizard heaved, and behind her the dead said it again.

"Help me."

Then cloth dragged on stone. When Seralune turned her head, Nereth was getting up. She pushed herself up the wall a little at a time, with her burnt hand still at her ear and the other spread flat on the stone beside her, and when she was on her feet, the burnt hand came down.

"I said leave it alone…"

It was lower than before, and it shook. There was no "Your Highness" on it. Even shouting at her by the lamp, Nereth had called her that.

"Sit down, Nereth. Your hand's burnt." Seralune bent towards the root again. "I'm nearly finished. Just let me get it free."

With her head down, she heard Nereth come away from the wall: the flat, heavy steps she had listened to all the way down the slope, with one shoe scraping between them. They came as far as the lamp, and didn't stop there.

Then the light went off her hands, and Nereth's shadow lay over the hollow. Seralune drew one foot beneath herself, ready to stand, and looked up.

Nereth was standing over her. Her burnt hand hung at her side, level with Seralune's face, with the cuff still stuck to the wrist, and its fingers kept opening and closing. Her eyes weren't on the lizard any more. They were on Seralune.

"LEAVE IT!"

She bent and screamed it into Seralune's face, so close that Seralune felt the heat of it. She held the last word and went on screaming it until her voice cracked and tore, and her burnt hand had come up beside Seralune's head with the fingers spread, shaking.

Seralune flinched, and her whole weight went down on the stone, and the root lifted.

The leg came free all at once, and the lizard was out of the hollow and up onto her sleeve before she could draw her arm back, its claws pricking through the cloth. She held her arm still. It was lighter than she'd thought. It climbed past her elbow to her shoulder and stopped there, and turned its head, and looked at Nereth.

"Don't you scream at me." It came out higher than Seralune meant it to. "It's out. It's done."

Nereth's face was wet. Her eyes went to the lizard by Seralune's neck and came back to Seralune, and her lips drew back from her teeth.

Seralune's mana rose under her ribs so fast that she nearly doubled over, and all down the channel, out of every mouth at once, the dead screamed it.

"*HELP ME.*"

Nereth came at her.

She came with her whole body, shoulder first, and drove Seralune sideways off her knees onto the stone. The lamp went over. Seralune heard the glass break, and the flame went out, and the only light left was the cold white glow of the flowers along the wall, every one of them turned towards her.

Before she could get an elbow under her, Nereth was on top of her, and Nereth's hands were round her throat.

Seralune got both hands on Nereth's wrists and pulled. Her good hand had closed on the burnt one, and the skin slid under her fingers, wet, with the cuff stuck to it, and Nereth didn't even flinch. Neither wrist moved, and she couldn't get her fingers under them. Nereth leaned down over her with all her weight on her hands, and the breath Seralune had started to take stopped halfway, in her throat, and wouldn't go either way.

"Make them stop." Nereth's face was a hand's width from hers, lit from below by the flowers, white and wet. "Make them stop. Make them *stop*—"

Seralune clawed at her arms. Her nails caught skin and dragged. The cut in her palm split under its binding, and the pain went up her arm so fiercely that she let go with that hand and hit at Nereth's face instead. It glanced off a cheek and fell back onto the stone.

She kicked. Her heels skidded. She got one foot flat and heaved, and the borrowed dress twisted round her legs and held her, and Nereth rocked and leaned in harder.

Her mana was everywhere: in her chest, behind her eyes, in her throat under Nereth's thumbs, crowding up and up with nowhere to go. With every heave of it the voices came louder, out of every mouth in every wall, *help me, help me, HELP ME*, until she couldn't hear the water any more.

"*Stop it!*" Nereth was screaming into her face. "Stop *looking* at me! Stop *doing* it!"

Her sight was going, in from the edges.

*No.* It came from somewhere a long way down. *I didn't get out of that room to die on another floor.*

She got her hand up again, onto Nereth's face, and pushed, and it wasn't enough. Her sight had closed to a small grey place with Nereth's mouth in the middle of it, moving, and she couldn't hear what it said. Her legs were still kicking. She could hear her shoes on the stone, and she couldn't feel them.

Something cold moved across her neck, between Nereth's fingers.

Small claws went over her collarbone, quick and light. Something soft brushed past her cheek.

Nereth screamed.

One hand left her throat. Seralune caught the other in both of hers and wrenched, and twisted out from under it, and rolled.

The first breath tore its way in. She choked on it and coughed, and couldn't stop coughing, curled on her side on the stone with her throat raw, dragging in breath after breath, each one hurting worse than the last.

Nereth was still screaming.

Seralune tried to push herself upright. Her injured palm touched the floor and gave way beneath her. She fell onto her elbow, waited for the grey to retreat from her sight and scrambled onto her knees.

Nereth was on her knees by the channel with the lizard hanging from her face, its teeth sunk into her cheek below the left eye. Nereth tore it off and flung it. It struck the water and went under, and Nereth lurched after it, over the edge—

She caught the back of Nereth's dress with her good hand. The cloth pulled taut. Nereth's hip slid towards the water, and Seralune got her arm round her waist and threw her own weight backwards.

They fell together against the foot of the arch.

Seralune let go and pushed herself away at once, until stone stopped her back.

"Get it out." Nereth had both hands over her face, and blood was coming between her fingers. "Get it out of me. Get it *out*—"

"It's gone." It came out as a scrape, and it hurt. "Nereth, it's gone, it's in the water—"

"*It's in me!*"

She took her hands away.

The bite was a small thing, a ragged crescent below her eye. But lines were spreading out from it under the skin, dark and branching, the way the roots had branched over the stones, up past the corner of her eye towards her temple and into her hair, and Seralune could see them grow.

Seralune put her hand to her own throat. She couldn't help it.

Nereth's mouth opened.

"Help me."

It wasn't her voice. It was the girl's, hoarse and thin, the voice from the dead, and it had come out of Nereth's mouth.

Nereth went still. Her eyes widened, and her hand flew up and clamped over her own mouth. Above it, her eyes came to Seralune's, and for one breath they were Nereth's eyes, and they were terrified.

Then all down the channel, from every one of the dead, the voice answered her.

"*Help me.*"

Nereth took her hand away from her mouth and turned her face towards them.

"Shut up," Nereth said, in her own voice.

Her right hand came up off the stone towards the channel, towards all of them.

"Shut up. Shut up, shut up, *shut up*—"

There was a flame in her palm, and it wasn't small.

"Nereth, no—" It was hardly a voice at all. Seralune got behind her, put her good arm round her waist and pulled her back towards the thick foot of the arch. Her injured forearm caught across Nereth's chest. She held on as they struck the stone together. "Put it *down*—"

The air went.

It rushed past Seralune's face all at once, towards Nereth's hand, and dragged her hair forward over her eyes and the breath out of her chest. The water in the channel humped up and ran towards them. Every white flower on every wall bent on its stem towards that hand.

Then the light in the roots went out, all of it, as far as she could see, at once, and the only light left was in Nereth's palm, and it was white.

Seralune never saw it leave her hand.

There was white everywhere, and in it she could see everything at once: every root and every stone, and every one of the dead along the walls with their faces and their open mouths, all the way to where the channel turned, as clear as noon.

Then she couldn't see anything.

There was no sound at all. There was no water, and no breathing, hers or Nereth's. She couldn't hear the falls, and she couldn't hear anything in the place where the falls should have been, and she thought the white had taken every sound there was.

Then it came.

It didn't come as a sound. It came as a weight, all over her at once, through her teeth and into her bones, the way the cracking had in the moment before she fell onto the stone in the room with the crystal, and it didn't stop. The arch shook at her back. The floor struck upwards beneath her. Something hit her right shoulder and her arm went numb, trapped between Nereth and her own body. Somewhere behind them the roof was coming down, and each impact came through the ledge.

She kept her left arm tight round Nereth's waist.

Nereth had gone rigid in her arms, with her arm out straight towards the channel, and all of her was shaking, a hard, fine shaking that went on and on.

When she could see again, the fire was going away from them.

It filled the channel from wall to wall and tore down it faster than she had ever seen anything move, and everywhere it reached, the walls lit from inside. The roots flared white. The dead burned. The water in the channel burst up white and was carried away with it. Then it was gone round the turn, and behind it the channel was black.

From somewhere past the turn, a blow came back to her through the stone, under her and through the arch at her back. Another came after it, further off, and another further still, each one fainter than the last, going away from her into the mountain.

The mountain answered. She felt it before she understood it: a long, deep shudder that began far off and came back up through the stone towards her.

The wind came in.

It came up the channel from round the turn, cold, and tasting of something she didn't know, and the fire came back with it.

It rolled round the turn and up the channel towards them, filling it from side to side, and there was nowhere to go. There was the arch at her back and the wall beside her and Nereth in her arms, and that was all there was.

Seralune pulled Nereth's head in against her chest, put her own face down into Nereth's hair and shut her eyes.

*Don't let go.*

It reached them, and went round them.

She felt it go, roaring on both sides, so close that it dragged at her skirt. Her hair didn't catch. Her skin didn't burn. There was no heat in it at all. She opened her eyes on fire a finger's width away. It broke in front of them, passed down her left side and her right, and filled the arch overhead. Behind her, it struck the fallen stone with a roar. It went past and past and past, and none of it touched them.

A piece of stone struck Seralune's shin. She cried out, and the sound was lost in the fire.

Then the fire had spent itself on the fallen stone behind them, and the roar stopped.

She couldn't hear anything but a thin, high whine inside her own head. She sat in it with Nereth in her arms and waited, and the sounds came back to her through it slowly.

Stone was still falling somewhere, in long rattling runs, then in single pieces, further and further apart. Water dripped onto hot stone and hissed. The wind went on coming up the channel, cold, into her face.

She listened under all of it for the voices.

They didn't come. Not from the walls, and not from Nereth.

Here and there along the walls, a root was still burning in a crack, small and low. It was the only light left.

Nereth stopped shaking. Her arm came down across Seralune's knees, and lay there.

There was no flame in her hand any more. The fingers were open, and they didn't close, and when Seralune touched them, they were cold.

Seralune bent her face to Nereth's mouth. For a long time there was no breath at all. Then there was one, very small, against her cheek.

"Nereth?" It was hardly a sound at all.

The breath came again. That was all.

"Nereth." It hurt more the second time. "It's over. You can open your eyes."

She tried to wake her first.

She shook her by the shoulder, and pinched the back of Nereth's hand as hard as she could, and Nereth's head only rolled against her arm, and her eyes stayed shut.

*Then I'll carry you.*

Seralune pulled the scorched shawl from beneath her elbow and laid it over the stone. Moving Nereth onto it took long enough that she had to stop twice to draw breath. Then she used it beneath the shoulders to pull her upright against the foot of the arch, keeping her face away from the rough stone.

Nereth's head fell forward. Seralune put it back against the wall and crouched with her own back towards her. She drew the arms over her shoulders and leaned forward until Nereth's weight settled against her.

She caught both wrists in her good hand, beneath her chin.

Nereth's arms lay across her throat.

Seralune's breath stopped. It was the stone under her back again, and the hands, and she let go of the wrists and lurched forward onto her own hands. Nereth slid sideways off her back onto the shawl.

She stayed on her hands and knees with her head down, dragging air in. Every breath went through her throat with a thin, scraping whistle, and none of them was enough.

*It's an arm. It's only her arm. She can't hurt anyone now.*

She sat Nereth up against the arch again and knelt with her back towards her. This time she drew the wrists well below her collarbone and held them against her chest with her good hand. She reached back with her other arm and hooked it beneath one thigh, keeping the cut hand open. The movement pulled on her right shoulder so fiercely that she nearly let go.

She leaned further forward and tried again.

When she stood, she stayed bent beneath the weight, with one forearm wedged beneath Nereth's thigh and the other hand holding the wrists down. The shawl lay on the stone behind them. She couldn't pick it up too.

Nereth's other foot dragged on the stone behind her. Her head hung against Seralune's ear, and her breath came there, small and wet. Every time it caught, Seralune went still and waited for it to be words. It never was. She was so much heavier than Seralune had thought anyone could be. Seralune's legs had been shaking since the floor by the hollow, and now they shook worse. Her right shoulder had feeling in it again, and all of it was pain.

She took a step, and another. The heat of the stone came up through the soft soles of her shoes, so that she couldn't stand still on it for long.

The channel was bare now, black, and the stone ticked as it cooled. By the roots still burning in the cracks she could see that where the dead had been, there were only shapes on the walls, paler than the stone round them, and one of them still had its knees drawn up. She didn't look at them for long. She kept her face in the wind, and looked at the next place to put her feet.

Her right arm began slipping. She pushed the forearm further beneath Nereth's thigh and cried out before she could stop herself. Blood from the binding darkened Nereth's skirt below her supporting arm.

She stopped beside a low, flat stone and lowered Nereth onto it. Her legs shook so badly that she sat down on the floor before they could drop her. Cold air reached her face. She breathed it in and watched Nereth's chest until she could see it rise beneath the torn dress.

Then she pulled her upright again.

Further on, her foot turned on a loose stone. She dropped to one knee, released Nereth's wrists and threw out her good hand. Nereth began sliding over her shoulder. Seralune twisted onto her hip, keeping the injured forearm hooked beneath the thigh, and got her other hand behind Nereth's head as they came down together.

They stayed there for a while.

Seralune's knee had gone numb. She wanted someone to come and take the weight from her. Nereth breathed beside her, and when Seralune tried to get her up again, it took so long that she cried with rage doing it, without a sound, because she had no voice left to make one.

*Get up. You're not finished. Get up.*

The wind was in her face the whole way. She had gone round the turn and a long way past it before she saw where it came from: a grey place at the end of the channel, very faint, with stars in it. Somewhere along the way the stars went out of it, and it was only grey, and the grey grew until it stopped being a far-off light and became a ragged hole with sides to it, and then there were no sides, and she was out on broken stone in the open air. Her legs went. She let Nereth down off her back onto the stone, as gently as she could, which wasn't very.

She lifted her head and looked.

The mountain had been opened. She had to tip her head right back to see the top of the hole they had come out of, and its edges had melted and run down and set hard again, and they still shone. Below it, where the side of the mountain should have been, a long black furrow ran away down the slope, wider than any road she had ever seen: broken rock and burnt earth and the stumps of trees, with fires still burning along both its edges and smoke going up from them, all the way down until she lost it in the grey. Beyond the fires, the trees hadn't burned. They lay flat, every one of them, all pointing the same way, away from the hole, as far as the firelight reached.

All her life she had heard what Thaer could do. She had never watched him use that power, and she had imagined a great many things from what people said.

She had never imagined this.

She looked down at Nereth's hand, lying open on the stone, and couldn't make the two things fit.

Off to one side, well away from the furrow, there were lights.

They lay along the edge of the water at the bottom of the mountain, small and yellow, a great many of them. As the sky greyed, she began to make out roofs behind them, and a wall running out into the water, and dark shapes along it that she knew after a while for ships. Beyond them, the water went on, flat and grey, until she couldn't tell where it stopped and the sky began.

*Nereth, it's the sea.*

She looked round as though she might find her standing there to see it.

Nereth lay with her face turned up to the sky. In the grey light the lines from the bite were black, going purple at their edges, and they ran from under her eye up into her hair. Her eyes were shut. In the cold air her breathing had changed. It was slower than it had been in the channel, and deeper, and it went all the way down.

*She told me to leave it.*

The creature had been caught. Nereth had been looking at something that wasn't there long before they found it. Seralune knew both things, but neither made the thought go away.

A town meant doors, water, someone who might know what to do for Nereth's face. Someone who could help her carry.

Seralune studied the ground below them. To the side of the ruined slope, a narrow strip of earth descended between rocks. Further down it disappeared behind trees still standing. She couldn't see where it went after that.

She would have to try it.

She pulled Nereth upright, then knelt with her back to her, drew the wrists down against her chest and hooked her injured forearm beneath the thigh. Standing took longer this time.

Down in the town, a bell had begun to ring, and it went on ringing.

The lights were still a long way below them when she started down.
