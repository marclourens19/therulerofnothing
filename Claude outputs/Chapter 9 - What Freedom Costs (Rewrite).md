# What Freedom Costs

The cell stood open between them.

Darcy looked at the door before she looked at either of them.

"Lock it."

Silas kept one hand on the iron frame. "You haven't heard why we're here."

"Marta sent you."

The certainty in her voice stopped him.

Darcy pulled her sleeve down over her forearm. The cloth caught on one of the newer burns, and something tightened at the corner of her mouth before the mark disappeared.

"She heard about the transfer." Her eyes went over the stolen armour, then the blood drying through Alaric's wrappings. "She couldn't come herself, so she sent the only man in Kelmend fool enough to walk into a fort before dawn — and someone the garrison wouldn't think to be afraid of."

Her gaze stayed on Alaric's hands.

"You shouldn't have got this far."

"But we did," Silas said, his grip tightening on the door. "Hear us out before you decide what that means."

Darcy looked from him to the open cell. "You've come to take me somewhere."

"If you choose," Alaric said.

Her eyes came back to him.

He stepped inside so the open door was behind him. The sleeve covered her burns now, but he could still see them — some thin enough to have been made with a single finger, others curving all the way round her arm.

"Marta can get you out of Kelmend," he said. "She's waiting past the wall with provisions. Food, coin, a road ready."

Darcy didn't move.

"You wouldn't belong to the fort anymore." He was hurrying now, trying to get it all out before she could refuse. "You could go wherever you wanted. Somewhere they couldn't do this to you."

Her eyes went past him, through the open door and down the corridor she'd never been allowed to walk. Her fingers tightened on the edge of the desk. Not much. Enough to turn the knuckles white.

Then she let go.

"No."

Somewhere above them, a door opened. Boots crossed the command level, stopped, turned, and crossed it again.

Silas glanced out into the corridor. Darcy didn't.

Alaric had expected fear. He'd expected questions, suspicion, a demand for proof that Marta was really out there somewhere beyond the stone. He'd braced himself to persuade her. He hadn't braced for an answer that sounded finished.

"You don't understand what they're going to do to you," he said, too fast.

"Captain Gilmot has spent years making sure I understand exactly what he intends."

Silas came in and drew the door closer without shutting it, his attention still on the corridor. "We heard him with the Natharul officer. If you disappear, he means to punish the Faint quarter."

Darcy's face didn't change.

Alaric could hear the command room again. *Its adults will be suppressed. Fire companies will clear the structures.* The paper turning under Gilmot's hand. The flame bending towards him.

"If you leave," he said, "he'll say the whole quarter helped steal you. He told the elf he'd burn it before the second morning bell."

Darcy lowered her eyes to the papers on the desk.

"He said the adults would be suppressed." The next words fought him. "The elf asked about the children."

One of Darcy's ink-stained fingers stopped on the edge of a map.

"What did Gilmot say?"

Alaric swallowed. "That there are always children."

She took a breath. Then another. Neither went deep enough.

"The woman who carried laundry up to the command rooms had two sons," she said. Her voice had gone very quiet. "The older one charged Wind cores. The younger worked near the ovens whenever the kitchen let him close enough to the heat."

Her thumb rubbed once across the ink on her forefinger.

"I always knew when she was coming. One wheel on her cart hit the floor differently from the others. Before she reached my door, she'd lift the left side so it would stop complaining."

She was looking at the map, but the lines under her hand belonged to some other country and some other battle.

"I refused a river assault last winter. The thaw had broken the eastern bank. Gilmot wanted three companies across before the water rose. I told him the bridge would go under the second."

Her voice didn't change.

"He brought her upstairs and fastened an iron collar round her throat. Every time my quill stopped, he heated it. When I wrote, he let it cool."

Alaric's fingers curled. The dried blood inside his wrappings cracked against his skin.

"Did he —"

"I finished the order." She kept her voice level. The effort showed only in the hand pressing harder into the desk. "He let the collar cool. Then he asked me whether I'd learned."

Her next breath caught before she could hide it.

"He heated it again before I could answer."

Alaric couldn't make himself ask the rest.

"The bridge went under the second company," Darcy said. "He changed the record before it reached the capital."

She said nothing about the woman. She didn't need to.

"He's killed to correct me before. He'll do it again. If I disappear tonight, he won't start with one person."

"Then he'll hurt you whether you stay or not."

It came out of Alaric harder than either of them expected. Darcy looked up.

"Yes."

There was no self-pity in it at all. That made it worse.

"Marta can get you out of his reach." He stepped closer, and his ankle didn't follow cleanly. Pain shot up his leg, and he caught the edge of the desk before it gave. "If you stay, they put you on that ship. They take you to a frontier and make you plan another war for them."

"And if I leave, hundreds of people become the punishment."

"Then warn them. Give them time to run."

"Run *where*?" Her composure finally cracked. She pressed both hands flat on the desk and leaned towards him. "They live outside a walled town. Gilmot's given them until the second bell. Half the adults have spent the night draining themselves into that core over our heads, and the rest would be carrying children. Tell me which road they take."

Alaric looked at the maps on the wall. Not one of them showed the Faint quarter.

"Marta knows people," he said.

"Does Marta have somewhere to hide hundreds of people?"

He had no answer.

Darcy held his eyes. "Do you?"

"No."

It came out through his teeth.

He could see the broken well waiting past Kelmend's wall. Marta's cart. Wena under the canvas. A road leading somewhere he couldn't even name. Darcy could have given that road a direction. Instead she'd laid a whole district across it.

"I know what staying means," Darcy said. "I still can't leave them to him."

Alaric's throat closed.

"We came all this way."

It was out before he understood what it gave away.

Silas's head turned slightly. Darcy studied Alaric's face.

"No," she said. "You came for more than me."

Above them, the Wind core pulsed. The vibration came through the desk into his palms.

He could have lied. The fort was waking up, the cell stood open, and Marta had sent him to help a woman choose freedom. He'd let himself believe that choice would lead him straight to his own past.

"Marta said you might know where I should look," he whispered.

Darcy waited.

"I don't remember who I am."

It thinned his voice until the next words barely made it out.

"There was a battlefield. Thousands of dead. Humans, elves, beastfolk. Machines bigger than carts." The cell walls drew in around the memory — mud against his face, crows tearing at whatever the armour had failed to protect, the perfect circle where the ruin stopped. "And I was the only one alive."

Darcy's hand came off the map.

"Where was it?"

"On a farm east of Kelmend. Gerolt's farm. Gerolt found me there."

Her attention sharpened on the name. "Where is he now?"

Alaric looked down at the blood on his wrappings.

"He's dead." His voice broke on it. "He died so I could live."

Darcy shut her eyes.

The lines of her face didn't break. They only got harder to hold.

"Does Marta know?"

"I told her."

She opened her eyes. Grief had come into them, but the fort left her nowhere to put it down.

"And she offered to help you find out what happened."

"Only after I'd agreed to carry her offer."

Darcy didn't look away. "But you came hoping I'd help you."

"Yes."

Admitting it hurt more because he gave it freely.

"Then this was never only about freeing me."

"No." He made himself hold her eyes. "But Marta never promised me you'd help. I just hoped you would."

Darcy looked at him long enough that he felt every selfish part of that truth sitting there in plain view.

Silas moved beside him. A muffled order came through the ceiling, and another pair of boots joined the first.

"We're out of time." Silas looked at the corridor, then at Darcy. "Marta's waiting past the wall. Come with us now. Once you're outside Kelmend, nobody picks the road for you."

"I said no."

"You're frightened of what Gilmot will do. That's not the same as wanting to stay."

"I know what he's doing to me, Silas. I *know*." Her voice rose, and she forced it back down. "That doesn't mean he's bluffing about those people."

Silas's jaw tightened. "If we leave you, he puts you on that ship."

"Yes."

"He'll burn you again."

Darcy's fingers touched the hidden bands under her sleeve. "Yes."

Silas took one step towards her. She didn't move away.

"Your decisions always leave someone else to bury the dead," she said.

Silas stopped as if she'd struck him. Alaric had heard Marta speak to him in that same voice, and he'd gone as quiet then.

Then Silas took another step.

Alaric moved into his path.

His ankle nearly betrayed him. He shifted his weight onto the other leg and stayed there, between them.

Silas looked down at him. "Move."

"No."

"You think leaving her here is kindness?"

"No." Alaric's voice shook. He held it anyway. "She answered."

Silas could have moved him with one hand. Alaric knew it. Darcy knew it.

Silas's eyes went past him, to Darcy's covered arm. The cell door stood open behind them.

He stepped back.

He didn't apologise. Darcy didn't thank him for stopping before he touched her.

Something heavy crossed the command level overhead. Dust shook loose from the ceiling and drifted down across the maps.

Darcy looked at Alaric.

"Describe the machines."

The change came too fast for him to follow. "What?"

"The machines on Gerolt's farm. Describe them."

He went looking for the field in his head.

"Metal. Silver in places. Some were shaped like carriages, but there were no horses. Their sides didn't have any rivets that I could see. No joins."

"Did they carry weapons?"

"I think so."

"Tell me what you saw, even if you don't know what it was."

He tried again. "One had a long opening in the front. Like a mouth made for fire. Another had metal legs under it. They were broken, but the ground round them was burned."

Darcy crossed to the maps on the wall. Her legs steadied faster this time, though one hand hovered near the desk in case they didn't. She pulled the pin from the corner of an unused sheet, turned it over, and scratched a circle onto the back with a broken quill. Inside it, she drew a smaller one.

"Behind the thickest armour, near the middle," she said, keeping her voice low. "Look for an orb small enough to carry."

She darkened the inner circle until the quill tore through the paper.

"The centre will be black if its mana is spent."

"What is it?"

"A war core. Soldiers feed their mana into it to drive the machine." She glanced at the door as another tremor went through the fort.

"Why would an empty one tell me anything?"

Darcy looked at the black centre she'd drawn.

"The first mana poured into a core leaves an impression. That, and the way the shell was made, might tell us where the machine came from — and how old it is."

She held the drawing out, then looked at his blood-soaked wrappings and laid it on the desk instead.

"Remember the shape. Paper won't survive the route I'm about to give you. Take an unbroken one. If the shell's cracked, or the centre's turned to powder, leave it."

Alaric fixed the shape in his mind. "If I find one, can you read it?"

"Not here."

That opened the same hollow as her refusal had. Then she went on.

"My father can."

"Where is he?"

"Favale."

The name of the Holy Capital carried a great distance inside it.

"Ask for Helmi where the western aqueduct meets the old river road. They'll know his house. He was a Vault Archivist in the Great Library."

"He doesn't work there anymore?"

"They dismissed him when he objected to them taking me." She folded the edge of the paper under one finger. "They threatened his freedom and everyone in his household. An Eminent Water『Affinity』didn't give him the right to keep his own child."

"Will he help me?"

"If he believes I sent you."

"How do I prove that?"

For the first time, Darcy looked at something the cell didn't hold.

"Before they took me, he promised we'd follow the river until it reached the sea. We'd leave Mydea if we had to. Find a shore where no examiner could decide who owned me."

Her mouth moved round the memory of a smile without ever becoming one.

"Tell him water always finds its way to the ocean."

Alaric repeated it once under his breath. She listened until she was sure he had every word.

"Then tell him I'm still waiting."

A horn sounded overhead.

The note hit the stone and went all through the fort.

**BRAAAAM.**

Darcy's head turned towards the corridor. A second horn answered from the eastern wall. Shorter. Twice.

Silas reached the cell door.

"That's the service-yard alarm," Darcy said, looking at the stolen armour. "They may have found the man you took that from."

Wood broke somewhere high above the lower cells. A distant voice started shouting. Another joined it. The Wind core surged through the walls, blue-green light racing along a copper line over Darcy's cell and vanishing into the next.

**KRRRNNNK.**

The airship pulled so hard that dust ran down from the ceiling in thin lines.

Boots began coming down towards the lower cells. Not one pair. Many.

Silas drew the sword at his hip. "Which way?"

Darcy took the drawing from the desk, crossed to the open doorway, tore it in half and fed both pieces to the caged lamp. Fire climbed the paper and blackened the ink.

"Go back towards the iron gate. Before you reach the gaoler, there's a narrow passage behind the opposite row of cells. Follow it past the last door. The inspection grate is at the end. The long key with two broken teeth opens it. Follow the throat down until the water turns east."

"Where does it come out?" Alaric asked.

"Outside the eastern wall. Near the Faint quarter."

"Why isn't it guarded?"

"Because Captain Gilmot made me correct the plans for this fort." She watched the last scrap of paper curl into the flame. "I corrected everything except that."

The first clear order reached them down the stair.

"Seal the lower cells!"

Silas moved into the corridor.

Alaric didn't.

Darcy went back to the desk and lowered herself into the chair. She straightened the transfer order under one hand and pulled the unfinished map across the space where the drawing had been.

"Lock it," she said.

Silas looked back. "They already know someone's in the fort."

"They don't know you reached me."

"The grate will tell them."

"The open door will tell them first." Darcy picked up the broken quill, though her fingers had started to tremble round it. "Lock it. They might waste time believing you never got to me."

Alaric looked at the open space between the bars. He'd crossed a river for it. He'd gone into this fort beside Silas and left blood in its passages to put that opening in front of her.

And Darcy had decided what it meant.

Silas pulled the door shut. The key went in.

**KLIK.**

It was a smaller sound than the horn. It followed Alaric much further.

Darcy dipped the quill. Her eyes stayed on the page.

"Alaric."

He turned back.

She didn't look up. "Marta will have packed food for me. There'll be apple in it. There's always apple." The quill hovered over the map. "Tell her I'm sorry I won't get to take it out."

He didn't understand it. He could see from the way she held the quill that he wasn't meant to.

"I'll tell her."

"Go."

He stepped away from the bars. The scratch of her quill followed him into the passage.

---

Silas led them back along the lower corridor, keeping close to the cell doors. Away from the lamps, the stolen armour looked darker. Blood had dried along one edge of the breastplate, and every step made the plates complain against a body they'd never been fitted for.

Alaric's disguise didn't hide much anymore. One patched knee had split. The guard's blood had dried almost black across the grey tunic, and the wrapping on his right hand had started to come loose. Every time his fingers brushed the wall, a thin smear of red was left on the stone behind him.

The prisoners watched them pass.

The old man had stopped dividing his bread. One piece was still in his fingers. The other lay on the floor, untouched.

The woman was still holding her child under her coat. The child was awake now. A pair of eyes watched Alaric over the edge of the cloth.

The gaoler's keys knocked against Silas's thigh. Once. Again. Every cell heard them.

Nobody asked him to stop.

That made walking past worse.

Alaric kept his eyes forward, but the small sounds followed him — chain shifting, cloth moving on stone, someone drawing a breath and holding it until the keys had gone by.

Near the iron gate, Silas turned into the narrow passage Darcy had described. It ran behind the opposite row of cells and ended at an iron grate barely wider than his shoulders. Rust had built up thick between the bars. A shallow channel ran under it, carrying black water into a dark too low for either of them to stand up in.

Silas pulled off the helmet, unhooked the dead guard's horn from his belt and left both against the wall. He crouched at the lock, found the long key with two broken teeth and fitted it. It went in and wouldn't turn. He eased it back.

Beyond the gaoler's stair, boots were gathering.

"Find out why the lower bell didn't answer."

"Captain wants every cell counted."

"Then wake Veller."

"He should be awake."

Silas turned the key the other way. The lock clicked. He pulled, and the grate's lower edge dragged across the stone.

**SKRRRRAAAAPE.**

The sound tore down the passage. Every voice on the stair stopped.

Silas grabbed Alaric by the back of his shirt and shoved him at the opening. "In."

Alaric went down into water that stank of rust and old filth. There was no room for his knees. He pulled himself along on his forearms, dragging the swollen ankle over the grate, and bit into his sleeve to keep from crying out.

Silas came in backwards, holding the grate up with one hand while the other felt for the lock from the inside.

Boots reached the lower passage. "Veller?"

Silas lowered the grate. The iron met stone with a dull knock. He turned the key once, pulled it free, and let the whole ring drop into the channel between them. Water closed over the keys.

"Here!" a voice shouted beyond the bars. "The inspection grate!"

Silas twisted round in the narrow space, wrenching the borrowed armour past the stone. His hand slapped the sole of Alaric's boot.

"Go."

Iron rang behind them. Alaric dragged himself round the first bend, where the lamplight disappeared and his loose bandage snagged under his hand and peeled away from the wound. He felt the blood go into the cold water and couldn't stop to bind it.

The ceiling forced his head sideways until his cheek was pressed to wet stone, and the river came back to him — cold round his wrists, his feet leaving the bottom, his next breath somewhere he couldn't reach.

*Not again.*

He found a seam between the stones and pulled towards it. Then another. At the third, his hand found no floor at all. He slid down into a wider basin, water closing over his chest, and cracked his head on the arch as he came up.

"Quiet," Silas hissed from the passage.

Alaric clamped both hands over his mouth.

The basin split three ways under the stone. Water came in through two mouths and left through the third, running east. A thin iron grating was set into the ceiling. Dawn hadn't reached it yet, but lamplight flickered across the gaps as soldiers ran along a corridor above.

Silas forced himself out of the channel and landed hard beside him, one knee going under the water. The borrowed breastplate had bent in along his ribs.

For a few breaths, neither of them moved.

Voices gathered above the grating.

"Bring the lamp closer."

"There's blood."

Alaric looked down. The black water hid his hands.

"Not here. Along the wall."

Boots crossed directly overhead. Another voice answered from further away — younger, and suddenly unsteady.

"Sirden?"

The name came down through the grating.

Alaric stopped breathing.

The guard came back to him — his weight on top of him on the service passage floor, the forearm crushing his throat, his breath hot with anger, the dagger between them, the last tightening of those fingers round Alaric's hand before they let go.

He'd been given a name after he died.

Sirden.

"He's dead," someone said above them.

**THOOM.**

The basin pulsed with Alaric's heartbeat.

"Captain needs to see this."

"The prisoners?"

"Still locked in. Veller's alive. They went down the drain."

**THOOM.**

Sirden's hand closed round his wrist again.

*Wait.*

That word had been Alaric's. He'd said it after the dagger went in — too late to be a warning, too helpless to be mercy.

The water moved against his chest. He knew he should follow it east. Darcy had told him to. Silas was already checking the third opening. His body understood every danger still around them.

It wouldn't move.

A hand closed on his shoulder.

He flinched so hard the water slapped the roof.

Silas held him there. His face had lost what colour the dark allowed it, and there was pain under the sharpness in his eyes, but his grip didn't tighten.

"Stay close to me, Alaric," he said, low enough that the words belonged only to the space between them. "We get through this first."

Alaric looked once more at the grating.

Then he moved.

---

They went into the eastern channel as iron started hammering on the locked grate behind them.

This passage let them rise to a crouch. Blue-green light pulsed through cracks overhead as the airship strained at its chains. Alaric kept one hand on the wall until the next bend brought them up against iron.

An older grate had collapsed at an angle across the drain, its frame twisted between the walls. Above the water there was a gap no wider than his head. Branches, rags and years of filth had packed themselves against the bars, and the current forced its way through in thin, angry streams.

Alaric tried the nearest bar. It didn't move. Silas reached past him and pulled once. Rust flaked off into the water. The frame held.

Behind them, a faint metallic crash came down the tunnel. They'd opened the inspection grate.

Silas looked at the narrow space over the blockage, then at Alaric's shoulder.

"You won't fit."

"Neither will you."

Silas studied the rusted joint instead of answering. "I only need enough room to turn one shoulder."

He pulled the glove off his right hand and closed his bare palm round the corroded joint that held the grate to its frame.

Orange light appeared between his fingers.

The drain changed colour. No flame rose from his skin. The iron itself started glowing under his hand, and the water hissed against it and burst into steam.

**HSSSSSSSS.**

Alaric turned his face away. The heat touched the old blood on his bandages and tightened the cloth over his wounds. Silas's breathing roughened. The joint went from black to red.

"When I tell you," he said through his teeth, "push the bottom bar."

Footsteps came into the drain behind them. Water splashed under several pairs of boots.

"Light ahead!"

Silas's fingers were shaking round the iron.

"Now."

Alaric braced his left boot against the wall and drove both hands into the bar. Pain split across his palms. The grate held. His swollen ankle folded and threw his shoulder into the frame.

"Again, Alaric!"

He thought of Darcy going back to her desk. The woman holding her child behind the bars. Sirden's name arriving too late to change what Alaric had done.

He pushed until something inside the wall screamed.

**KRRRAAANG.**

The heated joint tore loose. The grate twisted in the current. Silas caught it before it could swing down on them and forced the bottom edge aside. The gap was still narrow. It was enough.

"Through."

Alaric ducked under the broken frame. Jagged iron caught the back of his shirt and ripped it from shoulder to spine. He landed on the far side and turned straight round.

Silas hadn't moved.

His right hand was curled against his chest. Blisters shone across the palm. The orange light had gone, and his fingers wouldn't close.

The lamps behind him turned the water pale.

Alaric reached back through the gap. "Give me your arm."

Silas looked at the offered hand. His face was impossible to read.

Then he gave Alaric his left wrist.

Alaric pulled while Silas lowered himself under the frame. Armour hit iron. The breastplate caught. Silas twisted, failed, and went under the rising water.

The lamps grew brighter.

Alaric planted both feet, whatever his ankle thought about it, and pulled again.

Silas tore free with a splash that threw water up the roof. A spearhead struck the grate where his head had been.

"Move!"

They ran as well as the drain let them. The water fell away first. Stone turned to mud under their boots. Cold air found Alaric through the dark, carrying smoke, damp canvas and the distant smell of cooking grain.

Past the last bend, the drain mouth was full of grey morning.

---

The culvert came out under Kelmend's eastern wall, its broken cover half hidden in reeds and a bank of discarded timber. Alaric gripped the outer stones and hauled himself into the ditch beyond.

Air went into his lungs without hitting a ceiling. For a moment, that was all he could feel.

Then the eastern road answered with hooves. A great many of them.

Silas came out behind him and dragged the broken cover back across the opening. It wouldn't hide anything from someone who knew where to look, but it darkened the mouth enough to buy a few breaths.

"Down."

They pressed themselves in behind the timber.

A Natharul company was riding towards the gate. Their armour caught the thin dawn in pale lines, and the layered voices moved among them as they came — one sentence split between several mouths, meaning passing from rider to rider faster than ordinary speech could carry it.

At their head rode a woman with white bandages across half her face.

The cloth had darkened over the ruined left eye. Under it, the wound Gerolt had given her pulled her brow and cheek into a shape that hadn't existed at the farm. One arm was still bound against her body. She held the reins in the other.

Alaric knew her before the horse turned its head.

The river came back cold round his waist. Gerolt stood alone beyond the wheat.

Her one eye passed over the ditch.

Moved on.

Came back.

It found Alaric between the timbers.

Her whole body changed. Shock opened her face for less than a heartbeat. Then fury closed it again, so completely that the layered speech around her broke apart.

"There!"

Her heel struck the horse.

The animal left the road before the riders behind her had understood the order. Mud sprayed up under its hooves. She leaned low over its neck, her bound arm unmoving, her good hand tearing the reins round towards the ditch.

Silas grabbed Alaric's collar. "Run."

They broke from cover.

The eastern wall curved away ahead of them, its foot crowded with lean-tos, drying frames and narrow paths nobody had ever meant to be wide enough for a horse. Alaric followed Silas between two sheds as the rider reached the ditch behind them.

The reins dropped across the horse's neck as her good hand opened.

The ground cracked under the horse.

**KRROOM.**

The path lifted.

A ridge of packed earth tore up beside Alaric's boot. He threw himself forward as it slammed across the gap and hit the wall where his ribs had been. Mud and broken stone filled the air.

Silas turned and raised his good hand. Fire flickered once across his fingers, then died. Whatever he'd forced into the grate had cost him more than skin.

"Keep moving," he snarled, the anger aimed as much at his own hand as at the rider.

They plunged into the first line of shelters. The horse couldn't follow.

The woman came out of the saddle before it had stopped. Her boots hit the mud. One knee buckled, but she caught herself on the wall and came up again.

"Seal the eastern paths!" several Natharul voices called behind her. "The dark-haired human — alive — the older one —"

She didn't wait for the order to finish. She came after them.

Alaric ran.

His ankle was past pain now. It folded without warning and only recovered because the next step demanded it. Every breath went in too shallow. The torn shirt clung to his back while the cold morning got in through the rip.

Silas stayed half a pace behind. Every time Alaric stumbled, Silas's hand was there, shoving him on before she could close the gap.

The path split round a row of washing vats. Silas took the narrower side. Alaric followed and nearly ran into a man carrying two empty buckets on a pole. The man pressed himself flat against a shelter without a word. Blue-green bruises ringed both his wrists — the same colour Alaric had seen on the workers under the airship.

They passed a woman binding cloth round a child's foot. The bandage had been washed so many times it had lost its colour. Three more children slept under an awning beside her, pressed together for warmth under one torn blanket.

Nobody asked why armed men were running through their homes. People moved out of the way.

They knew what happened when they didn't.

Behind him, the Natharul woman came into the path. Her shoulder hit a roof support and the canvas came down between them. She tore through it with her good hand, shouting in the layered tongue, and the riders behind her took a heartbeat too long to pass it on.

Silas pulled Alaric round another corner.

The shelters grew thicker. They leaned against Kelmend's outer wall as if the town had shaken them off itself and left them where they fell. Timber ended in different places. Roofs were made of canvas, beaten tin, and pieces of broken doors. Thin smoke leaked out of holes cut above little cookfires. Water pooled under every walkway, because nobody had ever built a drain to take it away.

A girl no older than ten stood by one fire, stirring a pot with both hands. A raw red line ran all the way round her throat.

Alaric looked back once. The white bandages had disappeared behind the fallen canvas. They wouldn't stay there.

"Which way?" His breath broke the words apart.

Silas searched the roofs. "The broken well's past the eastern kilns."

"Is Marta there?"

"If she made it."

*If.*

They kept moving.

The first morning bell rang out inside Kelmend. Its clean note carried out over the wall and across all the roofs the town refused to count as streets.

People woke under it.

A man with one leg dragged himself out of a doorway and reached for a crutch mended with wire. Two boys carried a sleeping woman between them, because neither of them was tall enough to carry her alone. By a gutter, an old woman divided a heel of bread into five pieces, each one too small to hide inside her palm.

Down under the fort, hundreds of people had fitted inside two words.

Out here, the number wouldn't stay whole.

Bruised wrists. Empty bowls. Children already awake because hunger had ended their sleep before the bell could.

Darcy had looked through an open door and seen every one of them.

Then she'd asked Alaric to close it.

He followed Silas deeper into the Faint quarter while the alarm rang on behind them.
