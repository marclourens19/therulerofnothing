## Story North Star

> Alaric matters because, despite being surrounded by ancient powers, predetermined souls and world-ending magic, he continues choosing what kind of person he will be.

Alaric must remain emotionally and practically human. His significance should not come from secret tactical mastery, effortless superiority or an inherited right to rule. Although impossible powers lie dormant within him, the person he becomes is determined by his choices.

Fate may bring Alaric and Seralune together, but fate cannot make them love one another. Their relationship matters because they continue choosing each other even after learning that the universe intended them to meet.

## Core Premise

An ordinary human boy awakens a thousand years after his existence was erased from history, with no memory of who he was or how he came to lie in the middle of an impossible battlefield.

Approximately 1,020 years before the present, Alaric and Seralune were born at the same instant as two halves of one divided soul. They remain complete, unique individuals with separate wills and personalities; the division creates metaphysical compatibility, not emotional incompleteness or compulsory love. Alaric received four dormant Veiled Affinities — Light, Dark, Time and Spirit — with almost no mana. Seralune received infinite mana with no Affinity through which to shape it.

They found and freely loved one another. When their souls aligned, Seralune's mana flowed through Alaric's Affinities and produced power neither human personality could safely contain. Their early alignments caused uncontrollable metaphysical effects and deaths around them. Persecution, racial hatred, their refusal to remain separated, and increasingly catastrophic consequences eventually drove humans, elves and beastfolk into a coalition against them.

When they were twenty, one thousand years before the present, the coalition cast the most forbidden spell in existence. It was designed to delete Alaric from existence and collective memory, thereby breaking the condition that made Seralune effectively invulnerable. Instead, Alaric's hidden Affinities resisted the ritual and displaced him and the battlefield around him one thousand years forward. The erasure of his name and place in memory largely succeeded; his execution did not.

Seralune remained behind. Her memories were rewritten around Alaric's absence, leaving her childhood, family and personal identity apparently intact while removing Alaric, the war and the causal meaning of many experiences. In a catastrophically weakened state, she was sealed in unconscious magical stasis by her elder brother, Natharul's Dark bearer and most powerful warrior. She remained unconscious for the millennium and physically and consciously twenty years old.

Alaric's re-entry into the world breaks the ancient severance at the exact same moment and wakes Seralune. Volume One therefore begins with two simultaneous awakenings: Alaric wakes outside with freedom but no identity; Seralune wakes inside a royal prison with an assigned identity but no freedom.

The intended series ending is Alaric and Seralune's freely chosen sacrifice to remove magic and『Affinity』from the living world. They act out of love for each other, their friends and future children whose lives should not be assigned a worth by magical strength. Their friends gain the chance to choose their own futures; the couple reunites in the Soul World as two distinct people, finally able to love without endangering anyone. Removing magic does not automatically cure prejudice or abolish every oppressive institution.

**Status:** agreed emotional destination for the entire series, not Volume One. Both sacrifices and the reunion are intended; their necessity, mechanism and compatibility with the Soul World's rules remain to be established.

## Creative Direction

### Genre and Tone

- Epic fantasy with mystery, horror, political conflict and intimate character drama.
- The story may contain blood, death, cruelty, slavery, sexuality and other mature subjects. These elements should serve character, atmosphere or consequence rather than exist solely for shock.
- The world should feel old, inhabited and independent of the protagonist.
- History survives through incomplete records, propaganda, religious interpretation, contradictory witnesses and myths that contain fragments of truth.
- Alaric begins as a nobody in a world inhabited by ancient, immensely powerful people. His ability to change fate must come from persistence and moral choice rather than conventional dominance.

### Influences

- **Final Fantasy XIV** — interconnected history, sympathetic antagonists, conflicts whose roots extend far beyond the protagonist's lifetime.
- **Re:Zero** — sustained character pressure, painful consequences, mystery-led revelations, arcs that repeatedly test the protagonist's values, tonal whiplash, compulsive/spiraling internal narration under stress.
- **The Lord of the Rings** — grounded characters, a world larger than the immediate plot, history that carries emotional weight.

These works are guides to emotional and structural qualities, not templates to reproduce.

### Villain Design

Seralune's eventual antagonism should remain emotionally understandable even when her actions become indefensible. Give her a loss, love or conviction powerful enough that the reader understands why she refuses to surrender it, even while recognising the harm she chooses to cause.

## Magic and 『Affinity』

### The Eight Affinities

Every soul is born tethered to an『Affinity』, though the strength of that connection varies enormously. Some possess an affinity too faint to produce usable magic. Across most of Lazaran, that weakness is treated as evidence that they cannot govern their own lives, allowing states and powerful households to place them under service, stewardship, debt bondage or compulsory labour.

Mana and『Affinity』are separate. Mana is the fuel; Affinity determines the form that fuel can take. Vast mana without an affinity is as limiting as a strong affinity without sufficient mana.

|Group|Affinities|Function|Distribution|
|---|---|---|---|
|**The Common Four**|Fire, Water, Wind, Earth|Govern the physical forces civilisation is built from.|Widespread, strength varies.|
|**The Veiled Four**|Light, Dark, Time, Spirit|Govern metaphysical forces acting on life, death, causality and the soul.|Normally only one living bearer of each exists at a time.|

When a Veiled bearer dies, that affinity passes to a newly born soul. Under the natural order there are never more than four recognised metaphysical bearers alive at once.

> The Four shaped what could be touched. The Four beyond them governed what could not. Together, they were the Eight.

**Terminology:** "Common Four" / "Veiled Four." Different cultures may use different names.

### The Living Bearers of the Veiled Four — Current State

The Veiled Four are not an abstract classification. Each currently has a single living bearer, and each bearer is a named, individually powerful figure whose existence shapes the political map of the world — closer in narrative weight to an Archbishop of Sin (_Re:Zero_) or an Ascian (_FFXIV_) than to an ordinary magic-user. Possessing one is not a talent. It is closer to being a living weapon of state, whether or not the bearer wants that role.

**Working current distribution:**

|Affinity|Traditional Role|Current Bearer / Holder|Political Home|
|---|---|---|---|
|**Spirit**|The Hero — protector, bearer of the legendary spirit blade|Elf (Natharul)|Natharul|
|**Dark**|The Severer / Final Warden — keeper of endings, punishment and conceptual severance|Thaeroval, Seralune's elder brother and an elven prince|Natharul|
|**Light**|Holy priest/priestess|Human, a priestess|Mydea|
|**Time**|_Role to be defined — see below_|Beastfolk|Kozmagar|

**Natharul currently holds two of the four living Veiled bearers.** This is the direct, concrete source of their military and political dominance — not reputation or numbers, but the fact that Natharul can field two individually apex-tier metaphysical powers as instruments of state. The traditional "Hero" archetype — protector, chosen champion, wielder of the legendary spirit blade — is currently an elf serving Natharul's crown, which is a pointed subversion worth keeping deliberately visible in-world: the role meant to defend the world from catastrophe is currently in the service of the power most people would say the world needs defending _from_.

**Dark and conceptual severance — established direction:** Dark does not represent moral evil. It governs severance, absence, endings and the breaking of connections. Seralune's brother can use his single-edged elven sword to sever physical matter, a magical manifestation from the mana or causality sustaining it and, at full scale, abstract connections such as memory, identity, an oath, an Affinity or a soul-bond. Distance and ordinary cover do not protect a chosen target; the edge can continue across rooms, landscapes and even an ocean without losing force. The power only severs, ends, removes or disconnects. It cannot create, heal, repair, reveal hidden information or restore what has already been lost. Drawing the sword does not automatically release its full pressure. Thaeroval can make a narrow, controlled severance such as the Chapter 5 cut through Cyrandor's ward and arm without overwhelming everyone nearby. An unrestricted regional cut requires him to drive Dark through the exposed edge deliberately; black light consumes its reflections and the released pressure affects nearby bodies and machines before the stroke.

The blade's limiting price is human rather than physical. Full conceptual severance leaves Thaeroval standing without visible injury or exhaustion while permanently eroding his emotional connection to other lives. Centuries of use have reduced much of his ordinary warmth and compassion. Each unrestricted cut makes duty easier and human understanding harder. His attachment to Seralune remains deliberately protected for now; whether he could ever sacrifice that final attachment remains open. This cost must initially be demonstrated through changes in behaviour rather than explained in dialogue or narration.

Seralune's brother is Natharul's most powerful warrior and greatest living war hero, but he is not the formal Spirit-role called **the Hero**. He is a prince, bears the military title **First Blade of Natharul** and serves as the crown's living weapon. He appears approximately twenty-five to thirty and has not visibly aged during Seralune's thousand-year confinement. His father and the army use his loyalty and heroic identity to direct him. He obeys because protecting his family and kingdom has consumed nearly every identity he might have possessed outside being a hero.

His defining code is that he will not draw his full blade against anyone he considers too weak to survive it. This is genuine mercy and a serious flaw. He still divides the world according to strength and grants himself the authority to decide who requires protection. He can restrain, imprison and overrule the weak while telling himself he has not harmed them. His love for Seralune overrides even this code: he would not kill her, but he sealed her without consent because preserving her life mattered more to him than allowing her to choose it.

**Working direction for Time's traditional role:** undefined culturally, but functionally, Kozmagar's Time bearer is very likely one reason the country remains unconquered—foresight, stalled or undone invasions, or otherwise making any Natharul campaign there prohibitively costly or impossible to complete. This gives Kozmagar's continued independence a concrete causal reason rather than leaving it as an arbitrary gap in the map.

**Mydea's survival now has real teeth.** Housing the living Holy/Light bearer — a plausible direct counter or purifying force against Dark and Spirit — is very likely the actual reason Mydea survived eighty years past the original Conquest untouched. This reframes the open question of _why the Mydean Campaign happened twenty years ago, when it hadn't for eighty years before that_ — the strongest working answer is now: **something changed that made Natharul willing to risk confronting Holy directly**, where previously the cost had not been worth it. This should remain an explicit open question in-story (see Priorities, below), but it's now a load-bearing one with an obvious, satisfying shape once you're ready to answer it.

**Natharul's motive, sharpened:** "the elves want control of these" reads less like abstract supremacism and more like an ongoing, active campaign of consolidation — using two Veiled bearers already in hand to acquire, neutralise, or subordinate the remaining two. That gives Natharul's entire foreign policy a clear, single throughline (finish the set) rather than generalised conquest for its own sake, and it means Mydea and Kozmagar are not simply the last unconquered countries — they are, specifically, **the last two obstacles between Natharul and total possession of the Veiled Four.**

**What this means for Alaric.** He does not merely violate an abstract rule that "only one bearer of each Veiled affinity may exist." He is, right now, an undetected fifth party sitting on live, secret duplicates (or fragments, or something categorically different — still open) of Spirit and Dark, both of which Natharul already treats as instruments of state, and of Light, which Mydea's entire survival strategy depends on being unique and irreplaceable. If the truth of what he carries ever surfaces, every major faction in the setting—including Natharul, Mydea and Kozmagar—has an independent, entirely non-romantic reason to want him found, controlled, studied or destroyed. This should be held in reserve as a second, faction-driven engine of danger, separate from and initially unconnected to the Seralune bond, for use whenever the story needs pressure that has nothing to do with fate or love.

### Alaric's Exception

Alaric contains all four Veiled Affinities at once, dormant and locked within his soul — something impossible under the accepted natural order. His affinities are undetectable, and the four recognised living bearers — currently two elves (Spirit, Dark) serving Natharul, a human priestess (Light) in Mydea, and a beastfolk bearer of Time in Kozmagar, see _Magic and Affinity → The Living Bearers of the Veiled Four_ — apparently continue to exist while he carries duplicates, fragments, or something more fundamental. Neither Alaric nor the world initially knows what is inside him, or that what he secretly carries would make him a direct, undetected contradiction of four living bearers across three political powers.

**Resolved:** Why he carries all four and has almost no usable mana — see _The Ancient Sin_, below. His affinities protected his soul from a spell meant to kill him; the spell's failure to kill him is directly why he was displaced instead.

**Still open:**

- Are his affinities originals, duplicates, fragments, or something categorically different from those held by the four recognised bearers?
- What unlocks each affinity individually, and what does using one cost him?
- Is "only one bearer of each Veiled Affinity" a cosmic law, a religious belief, or an incomplete observation?

### Elemental Hierarchy

Affinity has shaped status, labour, law, warfare and racial prejudice. Magical strength is treated not merely as ability but as evidence of a person's value and capacity for self-rule.

Every child receives a preliminary Affinity indication at birth and a legally binding assessment at age ten. Mana capacity and Affinity strength are recorded separately. In Mydea, a Water assessment uses a marked silver cup: the child manifests water into it, and fixed measures determine whether the output reaches the Civic Threshold. Darcy produced only three drops, below the lowest measure, and was made to repeat the test three times before the other waiting children. The equivalent tests for other affinities remain open, as does whether results can be falsified, bribed or manipulated.

**Working social classification:**

|Class|Meaning|Typical status|
|---|---|---|
|**Exalted**|Extraordinary, nationally significant Affinity and usable mana|Royal, military or religious elite; treated as natural rulers|
|**Eminent**|Strong, reliable magical capability far beyond ordinary civic use|Officers, wealthy households, prestigious professions|
|**Common**|Usable Affinity sufficient for ordinary magical labour|Full legal citizens, tradespeople and soldiers|
|**Faint**|Affinity below the legally defined Civic Threshold|Placed into service, stewardship, debt bondage or compulsory labour|
|**No recognised classification**|No detectable『Affinity』at all|Believed impossible; no legal, magical or religious term exists. Gerolt privately calls Alaric **Empty**, and Marta later repeats the description.|

**Ruler** is not a fifth output rank above Exalted. It is the title given to the unique living bearer of one of the Veiled Four. Exalted describes measurable magical strength; a Ruler possesses metaphysical authority that cannot be expressed by the same scale.

Most states do not call the treatment of Faints slavery. Their legal doctrine claims that those unable to contribute sufficient magic to society's defence and maintenance must repay the protection supplied by stronger citizens through service. Under so-called **protective stewardship**, a Faint may be unable to own land, choose an occupation, travel without authorization, refuse an assigned custodian or give testimony equal to that of a stronger citizen. Wealthy families may retain a weak child by paying a prohibitive bond; poor families frequently lose theirs.

The hierarchy varies in form rather than moral foundation:

- **Natharul:** explicit magical caste law. Weak elves are servants or state dependants; conquered humans and beastfolk receive even fewer protections.
- **Mydea:** publicly distinguishes itself from Natharul while preserving the same hierarchy through protective stewardship, debt bondage, military necessity and compulsory state labour.
- **Conquered territories:** tribute and labour quotas allow local rulers to surrender weak citizens to satisfy Natharul demands.
- **Kozmagar's beastfolk societies:** reject inherited magical rank as a basis for personhood. Strength may earn situational respect, but weakness does not remove ownership of one's life.

The system requires a genuine middle class of Common-Affinity citizens. Not everyone is either ruler or slave. The horror lies in a spectrum accepted as natural, with every class encouraged to look downward.

**Affinity perception:** powerful or unusually sensitive magic-users can perceive the outward mana shape associated with another person's『Affinity』. This is not mind-reading and does not reveal exact strength, intent or hidden techniques. Magical exhaustion can diminish the signal, but even an unconscious or depleted person retains an identifiable shape. Alaric's complete lack of that presence therefore feels fundamentally different from a Faint or exhausted mage. Marta can recognise this absence through her Eminent Wind『Affinity』and experience; Silas notices it but initially mistakes it for exhaustion.

## Souls and the Soul World

### Established

- A soul is the person without the body.
- After death, a soul passes into the Soul World.
- Souls are eventually used to bring new people into the living world.
- Souls can be separated under exceptional circumstances, particularly involving the Veiled Four.
- Souls can be damaged, erased or destroyed.
- Ordinary reincarnation does not necessarily preserve conscious identity or accessible memory.
- **Alaric and Seralune were born at exactly the same moment as two halves of one divided soul.** They are nevertheless complete, unique people. Shared origin does not create consent, dictate morality or make their love automatic.
- **Elven royal/noble bloodlines age at a vastly slower rate than humans.** Seralune's family and certain noble childhood companions can therefore survive the millennium, though most ordinary people she knew cannot.
- **Seralune did not consciously live through the millennium.** She entered stasis at twenty and awakens physically and consciously twenty when Alaric returns. Her chronological age exceeds one thousand, but her lived age does not.

### Open Questions

- Why must every ordinary soul be tethered to an affinity?
- Who or what governs the movement of souls?
- Is the Soul World a place, a state of existence, or both?
- What determines a soul's next life, and how long does it remain in the Soul World?
- What is the difference between destroying a soul and erasing a person's existence from history?
- Can a damaged soul heal?
- What happens to a Veiled affinity when its bearer's soul is destroyed rather than naturally returned?

## Mydean Belief — The Eight, the Turning and the Last Dark

Widespread Mydean beliefs — not necessarily an accurate account of the universe. Other kingdoms and peoples hold their own explanations of magic, death and the soul; Natharul should not borrow Mydean language or theology.

### The Eight

Mydean religion recognises all Eight Affinities as the complete order of existence. The Common Four are invoked in everyday life; the Veiled Four are treated with greater solemnity or fear because their bearers are unique and rarely encountered.

**Favale** is worshipped in Mydea as the God of Light. Mydea's capital, the **Holy Capital of Favale**, bears the god's name and presents itself as the religious centre of Light worship. Whether every culture's accounts of the gods describe the same beings or historical forces remains open; the name and worship of Favale are established cultural facts.

- **"By the Four"** feels immediate, human, commonplace — used for the tangible world.
- **"By the Eight"** feels formal, sacred, unusually serious — temples, royal vows, funerals, sacred law.

### The Turning

The common belief that the dead pass through an unseen realm before returning to the world in another life. Ordinary Mydeans do not know the true nature of the Soul World; religious teaching may contain both truth and error.

### The Last Dark

The feared state of a soul cast outside the Turning, unable ever to return. Whether it truly exists is unknown to ordinary Mydeans — the belief unknowingly echoes the real possibility that a soul can be destroyed or erased entirely.

### Common Expressions

- **"By the Four"** — mild oath, surprise or alarm.
- **"Four preserve us"** — frightened appeal for protection.
- **"By the Eight"** — solemn invocation, uncommon in casual speech.
- **"Before the Eight"** — sacred, royal or funerary vow.
- **"The Last Dark take you"** — a severe curse.
- **"What in the Last Dark…"** — anger or bewilderment.

Use sparingly and naturally.

## The Ancient Sin — ESTABLISHED CORE, OPEN MECHANICS

### The Great Remaking and Alaric's Childhood — Author Only

The Great Remaking was an age of wars roughly one thousand years ago, when hostile countries unleashed enough magical power to destroy nations and remake the map. This destruction was already happening before Alaric and Seralune's combined power became a catastrophe; they did not originate every ancient war or ruin.

Alaric was born to human commoners. His mother was Faint, and his father died at a demon lord's hands during the Great Remaking. After elves destroyed his homeland, the queen of Natharul found him as a baby and took him into her household as her ward. He and Seralune grew up alongside one another. He was not adopted as a royal son or raised as her brother.

His mother's fate after the attack, the demon lord's identity, the queen's reason for being there and the precise household arrangements remain open. Her presence does not establish that she ordered or participated in the attack.

Alaric and Seralune later became part of a resistance-like movement and fought warbands. As their love unlocked the bonds between their powers, the resulting destruction spread beyond their intentions and understanding, destroying civilizations. Millions died; nations that had hated and fought one another united to stop them. Their original wish to resist violence does not erase those victims. The movement's organisation, campaign sequence and the final event that brought the alliance together remain undecided.

### Forbidden Relationship — Author Only

The king hated Alaric and Seralune's relationship, and Thaeroval disapproved. The queen knew they loved one another but feared the consequences of their running away together. Her fear of that departure is distinct from her later rejection of Seralune's sealing; neither secret assistance with the earlier escape nor a specific attempt to prevent it has been established.

The shared memory planned for Chapters 15–16 concerns their forbidden love: Alaric arrives late at Seralune's bedroom to discuss his departure, suggests she come with him, and finds that she wants to go although he does not yet know how to make it possible. That underlying history concerns her desire to be with him without status or wealth; the revised on-page fragment conveys warmth and familiarity without recovering that choice in dialogue. He is late because he hesitates outside her door: asking her to leave with him has become real, and he cannot promise safety or explain where they would go.

**Fragment-only reveal rule, revised 31 August 2026:** the selected Chapter 15 fragment is image-led: laughter, **“You're late again” / “I know”**, lamplight, the tapping window latch and Alaric's bare left palm against her cheek. He retains her face and knows she was an elf. The promise and departure discussion are not recovered, and he does not state that she loved him. Chapter 16 overlaps this same encounter without restoring those lines or revealing its missing context. The escape plan, family opposition, wardship, full relationship history and ancient catastrophe remain author-only. See [[Chapter 15 - Story Design]] and [[Chapter 16 - Story Design]] for the current staging and limits.

### The Rise of Alaric and Seralune

Alaric and Seralune were born at exactly the same moment as two halves of one divided soul. Separately, each appeared magically incomplete: Alaric carried all four Veiled Affinities without the mana required to use them, while Seralune possessed infinite mana with no Affinity through which to shape it.

Their love was freely chosen rather than created by this condition. When their souls willingly aligned, however, Seralune's mana found shape through Alaric. The resulting power exceeded what their human personalities could safely govern. Alignment disturbed nearby Affinities, bodies, souls and causality. Early incidents were accidental; people could die simply because a surge occurred near them. The world demanded permanent separation. Alaric and Seralune believed they could learn control and refused to surrender one another. Later disasters, persecution, racial hatred and their increasingly consequential choices transformed fear into war.

The ancient couple should not be completely innocent or simplistically evil. The coalition had legitimate reason to fear their uncontrolled union, while rulers also used that danger to defend the magical hierarchy and their own authority. Ancient Alaric may have made decisions the present amnesiac Alaric would reject. The exact disaster and choices that finally united the coalition remain open and must eventually be defined.

### The Architect

Thaeroval, Seralune's elder brother and the Dark bearer, already hated Alaric because he was human, because humans and elves had a long history of racial hatred, and because he believed Alaric had transformed Seralune into a danger to herself and the world. He was also Natharul's prince, greatest warrior and obedient royal weapon. His father, the king, and the army used his loyalty and heroic identity to direct him.

The brother found, designed or completed the forbidden ritual using Dark's authority over conceptual severance. His love for Seralune mattered more to him than his refusal to harm the weak. He accepted catastrophic collateral death because he believed deleting Alaric was the only remaining way to save his sister and the world. This choice is the central moral contradiction beneath his identity as a hero.

### The Erasure Ritual

The ritual was the most powerful spell ever attempted. It required the ancient bearers of all four Veiled Affinities and the mana of thousands of mages and warriors gathered around the battlefield.

**Working functional division:**

- **Light** exposed and isolated Alaric's existence so no part could remain hidden.
- **Spirit** anchored his soul and prevented ordinary death or reincarnation from becoming an escape.
- **Time** removed him from the present and every future attached to it.
- **Dark** severed his name, memory and place in existence.
- **Thousands of mages** supplied the raw mana necessary to impose the cut upon the world.

The ritual was intended to delete Alaric completely. Because Seralune appeared invulnerable while he lived and their souls remained aligned, the coalition believed erasing him would render her containable. The ritual's radius was enormous and its designers knowingly accepted that everyone close to Alaric would die as its price.

Alaric secretly carried the same four Veiled Affinities being used against him. They resisted the ritual from within his soul. The erasure of his name, records and place in collective memory largely succeeded, but the attempt to destroy him failed. The immense Time component displaced Alaric, the surrounding battlefield and everyone caught within the affected radius exactly one thousand years forward. Everyone else within that displaced ruin arrived dead.

The bodies, unknown banners, beastfolk and metallic war machines on the Silent Field are therefore ancient unless a later timeline-convergence mechanic is deliberately established. They should not currently be treated as ordinary Mydean Campaign equipment Gerolt ought to recognize.

### Seralune's Absence and Stasis

Seralune was running toward Alaric when the ritual struck. Their divided soul and protective alignment spared her from deletion and displacement. The perfect frost-rimmed circle on the Silent Field marks the place within the displaced battlefield where her body should have arrived but did not. It is the shape of a preserved absence.

The ritual rewrote Seralune's memory around Alaric's removal. She remembers her family, childhood, palace life and personal identity, but remembers neither Alaric nor the war. Her apparent continuity may contain unnaturally complete gaps: emotions without causes, places remembered without companions, and events whose surviving evidence contradicts the life she recalls.

After the ritual, she was catastrophically weakened. Her brother could seal her only because of that condition. He severed or suspended her waking consciousness and placed her into complete magical stasis without her consent. She did not experience the following millennium and did not age beyond twenty in body or lived consciousness.

Her brother's first defence upon her awakening is expected to be a selective truth: **she was dying or dangerously unstable, and he sealed her because it was the only way to save her.** The central lie is that it was the only choice and that saving her life granted him authority over it.

### The Dual Awakening

The instant Alaric re-enters the world on the Silent Field, the ancient severance begins to heal. Seralune's stasis breaks at exactly the same moment. Her brother is summoned by or senses the broken seal rather than waiting beside her.

Because Alaric was removed from collective memory, the brother does not consciously remember him. He remembers a massive war, Seralune's danger, the ritual and the belief that something had to be removed. Dark allows him to perceive that the old conceptual cut has healed, but not to name what returned. He may retain hatred, dread, bodily recognition or scars in his sword without access to their missing object.

The brother cannot simply repeat Seralune's seal. The first sealing depended upon her catastrophic weakness, the healed bond now resists the same separation, and he cannot bring himself to use his full blade against the sister he exists to protect. His hesitation becomes the eventual opening through which Seralune escapes.

### Still Genuinely Open

- What exact decision or catastrophe transformed fear of the couple into a united war?
- Did the ancient Light, Spirit and Time bearers knowingly accept the ritual's mass casualties?
- Why did the failed Time component choose exactly one thousand years?
- Did the brother have to erase his own memory of Alaric first in order to erase everyone else's?
- What permanent price did the brother pay for performing the conceptual cut?
- What does each modern culture now believe the ancient war was about?
- How much of Seralune's remembered personal history was deleted, and how much was rewritten into false continuity?
- Why does Alaric possess almost no usable mana: cosmic division, expenditure during the ritual, or both?

## Series Endgame — A World Without Magic

**Agreed author-only destination, 31 August 2026. This belongs to the conclusion of the entire series, not to any current volume ending.** The full relationship and thematic design is recorded in [[Story Questions and Brainstorming#Series Endgame — A World Without Magic|Story Questions and Brainstorming]].

Alaric builds an inseparable chosen family with Wena, Redd and Silas. Seralune's more tragic journey is sustained by her genuine friendship with Nereth. Ordinary affection, shared life and the companions' independent choices must earn the eventual sacrifice; their importance does not end when Alaric and Seralune reunite.

The couple eventually travels together, rediscovers its past and freely chooses love again despite being told they cannot be together. Their growing isolation from others is a dangerous phase to overcome, not the final ideal. Love for their friends makes the world's treatment of people according to inherited magical strength intolerable to them, and that concern extends to strangers and future generations.

**Intended outcome:** Alaric and Seralune surrender the lives they wanted and remove magic and『Affinity』from the living world. Their friends can choose their futures, and future children are no longer assigned magical worth at birth or condemned by the age-ten assessment. Everyone was already equally valuable; the sacrifice removes a basis of enforced hierarchy rather than creating human worth or automatically curing cruelty.

The final emotional image includes the companions' continuing lives and the couple's quiet reunion in the Soul World. Alaric and Seralune remain distinct people with their memories and love, able to be together without endangering anyone. Their sacrifice does not vindicate the prohibition upon their love, erase responsibility for earlier harm or undo their absence from their friends' lives.

**Still open:** why both deaths are necessary; the mechanism removing magic without destroying life or souls; protection for magical creatures and magic-dependent societies; Nereth's corruption; the friends' active contributions; affected people's agency; and how the reunion preserves two identities within established reincarnation rules. Severing souls' bindings to magical power is only a candidate mechanism. No chapter or volume outline is changed by this series-level direction.

## The World

The world is named **Lazaran**.

The three currently established countries are:

- **Mydea** — the human kingdom in which Alaric awakens.
- **Natharul** — the elven kingdom and dominant imperial power.
- **Kozmagar** — the country associated with beastfolk, demons and the unconquered territories shaped around Time.

**Continental geography:** Mydea and Natharul lie on separate continents, with sea between them. Travel between the two countries is not a continuous overland journey; Seralune's established route to Mydea crosses the sea.

### Natharul (Elves)

Natharul rule the world "with a fist of punishment" — not primarily through visible occupation, but through doctrine, reputation, and terror-enforced compliance. Most of the world lives under Natharul dominance without ever personally encountering an elf: through puppet nobility, tribute, decree, and the certainty of what happens to anyone who resists.

Natharul has **no capital city**. Its governing belief rejects the idea that one settlement should stand above or apart from the rest of the country: Natharul is presented as one indivisible whole, without an officially separate capital. This national unity is ideological as well as administrative and should distinguish Natharul from Mydea and Kozmagar.

**Core doctrine — the taboo against harming a Natharul:** Violence against any Natharul is answered with collective punishment, historically including the destruction of entire villages, bloodlines, or districts for a single act of resistance. This is not superstition or exaggeration — it is enforced, documented policy, and everyone in occupied or client territory knows it. It is the reason Gerolt's killing of one Natharul scout and maiming of another in Chapter 2 is not merely dangerous but potentially catastrophic for anyone connected to him — a thread that should be paid off later (has a reprisal already happened, off-page, by the time Alaric learns enough to ask?).

**Elven aging:** Royal and noble elven bloodlines age at a vastly slower rate than humans — a person who appears twenty may be centuries or a millennium old. This should be established as a known cultural fact (elves are long-lived) well before Seralune's specific age becomes relevant, so her eventual reveal recontextualizes existing worldbuilding rather than inventing a new rule to serve the twist.

**Natharul oaths (approved by Joshua, 22 Sept 2026; two or three per Natharul chapter at most):**

- **"Root and crown":** the oath of loyalty to the royal tree and house ("I swore root and crown").
- **"Before the Tree":** swearing truth, or a cry of shock or pleading.
- **"May your roots find stone":** a blessing on someone leaving or dying. Not yet used on the page.
- **"Rootless":** the worst insult, meaning traitor, exile or someone with no house.

**"Ruler":** the title for the unique living bearer of a Veiled Affinity. It first reaches readers in Ch 17, through Leorin. Mydean pilgrims call their Light bearer the church's "living saint" (Nyana, Ch 11: "The priests have a longer word"). "Ruler of Light" is to be planted before and in Favale.

### Mydea (Alaric's landing point)

Working direction: Mydea is a **nominally sovereign holdout kingdom** — one of the last, and possibly the last major one, to fall under direct Natharul pressure. Whatever kept it out of the original Conquest (below) for roughly eighty years — terrain, treaty, tribute, alliance, sheer inconvenience — either broke down or was deliberately ended by the Mydean Campaign twenty years ago. It now survives in a fragile, semi-sovereign state: not occupied outright, but living under the threat of what happened to it once already, twenty years ago, and could resume.

Mydea's capital is the **Holy Capital of Favale**, named for Favale, the Mydean God of Light. The capital is both a political seat and a religious declaration of Mydea's relationship with Light. The Great Library sought by Darcy is located there.

This is why armed Natharul scouts riding openly onto Mydean soil (Chapter 2) is shocking to Gerolt specifically — it isn't just personally dangerous, it's a potential violation of whatever fragile arrangement has kept a second, full-scale Natharul action off Mydean soil since the Campaign ended.

This creates two separate, non-aligned hunts converging on Alaric across Volume 1:

- **Natharul** wants him for what he may represent — an untraceable Affinity signature present at the site of a mass-casualty anomaly (the Silent Field).
- **Mydea** wants him found and handed over quietly, not out of loyalty to Natharul but out of terror that the killing of one scout and maiming of his partner on Mydean soil becomes Natharul's pretext to finish what the Campaign started twenty years ago.

Neither faction needs to know the true stakes (his soul, his affinities, Seralune) for this pressure to function as Volume 1's engine.

**Current frontier crisis:** renewed skirmishes between Natharul and Kozmagar's beastfolk have begun escalating into an elven move against Kozmagar. Natharul publicly describes the campaign as a defence of Mydea against beastfolk attacks. Mydea strengthens the frontier, secures the inland roads and supplies personnel and strategy to the elven advance. This is not an alliance between equals. Mydea cooperates because its rulers fear Natharul's wrath and hope usefulness will keep the elves from completing the conquest begun during the Mydean Campaign. Darcy's planned transfer to the frontier is part of this appeasement: the kingdom offers a captive strategist's mind while preserving the fiction that decorated Mydean officers produced her victories.

It handles grain, preserved food, timber, medicines, military equipment and passengers moving along Mydea's northern coast. White-and-gold sun marks appear throughout its harbour structures and public institutions. During the present frontier crisis, officials inspect foreign military cargo and record unlisted passengers before allowing them ashore. In Chapter 21, Seralune and Nereth enter Mydea there under the names **Sera** and **Nereth**, leaving an official record that can later be traced from Waluna.

### Kozmagar

Kozmagar is a distinct country rather than a generic name for every beastfolk territory. It possesses **three capitals**, each serving a different cultural and political function rather than submitting to one central seat.

1. **Zogkral** — the capital associated with demons, named for Zogkral, the God of Death.
2. **Aevar** — the central capital, named for Aevar, the God of Time.
3. **Unnamed trade capital** — a cosmopolitan city where all beastfolk are permitted to gather and trade. Humans and elves may also enter, but only under strict laws governing conduct and coexistence.

The third capital's name, exact laws, internal government and relationship to the other two capitals remain deliberately unresolved. It belongs to later arcs and should not be introduced into Volume One merely to explain the map.

Kozmagar's three-capital structure should not be treated as disorder. It reflects a different conception of unity: several recognised centres serving different peoples and purposes, in contrast with Mydea's single holy capital and Natharul's refusal to name any capital at all.

Kozmagar shelters the current beastfolk Ruler of Time. Natharul regards this bearer as an obstacle that must be eliminated rather than recruited. The exact identity, abilities and political relationship of the Ruler of Time remain unresolved. The bearer is one reason Kozmagar has resisted direct conquest, but that causal connection must remain protected until the story earns it.

### Inrandeel

Inrandeel is an enormous rainforest lying along one possible inland approach towards Favale. It is comparable to the world's largest tropical forests rather than an ordinary woodland. Dense cover, difficult navigation and dangerous inhabitants make crossing treacherous.

Known creatures include trolls, forest imps and other beasts capable of tracking travellers through vegetation. A skilled solitary traveller such as Silas can sometimes pass by concealing his movement and avoiding creatures he cannot defeat. A larger group, an injured traveller or a dog produces more sound, scent and disruption and is substantially harder to hide.

Inrandeel is not impassable and should not be treated as generically evil. Its danger comes from an ecosystem adapted to itself and indifferent to travellers. Its settlements, peoples, internal geography and relationship to the present Kozmagar campaign remain open.

### The Great Expanse

The **Great Expanse**, often shortened to **the Expanse**, is a vast inland region along another route towards Favale. Crossing it requires several days but brings travellers substantially closer to the Holy Capital. It is avoided because of scarcity, warped life and the remains of calamities no accepted history can adequately explain.

Modern people attribute the region to an unidentified ancient magical calamity. They do not know its true age, the names of the vanished civilisations or the number of cities once contained there.

The Expanse contains:

- barren stretches resembling empty desert;
- numerous nameless ruined cities;
- land altered by several visibly different forms of destruction;
- districts, foundations and ground removed as though they ceased to exist;
- surviving structures bent or dragged towards absent centres;
- vegetation reclaiming the land in warped and unnatural forms;
- altered light, sound, wind and colour;
- dangerous creatures changed by the region;
- an enormous central crater large enough to contain several cities.

The sand of the Expanse is not merely windblown. Residual mana moves it in vast, irregular flows capable of shifting roads, uncovering buried districts and carrying whole structures away from the places where travellers last saw them. Some buildings move slowly enough to be mistaken for failures of memory. Others are displaced across a route during a single period of rest. The effect grows more severe towards the centre. Maps record tendencies and surviving landmarks rather than permanent streets.

Prolonged exposure also distorts perception. Travellers hear voices where no speaker can be found, lose confidence in the direction of their own movement and may become convinced that hours of forward travel have carried them backwards. These experiences are produced by the overwhelming ancient mana saturating the region, but a present-day traveller cannot reliably distinguish a false sound from something living among the ruins. The voices do not provide trustworthy historical exposition.

At the centre lies a city-sized absence commonly described as a great sandfall: an empty crater large enough to contain a major city, with sand flowing continuously over its rim and disappearing below sight. The sand does not visibly accumulate or fill the void. Distortion, displacement and creature activity are strongest around this central wound. Its final public name remains open until it is established on-page; **the Great Sandfall** is the current preferred descriptive term.

Mana-warped humanoid creatures roam the deeper ruins after dark. Their posture and broken movement may resemble the walking dead, but their exact origin, biology and relationship to the people who vanished during the Great Remaking remain unresolved. They should receive a setting-specific form rather than reproduce the appearance or behaviour of conventional zombies. Daylight and the outer districts reduce their activity without making the Expanse safe.

The outskirts remain dangerous but comparatively stable. Settlements such as Foramen can survive where deep foundations, accessible water and less violent sand movement provide a defensible anchor. Movement and distorted perception intensify inward rather than affecting every part of the Expanse equally. Kurdag's routes rely upon persistent landmarks, differences in the sand and accumulated experience; they improve the chance of survival but cannot make a living landscape permanent.

Two colossal ancient figures stand approximately two days beyond Foramen upon Kurdag's western route. They face away from one another across a buried ceremonial avenue and rise high enough to be mistaken for distant cliffs before their forms become visible. Both were carved directly from the underlying bedrock, preventing the shifting sand from relocating them. Their damaged features no longer reveal whom they represented. They are surviving measures of the lost civilisation's scale and one of the route's few dependable points of orientation.

The Great Expanse remains traversable when approached cautiously by someone familiar with its margins. Its threats differ from Inrandeel: the forest conceals abundant living hunters, while the Expanse exposes travellers to scarcity, moving geography, perceptual distortion and things reshaped by the mana left inside abandoned civilisation.

**Author-only origin:** many of these civilisations were destroyed during the Great Remaking approximately one thousand years ago. Some of the destruction was caused directly or indirectly by the uncontrolled combined existence of ancient Alaric and Seralune. Present characters must not infer this from atmosphere, and Alaric experiences no automatic memory or supernatural recognition merely by entering the region.

The mana saturating the Expanse originated primarily from Seralune's uncontrolled overflow as it passed through the powers available through her bond with ancient Alaric. Because present Alaric and Seralune remain parts of the same divided soul, the residual mana affects Alaric less severely than ordinary travellers. He is resistant, not immune. The difference should appear through comparative experience—he retains direction or recognises a distortion before his companions—not through an explanation, effortless mastery or complete protection.

Silas crossed the Great Expanse in the past by keeping to the outlying ruins. ### Foramen — the Free Settlement

One enormous ruined city in the Great Expanse has lost its original name. Approximately half was removed from existence rather than collapsed, leaving roads, rooms, foundations and walls terminating at perfectly smooth boundaries without corresponding rubble. The surviving half has endured roughly one thousand years of decay and warped growth.

The present inhabitants call their settlement **Foramen**, a Kozmagari word brought by Kurdag meaning an opening through which something survives. Roughly two hundred people have occupied a defensible surviving district for only a few years. Distant travellers may mistake their fires for scavengers or bandits.

Foramen shelters escaped Faints, humans, a small number of elves, at least one beastman and several stronger people who joined voluntarily. The community rejects official Affinity classifications and does not subject children to compulsory age-ten testing. Strong magic is taught without granting superior status.

Residents build brown, open-roofed homes inside surviving shells. They import soil from beyond the Expanse and cultivate it in sheltered courtyards. Weak Earth, Water, Fire and Wind users combine limited abilities with ordinary labour: maintaining soil and walls, filling cisterns over time, preserving ovens and ventilating enclosed ruins. They trade goods and labour internally rather than using ordinary coin. Medicine and secrecy remain scarce.

A small council maintains food, shelter and security. **Kurdag**, an approximately fifty-year-old grey-white wolf beastman with an Exalted Earth『Affinity』, is its most influential voice. A rogue demon lord's incursion in Kozmagar killed his five-year-old daughter; a Faint woman saved his life and changed his understanding of strength. Kurdag carries a metal-banded staff, uses his arid-land knowledge to sustain the settlement and refuses to treat his power as a claim to ownership.

**Orieth**, an approximately forty-five-year-old elven woman with a Faint Wind『Affinity』, also serves on the council. She escaped enslavement in Natharul, bears whip scars and has lost half her right ear. Her unnamed four- or five-year-old daughter lives freely in Foramen.

Warped creatures dominate the ruins beyond Foramen after dark but do not enter the inhabited district. The residents possess conflicting explanations and do not know the truth. **Author-only:** the residual mana is uneven. A comparatively stable remnant of Seralune's ancient mana around Foramen repels or disturbs creatures reshaped by the more chaotic flows beyond it. Do not allow present characters to identify that protection or infer who helped destroy the ancient city.

Foramen's inhabitants do not know the city's true age or the origin of its missing half. They avoid deeper exploration, know the enormous central crater exists and report that the Expanse has recently become more dangerous.

### Two Historical Layers: the Conquest and the Campaign

Natharul's dominance over the known world is not a single, recent event — it has two distinct historical layers, and the gap between them matters.

**The Natharul Conquest (~100 years before the story's present).** The war, or series of wars, in which Natharul subjugated most of the known world — humans and beastfolk alike — establishing the "fist of punishment" order that now defines ordinary life almost everywhere. This is deep enough in the past that essentially no one alive remembers a world before it; Natharul rule is not a recent imposition to the living generation, it is simply how the world has always worked, in the same way a hundred-year-old empire feels permanent to those born under it. Only the very old, if anyone, would carry any personal memory of what came before.

**The Mydean Campaign (~20 years before the story's present).** A second, later, and more specific Natharul action — the conquest or subjugation attempt turned against Mydea in particular, roughly eighty years after the original Conquest had already settled the rest of the world. This is recent enough to sit within living adult memory, and it is the formative trauma of the current generation, not an inherited, secondhand one. This is the war that burned Redd's village and killed his family when he was eight.

**Established distinction:** the seamless silver war machines on the Silent Field are ancient. They arrived with Alaric and the displaced battlefield from one thousand years ago; they are not Mydean Campaign equipment. Present-day characters may initially misidentify them or draw false comparisons because the true history is unavailable, but author-level notes must keep the two eras separate.

**Gerolt's generation** would have been born and raised entirely under the old, already-total Natharul order (the Conquest predates him by decades), but would carry the Mydean Campaign itself as a comparatively recent, personal memory — not distant doctrine, but something he plausibly lived through as an adult. That sharpens his reaction in Chapter 2 even further: "riding openly through Mydean fields… they shouldn't be here, not ever" isn't abstract outrage at a system he's only ever known secondhand, it's the specific, remembered horror of a war he watched happen once already, now potentially returning.

**Irony worth preserving for later, not explaining early:** a thousand years ago, "the whole world" united _across_ human, elf and beastfolk lines to stop Alaric and Seralune's love. A hundred years ago, elves conquered and subjugated those same allied peoples. Whatever unity existed to stop two people in love did not survive a shared civilization for even a fraction of that time. Do not resolve why yet.

## Alaric

### Identity

- **Name:** Alaric. **Author-only meaning:** approximately "ruler of all." Never explain this in-story — the irony beside _The Ruler of Nothing_ should emerge for attentive readers, not be stated.
- **Race:** human.
- **Birth:** ordinary, loving human family, approximately 1,020 years before the story begins. Born at precisely the same instant as Seralune; both are physically twenty when the ancient ritual occurs one thousand years before the present.
- **Parents:** human commoners; his mother was Faint. His father was killed by a demon lord during the Great Remaking. His mother's subsequent fate remains undecided.
- **Upbringing:** after elves destroyed his homeland, Natharul's queen found him as a baby and brought him into her household as her ward. He grew up alongside Seralune, but was not the queen's adopted son or Seralune's adopted brother. Wardship does not make him a prince.
- **Current condition:** displaced one thousand years into the future with no autobiographical memory, as a direct consequence of the spell described in _The Ancient Sin_.

### Appearance

Dark-brown, naturally wavy hair; green eyes; fair/light complexion; lean young-adult build; appears roughly twenty. Awakens in unfamiliar clothing — soft, smooth, precisely stitched, fitted, with no tears or punctures despite the blood covering it. To Gerolt, recognisable as clothing but unlike anything he's encountered. Alaric's design should stay grounded — he could disappear into an ordinary crowd rather than read as a visibly marked hero.

**Author-only clothing origin:** the clothes were made in ancient Natharul at almost royal quality and were given to Alaric by Seralune before the erasure. Darcy may later recognise Natharul workmanship and an obsolete high-court construction, but she cannot identify Seralune as the giver from the clothing alone. The reason the garments survived the millennium, battlefield and river without tearing remains mechanically unresolved and should not be explained early.

### Opening State

Awakens in Gerolt's wheat field among the remains of the Silent Field. Remembers language and the meaning of ordinary things, but nothing personal. Gerolt temporarily calls him **Empty**, since no『Affinity』can be detected in him. His name reaches him through a fragmented, agonising vision at the end of Chapter 2; Gerolt is the first person in the new age to speak it back to him.

### Core Character

An ordinary person at the centre of extraordinary events. Frightened by danger, affected by violence — never helpless, childish, or cowardly. Courage means acting while afraid, not failing to feel fear. His defining quality is the ability to choose: whether to remain compassionate, whether to trust, whether to stand beside someone, what kind of person to become.

Early evidence: staying silent when calling out might endanger Gerolt; warning Gerolt during the attack despite exposing himself; feeling the moral weight of death rather than treating violence as spectacle; accepting help without surrendering his judgment; continuing to seek answers even when they hurt.

### Essential Guardrails

- Not a hidden tactical prodigy. No supernatural danger detection. Amnesia does not conceal effortless combat mastery.
- May learn practical skills over time, but growth must be earned on-page.
- Dormant affinities must not invalidate the importance of his ordinary decisions.
- Not morally important because fate selected him — fate gives him circumstances; his choices give those circumstances meaning.
- Must not become emotionally passive once Seralune enters the story.

### Magical Condition

Carries all four Veiled Affinities — Light, Dark, Time, Spirit — dormant and undetectable, in violation of the "one bearer per affinity" rule. Almost no usable mana (mechanism for _why_ still to be chosen deliberately — see Ancient Sin section). Powers begin locked; reveal gradually through consequence, never exposition.

**Accelerated recovery — established effect, open mechanism:** as the damaged soul connection between Alaric and Seralune gradually reopens, Alaric's natural healing begins proceeding substantially faster than expected. The connection is not yet complete or willingly aligned, so recovery still requires time; wounds do not vanish instantly and the effect does not restore stamina, experience or combat skill. Alaric, Silas and Redd initially attribute the improvement to rest and Marta's treatment. Whether the effect is caused by faint Time leakage, shared vitality or another property of the divided soul remains unresolved. No viewpoint narration may identify Seralune as the cause before the characters possess evidence.

**Chapter 18 development:** Alaric begins carrying the wrapped ancient core in a satchel against his clothing. The core remains dormant after Chapter 15 and must not function as a repeatable memory device. Silas gives him a worn spare sword and begins teaching conventional fundamentals. Alaric starts badly, particularly when required to commit to a strike, and improves only through repeated practice. Redd naturally shortens his name to **Al**. Alaric accepts the nickname as part of his present life; Silas does not adopt it immediately.

### Internal Conflicts

- Identity versus inheritance: is he obligated to become who he was before?
- Choice versus fate: if the universe designed his bond with Seralune, can any decision between them feel free?
- Ordinariness versus power: how does he keep his humanity once he learns what's inside him?
- Love versus responsibility: what happens when protecting Seralune conflicts with protecting everyone else?
- Memory versus accountability: can he be responsible for a version of himself he cannot remember — including, eventually, a version of himself whose love started a war?

### Long-Term Arc

Begins with nothing — no name, no history, no accessible power, no place in the world. Others will try to define him through prophecy, ancient history, his affinities, and his bond with Seralune. His arc is the creation of a chosen identity, not merely the recovery of a lost one. The central question isn't only _who was Alaric_, but _who will he decide to be now_ — including, eventually, whether he wants to become the man whose love once justified a war against the world.

### Open Development Questions

- What did Alaric want before the ancient catastrophe? What was his relationship with his original family?
- What personal flaw existed before his memories were removed?
- Which qualities survive the loss of memory, which must be rebuilt?
- What would make the new Alaric reject the wishes of his former self?
- What does he want that has nothing to do with Seralune?
- When does he first choose responsibility despite having the option to walk away?
- What would he refuse to do even to save Seralune?
- What does "ruling" eventually mean to him?
- How does he respond when people value his power more than his personhood?

## Seralune

### Identity

- **Name:** Seralune. **Race:** elf, royal bloodline of Natharul.
- **Chronological age:** over one thousand years. **Physical and conscious age:** exactly twenty. She spent the intervening millennium completely unconscious in stasis.
- **Birth:** born at exactly the same moment as Alaric, as the second half of their divided soul.
- **Position:** princess of the High Kingdom of Natharul.
- **Father:** the reigning king of Natharul, still alive in the present age.
- **Mother:** the estranged queen of Natharul, alive but absent after fleeing the kingdom in horror at Seralune's sealing.
- **Elder brother:** Thaeroval, Natharul's Dark bearer, most powerful warrior and principal protector/captor.
- **Cousin:** Leorin, head of Natharul's royal court in the present age.
- **Initial condition:** awakens at the exact moment Alaric returns, with no conscious experience of the millennium and no memory of Alaric or the ancient war.

Seralune remembers her childhood, family and apparently ordinary personal life from one thousand years ago. The erasure has rewritten continuity around Alaric's absence. She initially has no idea that there was a war, that she loved anyone, that her brother designed the ritual or that she was sealed without consent.

Seralune's mother opposed the decision to seal her. After her husband authorised the act and Thaeroval carried it out, the queen abandoned Natharul rather than remain beside them. She is still alive, but her present location and activities remain unresolved author-level questions. Seralune wakes believing her mother should still be in the palace and does not initially know that her sealing broke the royal family apart.

### Appearance

Long black hair, green eyes, sharply pointed elven ears, extremely fair complexion, slender young-adult build, appears roughly twenty. Beautiful and distinctly elven without reading as emotionally inhuman. Her design can carry the elegance expected of a Natharul princess, but clothing, posture and surroundings should also communicate restriction — she has been treated as a royal object, anomaly or danger, not allowed to develop freely.

### Core Character

Not simply Alaric's lost lover or the source of his future conflict — an independent protagonist with her own desires, frustrations, loyalties and moral boundaries that exist before he re-enters her life.

Her life is shaped by contradiction: the greatest magical capacity in known history, and no natural affinity to express it through — simultaneously invaluable and, by her society's hierarchy, fundamentally defective. She is chronologically ancient but experientially twenty. The people she loves have lived a millennium without her, while she experienced no passage at all.

Her eventual antagonism should grow from understandable choices, not a sudden turn to evil. Readers should understand the wound, belief, or love driving her while still recognising the harm she chooses to cause.

**Established Volume One wants:** truth, freedom and eventual reform of Natharul. Seralune despised cruelty and the treatment of weak people before stasis, but she must discover what Natharul has become during her absence rather than enter the present with complete political knowledge. Her immediate objective is to understand why she was sealed and escape before her brother can contain her again.

Her greatest early virtue is her refusal to accept that another person is an object. Its dangerous extension is her willingness to override another person's stated wishes when she believes she knows how to protect or free them. She has learned this model of love from her brother.

### Essential Guardrails

- Remains a co-protagonist even after becoming the antagonist.
- Her actions must retain personal agency — possession or fate should not excuse every choice.
- Must possess goals beyond loving, finding, or avenging Alaric.
- Her suffering explains her decisions but does not automatically justify them.
- Should not become unstoppable without meaningful limitations, costs, or consequences.
- Her love for Alaric must arise from knowing and repeatedly choosing him — once she has the chance to — not merely from a divided soul she didn't consent to.
- Her perspective should sometimes reveal truths Alaric cannot see, while remaining capable of its own bias and self-deception.
- Her first resistance must not resemble trained infiltration. She does not count guards or acquire sudden tactical competence upon awakening; agency begins through questions, observation, withheld trust and the decision to leave.

### Magical Condition

Infinite, inexhaustible mana; no natural elemental affinity of her own. On her own: limitless fuel, nothing to shape it with. When her soul willingly aligns with Alaric's, her mana can flow through his locked Veiled Affinities, potentially letting her wield Light, Dark, Time and Spirit upon herself and the world — Time, notably, may allow her to prevent or reverse physical injury, making her extraordinarily difficult to overcome once the bond is active. Mutual willingness enables the union; neither fate nor power creates the emotional relationship itself. Costs and limitations still need definition.

Without Alaric, Seralune may be able to supply mana to an already shaped enchantment or overload a structure whose runes provide form, but she cannot independently create elemental effects. Whether this mechanism is kept must be decided before her escape is drafted.

**Volume 1 note:** any soul-contact in Volume 1 must be involuntary and momentary, not the willing union described above — see _Volume 1 Closing Beat_, below. Save the full bonded power, and the mortality cost of true union, for a much later, deliberately chosen turning point.

**Established lore (Joshua, 22 Sept 2026):** Seralune's mana is designed to connect truly only with Alaric. Every other connection it makes is corrupted and produces inconsistent, harmful results rather than controllable magic; Nereth's corruption is the example. Clean, right-feeling resonance belongs only to the bond. **On the page:** in Ch 16, for one heartbeat, her mana pours in one direction, "somewhere inland in the dark", at the moment Alaric lifts the orb. Nereth's scars flare at the same moment; she hides her hand under the blanket and says nothing. In Ch 17 the corrupted old channels under Natharul "ring" that same night. This is why, in Favale, the tear reaches Alaric: the one true connection pulling through a world of corrupted ones. **The rule for Nereth (Joshua, Ch 24):** Seralune's feelings feed Nereth's corruption. It flares when her mana surges (fear, grief, anger) and eases when she's calm. Counting ("One breath first") helps. **Ch 24:** when a pilgrim mob seizes Nereth, Seralune's terror sends her mana out through every hand touching them. It goes into Nereth's corruption and out into the fists holding her, and into the hands on Seralune herself. The kind thatcher Osric's own Fire turns inward (orange with a white heart, black-purple veins up his arms) and burns him from the inside while his seven-year-old daughter Wren watches. A Water woman coughs up water; an Earth man breaks his leg when the road splits under him. Seralune now knows her power corrupts whatever it reaches, and ends the chapter holding herself still and counting all night, rebuilding her locked room around herself.

### Internal Conflicts

- Duty versus autonomy: daughter and princess, or a person allowed to choose her own life?
- Worth versus usefulness: does anyone love her, or only what her power might become?
- Love versus possession: can she love Alaric without treating him as the missing part of herself?
- Justice versus vengeance: at what point does correcting the world's cruelty become punishing the world for hurting her?
- Fate versus consent: if their souls were designed to reunite, how can she know whether Alaric freely chooses her?
- Salvation versus domination: can she remake an unjust world without denying everyone else the right to choose?
- Identity versus erasure: what does she owe the version of herself whose love, war and choices were removed from memory?
- Protection versus consent: can she reject her brother's benevolent captivity without later imposing her own idea of salvation upon others?

### Long-Term Arc

Begins newly awakened, imprisoned and defined by the crown's fear and selective truths. Volume One takes her from awakening to escape. She discovers that her brother genuinely saved her life but denied her ownership of it, that her mother rejected the sealing and fled, and that the kingdom she remembers has hardened into an oppressive empire. Her later power lets her reject every institution that controlled her, but freedom without restraint can become domination. Her antagonist arc should preserve the painful possibility that she correctly identifies the world's injustices while choosing an increasingly destructive answer.

## Their Divided Soul

Alaric and Seralune were born at the exact same instant as two halves of one divided soul. Alaric's half received the four Veiled Affinities with almost no mana; Seralune's half received infinite mana with no Affinity. This division explains their magical compatibility but does not make either person incomplete. Each possesses a complete will, personality, moral responsibility and capacity to form relationships independent of the other.

Their ancient love was freely chosen. Their present-day love, if it returns, must be chosen again between the people they become now. Fate repeatedly creates pressure and opportunities for contact, but cannot dictate trust, consent or allegiance. The revelation that they loved before should complicate their relationship rather than validate it automatically.

Alaric's re-entry into the present reopens the ancient connection strongly enough to wake Seralune, but Volume One contact remains involuntary and fragmentary. Neither should understand that the other exists during the opening movement.

> Fate may ensure that they meet. It cannot decide what they do once they have.

### Central Relationship Questions

- Would they still choose each other if their power disappeared?
- Does either fear the connection manufactures their feelings?
- What do they admire in each other beyond magical compatibility?
- What beliefs place them in genuine opposition?
- When does Alaric first refuse Seralune for her own sake?
- When does Seralune first choose Alaric's freedom over her desire to keep him?
- Can they love each other while fighting over the fate of the world?
- Once Alaric learns that a version of himself already loved her once, at terrible cost — does that make loving her again feel like fate, or like a warning?

## Supporting Cast — Volume 1

### Thaeroval — Seralune's Elder Brother / First Blade / Dark Bearer

Elven prince, **First Blade of Natharul**, Natharul's most powerful warrior and greatest living war hero. He appears approximately twenty-five to thirty and has not visibly aged across the millennium. He is not the formal Spirit-role called the Hero; he is a hero by reputation, conduct and the identity imposed upon him by the crown. His father and the army use him as a living weapon, and he obeys from loyalty to family and kingdom.

He deeply and genuinely loves Seralune. He disapproved of her forbidden relationship with the human ward raised in the queen's household. One thousand years ago he hated Alaric because Alaric was human, because he believed the human corrupted or radicalised his sister, and because the couple's uncontrolled union killed people. He designed or completed the erasure ritual, accepted its mass casualties and later sealed Seralune while she was weak. He regards these acts as the terrible choices required to save her and the world.

Dark grants authority over severance and absence. His single-edged elven sword can sever physical matter, spells, sustaining magic and abstract connections. Distance and ordinary cover offer no protection, and an unrestricted cut may continue across an entire landscape. Drawing the weapon and releasing its unrestricted edge are separate acts: controlled cuts create no regional pressure, while a full release visibly carries Dark into the blade and affects nearby bodies and mechanisms before the stroke. The sword can only sever; it cannot create, heal, repair, reveal or restore. Full severance permanently erodes Thaeroval's emotional connection to other lives while leaving him physically unharmed. He will not draw its full edge against anyone he considers too weak to survive it. This code is sincere but paternalistic: he believes the strong must protect the weak, while Alaric will eventually argue that strength should not determine who has the right to choose.

His code remains sincere but does not prevent cruelty. In Chapter 5, he gives Cyrandor one opportunity to release the life-anchored barrier, then severs the elderly servant's anchored arm below the elbow from across the passage. He deliberately chooses a survivable cut instead of Cyrandor's head or chest. The effortless amputation establishes both the terrifying reach and precision of Thaeroval's sword and the paternalism beneath his heroism: he refuses to kill a weaker man while granting himself complete authority to decide how much pain and mutilation protection justifies.

When Seralune awakens, the brother remembers the war and the act of erasure but not Alaric. He senses that an ancient conceptual cut has healed. His first response combines overwhelming relief, terror and the intention to protect Seralune by containing her again. When pressed in Chapter 4, he honestly tells her that one thousand years passed. He withholds what happened during that interval because he genuinely believes her missing memories are bound to the ancient disaster and that forcing them back while her mana remains unstable could endanger everyone nearby; the same silence also protects his guilt. She escapes not by defeating him but because he hesitates to use the force required to stop the person he loves most.

**Inserted Chapter 17 development:** on the third night after Seralune leaves Waluna, Thaeroval questions the maimed Cyrandor and learns that the queen wanted Seralune beyond Natharul's control. A missing lily signet directs his investigation towards Waluna's ships without giving him Seralune's complete route. The king returns from the Silent Field, identifies the armies as belonging to the Great Remaking and orders Thaeroval to recover and reseal Seralune by any means necessary. Thaeroval accepts the recovery and resealing but does not answer the order to kill her if containment fails.

During the same chapter, the intelligent source of the aqueduct corruption emerges upon Natharul's southern terraces and drives hundreds of soldiers to kill one another by intensifying existing fear, grief and suspicion. Thaeroval ends it with one unrestricted cut that divides the creature, every connected root, the southern mountain, the sea, the seabed and the clouds beyond. The returning water strikes Waluna's outer headland before a reduced surge reaches the harbour, damaging piers and storehouses, injuring civilians and drowning six people in the lower harbour: a fishing family including a seven-year-old, and two dock hands (rewrite pass, 22 Sept 2026). The attack leaves Thaeroval physically untouched while permanently diminishing his instinctive compassion for strangers. He conceals evidence that Seralune's lower wards fed the corruption, accepts a seal-derived restraint from the king and leaves alone for Waluna aboard a small royal aircraft.

Severing the intelligent source ends its control of the connected roots and corrupted fauna but does not purge corruption already established inside a living body. The soldier removed from the terrace with black-purple veins remains infected and isolated after the source dies, following the same principle established by Nereth's continuing condition.

**Rewrite-pass canon (Ch 17, 22 Sept 2026):** the old channels under Natharul "rang" in the middle watch, "like something had called down them". It was the same night as the Ch 15–16 shared memory, when Seralune's mana found a direction (author-only: the corrupted connections reacting to the true one). Told that six drowned at Waluna ("The youngest was seven"), Thaeroval asks only "Is the eastern pier standing?" Leorin now suspects him of concealing the lower-ward report, and is the first to put the word "Ruler" in front of readers: "And when Mydea asks why a Ruler of Natharul has crossed into the country of another Ruler, uninvited?" This sets up the Holy bearer's protection at the end of Volume One as the one place Thaeroval can't reach without war. Approved Natharul oaths are first used in this chapter (see Natharul).

**Ch 27 (reported by Nereth):** late for council, he once walked straight through the palace drying yard on a washing day and came out with a wet pillowcase on his head, then folded it, corners and all, and handed it to Maerith, the head laundress, who kept it in a drawer to show new girls ("A prince folded that"). Nereth also tells Seralune that numbness makes her "sound like your brother… Yes. No. Just like that" (the flatness severance has left in him).

**Ch 29 ("Against the Vigil"):** he reaches Favale by the eve of the Longest Light (how is hidden) and waits in the ruin of the lily house, knowing she will come. He knocks twice on its lone doorpost. He admits he knew of the Natharul warrant twenty years ago and did not ride with it or stop it ("I let a great many things burn. This was one of the smaller ones"). He knows from Nyana that Nereth needs a healer. He calls her only "Seralune", never the "Sera" he read at Meren. When she runs, he says "So you have chosen force, then", **loses control for one breath**, and punches Nereth through a house wall with his fist (no sword, no Dark); an unknown old woman inside dies. He masks it at once. He accepts her surrender with "I'll carry her", sends the watch away with a look, splints Nereth's arm and covers her with his cloak, then washes his knuckles in the shrine basin long after they are clean. Asked whether they will let him on the stair: "No one will stop me." At dawn he carries Nereth towards the stair.

**Volume One function:** Seralune's protector, captor and primary personal antagonist. He should remain emotionally sympathetic and survive her escape. His pursuit must arise from genuine fear for Seralune and the world rather than simple possessiveness.

### The King of Natharul — Seralune's Father

**Ancient relationship:** he hated Seralune's forbidden love for Alaric, the human ward raised in the queen's household. His attitude towards Alaric's childhood residence and the terms under which he tolerated it remain undecided.

Still alive and reigning in the present age. He authorised Seralune's sealing and continues to use Thaeroval's loyalty, strength and heroic identity as instruments of the crown. He regards Seralune as both his daughter and a destabilising magical danger, but when those identities conflict, royal security wins.

At the beginning of Chapter 4, the king is absent from the palace. Eldrin and Liluth's patrol sent a preliminary report about the Silent Field before approaching Gerolt's cabin. The king departed to inspect the impossible battlefield personally, leaving the crisis created by Seralune's awakening in the hands of Thaeroval and Leorin. Whether he has already been informed that she is awake remains protected from the reader.

### The Queen of Natharul — Seralune's Mother

**Ancient household:** she rescued the infant Alaric after the destruction of his homeland and raised him as her ward, not her adopted son. She later knew he and Seralune loved each other, but feared them running away together. No secret escape arrangement or specific intervention by her is established.

Alive but estranged from Natharul. She was appalled by the decision her husband and Thaeroval made to seal Seralune. After the sealing, she fled the kingdom rather than accept or legitimise what they had done. Her current location, loyalties and life during the intervening millennium remain unresolved.

Her absence should initially appear to Seralune as another impossible gap. Thaeroval cannot answer “Where is Mother?” without exposing both the scale of elapsed time and the damage his decision caused. The queen's departure preserves the truth that Seralune was not unanimously abandoned by everyone she loved.

Before fleeing, the queen placed a private instruction into the hands of a very small group of household loyalists: if Seralune ever awakened, they were to prevent the court from containing her again and preserve a path by which she could seek her mother. The queen did not leave a permanent exact location. The surviving route begins with a contact or trace in Favale, Mydea's Holy Capital, forcing Seralune towards the human kingdom she was raised to distrust. This is a starting point for the search, not confirmation that the queen currently lives there.

### The Queen's Order

A small, secret and generational chain established by the queen after Seralune was sealed and before the queen abandoned Natharul. It is not a large resistance movement or an organisation capable of opposing the crown directly. Its strength comes from patience, obscurity and duties distributed among people the royal court overlooks.

Because most ordinary elves do not live for a millennium, the queen's command passed through families and chosen successors. No present ordinary member personally received the original order from the queen. Each generation preserved only what its role required:

- watch for signs that Seralune had awakened;
- maintain access to old servant routes beneath the palace;
- preserve the queen's private signet and identifying phrase;
- keep the first link in a route leading towards Mydea;
- prevent the knowledge from dying with any single keeper.

The Order does not possess the complete truth of the Ancient Sin, Alaric, the erasure ritual or Seralune's condition. Compartmentalised knowledge allowed it to survive, but also means its members can offer direction rather than a clean explanation.

Nereth is **not** secretly a member. Her decision to help Seralune belongs to her own conscience and occurs only after the awakening.

**The Favale keepers (Ch 27, "The Secrets We Hide"):** the Mydean end of the route was **a human family** in a house at the top of the Lily Steps in Favale's old quarter. They kept "an elf's secret" for generations, mother to daughter and father to son, and carved the queen's **closed lily** on their wall so whoever came would know the house. **In the last summer of the Mydean Campaign (about twenty years ago)**, riders in pale armour came under a white flag with a warrant bearing the royal tree. The temple read it on the steps and stood aside. **All seven came out on their own feet** (the grandmother first; three children, the smallest carried in her mother's arms) and were **taken alive, north**. The riders burned the house and sat their horses in the street until the roof fell in, so nothing could be carried out. "That autumn, the war ended. Some said that was the price of the peace." The temple struck the family's name from the rolls, built a small sun shrine in the ruin "to make the ground clean", and left the lily stone so people would ask. An old priest has kept the shrine nineteen years and tells the story as a lesson ("Secrets don't keep in the Light"). **Nothing of the queen's is left.** Author-only hint, never stated on the page: the queen's trail was one of the Campaign's aims (part of "why Mydea, why then"). Whether any of the seven still live in Natharul is open (Volume Two).

### Cyrandor — Keeper of the Queen's Order

An elderly ordinary elf employed within the palace's domestic service, first seen carrying folded linen in Chapter 4. He falls to his knees and cries upon recognising Seralune because his family preserved the queen's command across generations and he has spent his life uncertain whether the duty would ever become real.

Cyrandor never personally knew the queen. He inherited the role of current keeper from an earlier generation. His apparent insignificance is his protection: the court sees an ageing linen servant where the Order sees the holder of a key, a route and one unfinished command.

He possesses no hidden mastery equal to Gerolt or Thaeroval. He neutralises the guards outside Seralune's room through a prepared sleeping draught, knowledge of the relief rotation and the access granted by service work. His practical tools are keys, servant passages, timing and the habit powerful people have of failing to notice those beneath them.

In Chapter 5, Cyrandor gives Seralune an unmarked cloak, proves his connection to her mother and names Favale, the Holy Capital of Mydea, as the beginning of the queen's surviving trail. This inherited lead is Seralune's own source for the destination; she has no knowledge of Darcy's separate instruction to Alaric or of Helmi. Thaeroval has already confirmed the thousand-year interval; Cyrandor does not call her chamber a prison or explain the ancient sealing. His task is to create a choice and a direction, not deliver the truth of the story.

Cyrandor knowingly places himself between Thaeroval and Seralune at the hidden southern stair. He uses a limited, life-anchored binding that cannot defeat Natharul's strongest warrior but can buy seconds. Thaeroval orders him to release it; Cyrandor refuses. With a slight movement of his sword from across the passage, Thaeroval severs Cyrandor's anchored arm below the elbow. The detached hand remains closed around the blackened key as the broken connection collapses the barrier.

Cyrandor survives the amputation and is left behind for capture. Seralune sees that he is alive and chooses to continue after he silently refuses rescue, respecting the choice he created for her rather than wasting it. His survival keeps him structurally distinct from Gerolt, preserves the Queen's Order as a living thread inside Natharul and leaves open imprisonment, interrogation, rescue or a later reunion. He is now physically marked as the last surviving keeper rather than functioning as a second elderly protector who dies to launch a protagonist.

### Leorin — Seralune's Cousin / Head of the Royal Court

Seralune's cousin and the present head of Natharul's royal court. He is visibly older than the young relative Seralune remembers: silver at the temples, fine lines around the eyes and the settled authority of a life she did not witness. Elven aging keeps the change restrained but unmistakable.

Leorin provides the controlled, institutional response to Seralune's awakening. Where Thaeroval reacts as a brother, Leorin immediately secures passages, summons the court's magical specialists and treats the broken seal as a crisis of state. He once addressed Seralune familiarly; calling her “Your Highness” upon seeing her awake gives her the first personal evidence that their relationship and the world around it have changed.

During the king's absence, Leorin is the highest civil authority present. He pressures Thaeroval to prepare renewed containment before the old bindings lose their hold, but his precise feelings about the original sealing remain open.

### Eldrin (deceased, Ch.2 — unnamed in-text)

### Redd — Orphan Companion / Hidden Earth Potential

Redd is approximately twenty-eight years old. Natharul forces burned his village during the Mydean Campaign when he was eight, killing his family and leaving him to survive alone. He grew up in and around Kelmend's neglected districts, learning streets, abandoned routes and the practical methods by which an unwanted child remains alive.

Before joining Alaric, Redd lives alone in an ordinary cottage left from an older, abandoned section of Kelmend's Faint quarter. No master owns him. He has never undergone an official Affinity test and assumes his inconsistent Earth Magic means he is Faint. In truth, his Earth『Affinity』has latent **Exalted** potential. Lack of teaching and control—not lack of strength—makes even small spells unreliable. Properly trained, his eventual ceiling may approach Kurdag's and surpass Silas in a direct fight; at present Silas would defeat him easily and Kurdag exists on an entirely different practical level.

Redd carries his father's sword as a family heirloom. Possessing it does not make him an accomplished swordsman. His other strengths are local knowledge, improvisation, resilience and an ability to find humour without requiring the world to become gentle first.

**Personality and voice:** energetic, funny and inclined to carry pain through jokes. His humour should recall the warmth and companionship of Merry and Pippin without copying their speech or reducing him to comic relief. He does not joke through every crisis. When the humour disappears, the reader should feel why.

**Volume One entry:** Redd sees Alaric and Silas evading the authorities and leads them through Kelmend's neglected ways because he recognises that elves are pursuing them. His hatred of Natharul gives him an immediate reason to help, but his usefulness earns his continuing place. When elves later search his cottage, he loses the only private home he has known. In the rewrite pass the searchers burn it (Ch 15).

Redd remains angry about the intrusion but refuses to let the house become the limit of his future. He travels towards Favale because he has nowhere safe to return, wants to learn what Helmi discovers about the ancient core and chooses adventure and companionship over renewed isolation. He openly begins considering Alaric, Silas and Wena his friends.

**Chapter 18 development:** during Alaric's first sword lesson, Redd naturally shortens Alaric to **Al**. He watches and jokes rather than revealing hidden sword mastery. Wena begins staying beside him while Alaric trains and follows him during ordinary camp movement. When Redd admits that the group has become his friends, Silas acknowledges his place by assigning responsibility rather than speaking the same feeling aloud.

**Long-term friction:** Redd's hatred of elves remains grounded in witnessed atrocity and must not be dismissed as simple ignorance. His growing friendship with Alaric will later collide with Alaric's ancient bond to Seralune. That conflict should challenge both men rather than prove one morally uncomplicated.

### Silas — The Mercenary

Met on the road to Kelmend, where he helps Alaric and Wena evade Natharul pursuit. His decision is not initially kind: he recognises Gerolt's split-shield token and understands that delivering its bearer may reopen a door Marta has closed to him. Approximately forty-five years old. Cocky, cutthroat, pragmatic and unsentimental — he bribes, swears, retreats when a fight is poor and does not care whether survival looks honourable.

**Appearance:** weathered and scarred, with dark stubble and an old pale scar running from his left cheekbone towards his jaw. He wears practical leather beneath a weather-dark cloak.

**Affinity and combat:** upper-Common Fire『Affinity』. He produces fire naturally without requiring tinder, oil or another source. He can summon flame into his hand, release brief close-ranged bursts, sheath a treated blade in fire and force heat through metal or flesh by contact. Prepared fuel can preserve or spread his fire without continued mana, but it is an aid rather than its source. He cannot cast battlefield-scale fire or defeat an elite Natharul warrior through raw power. Sustained use overheats his hands and weapon and drains him quickly. His real danger comes from anticipation, terrain, concealed weapons, deception and refusing fair fights.

**Backstory:** twenty years before the present, during the Mydean Campaign, Silas was the recognised leader of a talented mercenary group assigned to gather intelligence on Natharul raiding parties operating inside Mydea. The objective was reconnaissance and return, not decisive engagement. Marta’s father joined the expedition as a soldier and intelligence gatherer.

Silas’s confidence had become arrogance. When a Natharul unit appeared to retreat into a narrow, dried river gorge, he abandoned the limited objective and ordered pursuit despite warnings that the withdrawal looked deliberate. Hidden Natharul forces held the ridges and closed both exits once the group had entered. Every other member—including Marta’s father—was killed. Silas returned alone and honestly admitted that he had given the order.

The Broken Shield network stripped him of active standing for exceeding the mission, causing the deaths of his group and destroying the intelligence operation. Marta ended direct contact with him. She did not create the network’s judgement and cannot overturn it alone, but her sponsorship could give him a path back. Since his expulsion, he has performed occasional deniable jobs through intermediaries connected to her network without being restored to active standing. Marta does not currently employ him directly and will not receive him willingly. He uses Gerolt's token and Alaric's safe delivery to force an audience and seek another chance to serve.

**Relationship with Marta:** Silas and Marta once had a romantic relationship concealed from the wider Broken Shield network and community. Marta is Gerolt’s niece; Gerolt knew and disapproved. Marta ended contact after Silas returned alone from the expedition that killed her father. Silas remains in love with her. Marta’s present feelings remain open until her character and Chapter 7 are designed more fully.

Silas tells himself that he remains near Kelmend because Marta could sponsor his return to the Broken Shield network. That motive is real, but incomplete. He also stays because proximity allows him to quietly help Marta and because leaving would require accepting that their relationship is over. His love explains his behaviour without entitling him to forgiveness or renewed affection.

**Present life:** Silas lives in a modest dwelling near the river outside Kelmend and remains close to the town. He survives through escort work, smuggling, hunting fugitives and other odd jobs. He also undertakes difficult work that quietly benefits Marta or the Broken Shield network, hoping reports of his usefulness will reach her. He pretends this proximity is practical, but he has spent years trying to earn back both the place and the person he lost.

**Chapter 6 setup:** Silas follows the nearby Natharul pursuit because it threatens his home and working routes, then finds Alaric on the riverbank. He hides Alaric and Wena in a shallow limestone cave used as an emergency shelter and cache. When a scout enters, Silas kills him at close range and sends his horse downstream to misdirect the search. The cave contains a charcoal-black Mydean messenger’s coat from an earlier job, which Alaric wears over his ancient clothing. The coat has a high standing collar, an asymmetrical overlapping front and a layered shoulder mantle matching the Volume One cover.

His old cockiness survives in altered form. He distrusts causes, grand plans and heroic sacrifice because he once assumed talent would protect everyone who followed him. He now makes cold decisions early, and those decisions should sometimes be proven right in ways that discomfort Alaric. His deeper flaw remains that he still believes his own judgement is better than everyone else's.

**Function:** fills the competence gap Alaric lacks without becoming a second Gerolt-shaped mentor. His relationship with Alaric begins as transport rather than guardianship. He should not become a cynic who simply learns to care; his arc is a slow, reluctant and never-fully-admitted movement from calculation towards loyalty.

**Road knowledge and Chapter 18 development:** Silas has travelled through both Inrandeel and the Great Expanse. He can cross Inrandeel alone by hiding from creatures he cannot defeat, but knows that Alaric, Redd and Wena would make concealment unreliable. He resists the Expanse because familiarity has taught him caution, then accepts the route once the group rules out the occupied road and rainforest together. He gives Alaric a worn spare sword permanently and teaches ordinary stance, movement and committed action. Training reveals care through time and correction without turning Silas into another paternal mentor. He intends to remain with the group through Favale because he means to keep his promise to Marta.

**Chapter 7 function:** Marta names him for the first time on-page and tells him he has no right to stand at her door. He accepts this without defence. Alaric gives only a limited, factual account of Silas saving him and Wena; Marta allows Silas inside because exposure would endanger everyone, not because the past is forgiven. During the evening bargain, Marta orders Silas to make himself useful: scout the garrison's lower streets, help Alaric reach Darcy and get both of them to the broken-well rendezvous if Darcy chooses to leave. Silas initially supports the extraction because he hopes helping Marta will impress her. Marta explicitly treats his competence as a tool rather than renewed trust, and success earns usefulness rather than forgiveness. His motive must not entitle him to renewed intimacy and should deepen once Darcy's human cost becomes real.

**Possible Movement III function:** likely voice arguing against the reckless action that leads to the volume's climax — his warning may go unheeded and be proven right in the worst possible way as the volume ends.

**Rewrite-pass canon (22 Sept 2026):** in Ch 18 he hears soldiers at an inn say the scarred elf will question Marta after closing, and chooses not to go back: "If I go back, she's dead… That's the only thing I can still do for her." In Ch 20 Liluth recognises him as "the one in the stolen armour" and taunts him with Marta's silence, and he fights her without a word; afterwards he tells Alaric, "She held." ### Marta

Approximately forty years old. A strikingly beautiful, red-haired and elegant innkeeper near Kelmend's southern gate, Gerolt’s niece and the contact his token leads Alaric to. She possesses an Eminent Wind『Affinity』and is powerful and experienced enough to sense the outward mana shape associated with another person's Affinity. Alaric feels to her like a living absence rather than a weak or depleted mage.

Marta is sharp-tongued, observant and deeply suspicious of ulterior motives. Her influence in Kelmend comes from information, long relationships, the usefulness of her inn, her magical standing and the fact that several guards are attracted to her. Attraction may soften individual guards but must never be the sole explanation for her authority. She regularly bought produce from Gerolt for the inn, which is why Wena recognises and runs to her in Chapter 7.

Gerolt's split-shield token belongs to the Broken Shield network. It proves that Gerolt chose to place its bearer under an old obligation, earning Alaric a hearing and temporary shelter rather than unconditional protection. Marta functions as Volume 1's second mentor figure but deliberately **not** as a Gerolt replacement: she feeds, questions and treats Alaric because she needs the truth, honours Gerolt and wants something in return.

Marta and Silas once shared a relationship concealed from the wider network and community, though Gerolt knew and disapproved. Her father later joined Silas’s reconnaissance expedition to gather intelligence on Natharul raiding parties. Silas abandoned the mission’s limited objective and ordered the pursuit that led the group into a trap, killing her father and everyone except Silas. Marta ended direct contact after Silas returned and admitted what he had done. Silas remains in love with her and has spent years performing work that indirectly protects or benefits her, partly hoping to prove himself worthy of another chance. Marta's present response is anger, grief and a refusal to let him use Alaric as an excuse to return. Whether any romantic feeling survives beneath that anger remains open. Her refusal to receive him must be treated as a legitimate boundary rather than an obstacle romance entitles him to overcome.

In Chapter 7, Marta recognises Alaric, Wena, Silas and Gerolt's token in that order. Alaric confirms Gerolt's death; Marta remains composed, closes her eyes and calls her uncle an old fool through a slight tremor. She turns cold towards Silas, prevents a Mydean patrol from entering the inn and redirects the soldiers towards the abandoned grain sheds along Kelmend's western wall. After discovering that Alaric has no detectable『Affinity』at all, she repeats Gerolt's private description of him as **Empty** and warns that no one can be allowed to learn what he is. This is not a formal category: the condition is considered so impossible that no accepted word exists for it.

Marta's childhood friend Darcy is being held as Mydean military property and transferred at dawn. Marta has no complete extraction method when the deadline arrives. After Alaric sleeps through the day, she offers a transaction: secrecy, passage beyond Kelmend and help investigating the Silent Field in exchange for carrying her offer to Darcy and helping her reach safety if she chooses to leave. Marta assigns Silas the competence role he is suited for while remaining openly hostile: he must find an opening through the garrison's lower streets, keep Alaric alive and bring Alaric and Darcy to the rendezvous. Wena remains with Marta so the searched-for boy-and-dog pairing is not seen together. This request must arise from Marta's love and desperation without becoming a neutral mission briefing.

### Kelmend's Faint Quarter

A slum settlement clings to the outside of Kelmend's wall. Kelmend has roughly two to three thousand permanent residents, several hundred of whom are Faint; many live in this quarter alongside their families and others denied secure places within the walls. Soldiers, transient labourers and passing traders can temporarily swell the settlement without being counted among its permanent population. Kelmend keeps the Faint close enough to provide labour while placing them far enough outside respectable civic life that stronger citizens can ignore their conditions. The quarter should reveal Mydea's Affinity hierarchy through housing, policing, hunger and restricted movement rather than exposition alone.

A broken well stands at the quarter's outer edge. For the Darcy extraction, Marta chooses it as the rendezvous: she will wait there with Wena and provisions while Silas helps Alaric and, if she consents, Darcy escape through a separate route. Redd later leads Alaric and Silas through the quarter's neglected routes and takes them to his cottage in its abandoned older section.

### Darcy — Captive Strategist

Approximately forty years old and Marta's childhood friend. Darcy has always possessed an exceptional memory, a keen eye for history and an ability to understand how institutions, armies and material systems fit together. She is widely considered by those close to the truth to be one of Mydea's greatest living minds.

Her Water『Affinity』is extraordinarily weak—barely enough to produce a few drops. Mydean law therefore classifies her as Faint and denies her ordinary personhood despite the state depending upon her intelligence. She is both property and prisoner. Her strategies have allowed Mydea to win numerous battles, but the officers controlling her present those victories as their own.

Captain Gilmot controls Darcy directly. He confines, exhausts, humiliates and physically punishes her to enforce obedience and protect his ownership of her achievements. His cruelty should make the reader uncomfortable because it expresses the Affinity hierarchy in practice, not because the story accumulates suffering for spectacle. He treats abuse as the maintenance of a valuable state instrument. The most effective scenes connect punishment to her intelligence: correcting him publicly, challenging the use of a plan or refusing to let him claim that rank created the victory.

Darcy is due to be transferred from Kelmend early in the morning after Chapter 7. Mydea intends to use her at the frontier as both defence against the expanding conflict and strategic aid offered to Natharul's campaign against the beastfolk. The transfer is an act of appeasement towards a stronger kingdom.

Darcy genuinely wants freedom, but initially refuses Marta's escape offer because Captain Gilmot has threatened to slaughter the entire Faint quarter if she leaves. With several hundred Faint residents in Kelmend, Darcy believes accepting freedom would condemn people who have no part in the attempt. This threat and her refusal must not be revealed in Chapter 7. When Alaric reaches her, the refusal transforms the mission rather than ending her agency: Darcy remains in Kelmend for the time being but chooses to guide him towards evidence worth recovering from Gerolt's farm. Alaric cannot liberate her by overruling her stated wishes.

Darcy does not already know Alaric or the truth of the Silent Field. When she eventually sees his ancient clothing, she can identify it as Natharul workmanship of almost royal quality while recognising that its pattern belongs to no living house or current style she knows. Marta has taken those garments before Alaric enters Fort Kelmend, so Darcy does not inspect them during their first meeting. Her long-term investigative value lies in knowing where answers might survive. The working path is a stealth return to the Silent Field for material evidence—preferably an intact ancient war core recovered from one of the damaged machines, with a beastfolk banner as an alternative or secondary clue—followed by the Great Library in the Holy Capital of Favale.

### Suppression of Gerolt's Farm

At the time of Chapter 7, the impossible battlefield on Gerolt's farm has no established public name. The Mydean military is actively concealing its scale and nature. Thousands of dead soldiers appearing near Kelmend could cause panic and convince the population that war has already broken out inside Mydea. Guards therefore refer only to **Gerolt's farm** or an unspecified **incident**, lower their voices when forced to mention it and disclose details only when necessary to the search. **The Silent Field** remains the narrative and planning name until the location receives that name publicly later in the story.

### Captain Gilmot — Commander of Fort Kelmend

Gilmot is the decorated Mydean officer who commands Fort Kelmend and owns and controls Darcy under state authority. He claims her strategic victories as his own and is being rewarded for achievements produced by someone the law calls incapable of self-rule. He should be detestable without becoming arbitrary: his violence protects his reputation, enforces the caste hierarchy and keeps a valuable prisoner compliant.

Gilmot possesses an **Eminent Fire『Affinity』**. He uses controlled heat to punish Darcy while deliberately preserving the hands and mind he requires for strategic work. The resulting burns are measured rather than uncontrolled battlefield injuries.

Before Darcy's transfer, Gilmot establishes collective punishment as the mechanism preventing her escape. If she refuses frontier service, leaves military custody or is removed by another party, he will classify Kelmend's Faint quarter as complicit in the theft of state property and burn the district without evacuating its children. He communicates this as administrative necessity rather than a theatrical threat. His exact age and appearance remain open. Whether he survives the Darcy storyline to become an ongoing human antagonist also remains open.

**Rewrite-pass canon (22 Sept 2026):** Gilmot travels beside Darcy to the frontier. His written order stays on Kelmend's wall with the garrison commander, and if Darcy is ever lost, "one bird brings it home". He is calm and teacherly with Darcy and brutal with Tobin. He is alive at the end of Volume One. Killing him is the Volume Two mission, because it ends his threat and lets Darcy choose herself.

### Dramatic Question

> Can Alaric and Seralune claim ownership of their lives in a world that believes power gives others the right to decide for them?

They do not meet in Volume One. Their stories form a dual journey linked by simultaneous awakening, thematic contrast and a final involuntary soul-contact.

### Movement I — Two Awakenings (~5–7 chapters, Chapters 1–3 written)

**Alaric:** Gerolt dies defending him. Alaric takes Wena and flees toward Kelmend with the split-shield token. Survival remains physical, frightened and unglamorous. Gerolt's final stand resolves primarily through sound. Wena tries to return; Alaric accepts responsibility for her and continues west. Human authorities then reveal that Mydea is not automatically safe for a stranger with no detectable『Affinity』.

**Seralune:** she awakens at the exact instant Alaric returns. Chapter 3 introduces this as a short closing coda after Alaric survives the river and encounters the mercenary, with Chapter 4 providing her first full viewpoint chapter. Thaeroval arrives in response to the broken seal and reacts with genuine love and terror. Leorin, Seralune's visibly aged cousin and the current head of the royal court, assumes control of the institutional response. The king is absent, travelling to inspect the newly reported battlefield. Seralune discovers accumulated contradictions: Leorin has visibly aged, the palace has changed, her remembered attendant is unknown and both court and family fear what she may remember. Thaeroval confirms that one thousand years have passed but refuses to explain the missing interval because he believes restoring her memories while her mana is unstable could endanger others. A wardwright enters despite Thaeroval's prohibition, the lamps answer Seralune's unshaped mana and the door is locked from outside. Chapter 5 continues directly from the turning lock. Cyrandor, the current keeper of an inherited order left by the queen, offers Seralune a route towards Mydea. Seralune chooses to leave, exposes the escape by refusing to abandon Nereth and directly rejects Thaeroval's demand that she return. Cyrandor loses an arm buying the seconds required for Seralune and Nereth to reach the passages beneath the palace, but survives and is left behind for capture. With Cyrandor's old southern route lost, Nereth's knowledge of the present palace and its working drainage channels carries the escape forward. This is a partial breakout rather than her complete escape from Natharul. Chapter 6 returns to Alaric and resumes the mercenary encounter established at the end of Chapter 3.

### Movement II — The Places Assigned to Them (~10–14 chapters)

**Alaric:** Chapter 7 begins at Marta's door before sunrise. The token earns limited trust, not unconditional shelter. Marta recognises Wena, learns of Gerolt's death, refuses Silas and then admits him only after Alaric gives a limited defence and the approaching patrol makes exposure dangerous. Without inventing a sighting, she prevents the patrol from searching her inn and redirects it towards the abandoned grain sheds along Kelmend's western wall. She questions and treats Alaric, identifies Gerolt's token as Broken Shield property and discovers that he has no detectable『Affinity』. With no recognised term for such an impossibility, she calls him **Empty** as Gerolt did.

After Alaric sleeps through the day, Marta offers a concrete, transactional path to answers. Her childhood friend Darcy—an extremely weak Water user, captive strategist and one of Mydea's greatest minds—is being transferred at dawn to support the frontier response to Natharul's offensive against the beastfolk. Marta will provide secrecy, passage beyond Kelmend and help investigating Alaric if he carries her offer to Darcy and helps her reach safety if she accepts. No complete extraction method exists yet, but Marta assigns roles: Silas scouts the lower streets and keeps Alaric alive; Alaric speaks to Darcy and protects her choice; Marta takes Wena and provisions separately to the broken well at the edge of Kelmend's Faint quarter. Alaric accepts partly to prove that Gerolt did not die saving someone capable only of becoming a burden.

The extraction attempt begins early in Movement II rather than being held automatically for the volume climax. Darcy initially refuses to leave because the captain has threatened to slaughter Kelmend's Faint quarter if she accepts freedom. The conflict must preserve her agency: Alaric and Marta can create an opportunity, but they cannot overrule her choice or remove her while hundreds of Faint residents remain under direct threat.

The mission introduces the Affinity caste system through lived consequences, not lecture. Alaric meets the Mercenary, the Orphan and the Kelmend Faint Boy across separate pressures rather than in one gathering scene. Liluth pursues him from personal grief. Mydean authorities pursue him because a survivor with no detectable『Affinity』, Eldrin's death and Liluth's maiming could provoke imperial retaliation. A strong-Affinity rival or officer may embody the sincere belief that hierarchy preserves civilisation.

When Alaric reaches Darcy, his ancient clothing is with Marta and cannot be inspected during the cell conversation. Darcy instead proposes a larger investigative path: recover physical evidence from the increasingly occupied Silent Field through stealth, then eventually bring it to the Great Library in the Holy Capital of Favale. An intact ancient war core recovered from one of the damaged machines is the preferred objective; a beastfolk banner may provide secondary proof that the accepted history and geography are false. Darcy may recognise the clothing's obsolete, almost royal Natharul workmanship only after she sees it later.

**Seralune:** reconstructs the changed kingdom and the falsity of her own remembered life. She sees how Natharul classifies the weak, how her family speaks of Veiled bearers as instruments and how her brother's love functions as control. Her goals become truth, freedom and eventual reform. She plans escape while her brother attempts to contain her without treating her as an opponent. Early choices should show her virtue becoming dangerous: she intervenes to protect others even when they ask her not to, because she believes accepting their suffering would be immoral.

### Movement III — Freedom and Its Cost (~4–7 chapters)

**Alaric:** completes the first act of liberation by helping Darcy obtain the ability to choose escape rather than treating her as cargo. The developing group then attempts the stealth return to the Silent Field or prepares the route by which that evidence can be recovered. The exact volume boundary remains open, but Volume One must complete at least one concrete objective—Darcy's chosen liberation—and provide physical evidence linking Alaric and the field to the ancient world. Alaric chooses responsibility towards his companions over the quickest path to his own identity.

**Seralune:** fully escapes Natharul in Volume One. She does not overpower her brother. The ancient seal is already weakened, and he hesitates when stopping her would require using lethal or soul-damaging force against the person he most wants to save. She uses that hesitation to cross the final boundary. He survives and becomes a continuing protector, pursuer and tragic antagonist.

**The closing convergence:** at closely parallel moments of crisis, Alaric and Seralune make brief, involuntary soul-contact across distance. Her mana finds his Affinities for an instant. The resulting violet-frost effect echoes the Silent Field and must feel frightening rather than empowering. Neither understands the other or the connection.

**Possible cost, still requiring deliberate confirmation:** the Kelmend Faint Boy may die from this effect. If retained, he must possess an independent family, desire and causal reason to be present; he cannot exist solely to accept Alaric and then die. His death should demonstrate why the ancient world feared the couple's uncontrolled alignment, not function only as emotional punishment.

### Volume One Resolution and Promise

- Alaric helps Darcy choose and obtain freedom, gains the beginnings of a small travel group and secures or begins pursuing physical evidence connecting him to the ancient world.
- Seralune completes her escape and chooses freedom over her brother's protection.
- Both reject the places assigned to them by strength-based society.
- Their first contact proves that the power linking them can kill indiscriminately.
- The longer Alaric thread leads from the Silent Field towards the Great Library in the Holy Capital of Favale, where Darcy can pursue the truth and obtain lasting freedom. Whether the group reaches the library before or after the Volume One boundary remains open.
- Seralune discovers the world beyond Natharul while her brother and multiple states respond to both protagonists' reappearance.

## Reveal and Continuity Rules

1. Chapter 1 narration calls Alaric only "the boy." His name is unavailable to him and to the reader.
2. His name returns near the end of Chapter 2, through a broken, painful vision, before Gerolt speaks it aloud.
3. The origin of the name remains unexplained in-story. Never state it travelled through time or that Seralune returned it to him.
4. Seralune's early voice/presence in Alaric's visions remains unidentified in-text. The reader may suspect a connection; narration should not confirm it prematurely.
5. Alaric's visions are physically agonising — invasive fractures, never convenient exposition or gentle dreams.
6. The Veiled Four remain secret. Alaric initially appears to have no『Affinity』at all.
7. Alaric possesses no unexplained tactical fluency. Fear and observation stay proportionate to an ordinary person in danger.
8. Seralune must receive genuine protagonist treatment from her introduction onward — an immediate desire and conflict independent of Alaric.
9. Never explain Alaric's name meaning in the narrative. The "ruler of all" / _Ruler of Nothing_ contrast is for the author and attentive readers only.
10. Fate creates pressure, not consent. Neither character's moral decisions can be dismissed as something destiny made inevitable.
11. **The full truth of the Ancient Sin (this document) is author-level knowledge.** In-world, it should surface only as contradictory myth, propaganda, incomplete record, and fragments — never as a clean exposition dump to either protagonist or the reader. The first explanation any character encounters for the war, the Silent Field, or Seralune's imprisonment should not be the complete truth.
12. **Eldrin's name is reader-only.** Alaric should never learn it on-page.
13. **The Kelmend Faint Boy's death remains a working possibility rather than fixed canon.** If retained, his life must be developed independently and his presence at the climax must follow established causality. The death must reveal the indiscriminate danger of soul-alignment rather than exist only to punish Alaric or manipulate the reader.
14. **Volume 1's soul-contact is involuntary and momentary — not the willing union described under Seralune's Magical Condition.** The full bonded power, and the mortality cost of true union, are reserved for a later, deliberately chosen turning point and should not be spent here.
15. **Seralune wakes at the same instant Alaric returns, but narration should not confirm causality.** Structural juxtaposition and shared sensory signatures may invite suspicion without explaining the divided soul.
16. **Seralune did not consciously experience the millennium.** Do not write her with one thousand years of lived knowledge. Her family and surviving nobles have aged and changed without her.
17. **Seralune's brother cannot name or remember Alaric in Volume One.** He may recognize that an ancient severance healed and retain emotional or magical scars, but the missing object of his hatred remains absent.

## Discarded or Superseded Ideas

- **A world-ending event that repeats every thousand years:** discarded. The millennium gap matters; it should not become a cyclical apocalypse.
- **Alaric as secretly tactically gifted from the beginning:** discarded. He may learn and grow, but Chapter 1 promises an ordinary, frightened, morally decent person — not a hidden combat prodigy.
- **Love as an automatic magical command:** superseded. Fate and the divided soul create connection and potential; love remains a choice.
- **Seralune as a victim of a secret someone else is keeping from an otherwise-intact person:** superseded by the memory-loss reveal — she is investigating her own erased life, not someone else's withheld secret about an unchanged her.
