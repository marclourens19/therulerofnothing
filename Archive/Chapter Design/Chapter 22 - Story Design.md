# Chapter 22 — Story Design

_Working outline agreed on 5 September 2026. The chapter follows Alaric and his companions over roughly two days beyond Foramen, ending after they reach the colossi and secure water. The identity of the childhood voice Redd hears and the remaining execution details listed below require further design before prose drafting._

---

## Current Position

Chapter 20 ended before dawn with Alaric, Silas, Redd and Wena leaving Foramen by Kurdag's slower western route. Alaric intentionally killed Liluth after Silas, Kurdag and Redd created a narrow opening. One Natharul rider survived and remains bound in Foramen while its council decides what to do. The settlement suffered one death and structural damage. The group leaves without learning whether Liluth reported Foramen's location.

Chapter 21 followed Seralune and Nereth into Meren and onto the road towards Favale. Chapter 22 returns to Alaric in close third throughout. It begins later on the morning of the group's departure from Foramen and closes late on their second day of travel. Its exact calendar relationship to Seralune's arrival must be checked against the established timeline; consecutive chapter numbers do not require simultaneous events.

The travelling group still carries:

- the dormant ancient war core, wrapped and protected in Alaric's satchel;
- Silas's worn spare sword, now carried permanently by Alaric;
- Redd's inherited sword;
- blankets, food and the water remaining from Foramen;
- Wena's rope and the group's ordinary travelling supplies.

No item disappears for convenience. No new equipment appears without being acquired on-page.

## Kurdag's Route

Kurdag's directions establish the following sequence:

1. Follow the western ruins whose windows have melted closed.
2. Reach two ancient stone colossi facing away from one another, approximately two days beyond Foramen.
3. Turn south between them.
4. Avoid apparently level ground and remain upon the darker sand.
5. Listen for a buried water channel before looking for its opening.
6. Fill every water container there.
7. After the channel, expect three dry days without another dependable water source if the paths remain where Kurdag remembers them.
8. If the route fails, return to the opposing figures.
9. Do not follow tracks that begin curving back towards their maker.

The three-day warning applies **after** the buried channel. It does not begin immediately outside Foramen.

### The opposing colossi

The two figures are not ordinary roadside statues. They are immense ancient landmarks comparable in impact to mountains given human shape:

- each rises far above the surrounding ruins and can be seen from miles away when the sand permits;
- they were carved directly from the dark bedrock beneath the vanished city, making them among the few landmarks the shifting Expanse cannot relocate;
- they stand on opposite sides of a buried ceremonial avenue and face away from one another, watching different horizons;
- wind, sand and the ancient calamity have erased most identifying details;
- portions of their faces, arms and clothing may be missing, but their scale remains overwhelming;
- neither the group nor the reader learns with certainty whom they represent;
- they provide orientation and evidence of the lost civilisation's scale without becoming an exposition monument.

Their visual power should come from gradual revelation. What first appears to be two distant cliffs resolves into shoulders, heads and damaged hands as the group approaches. Avoid reproducing the exact design or cultural meaning of another fantasy landmark.

## The Great Expanse — Locked Environmental Lore

### A moving wound

The Expanse is not a motionless desert surrounding ruined cities. Ancient residual mana moves its sand in enormous, irregular flows. Roads disappear beneath it. Buried streets return. Structures can be shifted from one location to another while travellers are moving or resting.

This movement is physical, not merely hallucinated. A building seen behind the group may later stand beside or ahead of them. The land becomes less stable closer to the central crater.

Maps therefore record recurring routes and persistent landmarks rather than fixed geography. Kurdag's directions are valuable because they improve the group's chances, not because they make the route certain.

### Perceptual distortion

Long exposure to the Expanse's residual mana causes travellers to:

- hear voices without visible speakers;
- hear speech from the wrong direction;
- lose confidence in distance and direction;
- feel that they are walking backwards while moving forwards;
- mistake relocated structures for proof that they have circled;
- struggle to determine whether an experience is magical distortion, exhaustion or a living threat.

The voices must not deliver clean facts about the Great Remaking, Alaric, Seralune or the destroyed civilisations. They create uncertainty and emotional pressure rather than convenient exposition.

### The central wound

The centre of the Expanse contains an empty crater approximately the size of a huge city. Sand flows continuously over its rim like a waterfall and disappears below sight without visibly filling the absence.

The current preferred descriptive term is **the Great Sandfall**. Its formal or historical name remains open and must not be treated as established dialogue until chosen.

The central region contains the strongest:

- sand movement;
- spatial displacement;
- perceptual distortion;
- residual mana pressure;
- nocturnal creature activity.

The group is deliberately travelling around it. Chapter 22 must not carry them into the centre merely to display the spectacle.

### Outer and inner danger

The outskirts remain dangerous but can support life. Foramen survives because its district possesses comparatively stable foundations, an accessible cistern, defensible ruins and weaker sand movement.

Danger increases gradually inward. This distinction preserves the plausibility of Foramen and explains why Silas crossed the Expanse previously without mastering its deepest routes.

### Nocturnal creatures

Mana-warped humanoid creatures roam the deeper ruins at night. They may evoke the bodily unease of the walking dead, but their exact appearance and behaviour must belong to Lazaran rather than copying conventional zombies or another existing property.

Currently locked:

- they are humanoid and severely altered by residual mana;
- they are most active after dark;
- they make prolonged travel and exposed camps dangerous;
- their origin is unknown to the characters;
- the outskirts contain fewer of them than the central region.

Still unresolved:

- whether they were ancient inhabitants, later travellers or several kinds of victim;
- whether they hunt through sound, heat, mana or another sense;
- what daylight does to them;
- their final name;
- the precise visual form of their corruption.

Do not answer these questions accidentally during drafting.

## Hidden Relationship to Alaric and Seralune

**Author-only truth:** the Expanse is saturated primarily by Seralune's ancient uncontrolled mana as it passed through the powers available through her bond with Alaric. Their combined existence contributed directly or indirectly to the destruction of several civilisations during the Great Remaking.

Present characters do not know this.

Because Alaric and Seralune remain two unique individuals formed from one divided soul, the residual mana affects Alaric less severely than it affects Silas, Redd or ordinary travellers.

This resistance has strict limits:

- Alaric is not immune.
- He cannot command the sand.
- He cannot identify the voices or recover history from them automatically.
- He does not understand why he remains oriented longer than the others.
- Resistance cannot make him instantly competent or turn him into the group's unquestioned guide.
- His comparative clarity may help the group only after other characters' knowledge and choices remain important.

The difference should be demonstrated, never explained. For example, Silas and Redd may become certain that the group has turned backwards while Alaric retains an uneasy sense that they have continued forwards. Nobody should conclude that the mana belongs to Seralune.

---

## Silas's Previous Crossing

Silas has crossed the Great Expanse before, but he survived by remaining among the outlying ruins. He knows:

- that fixed maps cannot be trusted;
- how quickly water becomes more important than distance;
- that night makes the ruins substantially worse;
- several behaviours that keep a traveller alive along the margins.

He does not know:

- every route through the deeper western passage;
- how to predict each structural displacement;
- what created the Expanse;
- why Alaric is less affected;
- what exists at the bottom of the Great Sandfall;
- whether Kurdag's landmarks remain where he last found them.

Silas's competence must remain valuable without making Kurdag's warning or the moving geography meaningless.

## Chapter 22 Craft Boundaries

The chapter must obey the established Chapter Craft Writing Rules.

Additional boundaries for this location:

1. Do not repeat the Natharul aqueduct's corruption. The Expanse primarily attacks space, direction, memory and perception rather than spreading the same biological network.
2. Do not use every danger at once. Select only the environmental effects required by the final chapter design.
3. Do not turn voices into exposition.
4. Do not trigger another automatic sequence of ancient object, headache, **THOOM**, collapse and memory.
5. Do not make Alaric's resistance feel like immunity or a newly mastered power.
6. Do not let the ancient core activate unless later answers explicitly require it.
7. Do not resolve Redd's true Affinity level through one convenient spell.
8. Do not introduce another pursuer immediately after Liluth merely to maintain action.
9. Do not return to Foramen or resolve the prisoner's fate from Alaric's viewpoint without a believable source of information.
10. Do not send the group into the Great Sandfall after Alaric explicitly chose the safer route for his companions.
11. Keep the aftermath of Liluth visible through changed behaviour and choices rather than repeated thematic dialogue about killing.
12. Every conversation must sound like these specific people responding to one another, not the world bible speaking through them.
13. Redd does **not** locate the buried water through Earth magic. Finding the channel must depend upon Kurdag's directions, ordinary observation and group effort.

## Dramatic Purpose and Companionship

Brave companions overcome the Expanse together. Readers should enjoy travelling with Alaric, Silas, Redd and Wena, then fear for the ordinary affection they have begun to share. The emotional reference is the travelling friendship of Final Fantasy XV: shared meals, irritating habits, reciprocal care, private vulnerabilities and anticipation of a future together. Preserve this group's own personalities, history and age differences.

The practical objective is to reach the colossi, locate Kurdag's buried channel and secure enough water for the next three dry days. The objective is completed before the chapter ends.

The emotional movement runs from tentative ordinary companionship, through disrupted judgement and mutual dependence, to relief and deeper trust. Redd becomes the most severely affected companion. His vulnerability exposes something beneath his humour, while his resourcefulness and ability to care for his friends remain present.

Contributions overlap rather than arriving as separate turns to demonstrate usefulness. Silas provides experience and precautions; Redd reads damaged buildings and improvises; Alaric retains greater clarity while still needing help; Wena has needs, preferences and bonds with each man. Develop the Silas–Redd relationship as well as each man's bond with Alaric.

The Expanse remains extremely dangerous. Conversations occur alongside navigation, watching the ruins, caring for Wena and conserving supplies. Familiarity helps the group notice when someone's behaviour changes. An affected person's actions may look incomprehensible to the others while feeling entirely justified to that person. Neither friendship nor Alaric's resistance instantly cures exposure.

## Scene Sequence

### 1. Later that morning — an imperfect breakfast

The group has put distance between itself and Foramen. Silas insists they eat before continuing. They are exhausted after the attack and departure.

Redd complains about the food. Wena gravitates towards whoever has the largest portion. Alaric gradually joins the conversation. His sword is clean, but he keeps it farther from his hands than usual. Account briefly for cleaning it during the elapsed morning if Chapter 20 has not already done so. Liluth's death appears in behaviour without restarting the whole discussion of killing.

They begin anticipating a proper meal together in Favale. Redd describes what they should order; Silas objects to the expense. Alaric realises he does not know what food he likes. His companions include him in the prospect of finding out. This plants a modest shared future for the closing scene to revisit.

### 2. Establish their travelling habits

Silas follows the melted-window buildings from Kurdag's directions. Redd notices possible passages and checks unstable floors. Alaric watches the surroundings and helps manage Wena. Establish their formation and precautions clearly enough that later departures from them are legible.

Silas tells them to report strange sounds or sensations. He admits that his previous crossing followed the outskirts and this route is unfamiliar. His competence includes acknowledging the limit of his knowledge.

Through gaps in the ruins, they glimpse two distant shapes resembling cliffs. Silas suspects these are Kurdag's landmarks. Their true scale and shape become clear only much later.

### 3. The Expanse visibly moves

They pass a broken tower with a distinctive arch. Later, Redd notices it standing where it cannot be if their route has remained straight. The group disagrees briefly about whether they have turned.

Sand then flows across their route, physically carrying a section of ruined wall. The visible movement establishes that the terrain itself can change. Show one coherent displacement rather than a collection of unrelated spectacles.

They wait for the movement to ease and locate the continuation of the dark sand. Their caution succeeds but costs daylight, creating pressure on their shelter decision.

### 4. Redd cannot account for everyone

Redd asks them to stop because he believes someone is missing. Silas, Alaric and Wena are all present. He counts them and cannot explain why the number still feels wrong.

He dismisses it, but Alaric notices him looking back repeatedly. Later, Redd answers a voice neither man heard. Silas responds seriously, draws the group closer and shortens the distance between them.

Alaric has heard something indistinct himself, but it never felt convincing enough to answer. He attributes the difference to the others' exhaustion. The narration offers no explanation of his connection to Seralune.

### 5. Choose another shelter

Their intended refuge becomes inaccessible when sand rises across its entrance. They witness the change before entering and avoid being trapped.

Redd identifies another surviving structure with a raised stone floor. His useful judgement continues alongside his symptoms. Silas checks the approach; Alaric finds a second way out. Their choice is based on limited evidence and growing time pressure.

They reach it before dark, secure their belongings and settle Wena. Establish both openings, positions and available barriers before the crisis. Their preparations create a credible chance of surviving the night.

Movement begins outside among the ruins.

### 6. The first close encounter

A humanoid figure crosses an opening with a gait that makes Alaric uncertain what he has seen. Another follows farther behind. Keep the creatures' introduction restrained and physically readable. Do not reveal their origin or explain a complete hunting system.

The group stays quiet. Silas prepares to defend the entrance. Redd has stopped watching the figures and is listening to a voice farther down the street.

The voice belongs to someone from his childhood, whose identity remains to be chosen by the user. Everything is filtered through Alaric: Redd's reply, his urgency and his insistence that they cannot abandon that person outside. There is no switch into Redd's memories or confirmation that the voice is a real spirit.

### 7. They must bring Redd back

Redd moves towards the exit while the others watch the street. Alaric catches him at the threshold. Redd struggles because he believes Alaric is preventing him from helping the person he hears.

A few words reveal how young Redd was when he lost that person. Avoid a complete history of the attack on his village or an eloquent confession during danger.

Their struggle draws a creature closer. Silas holds the entrance while Alaric tries to bring Redd inside. Alaric cannot reason the distortion away and cannot manage the situation indefinitely by himself.

Redd recognises that Alaric is in immediate danger because of the struggle. While still hearing the voice, he helps pull Alaric back inside. This is a limited act of care amid impaired judgement, not a complete recovery. It buys the seconds the group needs.

Together they secure the entrance. If Silas uses force, keep it brief and tied to defending the opening. The exact action and creature response require design; this is a close survival encounter rather than an extended battle. Track Wena and the protected core throughout. Nobody's belongings can quietly solve a problem unless established earlier.

### 8. Endure the night

The group remains awake for much of the night. Redd continues to hear the voice intermittently. Silas is also affected; a possible manifestation is repeatedly hearing footsteps within the shelter. He can still compare what he hears with his companions' observations.

Alaric helps them retain immediate facts: who is beside them, which entrance they used and where the supplies are. His comparative clarity supports the precautions Silas and Redd helped put in place. He cannot judge every danger or guarantee a correct route.

Wena settles against Redd once he stops struggling. He keeps a hand in her fur. They share watch duties, water and the uncomfortable floor. Their care is expressed through these tasks.

Do not imply that sunrise automatically ends all mana exposure. Reduced disturbance, rest and withdrawal from the worst local concentration may permit clearer judgement, but the precise recovery conditions must remain consistent with the later Expanse rules.

### 9. Morning awkwardness and acceptance

Redd remembers enough to feel ashamed. He attempts a joke but cannot finish it. Allow the failed attempt to remain uncomfortable without humiliating him or immediately supplying a reassuring speech.

Alaric asks for help with an ordinary task, such as fastening a strap or checking Wena's rope. Silas gives Redd food and tells him when they are leaving. Their ordinary inclusion communicates that his place among them survived the night. Care must not depend upon Redd proving useful again.

If Redd voluntarily shares anything, use one small personal memory of the person he heard. A remembered habit can carry the feeling without narrating his entire childhood. The companions do not interrogate him.

Compress the remaining travel through selected decisions and exchanges. Exhaustion and symptoms can persist without repeating the night's crisis.

### 10. The colossi and the recovered water

Late on their second day, the sand clears and the distant cliffs resolve into shoulders, faces and immense bodies carved from bedrock. Surviving buildings barely reach their feet. The figures face opposite horizons.

Let the scale interrupt their exhaustion. This is an earned moment of awe and a dependable landmark after uncertain travel. No identification, historical lecture or involuntary memory accompanies the revelation. They remain on the western route around the central danger.

They follow Kurdag's directions south and listen. Silas recognises running water. Redd finds a workable approach through damaged stone. Alaric helps clear the opening and lower their containers. Select concrete, compatible tasks during drafting and keep the channel's access physically clear.

Redd uses no Earth magic to locate the water. A displaced entrance is optional, not an additional required complication. Avoid turning this success into another prolonged underground escape.

They replenish their supplies before the ending. Wena drinks and behaves like an impatient dog rather than a mystical guide. Three dry days still lie ahead, but the immediate objective is complete.

## Closing Image — A Meal Still Ahead

They rest beside the channel in the vicinity of the colossi, with its entrance close enough to the monuments for their scale to remain present. Keep this geography consistent with turning south between the figures to find the water.

Redd returns naturally to the meal they promised themselves in Favale. He asks Alaric what he wants, and Alaric has thought of an answer. The exact food remains to be selected in keeping with what Alaric has actually tasted or heard described.

The callback should sound like a conversation resumed by tired friends. Nobody explains its significance. The ending offers affection and earned confidence while the Expanse and the remaining journey retain their danger.

No fresh pursuer, core activation, apparition or manufactured threat follows this moment.

---

## Character Movement

- **Alaric:** troubled by Liluth's death, but increasingly present in the lives of his companions. His greater clarity helps without granting mastery. He ends with a small personal desire he hopes to share with them in Favale.
- **Silas:** retains practical value while acknowledging limits and accepting help. His concern for Redd appears through protection, observation and ordinary care after the crisis.
- **Redd:** contributes resourcefulness, suffers the most severe distortion, and still makes a limited choice to help Alaric. He experiences being cared for while frightened and embarrassed. His humour can return without concealing everything the reader has learned.
- **Wena:** participates in the group's ordinary life and must be protected during danger. Her companionship with Redd develops without replacing her bond with Alaric or giving her infallible senses.

## Remaining Decisions Before Prose Drafting

- Whose childhood voice Redd hears, and what ordinary words make him respond.
- What limited personal detail Redd may choose to share afterwards.
- The creatures' visible form and exact action at the entrance; their deeper origin can remain unknown.
- How Silas holds the entrance, how the group secures it, and any lasting cost in supplies, equipment or minor injury.
- The precise shelter geography and Wena's position during the struggle.
- Conditions that allow Redd to regain enough clarity to travel without implying daylight cures the mana's effects.
- The meal or food Alaric chooses for the closing callback.
- Target word count and exact calendar alignment with Seralune's thread.

The viewpoint, two-day progression, focus on companionship, Redd's vulnerability, monumental statues and completed water objective are now the agreed chapter structure. Further questions should resolve execution rather than reopen these choices.
