# Chapter 13 — Story Design

_Status: drafted as **What Remained**. Royal warship appearance and hovering/landing-craft staging updated following author approval on 31 August 2026._

## Core Direction

- **Viewpoint:** Alaric throughout, in close third person. No king, Silas or Redd viewpoint cut.
- **Opening:** Alaric's first clear waking after approximately two days of rest at Redd's cottage. He remembers fragments of Redd caring for him, not forty-eight hours of uninterrupted unconsciousness.
- **Journey:** leave the old quarter after dark, use Silas's unused downstream river crossing and approach Gerolt's farm cautiously.
- **Party:** Alaric, Silas, Redd and Wena. Marta has returned to her inn; Darcy remains in Fort Kelmend.
- **Immediate objective:** observe the occupied farm and assess whether recovering an intact war core is possible. They do not obtain it in this chapter.
- **Emotional movement:** accepting care → choosing to return → guilt and dread at Gerolt's land → alarm at Redd's hatred → seeing the king himself unsettled by the field.
- **Ending:** the group remains concealed while the king begins inspecting the ancient machines. Alaric can witness an abnormal reaction, not know its cause.

The central tension is that Alaric wants answers from the place he is most afraid to revisit. Returning is his choice, but he honours the limit he accepted: Silas is taking them to look, not promising entry or victory.

## Confirmed Decisions

1. Alaric receives approximately two days of meaningful rest before travelling. Both he and Silas feel somewhat better, but their injuries remain relevant.
2. Redd has been caring for Alaric. Alaric recalls brief impressions of being helped rather than a detailed account of every waking hour.
3. Silas has inspected the farm's approaches during that interval and seen soldiers arriving. His information is reconnaissance, not a complete map of an occupied camp.
4. Alaric wants to see the situation himself. Silas agrees to observation from concealment only.
5. Elven presence along the route is more frequent. Their search and the royal inspection make travel less free than it was before.
6. Silas knows a crossing farther downstream that nobody ordinarily uses. It is not the exposed main bridge or the place where Alaric nearly drowned.
7. The king of Natharul arrives aboard one of its elite royal warships: an enormous airborne metal fortress whose hull spans several fields. The main vessel remains hovering; the king and his escort descend in a smaller enclosed craft. The reference supplies broad silhouette, scale and layered construction, not an exact copied vessel, name or colour scheme.
8. Scouts reported a battlefield containing elven forces involved in a war the king knew nothing about. He comes to inspect it personally.
9. Silas identifies the king for Alaric. Alaric does not recognise him independently.
10. Redd knows the king ordered the attacks upon Mydea. Seeing him breaks Redd's restraint in a way that seeing ordinary elven pursuers did not.
11. Redd starts to unsheathe his father's sword; the earth immediately beneath him begins responding to his anger.
12. Silas knocks or forces him down before he exposes them, making clear that his behaviour jeopardises the whole group. This is restraint, not an extended fight or a blow that renders Redd unconscious.
13. Redd's local Earth disturbance is the intended magic beat. Do not add a separate earlier demonstration as though that suggestion had also been approved.
14. The king recognises the ancient design of the war machines. A banner is not the trigger.
15. His recognition that this army belongs to the war a thousand years ago is author knowledge only. No internal king narration or audible announcement of the date.
16. Alaric feels guilt and dread about Gerolt. The reference to a dream was corrected; no dream sequence is required.
17. Looking upon the field can bring back his headaches, briefly and incompletely. The stronger memory and unidentified young woman's voice remain for the later core/field encounter.

## Timeline and Chapter 12 Handoff

Chapter 12 ends with an intention to leave after dark, not with a guaranteed departure. Chapter 13 must account for the change rather than pretending that two days were always the plan.

**Working staging:** Alaric was not fit to make the journey when the first planned evening came. Redd helped him through intermittent waking, food, water and rest; Silas postponed departure and used part of the interval to inspect the approaches. Establish this briefly when Alaric asks how long he has been there. Do not turn it into another lengthy treatment or briefing scene.

- Silas's answer is approximately two days since they reached the cottage, not two days since Gerolt died or since Alaric first woke on the battlefield.
- Redd's presence explains who remained with Alaric and Wena while Silas scouted. Silas also needs rest; he has not spent every hour travelling or keeping watch.
- The cottage remains a modest home whose immediate pursuit was lost, not an undetectable safe house. Do not add an attack or discovery there just to force departure.
- A late-day clear waking permits departure after dark, preserving their original reason for travelling at night. Exact travel hours remain dependent upon the route and injuries.
- The king's journey began earlier in the story; Chapter 13 depicts his arrival to inspect the farm, not a new departure from Natharul after Alaric wakes.
- The royal airborne warship is distinct from Neldor's slower sea-going merchant vessel. Do not shorten Seralune's approximately week-long crossing to match this arrival.
- Record the two-day advance when returning to Seralune's viewpoint. Alternating chapters are not automatically simultaneous.

## Proposed Scene Progression

### 1. A clear waking, with fragments of care

Alaric wakes in the same cottage where Chapter 12 ended. Familiar room details and Wena establish continuity; no false imprisonment scare or fresh nightmare is necessary.

Brief impressions of Redd helping him sit to drink, changing water or remaining nearby show that the lost time contained care. Redd's patience is genuine. Do not later describe it as an act concealing his real personality.

Alaric asks how much time has passed. Silas tells him two days and briefly explains why they did not leave when planned.

His first movements establish improvement and limits. He can put his feet down and move more steadily, but his ankle cannot sustain a normal pace and his ribs and shoulder still object. Redd should not need to recite a medical inventory.

**Decision:** Alaric accepts that waiting was necessary rather than treating care as imprisonment. He still wants to continue towards the field.

### 2. Silas reports what he actually saw

Silas has observed soldiers arriving and increased elven presence on the approaches. He does not know everything inside the field or announce the king's arrival before he has evidence of it.

Keep the conversation practical and short. The core, Helmi and Favale are already established destinations; do not repeat Darcy's technical explanation or the entire Chapter 12 bargain.

Alaric asks to see the farm. Silas agrees to take him to a concealed viewing position. Going closer remains a later decision based on what they find.

**Boundary:** Alaric agrees to stop where Silas tells him. He does not secretly intend to run into the field alone, and he does not become a tactician because he wants answers.

### 3. Leaving Redd's home

Give the departure a small amount of ordinary human space without writing a second respite chapter. Redd collects provisions and takes his father's sword from the pegs. It is an ordinary, valued heirloom, not a newly revealed magical weapon.

Alaric carries only what he can manage. Redd has already offered to carry supplies; that contribution should remain useful without making him strong in every possible way.

Track the borrowed clothes, Alaric's original black garments, messenger's coat, Gerolt's token, Silas's stolen sword and the armour left beneath sacking. Do not silently put Silas back in a Mydean disguise for a journey where it may attract attention.

Redd can bring humour to preparation or the early road. He does not turn Alaric's wounds or Gerolt's death into jokes. The care and ease here establish the contrast with his later rage.

**Decision:** Redd leaves a home he has maintained and joins a dangerous journey voluntarily. Alaric's welcome does not absolve him of responsibility for his choices.

### 4. The altered route and downstream crossing

Elven movement makes open travel less safe. Let one meaningful observation or change of route establish that pressure rather than repeating several hide–wait–run encounters.

Silas guides them to his unused downstream crossing. Establish its usable footing before anyone steps onto it. His knowledge comes from living and working near the river, not from unexplained expertise introduced whenever the plot needs it.

**Staging latitude:** the exact physical form of the crossing is not yet fixed. It must be ordinary geography or neglected infrastructure that Silas has checked, passable by an injured man and a large dog. Do not invent a magical shortcut, an entire secret tunnel or a conveniently supplied boat without establishing it.

- A different crossing does not make the whole river calm. Account for width, current and footing at this particular place.
- Keep assistance proportionate to Silas's burned right hand and Alaric's injured shoulder and palms.
- Keep the dressings and supplies dry where possible; if the crossing wets them, acknowledge and deal with that before continuing.
- Alaric can fear the water because of Chapter 3 without repeating its near-drowning action.
- Wena crosses with them and remains controlled near patrol routes.

**Decision:** Alaric follows the tested route and accepts help. This is persistence and trust, not a sudden display of physical capability.

### 5. Familiar land becomes difficult to approach

Recognisable features of Gerolt's land bring guilt before the core investigation begins. The remains of the cabin may provide the first focus if the approach permits that sightline; do not force the geography to reveal every important object at once.

Alaric feels that Gerolt died because of him. That is his frightened judgement, not an objective ruling by the narrator. His memory of killing the guard can intensify the feeling that blood follows him without making the two deaths morally identical.

The first-kill aftermath belongs within this emotional movement. No separate confession or reassuring lecture is required, and Silas should not erase the guilt with a polished maxim.

Wena's response should be physical and specific: recognition of a familiar route, pulling towards a place or searching a scent. Keep her close before that response could expose them. No second prolonged dog-runs-back scene.

Looking towards the battlefield may cause a short headache. Alaric remains functional enough to perceive what follows. No collapse, prophetic dream, clear recovered scene or girl's voice is needed here.

**Decision:** Alaric continues to the observation point despite his dread, but does not abandon the agreed limit to search the cabin.

### 6. The royal battleship arrives

The occupied field is already active before the king arrives. Soldiers clear a modest landing strip beside the tents for the smaller craft, not a field large enough to land the entire battleship. The inspection receives precedence while the group remains concealed.

**Approved visual direction:** an immense, broad armoured body made of sweeping overlapping plates, with a densely ribbed underside, recessed magical channels and hanging defensive housings. Its silhouette can suggest an armoured creature or inverted fortress without making it alive. Use deliberate elven metalwork rather than the deck, sails and rigging of an ordinary sailing vessel. The supplied reference is inspiration, not a design to copy exactly; its red colouring is not fixed canon.

**Scale on the page:** Alaric initially struggles to recognise the passing shapes as one vessel. Its leading end has crossed the far fields before the stern clears the trees; the hull extends above several fields and dwarfs the camp. He has already seen the transport at Fort Kelmend. The revelation is the royal warship's overwhelming scale and military presence, not the existence of flight. Silas identifies its royal military class; do not invent a personal service history aboard it.

**Arrival sequence:** main warship passes overhead and holds high above the far side of the camp → a smaller enclosed vessel emerges from a bay beneath its hull → the smaller craft lands upon the cleared strip → its stair lowers → the escort, standard and king emerge. The smaller craft is itself longer than Gerolt's cabin, making its size relative to the hovering parent another scale cue.

**Continuity:** the main warship does not deploy landing legs, touch the ground or settle its weight upon the field. The metal locking sound and landing dust belong to the smaller craft. The continuous deep hum comes from overhead and helps mask Redd's later struggle. Distinguish the grounded craft's stair from the hovering vessel, and keep the king close enough for the existing inspection scene to remain observable from concealment.

Do not import names or insignia from the visual reference. Exact dimensions, the ship's name, propulsion mechanics, weapon capabilities and the king's personal name remain unestablished. Visible weapon housings do not authorise new attacks, magical detection powers or technical exposition.

Silas identifies Natharul's king. Supply a credible cue he can recognise, such as the royal standard and ceremonial escort, rather than giving Alaric unsupported knowledge of a stranger's identity.

Do not assume every soldier on the site is elven or invent exact unit numbers. Natharul's authority over the inspection is clear; the detailed division of duties can remain background staging.

### 7. Redd's restraint breaks

Seeing the monarch who ordered the attacks on Mydea is the trigger. Redd's reaction is not a general inability to function near any elf; he has already shown he can help people escape elven pursuit intelligently.

He starts drawing his father's sword. The earth immediately beneath him stirs. Use a small, local disturbance such as shifting soil or moving loose stones. The manifestation is involuntary and poorly controlled, not an enormous hidden reserve becoming visible to the army.

Silas catches the dangerous movement and forces him down before he exposes them. Choreograph this around Silas's usable left arm and body weight; he does not grip or strike freely with his burned right palm. Keep the sound and movement small enough to remain concealed.

Silas's anger is concrete: Redd is putting all of them at risk. No triumphant insult, lecture about how revenge works or immediate forgiveness is required.

Redd may identify the king as the man who gave the attack orders. That is enough personal context for this moment; do not insert his entire childhood account while they hide beside an occupied battlefield.

Alaric has now seen the man who cared for him endanger them. He should be unsettled without deciding that Redd's earlier kindness was false. A small contribution from Alaric can help keep the group still—controlling Wena, a direct request to Redd or helping prevent movement—without competing with Silas's physical intervention.

**Decision:** after the initial restraint, Redd must choose to stop fighting it and remain hidden. His anger does not disappear. He is neither knocked unconscious nor magically made compliant.

### 8. The king meets the machines

**Author knowledge:** the king recognises designs from the ancient army and understands that this is connected to the war a thousand years ago. The preliminary reports did not prepare him to find it here.

**What Alaric can witness:** the inspection changes when the king reaches a machine. He stops, doubles back to examine a structural feature or otherwise interrupts the confidence of his approach. His escort's response may make the change legible at a distance.

Choose gestures visible from the actual hiding place. A view sufficient to see a whole person halt is not automatically close enough to see trembling fingers or colour drain from a face. Night lighting, obstacles and the machine's position must support the observation. Do not solve this with an unexplained telescope or improbable overheard whisper.

Use the machines already established in Chapter 1: seamless silver war-carriages, a wreck upon jointed limbs and another with **one long opening in its seamless front**. Do not replace them with riveted modern cannons or change the opening to several slits.

No one says the machines are a thousand years old within Alaric's hearing. Neither Alaric nor his companions explains the king's response correctly on the narrator's behalf. They do not infer that the king knows Alaric, that Seralune caused the field or that their own identities are linked.

**Recommended ending emphasis:** the person whose arrival has made the field more threatening is himself visibly unsettled by what it contains. Alaric has returned to seek answers and learned that this mystery reaches the highest authority present. Keep the final effect observable and uncertain, not an omniscient statement that the king is afraid of Alaric.

End before obtaining the core, entering a machine, confronting the king or beginning another escape. The next chapter inherits a changed situation rather than a completed infiltration.

## Continuing Writing Rules

1. **Emotion is individual.** Alaric's guilt affects attention and movement; Silas's concern appears through limits and practical action; Redd's warmth is real, and his rage has a specific trigger. Do not use interchangeable trembling, anger tags or emotional explanations after every gesture.
2. **Speakers are always clear.** Anchor group dialogue with names or unobtrusive tags after action, interruptions and subject changes. Keep one person's action from misattributing another person's speech.
3. **Dialogue responds to the moment.** No chains of informational questions, aphorisms or competitive snark. Ordinary replies and simple 'said' tags are welcome. Remove redundant gaze actions rather than replacing each with a showier synonym.
4. **Close-third knowledge stays limited.** Narrate what Alaric can perceive, remember, reasonably infer or learn. The king's recognition and Redd's full capacity belong in planning, not Alaric's narration.
5. **Characters choose.** Alaric requests the return and accepts its limit; Silas chooses a cautious approach; Redd volunteers and must regain control. Injuries do not reduce Alaric to cargo or grant him sudden expertise.
6. **Vary rhythm.** Connected paragraphs carry preparation, travel and ordinary conversation. Isolate fragments and one-word lines only when perception genuinely narrows. Follow the current revised Chapters 1, 8, 9 and 12 rather than an older all-fragment draft.
7. **Sound has a physical source.** Aircraft vibration, metal and the river can affect the scene. **THOOM is Alaric's heartbeat inside his head only**, not the battleship's engine, Redd's Earth or an impact. No escalating string of sound effects in place of describing the arrival.
8. **Worldbuilding has an immediate function.** Show the difference between the royal aircraft and the wrecks without a technical lecture. Preserve established『Affinity』terminology and do not import names from reference media.
9. **Continuity before spectacle.** Track elapsed days, river position, available hands, carried objects, field lighting, distance and what can be heard. The king is not granted new all-seeing detection, nor are existing powers arbitrarily disabled to keep the group hidden.
10. **Preserve consequences without repeating scenes.** Rest genuinely helped. No extra torture tableau, underground crawl, near-drowning, cottage attack or dramatic medical collapse merely to add action.
