# The Last Keeper

The lock finished turning.

The door swung inward, one reluctant inch at a time, the hinges complaining with every one. Pale morning light slid in around its edge and stretched across the floor towards Seralune's bare feet.

She waited for Thaer to step through.

He didn't.

An old man stood in the doorway. Thin grey hair lay flat against his scalp, and deep lines crossed a face she had seen exactly once before — bent over linen scattered across a palace floor.

The servant who had dropped to his knees and wept when he saw her.

He was carrying another bundle now, but there was no white linen in it. The cloth was dark and plain, tied up with a narrow cord.

Behind him, something metal caught the light. Something lying across the passage floor.

An armoured boot.

Seralune took a step back.

The old man came in, pushed the door almost shut behind him, and bowed.

"Your Highness. If you wish to stay awake, you must come with me."

She stared at him.

"Who are you?"

"Cyrandor, Your Highness."

"What happened out there?" She looked past him at the boot.

"The guards are alive," Cyrandor said. "I had to put them to sleep. We need to go before someone finds them."

She crossed the room. He lifted a hand as if to stop her, then let it drop before she reached him. The door opened under her palm.

Two guards lay in the passage.

The first had slumped against the wall with one shoulder to the stone and his chin on his breastplate. An overturned cup lay by his hand, and something dark had run out of it into the cracks of the floor.

The second lay closer to the door. His sword was half out of its sheath. A length of folded linen was bunched under his cheek.

Seralune crouched beside him.

"Your Highness —" Cyrandor glanced down the passage again, but she was already bending over the man.

She pressed two fingers under his jaw.

A pulse answered. Slow. Steady.

She moved to the other guard. The same.

"You poisoned them." She looked up at the old man without taking her fingers from the guard's throat.

"A sleeping draught, in their bitterroot." Cyrandor checked the empty passage once more. "I bring the last watch their tonic most mornings. They'd no reason to question it."

His eyes dropped to the half-drawn sword. "One drank less than the other. I had to stop him calling out."

The linen under the guard's face stirred with his breathing. Now she understood the short struggle she'd heard — metal on stone, a boot striking once.

She looked at Cyrandor's narrow shoulders and thin, age-spotted hands. One sleeve was torn at the wrist.

"Will they wake?"

"They should." His eyes went to the man under her hand. "I measured what I gave them. I can't promise more than that."

She kept her fingers where they were a moment longer. Then she stood. Cyrandor stepped back into the room and she followed, pulling the door almost closed.

"And if I refuse to go with you?" she asked. "Will you let me stay?"

He stopped by the bed. His eyes went to the bundle in his arms before he answered.

"Yes, Your Highness. I won't force you." He set it down. "But please — hear me before you decide."

The cord came loose under his fingers. Inside were a plain grey overdress and a dark hooded cloak.

No royal green. No branching gold. Nothing that belonged to her at all.

"The wardwrights are coming back this morning," Cyrandor said. "If you stay, you'll be here when they bring that chain."

Cold slid under her skin.

"Thaeroval forbade them to touch me."

"He did." Cyrandor looked at the door. "His order didn't stop them coming in last night."

He said it quietly. It hit harder for that.

She looked towards the passage. The guards had been there for her protection. Thaer had said so.

He hadn't said whose protection needed the lock.

"Did he send you?" She was searching for some part of this her brother might have meant to happen.

"No. He doesn't know I'm here."

"Then why are you helping me?"

Cyrandor reached under his collar and drew out a fine chain. Something small hung from the end of it. He lifted it free and held it out to her.

A silver signet ring lay in his palm.

Seralune stopped breathing.

There was no royal tree on it. A single closed lily had been carved into its dark green stone, the petals folded so tightly that the centre almost vanished.

A shallow nick crossed the inside of the band.

She had made that mark.

She'd been seven, and absolutely convinced that anything her mother wore must fit her if she only pushed hard enough. The ring had slipped off her thumb and cracked against the edge of the south garden fountain. Her mother had stared at the damage for one long, awful moment — and then laughed so hard that the nearest guards had to look away to spare her dignity.

No one outside the family had ever known.

Cyrandor lifted the chain over his head and laid it in her hands. The ring was still warm from his skin.

"Her Majesty left words with it," he said.

Seralune waited.

"*Two knocks are a warning. They are not permission.*"

Her eyes went to the door.

Her mother had said that. Word for word. After Thaer had barged into a council lesson without waiting and caught Seralune doing an impression of their father in front of a room full of empty chairs. Nobody else had been there to hear it.

"Did my mother give you this?" She held the ring so tightly the nicked edge bit into her palm.

"Not me. The woman who kept it before me put it in my hands." His fingers had started to tremble. He closed them, and it didn't help. "My family served the queen. Before she left Natharul, she gave them a single command. If you ever woke, they were to reach you before the court decided what should be done with you."

"Before she *left*?"

It barely came out.

"Where did she go?"

"I don't know where she is now." Cyrandor kept his voice low. "The trail she left us begins in Mydea."

The word didn't belong in this room.

The human kingdom.

Seralune looked down at the ring again.

"My mother went to *Mydea*?" Her voice climbed, though she tried to keep it down.

"There was someone in Favale — the Holy Capital — who could help us find her. That's the direction that came down to me." He hesitated. "I can't promise that person is still waiting, Your Highness."

"Is my mother alive?"

The trembling spread from his hands up into his shoulders.

"I don't know." His voice wavered. "I wish I could tell you she is."

Honesty should have hurt less than one more evasion.

It didn't.

"Did she leave any instruction for me? Am I meant to go there?"

Cyrandor looked down at the signet in her hand.

"She left no command for you. Only that the choice should be yours."

Seralune looked around the room. The bed behind her. The fire nearly dead. Beyond the window, the city she didn't recognise getting brighter by the minute. She thought of the wardwright standing right there with his chain, of blades between her and the hearth, of Thaer asking her to trust him.

Two drugged guards outside her door. One trembling old man by the bed, waiting for an answer she hadn't given yet.

She closed her fingers around the ring.

"Why were you crying," she asked, "when you saw me in the corridor?"

Cyrandor went still.

He closed his eyes.

"Every keeper before me died waiting for that door to open."

He opened them again. "I'd started to believe I would too."

She looked at his lined face. The torn sleeve. The hands that had carried linen through these halls for a lifetime while kings and princes walked past without ever seeing him.

She wanted Thaer. She wanted him to come through that door himself, send the guards away, and give her one answer that didn't turn into three new questions.

He'd promised her first light.

First light had come.

Cyrandor had come with it.

"Give me the cloak," Seralune said.

---

The grey overdress was too broad across her shoulders and too short at the wrists. She pulled it on over the pale gown rather than waste time fighting the unfamiliar fastenings at her back. The cloak covered both and fell almost to the floor.

Cyrandor turned his back while she changed.

When she was done, he took the cloak's plain wooden clasp between his fingers. It slipped once before he managed to fasten it at her throat.

"You're frightened," Seralune said, watching his hands fall away.

"Yes, Your Highness." He pressed them flat against his sides until they were steady enough to reach for the door.

She slipped the chain over her head and tucked her mother's signet under the grey cloth.

"How do we get out?"

"The old southern wing. There's a servants' stair beneath it that goes down into the drainage chambers. The oldest channels run under the outer wall."

"Will they take us out of Natharul?"

"To the lower city. From there, we try for the southern shore." He opened the door and listened before stepping out.

She paused beside the nearest guard. His chest still rose under the armour. She watched it rise once more, then followed.

"Lead me."

Cyrandor moved. The first turning took them off the marble and into a narrow space inside the walls. The floor changed under her feet — rough blocks instead of polished stone, worn into shallow dips by generations of steps no court historian would ever have thought to write down.

Shelves crowded both sides of the passage, stacked high with folded pale cloth. The air smelled of soap and dust and hot water.

He went through it without hesitating once. At the far end, he lifted a hanging sheet to reveal a wooden door barely wide enough for his shoulders, took an iron key from his pocket, and turned it.

"How do you know your way through here?" she whispered.

"I carry linen through these rooms every morning." He opened the door onto darkness and listened. "Stay close until we reach the southern wing."

Three shallow steps down, and they came out into a corridor she very nearly recognised.

An old arch was still set into its western wall, half swallowed by newer stone. It used to open onto the winter kitchens. She remembered flour drifting through afternoon light, and servants pretending not to see a small princess stealing honey off the cooling bread.

Now pipes ran across the arch where the sunlight had been. She touched the old stone as they passed.

"The winter kitchens were here." She kept her hand on the arch even after Cyrandor had moved on. "When did they close them?"

"Before I entered service, Your Highness." He looked back at her. "I've only ever known it like this."

She let her hand fall and followed him.

Her legs started shaking before the next turning. She ignored them.

The passage sloped upwards. Every step pulled at something behind her knees. Her breath grew loud inside the hood, warm against her mouth, cold again as it left.

Cyrandor slowed down.

"Don't slow down for me," she said. "I can manage."

She took another step. Her knee buckled, and she caught the wall before he could reach her.

He waited and said nothing. She could feel heat climbing into her face.

"Just a moment," she said, palm flat against the stone. "Then we go."

Cyrandor watched the passage while she steadied herself. When she took her hand off the wall, he went on at a pace she could actually keep.

They crossed behind a metal grille that looked down into one of the palace's bigger halls. Servants moved below with baskets and covered trays. Guards passed between them in pairs, green cloaks swinging behind polished armour.

Nobody looked up at the grille.

She was still following him when a bell struck somewhere above them. The note shuddered through the walls. Came again. Then again, faster.

Down in the hall, everyone stopped. Orders cracked across the room. Trays were abandoned where they stood. Guards split towards the doors, and one of them pointed north — towards the passages that led to her room.

Cyrandor's hand closed on the edge of the grille. "They've found the guards."

Boots poured into the service corridors — close, and far too many for one patrol.

He turned from the grille.

"We need to move."

This time, when he took her arm, she didn't argue.

---

The palace closed in around them. Every open passage had voices in it. Every closed door felt like someone standing just on the other side.

He led her through a storeroom full of empty lamp cages, then behind a row of cupboards whose false backs opened into another servants' hall. They waited there while four guards ran past, white light jumping from one caged crystal to the next with them and fading behind their footsteps.

Seralune felt the lamps answer somewhere beneath her skin.

Her mana stirred.

She pressed a hand against her ribs and held it still. The nearest crystal brightened, just barely.

Cyrandor saw. He said nothing. The light dimmed again, and they went on.

At the next crossing, he stopped so suddenly she walked straight into his back.

Voices, coming up the passage ahead.

"Lord Leorin asked for what you witnessed. You'll give it to him."

"I've already told you."

Seralune knew that second voice.

Nereth.

Cyrandor pulled her into the shadow behind a linen press as two guards came into the crossing with Nereth between them. She was still in the same grey and green. One guard had her by the upper arm, and the cloth had gone pale and stretched under his fingers.

"I told you what happened," Nereth said. "The lamps got brighter, and the guards came in with swords."

"And you stood in their way," said the one holding her.

"They were pointing them at her. She hadn't hurt anyone." Nereth tried to ease his fingers loose without making a show of it.

The other guard gave one short laugh.

"You were brought here to heat washing water. Stick to that, and leave the wardwrights to their work."

Nereth dropped her eyes. The hand on her arm tightened.

Seralune moved.

Cyrandor caught the edge of her cloak.

"No." Barely a breath.

Nereth looked towards the sound and found Seralune under the hood. Shock opened her eyes wide. Then she shook her head, once — so small the guards never noticed.

*Go.*

Seralune looked at the fingers digging into Nereth's arm. At her lowered face. The men were talking around her as if she were a parcel being carried between rooms.

Cyrandor's grip on the cloak tightened.

"They'll close the southern passages if they see you," he whispered. "*Please*, Your Highness."

Seralune understood him perfectly.

She stepped out anyway.

"Let her go."

It carried across the whole crossing. Both guards spun around, and the hand on Nereth's arm loosened. It didn't let go.

"Your Highness?"

Seralune pushed the hood back off her face. "I said let her go."

The second guard recovered first. His hand drifted towards the horn at his belt.

"Your Highness, the attendant's under order —"

"Her name is Nereth. Use it."

For one breath, nobody moved. Nereth stared at her, and fear reached her face well before gratitude had any chance to.

"She acted above her station," said the first guard.

"She stood between me and drawn swords." Seralune's eyes went back to the hand still clamped on Nereth's arm.

"Lord Leorin requires her testimony."

"Then Lord Leorin can ask her for it without bruising her."

The second guard's fingers touched the horn.

Seralune looked him straight in the eye. "Don't."

His hand stopped.

For a moment, the authority she remembered still existed.

Then footsteps sounded behind them.

The guard lifted the horn.

A small glass vial smashed against the wall beside his head — Cyrandor's — and bitter pale vapour burst across the stone.

"Hold your breath!" Cyrandor caught her cloak and dragged her back from it.

The first guard doubled over coughing. Nereth drove her shoulder into his chest and tore free. The second got one broken note out of the horn before it turned into a cough.

It was enough. Answers rose from both ends of the corridor.

"South crossing!" someone shouted behind them.

Nereth grabbed Seralune's hand, and they ran.

Seralune dragged the hood back up with her free hand. The bitter smell chased them around the first corner. Her lungs burned from holding her breath, and when she finally let it go, she coughed into the inside of her hood.

Cyrandor shut a door behind them and turned the key. Fists hit the other side before he'd even pulled it out.

Nereth stopped at the top of a short flight of steps and rounded on Seralune.

"You should have *left* me." Her voice was shaking with anger, but she kept it low. "Now they know where you are."

"They were hurting you." Seralune loosened her grip so Nereth could pull her hand back. "I couldn't leave you with them."

The door shook in its frame. Nereth looked at it, then down the steps.

"We're trying to get out of the palace," Seralune said. "Do you want to come with us?"

Nereth turned to Cyrandor instead. "Are you taking her to the old south stair?"

He nodded. The colour left her face.

The door jumped again. Wood split beside the lock.

Nereth turned south.

"This way."

She took Seralune's hand again and led them down.

---

At the bottom, Nereth caught Cyrandor's arm when he turned towards a narrow gallery.

"Not there. They use it for the wardwrights' stores now." She pointed at a low door beside the laundry. "Through here."

Cyrandor looked at the gallery. Then he followed her. At the next turning, he stopped dead in front of a stairwell packed with stone and metal pipes, and looked to Nereth — who took them instead through a room so thick with steam they couldn't see across it.

Servants flattened themselves against the walls as the three of them passed. Overhead, the alarm kept ringing.

Seralune kept the hood low, though she couldn't hide her breathing anymore. Every step drove the pain higher up her legs. Nereth's hand became the only part of the corridor that stayed still.

"How far?"

"The next court," Cyrandor said, and glanced at Nereth. "If that stair's still open."

"It is." Nereth's grip tightened. "But we'll have to cross where the guards can see us. Keep your hood down."

Then the floor shook — not running feet, but something heavy striking stone several passages behind them. Shouting followed the crash.

Cyrandor looked back.

"Faster."

Nereth led them along the edge of the court and through a low doorway into the southern wing.

The change was instant. The warmth went out of the walls. Dust had gathered in the corners, disturbed by too few feet. The crystals overhead were older and dimmer, their light trapped behind cloudy surfaces.

Seralune knew the shape of those windows. They used to look out over the lower orchards. Now they were filled with stone.

At the end of the passage stood a room crammed with broken furniture. Nereth let go of her to help Cyrandor shove a tall linen cupboard aside. Its legs screamed across the floor.

Behind it was a wall. Only a wall.

Nereth stared at it.

"There's no door." She pressed a hand to the stone and looked at Cyrandor.

"It's hidden. Give me room." He got down on one knee and felt along the base of the wall until his fingers found a narrow crack. From inside his sleeve, he drew a strip of blackened metal no longer than his palm.

He fitted its end into the crack. Even knowing where to look, Seralune couldn't see a keyhole.

He turned it.

Something answered deep inside the wall — a heavy scraping she felt through the soles of her feet.

The stone split along a line so fine it hadn't been there a moment before. Cold air breathed through the gap. Nereth put both hands against it and pushed.

The hidden door began to move.

"Seralune."

Her name stopped everything.

Thaer stood at the far end of the passage. No guards with him. He didn't need them.

Dust lay across one shoulder of his dark armour. His sword hung bare in his right hand, the edge turned down towards the floor.

His eyes went from her to the cloak, then to the old servant kneeling beside the open wall.

"Come here," he said. Low, under the distant bells.

Her body leaned towards him before she could stop it.

One step.

Almost.

He lifted his empty hand.

The same hand that had held hers all the way through the unfamiliar palace. The same hand that had rested against her door a hundred evenings before walking in anyway.

She remembered the silver chain.

She stopped.

"Did you order them to lock that door?" She stayed right beside the opening in the wall.

His hand stayed out.

"No." He looked at Cyrandor, then back at her. "But I knew they had."

"You left me there." Her voice was shaking, and she hated that she couldn't make it stop. "You asked me to trust you."

"I was trying to find out what broke in the lower chamber. You should have been safe in your room." He took a step towards her. "I asked you to wait for me."

"I did. Morning came."

Behind her, the hidden door ground open another hand's width, stone scraping stone.

"Were you going to let them put that chain on me?"

"I would never let anyone hurt you." It came out fast, and he reached further towards her.

"I know you don't *want* to hurt me." She could barely look at his open hand. "I'm asking if you'd let them put it on."

His hand dropped. Only a fraction.

"Come back with me," he said. "I'll tell you what I can."

*What I can.* The same wall. Again.

She felt her mother's ring against her chest.

"You promised me that last night." She pressed her hand over the ring through the cloth. "I can't go back to that room and wait for them again, Thaer."

"You're following people you don't know."

"I *know*." Her fingers closed tight around the signet. "I don't want to be afraid of you. But you won't tell me what's happening. *He* told me what he knows."

His face didn't harden. That would have been easier. It opened instead, around something wounded.

"*Please*, Seralune." His voice had gone rough. "Come to me."

She wanted to. Wanting it hurt worse than all the running had.

She closed one hand in the cloak.

"I'm not going back."

She had to force every word past the knot in her throat.

For a moment, nobody moved.

Cyrandor pulled the old key out of the wall. The sound was very small.

Thaer's eyes left her.

"You'll go no further, old man." His voice hardened as it turned on Cyrandor.

Cyrandor stood up on shaking knees and put himself between Thaeroval and the hidden door.

"Nereth," he said, looking towards the stair.

She understood. She drew Seralune towards the opening, where a narrow staircase curved steeply down into darkness.

Thaer moved.

Cyrandor pressed the blackened key flat against his own palm.

Silver lines ignited across the stone.

They raced out from the hidden doorway, across the floor and up the walls in one blinding pattern, and in the space of two breaths the passage was closed. A veil of white metal hung from floor to ceiling, every thread quivering under a force Seralune felt in her teeth.

The light went *into* Cyrandor's hand and travelled under his skin. Thin silver branches spread across his wrist and vanished beneath the torn sleeve. He gasped.

Thaeroval stopped in front of the barrier, staring at the silver spreading under the old man's skin.

"Release the anchor," he said. "You know it won't hold me."

Cyrandor's legs shook harder.

"I know." His voice had gone thin. "I can't let you take her back."

Nereth stepped down onto the stair. Seralune stayed on the landing.

"Cyrandor."

He looked back at her. For the first time since he'd walked into her room, the fear on his face was completely uncovered.

He bowed.

Thaeroval lifted his sword. He didn't come forward. The whole length of the passage lay between them, the silver barrier trembling in the middle of it.

His wrist moved. The blade passed once through empty air.

It crossed none of the distance.

The cut did.

Cyrandor's body jerked.

His arm came apart below the elbow.

The forearm hit the stone, its fingers still closed tight around the blackened key.

For one heartbeat, nothing else moved.

Then the blood came.

It hit the floor and ran across the hem of Seralune's cloak.

The silver branches under Cyrandor's skin went dark. All across the passage, every line of the barrier went out with them.

A sound tore out of the old man as he dropped to one knee. His remaining hand clamped over the ruined sleeve above the wound, blood forcing its way between his fingers.

"*Cyrandor!*" She lunged towards him, and Nereth caught her hand from the stair.

"Your Highness, *come*." Nereth pulled. Seralune pulled back without even looking at her.

Cyrandor looked up.

The pain had drained every drop of colour from his face. But his eyes found hers.

Alive.

He shook his head. Once.

Thaeroval stepped across the place where the barrier had been.

The severed forearm lay between them, the hand still holding the key.

That stroke could have taken Cyrandor's head. Seralune knew it. She watched the old man trying to hold his blood in, and couldn't find a shred of relief in what her brother had chosen to spare.

Cyrandor's eyes moved to the open stair.

She stopped pulling against Nereth's hand.

"Go," she whispered — to herself as much as anyone.

Nereth went down, holding Seralune's hand until she followed. Cyrandor was still watching her when she turned away.

Behind them, the hidden mechanism groaned. The stone door began to close.

Thaer reached it before the gap had narrowed by half.

His sword came up.

Seralune saw the angle of the blade. She saw the line it would carve through the stone under her feet.

Through the stair.

Through her.

His eyes dropped to the step she was standing on.

Their eyes met through the narrowing gap.

The sword didn't fall.

Nereth hauled on a rusted lever set into the wall.

The upper stairs dropped away.

**CRRRAK —!**

Stone folded into the dark. Dust hit Seralune's face before she could breathe. The steps that were left lurched under her feet and threw her against the wall.

The door slammed shut above them.

Darkness came back — broken only by green light trembling over moving water, far below.

She pushed herself upright. Her knees burned. One palm had split open against the stone, and blood was welling up in the shallow cut Nereth had washed only hours before.

Above them, something hit the sealed doorway, and the whole stair shook.

Thaer could cut through it.

Thaer could cut through anything.

The next blow didn't come.

Nereth stood a few steps below, breathing hard.

"Can you walk?" She reached back up towards her.

Seralune looked at the blood along her hem. Then up at the stone that now stood between her and the only person she had ever trusted without a single question.

"Yes." She pressed her bleeding palm into the cloak and steadied herself.

She went down.

The stairs ended in water up to her ankles. It closed around her feet so cold it stole her first breath. A low passage ran south under the palace, its curved ceiling furred with moss, its walls cut by channels older than the city above.

The alarm reached them through the stone, muffled by distance. It sounded as if every bell in Natharul were calling for someone Seralune no longer recognised.

Nereth stepped into the water beside her.

"Which way?"

The old route had lived in Cyrandor's head. Nereth knew what was left of it.

She crouched and let the current run over her fingers. "The old south channel was sealed before I entered service. This runoff feeds the eastern culvert before it turns under the lower city."

"How do you know that?"

"They send us down to clear the drains when they back up." Nereth looked down the passages ahead. "I know the laundry channels, Your Highness. I've never been further."

She stood and took the right-hand passage first. Seralune touched the signet through her cloak — the closed lily pressing into her palm — and followed.

Towards the lower city.

Towards Mydea.

Towards a mother who might not be waiting anymore.

She turned with the current.

Behind her, through stone and distance, her brother's voice found its way into the passage.

"*Seralune!*"

She heard him.

She kept running.
