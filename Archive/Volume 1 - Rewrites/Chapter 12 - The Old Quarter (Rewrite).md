# The Old Quarter

The lane opened up ahead of them, and Alaric saw at once that he couldn't run it.

It was long and straight and empty. No corners. No hanging cloth, no doorway deep enough to duck into without being seen. Morning lay down the middle of the mud in one pale strip, and his ankle had started folding even when he put it down carefully.

It folded now. Silas caught his elbow before he hit the ground.

Behind them, a woman shouted.

Alaric didn't need to understand the words. He knew the voice.

To their left, people were gathering under a broad awning. Baskets rested against ankles. A man was hauling a handcart through the waiting workers while someone at the front argued about how many would be let through the gate. Beyond their shoulders, lines of washing crossed the mouths of several narrower paths.

"There," Alaric said.

Silas followed his look. "The well's the other way."

"She'll see us." Alaric pulled against the hand holding him. "All the way down. We can't —"

His breath ran out before the rest did.

Silas glanced back once.

"Go."

Alaric slid into the crowd sideways, trying to make himself smaller without moving his bad shoulder. A basket scraped his hip. Someone swore when his boot caught theirs, and his whispered apology vanished under the argument at the front.

Silas's stolen armour drew eyes. People made room for it before they looked at his face.

For a few steps, Alaric could see nothing of the lane behind them. A wool cap blocked it. Then a woman's shoulder. Then the loose canvas over a loaded cart.

He walked because everyone around him was walking. Every part of him wanted to run.

The awning ended beside a washing yard. Alaric squeezed between a cart shaft and a wall, catching the wood before it could knock into the woman waiting there, and turned towards the strip of daylight past the yard.

"Not that way."

A man stood by an open gate with a bundle of folded sacking under one arm. His brown hair stuck out over both ears, as if he'd slept badly on each side in turn. A green waistcoat showed through the front of a patched coat.

He nodded at the daylight Alaric had picked.

"Gate at the far end's chained. You'll be halfway up it when she catches you."

Silas moved closer to Alaric.

The man looked past them, and whatever he saw wiped the ease off his face.

"Quick. Through here."

He stepped back through the gate and pointed across the yard at a gap beside a lean-to. Through it, Alaric could see another lane.

An actual way out.

He went. Silas came behind him, his good hand near the stolen sword.

The man pulled the gate to without dropping the latch. He led them round the lean-to, past two tubs of soaking linen and into a passage where the roofs almost touched. Alaric's shoulder brushed damp plaster. He kept his hands tucked in so the loose wrappings wouldn't snag.

"Here." The man stopped beneath a short flight of outside steps. "Against the wall. Don't go past me."

Alaric pressed into the shadow under the steps. Through the narrow gap at the end of the passage, he could see the street they'd almost walked into.

White bandages crossed it.

He stopped breathing.

She came fast, for all the uneven fall of her boots. Her bound arm was clamped tight against her body. Her free hand caught the corner of a house and swung her round it before her bad leg had properly followed.

Her one eye searched the lane ahead.

Alaric's heartbeat struck the inside of his skull.

**THOOM.**

The man with the sacking lifted one finger without looking round.

Alaric held still.

Someone called from behind the elf. She snapped an answer and went on. Another shout followed, further off, but she was already past the gap.

The man waited until her footsteps were gone, then went the other way.

"She's not waiting for the others," he whispered. "Come on."

They crossed an empty yard and followed the backs of the houses. Alaric could hear her again, louder, her words hitting hard against each other. A wooden barrier rattled. The answer came from the street behind her, too far away to belong to anyone at her shoulder.

Silas looked back, but he didn't stop.

The man took them past a row of roofless privies and round a low wall, where he finally set down his sacking. He spread it over the stones and leaned across it as if he were only studying the lane beyond.

Alaric braced both forearms on the wall. His lungs kept trying to take in more air than his ribs would allow.

"You were at Marta's yesterday," the man said quietly.

Alaric lifted his head.

"With the dog. I was collecting sacks from the baker's across the way." His eyes went to Silas, then back. "She didn't look pleased to see you. Any of you."

"Where is she?"

"Took her cart out before the bell. Headed past the kilns."

Silas kept watching the lane. "Anyone follow her?"

"Not that I saw."

"And us?"

The man watched the gap in the wall for several breaths before he answered.

"Nobody yet. But I'd rather not hold a meeting about it here." He gathered up his sacks. "I'm Redd."

"Alaric."

Redd nodded, and his glance dropped to the foot Alaric was keeping off the ground. "Can you still walk, Alaric?"

He wanted to say yes without having to find out.

He pushed off the wall. Pain climbed from his foot to his knee, but the leg held.

"Yes."

"Good. Stay beside me."

Redd shortened his stride to match Alaric's without being asked.

---

They reached the kilns without seeing another soldier. At the last turning, Redd stopped to watch the path behind them, and Silas waited with him before waving Alaric on.

The broken well stood past the last kiln wall, where the ground began to rise away from Kelmend. Its wooden frame had fallen in. Someone had laid planks across the opening and weighted them with stones. On the far side, half hidden in a tangle of elder, a provision cart waited.

Marta stood beside it.

Alaric saw her hand leave the cart's handle as something moved under the canvas.

Wena's head came out first. She shoved through the hanging folds and dropped off the low bed of the cart before Marta could catch her. Her paws hit the ground, and she came at Alaric with her ears flat and her whole body swinging with her tail.

He tried to crouch. His ankle refused.

She reached him before he could work out what to do instead. She pressed against his legs, whined, and pushed her nose under his hanging hand.

"I'm here," he whispered.

She smelled of straw and the dried herbs Marta kept among her stores. He sank his fingers into the thick fur behind her ears, and she pushed harder, nearly knocking him backwards. He caught himself on Silas's arm.

"I'm here, girl. Wait."

She didn't wait. Her nose went over his clothes, stopped at the blood on his stomach, and came back to his hand.

Marta came towards them. Her eyes went over Alaric, Silas and Redd, and then past them to the path they'd come along.

"Where's Darcy?"

Alaric's fingers stopped in Wena's fur.

"She wouldn't come."

Marta didn't move.

"The captain said he'd burn the quarter if she left." He had to swallow before he could go on. "We heard him. She knew he meant it."

Above the fort, something let go.

Alaric felt it before he heard it: a long groan of iron that went down through the ground and into his bad ankle. Then the sound reached them from behind the kiln roofs, the same deep strain he'd heard all night through the walls.

**KRRRNNNK.**

The airship rose over Kelmend.

It came up slowly, as if the air were thick. Its hull was dark with oil, and blue-green light pulsed in its belly where the core sat, bright, then dim, then bright again. The chains that had held it all night hung loose from its sides. It turned above the walls, found its heading, and came on low over the kilns.

Its shadow crossed the broken well.

Nobody spoke. Wena pressed against Alaric's legs and flattened her ears at the noise.

Marta looked up. She watched the ship pass over her head, close enough that Alaric could see ropes shifting along its rails and small dark shapes moving on the deck. She didn't ask whether Darcy was aboard. He watched her understand it anyway.

"Did she know I was waiting?"

"Yes. I told her."

Marta's thumb pressed hard into the side of her forefinger until the skin under the nail went white. She kept watching the sky.

"I thought she might refuse," she said. It came out so quietly it was nearly lost under Wena's breathing. "I kept thinking that if she knew there was somewhere to go…"

She didn't finish.

The ship grew smaller. The throb of its core faded until Alaric couldn't tell it apart from his own pulse. Then there was only a dark shape against the pale sky, and then there wasn't even that.

Marta lowered her eyes.

"I'm sorry," Alaric said.

She looked at him properly then. Her gaze went over the torn shirt, the sagging wrappings, the way he was holding himself above the bad foot.

"You reached her," she said. "Thank you for trying."

Silas shifted beside him. Marta's eyes caught the hand he was holding against his chest.

"What have you done to that?"

"There was a grate in the way out. I heated the joint." He kept the hand where it was. The fingers wouldn't close.

Marta looked at the blisters. Her jaw tightened, but she didn't try to examine them out in the open.

Redd cleared his throat.

"My place is close. There's water, and they can sit down." He glanced back at the kilns. "I got them clear, but I wouldn't keep standing about out here."

Marta looked at him. "You're still in the old quarter?"

"Same house. The roof's better."

"How much better?"

"You can stand under all of it now." Redd's grin faded when Alaric shifted his weight. "It's dry. I'll take you."

Silas looked back the way they'd come. "Show me the approach before we go inside."

Redd nodded and put his sacks on the cart. He took the handles from Marta, and she came round to Alaric's side.

"Your left arm."

"I can walk."

"Then walk with someone holding you."

Alaric let her take his weight.

---

The houses got further apart.

At first Alaric thought the spaces between them were gardens. Then he saw thresholds under the weeds, and a hearth standing on its own where the walls round it had fallen. A row of doorways opened onto rooms full of nettles. Past them, an apple tree had grown up through a roof and spread its branches over the chimney.

People had lived here long enough to plant trees. They'd been gone long enough for the trees to move into their houses.

Redd followed a path worn through the long grass. It kept away from the open road, with the remaining walls between them and the lived-in part of the quarter. When the cart jolted over a buried stone, he looked back at Alaric and took the smoother strip along the next garden's edge.

"Most of them moved in closer to the wall years back," he said. "There's no work out here now."

"You stayed?"

"I came after they'd gone. Fewer arguments about where I slept."

His cottage stood near the end of the row. It was small, with a low roof patched in two colours of tile. The front window had one proper shutter and another made of boards that didn't quite match. Someone had swept the step. A pair of boots dried upside down on sticks beside the door.

Redd left the cart behind a standing garden wall. He showed Silas the narrow back yard and the gap from it into the next empty plot, and Silas checked both ways before coming to the door. Redd lifted the latch.

"Mind the step."

Alaric looked down.

"The one inside," Redd said. "I keep meaning to do something about it."

There was a second, smaller rise inside the door. Marta steered Alaric over it into a room that smelled of cold ash, dried mint, and wool left too close to a fire.

A table took up more of the room than the room could spare. Two chairs stood by it, one with a folded scrap of leather jammed under a short leg. A narrow bed fitted under the side window. Through an open doorway, shelves and a covered water barrel crowded a tiny kitchen.

Nothing matched. Everything had been put exactly where it was on purpose.

"The bed," Marta told Alaric.

He looked at Redd, who stepped aside. "Go on. I've had it all night."

Alaric lowered himself onto the edge of it. The mattress gave, and the relief of not standing hit his body so suddenly that he had to grip the blanket.

Wena pushed in between his knees. She turned twice, found neither way gave her enough room, and lay down with her head on his boot.

Redd watched her settle.

"Right. That's most of the floor gone."

"She can move," Alaric said.

"No need. I've been stepping over that loose board for three years. At least she's nicer to look at."

Redd went back out for the supplies, and Marta followed. Silas took up a place by the front window, the shutter open no wider than two fingers.

Alaric let his eyes go round the room. A sword rested on wooden pegs above a chest. The scabbard had been mended near the mouth. Its leather was darker and better kept than the belt hanging under it, and the grip had been wrapped so neatly that he couldn't find where the strip ended.

He looked away when Redd came back in, but Redd had already seen where he'd been looking.

"My father's."

"Do you use it?"

"Enough to know I'm better at running." Redd moved a bundle along the table to make room for Marta's basket. "You'll have noticed that was the bit I offered."

"A useful preference," Silas said from the window.

Redd grinned and dragged the short-legged chair away from the bed. The leather slid out from under it.

"Mind that bit," he said quickly, as Marta bent to pick it up.

Marta caught the chair before it tipped and looked at him.

"You had time to mend a roof."

"The roof was wetter."

She put the leather back under the leg herself.

---

Marta unpacked her basket on the table. Clean cloth, a stoppered jar and a roll of dressings came out from under the food. Redd brought a basin and water, set a kettle on the hearth, and started laying a fire. The first spark from his flint failed. He swore quietly, tried again, and cupped the little flame until it steadied.

Marta drew the sound chair up to the bed. "Let me see your hands."

Alaric put them on his knees. The strips Silas had tied round them were hard to tell from the cloth beneath anymore. One end hung loose from his right palm. Dark water had soaked into every fold and carried grit wherever the bandages crossed.

She began at his left wrist. He tried to watch. When the first strip lifted, he looked at the window instead.

"Tell me if I pull too hard."

"It doesn't matter."

Her fingers stopped.

"It matters to the skin underneath. Look at me, Alaric."

He did.

"I can soften it with water. You don't have to sit there and let me tear it off."

He nodded. He felt ashamed, and didn't know what he'd done to earn it.

She wet the cloth and waited. The room went on around him. A cupboard opened. Silas moved the shutter. Wena let out a long breath against his leg.

When she tried again, the cloth came away more easily. His hand underneath looked smaller than he remembered.

She turned it to the light. The cuts had opened again, but most of the blood wasn't in them. It was dark between his fingers and under his nails.

"It wasn't all mine," Alaric said.

Marta's grip didn't change. "All right. You don't have to tell me now."

He looked at Silas. Silas was still watching the approach, his burned hand held clear of the window frame. He didn't turn round. He didn't explain.

Marta changed the water before she took his right hand. He kept his eyes on Wena while she worked. The memory came back anyway: another hand closing round his own, trying to hold something in place. His fingers curled before he could stop them.

Marta let go at once. "A moment?"

He nodded. She waited, the wet cloth resting in the basin. Behind her, Redd nudged the kettle further over the flames.

After a while, Alaric opened his hand.

She cleaned the rest and bound both palms in fresh cloth. Then she helped him out of the ruined grey tunic. The rip down the back made it easier to lift away from his shoulder. The linen shirt beneath was wet through, its front stained where the guard's blood had soaked between the layers.

Redd had put a folded shirt and trousers on the chest under the sword.

"Those are clean," he said when Marta looked over. "They're what I wear when I'm trying to get paid properly."

Marta checked the shirt and brought it to the bed. "Turn round, both of you."

Redd faced the kitchen like a well-trained child. Silas stayed at the shutter.

Changing took longer than Alaric wanted anyone to notice. Marta eased the wet linen over his right shoulder and checked the dressing along his ribs. The drain water had got into that too. He breathed through his teeth while she replaced it, then let her guide his arms into Redd's shirt.

She moved Wena out from between his knees and bent to his boots. He reached down to help.

"Leave it. You've only just let me wrap those hands."

The easy boot came off first. The other let go of his swollen ankle slowly. Even the air on it seemed to hurt. Redd fetched a spare blanket from the chest without looking at the bed and passed it back over his shoulder. Marta folded it under Alaric's foot and unwound the filthy cloth from his ankle.

"It's worse," Alaric said.

"You've been running on it."

"Will I be able to walk?"

"Not on this, not yet." She went back to the swelling. "Rest it. We'll see how it is when you wake."

She bound it in fresh cloth, stopping whenever he needed her to.

Alaric let his head rest against the wall.

She helped him out of the patched outer trousers and the wet pair underneath, holding the bedcover across his lap while he changed. Something knocked against the bed frame as she folded them aside.

Gerolt's token.

He reached for the pocket before she could put the trousers down. Marta saw what he was doing and fished it out for him. She wiped it dry and set it on the bedcover beside his hand.

A dark line of filth had collected along the split in the shield. He rubbed at it once with his thumb, then stopped. There was fresh white cloth over the thumb now.

Marta opened the bundle Redd had carried in. Alaric's black clothes lay folded under the charcoal messenger's coat.

"These go with you. Keep them covered when you're outside. Nobody needs another look at that collar."

She wrapped them again and set them at the foot of the bed. His wet clothes went into a pail Redd brought from the kitchen. Alaric slid the token into the pocket of the borrowed trousers and started to lower his foot.

Marta caught his calf. "Keep it up."

He put his foot back on the blanket.

She stood and turned towards the window. "Now you, Silas."

"I'll manage."

Marta looked at the hand he couldn't close.

"Then manage those straps. Go on. Show me."

Silas's jaw shifted. He didn't try.

Redd took over at the window. Silas worked the glove off his left hand with his teeth, laid it on the table, unbuckled the sword belt one-handed and set it by the hearth. Then he sat in the chair Marta had left, and only then did he let her unfasten the armour.

The dented breastplate caught under his arm. He dragged in a breath as she lifted it clear.

"Stay *still*," she snapped. "I'm not doing this twice."

She stacked the plates by the hearth and threw one of Redd's sacks over them, leaving the sheathed sword and its belt uncovered. Then she brought his right hand over the basin.

Alaric looked at the burned palm. Blisters had risen everywhere the iron had touched. One had broken, showing a raw strip beneath the fingers. Silas kept his eyes on the window while Marta cleaned round it.

"He couldn't move the grate any other way," Alaric said.

"I heard him," Marta said without looking up. "He can answer for himself. Keep that foot still."

Silas glanced over and gave the smallest shake of his head. Alaric went quiet.

Marta worked with the same care she'd given Alaric's hands. When Silas flinched, she loosened the cloth. When he tried to help with his other hand, she pushed it away.

"No glove over this," she said as she tied off the dressing. "If it sticks to the burn, I won't be there to peel it off you."

"I'll leave it alone." He drew the hand back carefully. His face had gone grey under the stubble.

Redd looked back from the shutter. "There's a coat on the hook. No badge on it. That ought to be an improvement."

Silas looked at the patched brown wool. "You'll want it back."

"Only if you improve it. I've spent three winters meaning to mend those elbows."

The smile got out of Alaric before he could decide whether he was allowed it. Redd caught it and smiled back, then turned to the lane again. Marta helped Silas guide the bandaged hand down the loose sleeve.

---

The kettle was murmuring by the time Marta had packed the dressings away. Sunlight had reached the top of the side window.

Alaric watched it creep across a crack in the plaster. Nothing followed it in. No order came through the door. Redd lifted the kettle off the hearth with a folded cloth and filled three cups, then found a fourth behind a row of jars. It had a broken handle. He kept that one for himself.

Marta shared out bread and cheese from the provisions and set Alaric's on a little board beside him. Silas got up from the chair by the bed, and she sat down in it herself.

Silas carried the short-legged chair to the window, nudging the leather back under it with his boot. He'd left the borrowed coat open to give his bandaged hand room. He fetched the sheathed sword from the hearth and put it where his left hand could reach it.

Redd sat on the chest with his cup and his bread. Wena lifted her head.

"Oh, you've noticed this." Redd looked from her to the bread. "You slept through everything useful."

"Wena," Alaric murmured, as her tail thumped the bed frame. She glanced at him, then went straight back to Redd.

"Does she answer to that?"

"Usually."

"There's a fellow near the west gate with a dog like her. Tells everyone it's trained. He shouts 'sit' after it sits and 'come' after it's got to him. I watched them for half an hour once. He was never wrong."

Alaric looked down. Wena had slid one paw towards the chest without getting up.

"Stay," he said.

Redd grinned into his cup. The laugh hurt Alaric's ribs, and it got out anyway, barely more than air. He pressed a hand to his side.

Marta looked up sharply.

"I'm all right," he told her.

This time it was true enough.

Redd waited until he'd lowered his hand, then broke off a small piece of bread and glanced at Marta.

"A little," she said. "Then let her rest."

Wena took it with surprising care. Redd showed her his empty hand while keeping the rest of his portion well out of reach. She checked round him anyway before settling back beside the bed.

For a while, nobody talked. Alaric could hear Silas swallow. An insect worried at the side window, vanishing into the shadow of the frame and coming back to the same patch of light. The kettle ticked softly as it cooled.

He finished his bread and realised he'd been listening for the sound of a key.

There was no lock on Redd's door. A wooden bar leaned beside the frame, its brackets turned on their side.

Marta reached into the basket for another cloth-wrapped portion. She opened it halfway. Bread, and a small packet of dried apple.

She stopped. Her thumb stayed under the knot.

Alaric watched her fold the cloth back over it.

"For Darcy?"

Marta nodded without looking up.

"She used to pick the apple out of anything I baked. Said it spoiled the rest." Her thumb smoothed the fold for no reason. "I kept putting it in anyway. I thought she could have these on the road."

Alaric remembered Darcy's hand holding the quill over the map. The way she hadn't looked up.

"She told me to tell you something."

Marta's eyes lifted.

"She said you'd have packed food for her. That there'd be apple in it." He tried to get it exactly right. "She said, 'There's always apple.' And to tell you she's sorry she won't get to take it out."

For a heartbeat, Marta only looked at him.

Then she laughed. One short, astonished sound, the way you'd laugh at a friend who had managed to be exactly themselves at the worst possible moment.

"Of *course* she did," Marta said. "Of course she —"

The laugh broke in the middle.

She put her hand over her mouth. Her shoulders came forward. She didn't make a sound after that, but her whole body shook with it, and the wrapped portion slid off her lap onto the floor.

Redd got up quietly and went into the kitchen. Silas turned his face to the shutter.

Alaric didn't know what to do. His hands were bound. He couldn't get off the bed. So he sat there and stayed, because that was the only thing he had.

Wena got up. She nosed the fallen bundle, then pushed her head into Marta's lap and left it there.

After a long time, Marta's hand came down onto Wena's head.

"Silly girl," she whispered. Alaric couldn't tell whether she meant the dog.

She wiped her face with the heel of her hand, picked up the portion, and put it back in the basket without opening it again.

When Redd came back from the kitchen with nothing in his hands, nobody mentioned it.

"She helped me," Alaric said. "After she said no. She told me where to look."

Marta's voice was rough. "For what happened to you?"

He nodded. His fingers found the edge of the token through the borrowed trousers.

"There's something inside the machines on the farm. An orb. She called it a war core. If I can find one that isn't broken, her father might be able to tell where it came from."

"Helmi," Marta said quietly.

"In Favale."

Marta leaned back in the chair. Her eyes rested on the basket.

"Did she tell you how to find him?"

"Yes." Alaric looked at Silas. "I need to go back to the farm."

"You need to lie down," Silas said, turning from the window.

"I know. After."

"We came through the trees because the road was being watched. You think they've left the farm alone?"

"No."

Alaric looked at the swelling above the ankle bandage. He'd still have to walk there.

"She said an unbroken one. I don't know if there is one." He looked up. "But someone can read it if I find it. Until now, I didn't even know what I was looking for."

Silas looked at Marta. She was watching Alaric.

"Not before dark," she said at last. "You rest till then. That foot stays up and you leave those wrappings alone."

"I'll rest."

"And you don't go there by yourself."

He hadn't meant to look at Silas so fast. He tried to look away, but Silas had already seen it.

Marta set her cup down. "Silas. I need you to take him."

Silas looked at his bandaged hand. "If they've put a company on that field, I can't get him through it."

"Then find out what's there before you walk him into it. I'm asking you to get him to that farm and back out alive. Not to fight everyone standing on it."

"And if there's no way in?"

"Then there's no way in." Her voice hardened. "You tell him so, and you find somewhere to wait. You don't decide the risk is smaller because he can't judge it for himself."

Silas held her look. His jaw shifted. He glanced at Alaric's ankle before he answered.

"Then we wait. I won't take him in blind." He turned to Alaric. "We go when the light's gone. I look at the roads before we get anywhere near the farm. If I tell you we can't get closer, you listen."

"I will."

"And you don't go looking while I'm asleep."

Alaric looked at the foot propped on Redd's blanket. "I don't think you'd have much trouble catching me."

Silas's mouth moved very slightly. "I'd rather not find out."

Across the room, Redd brushed the crumbs off his palms.

"What happened on this farm?"

He'd stopped smiling, but it was curiosity in his face, not suspicion.

"A battle," Alaric said. "What was left of one. There were bodies everywhere."

"Near here?"

"East," Marta said. "My uncle's land."

Redd looked towards the shutter. "I've heard nothing about it."

"They don't want you to," Silas said.

"How many?" Redd asked.

The room was small and warm around him. Alaric tried to keep the answer inside it.

"Thousands. Humans. Elves. Others too."

Redd's eyes came back from the window. "Elves?"

Alaric nodded.

Redd looked at the sword above his shoulder. Only briefly. When he turned back, the ease had gone out of his face.

"And now they're hunting you."

"Yes."

Redd rubbed his palms on his knees and stood up. "Let me come with you."

Silas watched him. "Why?"

"Because I want to see it." Redd was looking at Alaric now, not the sword. "All that, this close, and they think they can keep everyone from knowing. I've spent the whole morning getting out of their way. I'd like to know what they're so desperate to keep from us."

"There may be soldiers everywhere you want to look," Silas said.

"I know how to stay out of sight."

Silas raised his bandaged hand. "So do I."

Some of the eagerness went out of Redd. His mouth tightened.

"I know," he said. "I'm not saying it's safe."

Marta looked at him across the table. "You've got a home here, Redd."

"It'll still be here when I get back."

"Getting back is what worries me."

He looked round the room: the patched shutter, the short chair, the cups.

"It worries me too," he said. "But I'd still like to go. I can carry the supplies, at least. Neither of them ought to be doing that."

Alaric waited for someone to explain what Redd wanted in return. Nothing came.

"You don't have to," he said.

"I know. That's why I'm asking."

Redd stayed by the chest, waiting. Alaric hadn't expected anyone to offer, not after seeing the state of them.

"I'd like you to come," he said. "I just don't want you thinking we know how to do this."

Redd glanced at Silas. "I hadn't got that impression."

"You stay with us," Silas told him. "If something interests you, you tell me before you walk towards it."

"Fair enough."

Redd gathered up the cups and carried them into the kitchen, leaving his father's sword on its pegs.

---

Marta left the rest of the provisions on Redd's table. Darcy's portion stayed wrapped among them. She divided the clean cloth and showed Silas which dressings were for his hand. He listened without interrupting once.

"You need sleep as well," she told him. "Take turns. Neither of you is watching anything properly once you've nodded off in a chair."

Redd dried his hands on a cloth. "I'll take over when he wakes me. I know what belongs on that lane and what doesn't."

Silas nodded, but he didn't leave the window.

Marta moved the clothing bundle from the foot of the bed to the chest so Alaric could stretch out. Wena got up with great reluctance while Redd pulled the bedcover straight. He set the folded blanket back under Alaric's ankle as Alaric lay down, then drew the cover over him.

Pain settled under his right shoulder. He shifted once, then stopped fighting the mattress.

Marta stood beside the bed with the empty basket on her arm. Her eyes were still red at the edges.

"Will they let you back in?" he asked.

"I went out through the workers' postern with a provision cart. I'll come back through it with one."

"They might ask where you've been."

"People have been asking me questions all morning." Her face softened a little. "Let me worry about mine."

Alaric looked at the covered bundle on the chest.

"Thank you. For bringing those. And Wena."

Marta looked down at the dog waiting by the bed.

"Take better care of each other on this road."

"I'll try."

She nodded, as though that was the answer she'd wanted.

Redd walked her out to the cart. Alaric heard the wheels start, catch on something, and roll on. Marta said something too quietly to make out. Redd answered, and her footsteps went on without his. As the sound faded, Alaric's eyes slid shut.

A little later, Redd came back in and dropped the wooden bar into its brackets.

**Tok.**

Alaric's eyes opened.

Redd was still inside. Silas was still at the window. The sound hadn't put anyone out of reach.

Wena laid her chin on the edge of the mattress. Alaric let his hand down beside her. Her nose touched the fresh cloth, warm and dry. He kept his fingers there until she settled on the floor, close enough that he could feel her breathing against them.

"Wake me before you go," he murmured.

Silas turned from the shutter.

"We're not leaving you here, Alaric."

He'd meant before they left for the farm. He thought he had.

He nodded anyway.

Redd started clearing the table. The basin emptied in the back yard. Cups touched the kitchen shelf. Silas shifted in his chair, and the short leg knocked softly against the floor.

"You've knocked it off the leather," Redd said.

"Fix the bloody leg," Silas muttered.

"I will. You can hold it steady for me when that hand works."

Silas said something too low for Alaric to catch. Redd laughed quietly.

Alaric could have asked what he'd said. He could have lifted his head and checked the window again.

He let his eyes close instead.

For a while he followed the voices moving round Redd's cottage. When he started losing the words, he let them go.
