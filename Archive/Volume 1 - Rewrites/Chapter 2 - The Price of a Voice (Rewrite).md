# The Price of a Voice

The fourth knock faded into the wood.

There was no fifth.

The boy stayed crouched behind the overturned bench with Gerolt's hand clamped on his shoulder. Neither of them moved. Even Wena had gone so quiet under the bed that he caught himself wondering, stupidly, whether she'd stopped breathing.

Outside, the torch hissed. Light trembled between the shutters and didn't move on. Whoever stood beyond that door was still there. Still waiting.

Then the layered voice spoke again.

Those same impossible words, sliding over one another, several tones crammed into a single breath. The boy couldn't find where any of it began or ended. Beside him, Gerolt gave no sign he understood a word.

The voice broke off. When it came back, the layering was gone.

"Farmer. Open the door. Tell us what happened in your field, and no other household need answer for your silence."

The boy understood every word of that. Each one was clean and exact and effortless, and each one dripped with the distaste of someone who thought talking to the people inside was already more courtesy than they'd earned.

A man's voice. Young, maybe. Very used to being obeyed.

Gerolt's hand left his shoulder.

*Stay*, he mouthed.

He crept away on hands and knees, keeping low beneath the thin bars of torchlight. At the dining table, he worked his fingers under the edge of a floorboard and pulled. The plank came up without a creak. There was a hollow underneath.

Gerolt reached in and drew out a long bundle of oil-darkened cloth. It fell open across his knees.

A sword.

Not a farm tool hammered into the rough shape of one, either. The leather on its grip had been worn smooth by a lot of hands-on years. Nicks scarred the guard. The blade was narrow and dark and clean, and its edge caught what little moonlight reached the floor.

Gerolt picked it up like the years since he'd last held it hadn't happened.

The boy stared.

The grumbling old farmer who'd hauled him through the wheat was gone. Or maybe this man had been underneath him the whole time.

Beyond the door, metal clicked softly against metal.

"Every moment you keep us waiting is added to your village's debt." Quieter now. The boy could still hear every word through the wood.

Gerolt didn't answer. He rose beside the table and took two steps towards the door, sword held low against his leg.

Then the ash moved.

It lifted off the dead hearth in thin grey threads. Then every loose grain in the cabin rushed towards the door at once, sucked forward like water down a drain.

Gerolt saw it too.

"Down—!"

He threw himself sideways against the wall beside the doorway, wedging his body behind the thick timber post and locking his free arm around it.

The world punched inward.

**KRAAACK.**

The sound hit the boy before he even saw the wood split.

The iron latch ripped free. The hinges shrieked out of the frame. The whole door came across the cabin in one piece, spinning through the dark with enough force to flatten anything in its way.

The boy hurled himself flat.

Its lower edge passed so close it combed splinters through his hair. It slammed into the back wall and burst apart, sending broken planks scything across the room.

Then the wind came in after it.

The bench tore out of his hands. The table flipped onto its side. Pots ripped off their hooks and flew across the cabin like thrown stones. A shutter wrenched loose and vanished into the night. The blanket wrapped itself around the boy's legs, then was torn away completely.

By the doorway, Gerolt's coat snapped and cracked into the room. He clung to the post with his head tucked down behind it.

The boy jammed his fingers into the gaps between the floorboards and held on.

For three deafening heartbeats, the cabin belonged to the wind.

Then everything dropped.

Wind『Affinity』.

That was what Gerolt had asked him to reach for, beside the fire. Not some quiet idea you talked about over stew. A force that could rip a home apart before the people inside it had the breath to scream.

An iron pot hit the floor and spun in widening circles.

The boy lifted his head.

A tall, slender figure stood in the empty doorway, framed by torchlight. His hair looked silver under the moon, bound neatly back from a face far too fine and unmarked to belong anywhere near a wrecked farmhouse. Dark armour covered him in close-fitting layers, the metal traced with curling lines that caught the torchlight like veins of gold.

Two narrow, pointed ears rose through his pale hair.

He looked over the wreckage. There was no alarm on his face at all. Just mild displeasure, like someone who'd stepped in something.

"Human," he called into the dark. "You were offered the mercy of answering alone."

He stepped over the ruined threshold.

Gerolt came up from behind the post at his side. No warning, no shout, no challenge.

The sword went into the soft hollow under the stranger's jaw.

Gerolt drove it upward through the neck until the guard hit flesh and the point burst out beneath the elf's other ear. The elf's mouth opened around a sound that never came. Blood spilled over his bottom teeth and poured out of both corners of his mouth.

He grabbed Gerolt's wrist and squeezed, hard enough that the farmer's knuckles went white around the hilt. His eyes were huge. He stared at Gerolt with his mouth still working, as if he were waiting for an explanation.

Then he tried to breathe.

Air bubbled through the wound. A thick, wet clicking filled his throat, and blood ran down the blade and into the collar of his armour.

The boy pressed himself harder into the floor. He couldn't stop watching the elf's mouth, waiting for a breath that wasn't coming.

Gerolt wrenched the sword sideways. Something inside gave with a wet, stringy tear, and the elf's grip fell open.

Outside, someone screamed. The layered language didn't sound patient anymore.

A second figure stood beside the horses with one hand thrust out towards the cabin. The same dark armour, lighter, under a grey travelling cloak. Pale hair had come loose across a face twisted with disbelief, and her pointed ears lay flat against her head.

The earth answered her.

The boy felt it before he understood it: a shudder running through the boards under his chest. One plank bowed upward. Then the next. Something was travelling beneath the cabin, splitting the floor in a straight line.

Straight towards Gerolt's back.

And Gerolt was still fighting to drag his sword out of the dead elf's neck, both hands locked on the hilt, his back to it all.

Silence would keep the boy hidden.

"GEROLT!"

The name ripped out of him.

Gerolt twisted towards the shout as the floor bucked under his feet. He couldn't get clear.

His body folded around something, and the boy didn't understand what until blood flooded down his coat.

Stone.

A jagged spear of it had punched up through the boards and buried itself under his ribs. For one hideous instant the wound gaped around a white curve of bone, then filled with blood. The stone snapped, leaving a broken length of it lodged deep in his side.

Gerolt screamed.

It was a raw, animal sound. Nothing like the voice that had told the boy to mind the hot bowl, or called him a heavy little shite. It filled the wrecked cabin and broke apart into a breathless groan as his knees buckled.

The dead elf slumped against his shoulder.

Gerolt tore his blade free and let the body's weight carry him stumbling forward through the doorway. The woman threw up her other hand and the ground split open between them, but Gerolt was already across it before it could widen.

His sword crossed her face.

The edge split her left brow, crushed through the eye beneath it and tore across the bridge of her nose, leaving her cheek hanging open to the teeth. Blood sheeted down her face. Her scream climbed until the voices layered inside it came apart from each other.

Gerolt swung again. She threw up an arm, and the blade broke straight through her metal bracer and bit into the bone. Her hand jerked once, then hung uselessly off the ruined limb.

Then the earth rose between them.

A slab of soil and rock slammed into Gerolt's chest and threw him back through the doorway. He hit the floor hard enough to shake the whole cabin.

The boy scrambled towards him.

Outside, the wounded scout lurched blindly to one of the horses, one arm swinging broken at her side. She caught the saddle with the other and tried to haul herself up. Her boot slipped in the blood running off her face. Slipped again. At last she dragged herself across the horse's back and found the stirrup.

Gerolt rolled onto his stomach and reached for his sword. His fingers found the hilt. The strength to get up didn't come with them.

The horse wheeled away. Hooves tore through the wheat, carrying the woman and her ragged screaming off into the night.

Gerolt watched until the torchlight vanished with her. Then he let his forehead rest against the floor.

A cough ripped through him. Dark blood spattered the boards under his mouth.

"Fucking elves," he rasped.

The boy reached him. Gerolt tried to turn over and stopped halfway with a gasp, caught on his side.

Blood was spreading out beneath him, running along the seams between the boards. The broken tooth of stone was still buried under his ribs, shifting with every shallow breath. Blood welled up around it whenever he moved.

The boy reached for it.

"Don't." Gerolt caught his wrist with a slick, shaking hand. "Leave it where it is."

So the boy bunched up a loose fold of Gerolt's coat and pressed it against the blood welling around the stone. Gerolt hissed through his teeth, but his hand settled over the boy's instead of pushing it away.

"Why did they come here?" The cloth was already soaking through under the boy's fingers.

"Natharul scouts." Gerolt swallowed, and every line in his face tightened with it. "Saw the field, maybe. Saw my light." His breath snagged on the stone, and he waited, teeth clenched, until he could speak again. "Doesn't matter now. She got away."

His eyes went to the dark beyond the doorway. The dead elf's horse was turning in circles under its empty saddle, snorting at the blood on the threshold.

The boy followed his gaze, and his stomach dropped.

"She heard me."

Gerolt looked at him.

"She heard me call your name." His breathing was coming too fast now. "She knows you weren't alone. Gerolt, what do I —"

"I know." It broke into a cough. Gerolt curled around the stone until it passed, blood shining between his teeth.

Before the boy could get another word out, a horn sounded from the direction the scout had fled. Low, long, and much closer than it should have been.

Another answered it from beyond the battlefield.

Then a third, so far off that the note reached them thin and wavering.

Gerolt shut his eyes.

"How many?" the boy whispered.

"Enough."

Under the bed, Wena started to whine.

It turned into frantic scratching, and then she was crawling out through the wreckage on her belly, ears pinned flat, creeping towards Gerolt. Her nose touched the blood soaking his coat. She flinched back from it, then pressed herself against his arm, trembling so hard her teeth clicked.

Gerolt laid one bloody hand on her head. His thumb moved once between her eyes, along the path of some old habit.

Wena licked the blood off his knuckles. He turned his hand over so she couldn't do it again.

"No, girl," he whispered. "Not this time."

The first arrow came in through the open doorway and hit the back wall with a hard wooden *crack*, level with the boy's head.

He ducked over Gerolt, his shoulder brushing the soaked coat. A second arrow punched through the broken window and buried itself in the table. A third rattled across the roof.

The horses were coming now. Not a faint tremble in the floorboards anymore. Thunder, rolling in across the fields.

Gerolt forced one hand inside his coat.

"Boy. Listen." He waited until the boy looked at him.

He pulled out a small iron token, dark with age and worn almost smooth. A split shield was stamped into one side. On the other was a single word the boy couldn't read.

Gerolt pushed it into his palm. When the boy tried to reach back towards the wound, Gerolt didn't let go of his hand.

"West. Across the river." His jaw tightened around a breath that wouldn't come cleanly. "Kelmend. Find the inn… near the southern gate."

The boy stared down at the token. Gerolt was telling him how to leave. He hadn't said a word about coming with him.

"Ask for Marta. Give her that." Gerolt coughed into his shoulder, and when he lifted his head there was fresh blood in his beard. "Tell her… Gerolt sent you."

"You're coming with me." The boy looked at the door, then back at the stone under Gerolt's ribs. "We'll go together."

Gerolt let out a short breath that might once have been on its way to a laugh. It collapsed into a cough instead, and blood ran over his lip.

"It's noth—"

He didn't have the breath to finish the lie.

His eyes moved around the ruined cabin. The broken table. The dead hearth. The stool on its side. The boy could still see him sitting there with his mug, asking questions nobody could answer.

"Can't walk," Gerolt said. "Look at me, lad."

"Then I'll carry you." The boy slid his free arm behind Gerolt's shoulders and tried to lift him. Gerolt cried out and clutched at the stone, and the boy lowered him again at once.

"You could barely hold yourself up this afternoon." Gerolt caught his sleeve before he could try again. "Don't, lad."

Another arrow thudded into the cabin wall. Then another.

Gerolt folded the boy's fingers closed around the token.

"Take Wena."

The dog lifted her head at her name.

"I can help you." The boy pressed his free hand against the folded coat again, even though the cloth was soaked right through. "Just tell me what to do."

"You already did," Gerolt said. His voice barely carried over the hoofbeats.

The boy shook his head.

This man had carried him out of a field of corpses. Washed the blood off his face. Fed him. Given him a place by the fire when he had nothing — not even a name he could offer in return.

He looked at the door. Then at the blood on his own hands. If he left, Gerolt would still be lying right here.

"I'm not leaving you." His voice shook. He pressed down harder on the coat, as if Gerolt might believe him if he could only make the bleeding stop.

"Wasn't asking." Gerolt held his eyes until the boy had to look away.

The hoofbeats grew louder.

Torchlight appeared between the wheat stalks beyond the doorway. One flame. Then another.

Gerolt saw them and reached for his sword. His hand closed around the grip. The blade scraped across the boards and wouldn't lift.

The boy grabbed his arm.

"Please."

Gerolt looked at him, and the hard lines of his face eased a little.

"On that field…" He dragged in a thin, shaking breath. "Found one lad still breathing."

His bloody fingers tightened weakly on the boy's sleeve.

"Don't make me die watching you stop."

Wena pushed her muzzle under Gerolt's hand. He stroked her once more, slowly this time, from between her ears all the way down the back of her neck.

"She'll stay if you do," he said. "That's why you have to take her."

The boy looked at the dog curled against Gerolt's side, then at the blood spreading out beneath them both.

"I can't." He shook his head. But Wena had started nosing at his hand too, searching him for some comfort he didn't know how to give.

"Stubborn little bastard," Gerolt murmured. "All that emptiness, and still no room in you for sense."

The pain reached the boy before the voice did.

It drove in through the back of his skull and hooked itself behind his eyes. His spine arched. His teeth slammed together hard enough to fill his mouth with blood. His heart hit the inside of his head like a battering ram.

**THOOM.**

He tried to scream — and heard the sound before his lips had even opened.

The cabin came apart.

The door hung whole on its hinges *and* lay shattered against the wall. The candle burned on the table *and* died between Gerolt's fingers. A warm wooden bowl sat in the boy's hands, hit the floor, and was whole again.

Gerolt stood over him, unhurt.

Gerolt bled out beneath his knees.

The images pressed through each other, one showing through the next like bones under skin.

The pain twisted deeper. Every nerve he had pulled in a different direction. Light forced its way under his fingernails, through his teeth, into the hinges of his jaw. His free hand clawed at the floor until his nails split against the wood. The token bit into his other palm, and he couldn't unclench it.

White opened up through the middle of everything.

A single thread of light stretched across it, trembling under some impossible strain.

At its furthest end, something moved.

Then a voice reached him. Only a piece of one.

*"——ric…"*

It passed straight through him and was gone.

The white convulsed. Pressure swelled inside his skull until he could feel his eyes straining in their sockets. His throat opened around a scream, and what came out sounded like it belonged to someone impossibly far away.

*"——Ala…"*

The fragments tore at the hollow place inside him.

He reached for them.

Agony ripped from his fingertips to the base of his spine. His body slammed against the floor. Gerolt caught the back of his head before it could hit again, and the effort dragged a strangled cry out of the farmer's ruined chest.

He still didn't let go.

The voice forced its way through one more time.

And this time, it arrived whole.

*"——Alaric."*

The name dropped into the place where his past should have been.

He braced for it to vanish, the way everything else had.

It didn't.

It *caught*.

He didn't remember a face turning towards it. A hand writing it. Anyone calling it across a room. But every scattered piece of him answered to it anyway.

*Alaric.*

The thread snapped.

The cabin crashed back in around him. Arrows. Hoofbeats. Wena's frightened whining. Gerolt's blood, warm under his knees.

The boy braced his free hand on the floor and retched bloody spit onto the boards. Pain pulsed behind his eyes with every heartbeat. He could barely see.

But the name was still there.

He lifted his head.

"My name," he whispered.

Gerolt stared at him, one wet hand still cradling the back of his skull.

"I know my name." The boy's fist tightened around the iron token. "It's Alaric."

For the first time since he'd opened his eyes in the mud, the word *I* had somewhere to stand.

Gerolt's lips moved before his voice caught up with them.

"Alaric," he said.

It came out soft, roughened by blood and pain. And then the old farmer's mouth pulled into a tired smile.

"There you are."

An arrow whipped through the doorway and buried itself in the wall.

Gerolt turned towards it. He planted one hand on the floor and forced himself up. The stone shifted under his ribs, and blood ran freely now, down over his hip and into his boot, but he dragged the sword up with him.

Outside, the horns sounded again.

Closer.

Gerolt raised the blade in both hands. His first try at a breath failed. He dragged in another.

"Take Wena and run, Alaric."
