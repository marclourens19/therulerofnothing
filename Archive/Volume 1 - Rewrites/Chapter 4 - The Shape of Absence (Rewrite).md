# The Shape of Absence

Thaeroval didn't move.

Seralune still had her arms around him, her cheek pressed to the cold plates over his chest. Under the armour, he was breathing too fast. Every breath lifted her a hair's width, then left him stiffer than before.

His eyes stayed on the broken circle.

"Thaer?" She loosened her arms and tried to see what he was staring at.

A tremor went through him. It started somewhere under her hands and ran up into his shoulders, and the point of the sword hanging at his side scraped a thin line across the stone.

She drew back far enough to see his face properly.

"What is it?" she asked. "You're frightening me."

That brought his eyes down to her at last. He fumbled at his hip once before the sword found its sheath. Then he reached for her — one hand touching her shoulder, stopping, moving to her back — and the other followed, and he pulled her into him.

It drove the air out of her. One arm locked around her shoulders. His other hand closed over the back of her head and pressed her face in under his chin, and his armour dug into her ribs hard enough to hurt. She tried to say something. His hold tightened before she could.

"Seralune."

His voice broke against her hair. He didn't say anything else.

She felt his breath on the crown of her head. Once. Again. Neither one was steady.

She put both hands flat against his chest and waited.

Thaer had held her before. Before he rode out to the border. Before ceremonies he knew she hated. Once, after a physician had confused cruelty with honesty and told her that all the mana in the world could not make an『Affinity』where there wasn't one.

Never like this.

"You're hurting me," she whispered.

His arms opened at once.

"Forgive me."

He didn't let go completely. His hands stayed on her shoulders, and his eyes went over her face — her brow, her mouth, the line of her jaw — like he'd stopped trusting that she was what he remembered.

"How long have you been awake?"

She glanced back at the dark behind her.

"I don't know. A while." Her throat hurt when she swallowed. "I couldn't see anything. I called for you until someone came."

His eyes dropped to the dust on her hands, then to her bare feet under the hem of her dress.

"Did you touch the circle?"

He asked it so fast that she looked behind her again.

"I woke up in it. I had to feel my way out."

His face went tight and closed.

She looked past him. Beyond the broken doorway was a stone passage she didn't recognise. White crystals had been set into the ceiling, their light too pale and too steady to be flame. It caught on his armour and the sword hilt at his hip. The edges of the ruined doorway were clean under the fallen dust, cut as if with a knife.

"Where am I, Thaer?"

Running footsteps answered before he could.

He turned, and he changed. His shoulders settled. The sword came free again. He stepped between her and the doorway in one smooth movement, the kind a body makes so often it stops needing a thought behind it.

People crowded the passage. Dark armour. Grey robes. A glint of gold at throats and wrists. The first guards reached the opening with weapons drawn and stopped so suddenly that the ones behind walked into their backs.

Nobody came in.

They looked past Thaer, and found her.

Seralune had stood in front of packed courts without lowering her eyes. She knew what admiration looked like, and curiosity, and the careful little disappointment that crossed people's faces whenever they learned what she couldn't do.

This was none of those.

One guard's knuckles whitened around his spear. A woman behind him put a hand over her mouth. Somebody started whispering the first words of a warding prayer.

"Lower your weapons."

The order came from further down the passage. The crowd split, and a man walked through it.

Black robes under a mantle of deep green, the borders worked in the branching gold she'd seen on every member of the royal court since she was small. A narrow circlet across his brow. Not a crown, but close enough to one that people moved out of his way before he had to ask.

She knew the way he held his mouth when something displeased him. She knew his ears were a little uneven, the right one sitting closer to his head than the left. She remembered ink all over those fingers, and one summer afternoon spent hiding from their tutors under the western stairs.

But there was silver at his temples. Fine lines around his eyes. Not the creases of a bad night or a long illness. Lines that belonged there. Time had moved into his face and made itself at home.

"Leorin?"

Her cousin stopped. For an instant, his composure slipped. His lips parted, and something moved through his eyes — warm, wounded, gone too fast for her to catch.

Then he bowed.

"Your Highness."

Two words, and he'd put more distance between them than the length of the chamber.

She looked at his hair again. *What happened to you?*

She almost asked. But Leorin had already turned to Thaer.

"How long has she been conscious?"

"Ask her," Thaeroval said. He stayed exactly where he was, between Seralune and the guards.

Leorin looked back at her, and the silence waited for her to fill it.

"I don't know," she said. "Why does everyone keep asking me that?"

Neither of them answered. Leorin took a step towards the doorway.

Thaer's sword came across the opening. It didn't touch him. It didn't need to.

Leorin stopped.

"Has she used her mana?"

"I'm standing *right here*," Seralune said, and stepped out beside her brother where Leorin couldn't pretend not to see her. "Ask me."

His eyes came back to her, and something in them softened. His face didn't.

"Have you used your mana?" he asked. Gentler. But he watched her hands while he waited.

She looked from him to Thaer.

"I tried to make light." Her fingers curled into her dress as the people in the corridor went very still. "It didn't work."

Relief went through the guards so quietly that she might have imagined it. She'd seen that same relief on physicians, whenever her failures proved they'd understood the world correctly after all.

It had never frightened her before.

Leorin turned to the chamber. From where he stood, he could see only part of the pattern cut into the floor. It was enough.

"Close the lower passages," he said. "No one enters without my authority."

Orders rippled behind him. Guards turned. Robes swept the stone.

"And send for the wardwrights. Every wardwright in the palace."

"No."

Thaer said it quietly, and every sound in the passage stopped.

Leorin looked at the blade between them.

"The circle is broken," he said. "They need to examine it."

"No one touches my sister." Thaer's grip tightened on the hilt.

Leorin looked past the sword at Seralune, and back at him. "Then take her upstairs. But let them through."

Neither man moved. The guards waited with their eyes on the blade.

Then Thaer lowered it and held his other hand out to her.

"Come with me."

She didn't take it.

"Where am I?"

"Beneath the palace." His voice softened as his eyes went to her bare feet. "Let me get you somewhere warm. Please."

Thaer had commanded armed companies before breakfast. He'd shut up their father's entire council with one look. She couldn't remember the last time she'd heard him ask anyone for anything.

She put her hand in his. His fingers closed around hers, gently, and didn't loosen once as he led her out.

---

The passage climbed.

Seralune felt every step in her knees. Thaer slowed each time her weight leaned into his arm, though she never asked him to. Behind them, Leorin was speaking low to the guards, his words blurring under boots and the soft whisper of Thaer's sword going back into its sheath.

At the first turning, Thaer murmured something to one of the guards, and the man hurried ahead without once looking at her.

None of it was the palace she knew. The walls were too bare. There were no windows onto the inner courtyards. At every turn, pale crystals burned inside cages of black metal, filling the air with a faint hum she could feel in her teeth.

They passed under one arch. Then another. The third had the royal tree carved into its stone.

Seralune stopped.

She knew that tree. Its roots were supposed to divide around four stars, one for each of the old houses that had first bent the knee to her family.

These roots circled nine. New branches crowded its crown.

"Thaer. That's wrong." She held onto his hand when he tried to keep walking. "There were only four stars."

He followed her eyes to the carving. His thumb moved once across the back of her hand.

"You remember it correctly." He glanced at the guards behind them. "We'll talk when we're alone."

He drew her on. She looked back over her shoulder until the arch disappeared behind Leorin and the guards.

The corridors past it started to look like home. The stone lightened first. Dark floors gave way to pale marble veined with green. Tall windows opened onto gardens silvered by moonlight. The palace rose up around her in all its familiar bones — wearing a stranger's face.

The curtains were the wrong colour. Statues stood in alcoves that used to hold flowers. People she didn't know gazed down from painted walls, their faces arranged into the solemn dignity painters give the dead.

She searched every one of them for someone she knew.

She found no one.

Servants pressed themselves flat against the walls as she passed. Some bowed. Others forgot to, until the person beside them tugged them down.

An old man carrying folded linen dropped to both knees.

"Your Highness," he whispered.

The linen slid out of his arms. Tears ran down his face, and he didn't try to wipe them away.

She tried to turn towards him, but her knees chose that moment to fail. Thaer caught her against his arm and kept walking, and she had to go with him or fall.

They stopped at a door carved with stone lilies. Thaer opened it himself, and warm air touched her face.

A fire burned behind a brass screen. Lamps softened the corners of a room laid out for someone who hadn't arrived yet: water steaming in a basin, nightclothes folded on the bed, a tray of fruit and bread by the hearth.

Somehow that frightened her more than the strange halls had.

"How did they know I was coming here?" she asked, watching the steam curl off the basin.

"I sent someone ahead." Thaer came in after her and waited while she steadied herself on the bedpost.

A young woman stood by the bed in the grey and green of a royal attendant. Seralune had never seen her before. The woman bowed without lifting her eyes.

"She'll stay with you," Thaer said.

"Where's Elowen?"

The name meant nothing to the attendant. Nothing at all.

Something tightened beside Thaer's mouth and was gone almost before it came.

Before he could answer, voices gathered in the corridor. Leorin had arrived.

"I need to speak to him," Thaer said, looking at the door.

"I asked you about Elowen, Thaer." She watched his face, waiting for the name to land somewhere.

"I heard you." His hand covered hers on the bedpost. "I'll come back."

She caught his wrist before he could turn away.

"Before evening?"

The old promise came out of her before she understood why she'd reached for it.

His face changed. Not fear this time. Something quieter, and worse.

He covered her hand with his own.

"Soon."

He freed himself gently, and left, and the door closed between them.

---

The attendant poured water into a silver cup. Her hands were shaking, and the pitcher tapped the rim with a small *clink*.

"What's your name?" Seralune asked gently.

The woman very nearly dropped the pitcher.

"Nereth, Your Highness."

"How long have you served my family, Nereth?"

"Since I was ten, Your Highness."

Seralune looked at her. A young woman who had been here long enough to grow up inside these walls, and Seralune had never once seen her face.

She took the cup. Nereth's fingers drew back before they could touch hers.

The water was cold against her teeth. She hadn't realised how thirsty she was until the first swallow hurt.

"Do you know Elowen?"

Nereth lowered the pitcher.

"No, Your Highness."

"She attends my rooms." Seralune waited for some flicker of recognition. "She was here yesterday."

Nereth's eyes lifted. For the briefest moment, Seralune saw pity in them. Then they dropped again.

"Forgive me, Your Highness. I've never heard anyone by that name."

Outside, Leorin's voice rose, not loud enough for words. Only the hard shape of an argument. Thaer answered more quietly, forcing Leorin down to meet him.

Seralune set the cup on the table and crossed the room.

Nereth watched her go and didn't try to stop her. Seralune stood beside the door and laid her fingertips against the wood.

The voices sharpened.

"You should have sent for the king the moment the chamber opened," Leorin said.

"It didn't *open*," Thaeroval said. "Something broke."

Seralune's hand pressed flatter against the door.

"Then he must be recalled. He left for Mydea before midnight. A messenger could still reach him before sunrise."

"No messenger leaves until I've spoken to her."

"You've posted guards outside her door and forbidden the wardwrights to touch her." Leorin's voice climbed again. "What am I to tell your father when he asks why no one informed him?"

Seralune looked back at Nereth. The attendant's face had gone perfectly empty.

"I'll answer him myself," Thaer said. "Give me until dawn."

"And if she remembers before then?"

Seralune leaned closer, until her hair brushed the wood.

A pause. Then: "She remembers her family. Nothing more."

"You don't know how long that will last."

Something hit the wall, not hard enough to break it, but hard enough to shake dust from the doorframe. Behind her, Nereth flinched.

"She is not something for you to examine."

"No. She's your sister." Every scrap of warmth left Leorin's voice. "That didn't stop what happened before."

The words hung there. Seralune waited for her brother's answer.

It never came.

Footsteps moved away from the door. Others followed. Orders passed down the corridor, too soft to make out.

Seralune stayed where she was.

*If she remembers.*

She went looking for the edge of whatever was missing.

The council bell. Her mother sending the pears back to the kitchens. Thaer promising to come back. The memories stood side by side without touching. Before them lay everything else — childhood, tutors, gardens, winters by the eastern hearth.

After them, there was nothing. No dark, no pain. Not even the sense that something had been taken.

Her life just stopped.

"Your Highness."

Nereth stood a few steps behind her, holding out the folded nightclothes. Seralune looked at the unfamiliar fabric.

"What did Leorin mean?"

Nereth's arms lowered.

"I don't know, Your Highness. I was sent to attend you. No one told me what had happened." Her fingers tightened on the folded cloth. She looked only a little less frightened than Seralune felt.

---

Seralune sat by the fire, still in the pale dress she'd woken up in. Nereth had combed the dust from her hair and washed the shallow cut across one palm. The food on the tray sat untouched.

Two knocks sounded on the door.

Thaer came in before she could answer. The old habit didn't loosen the knot in her chest at all.

Nereth bowed and moved towards the door.

"Stay," Seralune said.

The attendant stopped and looked from her to Thaer.

"Wait outside, Nereth," Thaeroval said. "I need to speak with my sister alone."

Nereth hesitated. Then she bowed to Seralune without meeting her eyes, and left, and the door closed.

Thaer went to the fire but didn't sit. Without the sword in his hand, he looked almost the way she remembered him — too serious, until someone gave him a reason not to be. A strand of dark hair had fallen loose across his brow.

She'd pushed that same strand off his face a hundred times.

Tonight, she kept her hands in her lap.

"How long was I in that chamber?"

Thaer looked into the fire.

"A thousand years."

The answer was too big to fit into the room.

She stared at his reflection in the brass screen.

"No." She turned from the reflection to him. "You were in my doorway *yesterday*. I was waiting for you to come back."

But Leorin's hair was silver. Nereth had grown up in this palace and never once heard Elowen's name.

She waited for him to take it back.

He didn't.

"How could I have been there that long?" Her voice was starting to shake. She knotted her fingers together to stop it. "Why was I there?"

Thaer crossed the room and went down on one knee in front of her.

"Before I answer, tell me what you remember of that day."

"I've already told you." She tried to keep the hurt out of her voice and failed completely. "You came to my rooms. Mother was fighting with the kitchens before that. Nothing happened."

"About the pears," he said softly. "They'd cooked them again."

She stared at him. For a moment, the two of them were standing inside the same memory.

"Yes. And you promised you'd be back before evening." She searched his face. "That's all I remember, Thaer. I don't remember going to bed. I don't remember anything after you left."

"Were you afraid?"

"Afraid of what?"

He shut his eyes, briefly. "I shouldn't have asked you that."

"Then why did you?" She leaned towards him. "What happened to me, Thaer?"

He reached for her hands. She let him take them and waited.

His palms were warm. A pale scar crossed the base of his right thumb. She didn't remember it.

"I'm afraid those memories could make your mana harder to control," he said. "It's already unstable. I don't know what forcing them back might do."

"To me?"

His fingers tightened.

"To anyone."

"And Mother? Where is she?"

He went still. She felt it happen in his fingers before she saw it anywhere else.

He looked down at their joined hands.

"I wish I knew," he said.

The room went very quiet.

"Is she dead?"

His head came up at once.

"Seralune, I didn't say that." He looked at her with so much pain that she had to look down at their hands instead.

She pulled one hand free. He let her.

"Leorin said there are guards outside."

"They're there to protect you."

"Would they let me leave?"

"No. I need you to stay here until morning."

The words were gentle. They still closed around her like a hand.

"I'm frightened, Thaer."

His whole face folded around that. He bowed his head until his forehead rested against the hand he was still holding.

For a while, neither of them said anything. She felt his breath on her knuckles. He stayed like that the way he used to when they were children, and one of them owed the other an apology neither could make themselves say out loud.

Her free hand drifted towards his hair. She stopped it halfway. She wanted to comfort him. She also wanted him to look up and answer her.

He lifted his head.

"I'll come back at first light," he said. "We'll talk again then."

"And you'll answer me?"

"I'll tell you what I can. Please, Seralune. Trust me."

*What I can.* Not everything. She heard the edge of it, even though he hadn't said it.

She looked into her brother's face.

He'd always been the one certainty in her life she never had to question. Whatever had happened, whatever Leorin believed, this was still Thaer.

"I trust you," she said.

Relief softened his whole face. And for the first time in her life, Seralune wished she were as sure of those words as he looked.

He stood and pulled her into his arms again. This time it didn't hurt. He pressed his lips to her forehead.

She almost asked him not to go. The words got as far as her throat, and stayed there.

Thaer crossed the room. Nereth was waiting outside the door. She came in as he left and put another log on the fire without looking at Seralune.

"Will you stay?" Seralune asked.

"Until I'm relieved, Your Highness." Nereth settled the brass screen back into place. Her voice had softened, though she still wouldn't look straight at her.

Seralune turned towards the window.

Behind her, the door closed.

On the other side, a lock turned.

---

She didn't sleep.

Nereth sat by the door with her hands folded in her lap. She didn't speak unless Seralune spoke first, and when she did, every answer seemed to pass through a careful little gate before it was allowed into the room.

Outside the window, Natharul waited in the dark.

The palace stood on a mountainside. She knew that much. She knew the black ridge beyond the eastern towers, and the white thread of water falling through the forest below. Everything between them had changed.

Lights covered slopes that used to be trees. Bridges crossed empty air between towers she'd never seen. Further down, the city spilled out past its old walls in terraces of silver and green, each level held against the mountain by columns of light.

She'd never seen Natharul so big. Or so strange.

Somewhere down there, a bell rang. Its note was higher than the one she remembered from the eastern tower, and it came from the west.

One. Two. Three.

She stopped counting.

Every time she closed her eyes, she tried to walk back through that day one moment at a time. The dress she'd picked for breakfast. The book left open by the window. The exact colour of the sky when the council bell rang.

And every time, Thaer left. And she could not remember a single thing she'd done after he was gone.

Below the floor, something heavy moved through stone. Faint at first. A dragging weight. A pause. Then three slow blows that trembled up through the legs of her chair.

Nereth looked at the floor.

"What are they doing?" Seralune asked.

"Repairing the lower chamber, I think, Your Highness."

"Why can't it wait until morning?"

Nereth kept her eyes down.

"I don't know, Your Highness."

Another blow came up through the stone. The lamps trembled, and Seralune reached inward without meaning to.

Her mana was everywhere.

It answered her fear the way it always had, limitless and instant, filling her before she'd even decided what she wanted from it. She tried to hold it still.

The crystals in the lamps flared brighter.

Nereth was on her feet so fast her chair hit the wall.

The light settled back to normal.

Seralune stared at the nearest lamp.

She hadn't shaped the light. She hadn't shaped *anything*.

"I'm sorry," she said.

Nereth stood with one hand pressed flat to the door, her face gone white.

"I didn't mean —"

The lock turned from the outside.

Guards came through before the door had finished opening. Steel appeared between Seralune and the fire. A wardwright in green robes followed them in, gripping a length of silver chain covered in tiny carved symbols.

Nereth hadn't called for them. The crash of her chair had been enough.

"Stop." Seralune raised both hands, staring at the blades.

She let the mana sink. It went down beneath her skin until only its impossible depth was left.

The lamps stayed dim.

Nobody lowered a weapon.

Then Nereth stepped in front of her, between Seralune and the nearest guard. Only after she'd done it did surprise show on her face, as if her feet had decided without asking her.

"Prince Thaeroval forbade this."

"Lord Leorin's orders govern this chamber," the wardwright said.

"The lamps brightened." Nereth's voice shook, but she didn't move. "That's all. She hasn't hurt anyone."

The wardwright looked past her at Seralune's hands.

"Did she shape anything?"

"No. I was watching her the whole time." Nereth's fingers twisted in the folds of her skirt.

The silver chain drew tight between the wardwright's hands.

"It was an accident," Seralune said, and lowered her hands slowly.

Nobody acknowledged that she'd spoken at all.

The wardwright watched the lamps. Their light stayed steady. At last the chain went slack in his fingers, and he stepped back.

"Wake Lord Leorin if it happens again."

The guards withdrew with him. The door closed. The lock turned.

Nereth went back to her chair and didn't make it. Her knees gave out a step short, and she caught the wooden arm with both hands and lowered herself the rest of the way down.

"Thank you," Seralune said, looking at the hands still clutching the chair. "For standing in front of me."

"They came in with swords, Your Highness. You hadn't hurt anyone." Nereth glanced at the door after she said it, as if someone might have heard.

Seralune looked back at the lamps.

"They're afraid of me."

Nereth didn't answer. This time, Seralune didn't make her.

The hours went on, and the fire sank low. Shortly before dawn, the lock turned again.

A guard stood in the open doorway.

"You're relieved."

Nereth got up, and paused beside Seralune's chair.

"Your Highness."

Seralune looked at her. The attendant seemed about to say something — but the guard shifted, armour scraping stone, and whatever Nereth had meant to say stayed behind her teeth.

She bowed and left. No one came to replace her. The door closed, and the lock turned again.

Seralune went back to the window. The dark pulled back from the mountain one shade at a time, uncovering the strange city underneath. Roofs first. Then bridges. Then the tallest branches of the forest, turning silver as the sun came closer.

None of it came with answers. No one had told her what broke under the palace, what she was supposed to remember, or where her mother had gone.

By the time the pale morning light reached the floor, she still hadn't slept.

Something heavy hit the stone outside her door.

She turned from the window.

Metal scraped across the corridor floor.

Then a second impact. Closer.

A short struggle. Cloth pulled tight. A boot striking stone, once.

Then nothing.

Seralune stood very still.

Footsteps came up to the door. Slow. Unhurried.

They stopped outside her room.

Two knocks.

Thaer had always knocked twice before coming in — as if the warning excused him from waiting to be let in.

The spacing was perfect.

This time, Seralune didn't answer.

The lock began to turn.
