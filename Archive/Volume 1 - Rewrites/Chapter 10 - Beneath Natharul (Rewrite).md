# Beneath Natharul

The bells had stopped reaching them hours ago.

The water hadn't.

It followed Seralune through the dark with a voice that changed every time the passage narrowed — a soft rush round her ankles, a hollow knocking somewhere under the stone, and every so often a single drop falling from somewhere too high for Nereth's flame to find.

**Tik.**

They walked until the next one came.

**Tik.**

It was never the same distance behind them twice.

Seralune looked back. Green light trembled over the channel they'd already crossed. It came from under the water rather than above it, thin threads caught in cracks in the floor, and it reached as far as the bend in the passage and stopped short of the dark where the palace was.

Nothing moved back there. No guards. No white flash of Thaer's sword. No old man stumbling after them with his sleeve ending where his forearm used to be.

Cyrandor's blood had dried into the hem of her cloak. Every step dragged the stiff cloth across her ankle.

Nereth stopped a few paces ahead.

"If you mean to turn back, Your Highness, do it before I forget which direction that is."

The flame over her palm leaned towards the current. It was barely bigger than a lampwick, but the low passage gathered its light and spread it across the water. Damp hair clung to the back of Nereth's neck. The bruise the palace guard had left was darkening round her upper arm.

Seralune faced forward again. "I was listening."

"To the water?"

"To whatever might be following us."

"If your brother decides to follow, Your Highness, listening won't help." Nereth's eyes went briefly to the stone above them. "Neither will running."

She walked on before Seralune could answer.

The palace had ended so gradually that Seralune couldn't have said where. First the metal cages had gone from the lamps. Then the lamps themselves had thinned out, until Nereth had raised one hand and made a small flame where the next should have been. The fitted floor gave way to stone worn into shallow grooves by years of water. Pipes went into the walls and didn't come out again. Tool marks softened, and then vanished under moss.

Now even the walls seemed unsure whether anyone had ever built them.

Roots crossed them in pale cords. Some sank into the mortar. Others went in one side of the passage and came out the other, holding chunks of stone between them as if the aqueduct was only still standing because the roots hadn't yet decided to let it fall.

Seralune stepped over one. Her bare foot came down on something sharp under the water.

Pain opened across her sole, and she caught the wall.

Nereth turned at once.

"I can walk," Seralune said.

"I know you can." For a second, concern got past the impatience in Nereth's voice. "Lift your foot, please."

A thin cut ran under her toes. It wasn't deep. Blood thinned into the current and was gone.

Nereth lowered the flame to look.

"The stone's cut you. It's shallow. But this water's been through places neither of us understands."

"You don't need to ruin your dress on it."

Nereth was already tearing a strip from the inside edge of her own underskirt. "If it festers, Your Highness, I'm the one who'll be carrying you. I have no wish to find out whether I can."

She folded the strip twice, pressed it to the cut and tied it off above Seralune's foot. Her fingers were quick. They didn't shake.

"You didn't leave me," Nereth said. "Back there."

Seralune watched the knot tighten. "No."

"Cyrandor paid for the time it cost us." Nereth pulled the knot hard.

"Thaeroval chose to take his arm."

"I know who swung the sword." Nereth's voice stayed very even. "I keep thinking he might have reached the stairs, if you hadn't stopped for me."

She stood and turned back to the current before Seralune could see whether anything else had come into her face.

They went on.

The passage split twice, and each time Nereth lowered her flame to the water and followed the current. At the third split, there was no current to follow. Three black openings waited beyond a chamber where silt had buried the floor and roots hung from the ceiling in still, pale curtains.

Nereth stood among them and held her flame low. "The current's gone."

"Then the laundry channels have ended."

"And so has the only route I knew." The flame over her palm drew up thin and high before she steadied it. "From here, we're following whatever survived underneath them."

She lifted the flame towards the left-hand opening. Fallen stone blocked it a few paces in. The right one dropped away so steeply the light couldn't find the bottom. The middle one carried no water at all, only a pale line of dried mineral along one wall.

Nereth looked at her. "Which one did Cyrandor tell you?"

"He didn't."

"Then we're guessing."

"He expected to be here."

Nereth lowered the flame. She didn't ask again.

Seralune touched the signet under her cloak. The closed lily pressed into her palm.

"Why did you help him?"

Nereth's eyes came away from the openings to her.

"You asked me that on the stair, Your Highness."

"I asked if you wanted to come," Seralune said. "I never asked why you stayed, when the guards found us."

Nereth stepped into the dry middle channel. Seralune followed.

"He taught me to read."

The answer came so long after the question that Seralune had stopped expecting one.

"When I came into service, the palace knew I could warm washing water. That was thought to be all the education someone like me needed." Nereth kept her eyes on the passage. "He found me crying over a laundry inventory. He taught me every word on it. Then he made me write a better one."

"How old were you?"

"Ten."

"How long ago?"

"Eight years." Nereth raised the flame as the passage climbed. "He asked my name before he asked what my fire could do. I remembered that."

"So you came because he asked you to."

"He didn't ask." Nereth's mouth tightened. "He was bleeding on the floor, and he looked at the stair. It was the last thing he needed from me."

The ceiling lifted after a few more paces. Water had once run high enough to polish the lower walls smooth under Seralune's fingers. Above that line, shallow carvings repeated along the stone. Two curved lines. A falling leaf. Two more lines. Another leaf.

She stopped under them.

Nereth's flame moved on, taking its light off the carvings.

"Wait."

Nereth turned back, and the light fell across the stone again. "What have you found?"

Seralune touched the nearest leaf.

She remembered standing under an open sky while masons cut these same leaves into pale new stone. She'd been small enough that the aqueduct wall had risen beside her like a cliff. Her mother had been holding one of Seralune's shoes in each hand, because Seralune had insisted on walking the unfinished channel barefoot, and Thaer had been following along the top of the wall, pretending nobody had told him to make sure she didn't fall.

The workers had lowered the last arch into place while court officials talked about water and the southern orchards. Seralune remembered none of what they'd said.

She remembered the mason who'd carved one leaf upside down.

She'd found it before anyone else. And her mother had made them leave it.

She searched along the wall until the flame caught a leaf whose point faced up.

There.

Her fingers settled into the old mistake.

"I know this place."

Nereth came closer.

"This was the southern aqueduct," Seralune said. "Before the palace grew out over the lower gardens."

"You remember it?"

"I watched them finish this part."

Nereth looked along the stone. No surprise came into her face. She'd used up all her surprise since Seralune woke. Something quieter came instead.

"How far did it run?"

"Under the orchards. Then along the southern slope." Seralune closed her eyes and tried to fit the old mountain under the palace she'd seen from her locked room. "There was a spillway, above the coast."

"The coast is where Cyrandor meant us to go."

"Yes. If the spillway's still there, it should bring us out above it."

"Then let's hope the spillway was built as well as these walls."

Seralune's hand stayed on the carving.

"The mason was called Avarin. He put the leaf upside down."

The smallest curve touched the corner of Nereth's mouth.

"Then I'm glad nobody corrected him."

She turned south, and Seralune followed the leaves.

---

The aqueduct remembered distance better than Seralune's body did.

The channel sank through chambers big enough to hold the room where she'd woken. Arches marched away into the dark, every one carrying the same leaves. In places the roof had split and roots had filled the gap. Elsewhere, the floor dropped away under pools so green and deep that the stone at the bottom disappeared.

They walked until Seralune's feet stopped feeling cold. Then until they stopped feeling like feet.

They rested once on a dry ledge while Nereth tightened the wet cloth round her cut, and again after a collapse forced them to climb over a heap of fallen stone. Neither of them slept. At the first ledge, Nereth drank from a thin trickle coming through clean stone before she let Seralune have any. By the second, green light was moving under the water, and neither of them touched it.

Whenever Seralune closed her eyes, she saw Cyrandor's arm hitting the floor. Whenever Nereth closed hers, her fingers moved, as if she were still holding Seralune's hand on the stair.

The bells came back, some time later, through layers of earth — too faint to count. A low note. Silence. Then several more answering from further down the mountain.

"Evening?" Seralune asked.

Nereth was leaning on the wall with her flame cupped close to her chest. "Unless Natharul's changed what the bells mean."

"It's changed everything else."

"That's true."

The bells faded, and they walked on into the night.

---

The first flower appeared where the carved leaves ended.

It grew from a crack above the water — white petals folded round a centre that gave off the same green light as the channel. It had no stem. Fine, clear roots joined it straight to the stone.

Seralune went closer, and the petals opened.

Light moved across her face.

Nereth's hand closed on the back of her cloak. "Don't touch it."

"I only wanted a closer look."

"Everything behind us should have cured you of wanting closer looks." Nereth didn't let go.

Seralune straightened, and Nereth released her. The flower stayed open, turned towards her. When Nereth walked past, it snapped shut.

Nereth stopped. She brought the flame nearer, and the flower pulled itself back into the crack so fast its roots made a faint scraping sound.

**Krik-krik-krik.**

Other sounds answered from beyond the next arch.

Seralune lifted her eyes.

Dozens of flowers covered the chamber ahead. They grew across the walls and under the surface of the water, their pale petals turning the aqueduct into a buried garden. Long leaves floated on the current without being carried along by it. Fungi climbed the pillars in shelves as clear as glass, and light moved through them in slow waves, green deepening to violet and fading back again.

For the first time since they'd gone underground, Nereth's flame wasn't needed. She closed her hand. The dark should have come back.

The garden went on breathing light.

Seralune stepped through the arch, and every flower in the room opened.

Nereth stayed behind her. The nearest leaves tilted towards Seralune's legs without touching them. Fine roots lifted out of the water and followed the swing of her cloak, then settled again.

"It opened for you," Nereth said. The dryness had gone out of her voice, and something uneasy had come into it instead.

Seralune watched a thin stem lean after her hand. "And it closed when you came near."

"Then watch what it does now."

Seralune looked back. Nereth hadn't come through the arch.

"What is it?"

"I don't know," Nereth admitted. "That's why I wanted you away from it."

The nearest petals turned towards Seralune. "We can't run from everything we don't understand."

"No. But we can stop offering it our hands."

Nereth called her flame up again and carried it in. The flowers nearest her shut. Roots withdrew from the water. Under a clear shelf of fungus, something small and many-legged scuttled into a hollow in the wall.

Nereth watched it go. "They don't like fire."

"Then keep it close," Seralune said.

Nereth lifted her hand between them. The garden made room for Seralune, and drew back from Nereth.

Seralune reached inward. Her mana waited under her skin the way it always had — vast, silent, without any edge to it. The flowers brightened before she'd even touched it.

She let the thought go. Their light dimmed with her.

Nereth had seen. "What did you just do?"

"Nothing."

"They brightened when you did that." Nereth kept her distance from the nearest flower. "Whatever it was, please don't do it again."

Something moved under the water.

Nereth's flame stretched longer. A creature slid between Seralune's feet without touching either of them — no bigger than her hand, with a broad clear body and far too many delicate fins. There was no blood in it. Pale roots filled the places where its veins should have been, and opened out of its back in a little fan of leaves.

It circled Seralune once. Then it swam towards Nereth.

She lowered the flame. The creature stopped. Its root-filled body drew tight, the fins along its back spread, and the water around it began to tremble.

Nereth closed her hand. The flame went out. The creature loosened, turned, and swam away.

Neither of them spoke until it had vanished under the flowers.

Seralune looked round at the pale garden again. *Beautiful* hadn't become the wrong word. It had become not enough of one.

They went on more carefully.

Past the garden, roots covered the carved walls completely. Some were thin enough for light to pass through. Others had grown thick as Seralune's waist, burrowing into the floor and coming up several arches further on. They ran against the current, as if water no longer told them which way the world moved.

The first body lay under one of them.

Seralune took it for stone.

A hand came up out of the growth, fingers open towards the water. Grey skin showed between bands of clear root. There was no rot on it. The body lay on its back under the surface, its chest split from throat to stomach by a pale trunk growing straight up through it.

Seralune stopped. Nereth stepped in front of her.

The corpse wore the remains of a worker's tunic, its metal buttons gone green against the cloth. Roots went in under the jaw and into the open mouth.

"How long?" Seralune asked.

"Long enough that we can't help him."

Nereth didn't look at her when she said it.

Further on, another hand broke the surface. Then part of a face. Then three bodies gathered round the base of a pillar, held upright by the growth coming out of their backs.

Their eyes were closed. One mouth opened.

"*Help…*"

It was a child's voice.

Seralune's heart hit her ribs once. Nereth caught her wrist before she could move.

The corpse's mouth stayed open. Roots trembled under the skin of its throat.

"*Help…*"

That one came from another body across the chamber. Not the same voice. The same pain.

A third corpse drew a breath it had no use for. "*Please…*"

Nereth pulled Seralune back. "They're dead."

"Something isn't."

"That's what worries me."

The cry came again, further south, beyond the bodies — frightened and breathless, followed by something scraping over stone.

Seralune looked into the next arch.

"I can hear it suffering."

"That might be exactly why it wants us to," Nereth said. Fear had got too close to hide under the impatience any longer.

"What if it really is hurt?"

Nereth's fingers tightened round her wrist. "It may well be. But the dead are carrying its voice for it, Your Highness. Whatever's in there knows how to make you come closer."

"I heard you." Seralune couldn't turn away from the cry. "I have to see. If we can help it —"

Another cry travelled through the rooted dead. It went into the corpse nearest Seralune as a shiver before any sound came out of its mouth.

The body had become an instrument. Whatever was waiting ahead was pulling breath through the dead to make itself impossible to ignore.

Nereth's face had gone very still. "We keep going south."

"I'm going to see what it is."

Nereth stared at her. Anger sharpened her whole face, because the fear had nowhere else to go.

"And if the suffering is bait?"

"Then something alive is being used to set it." Seralune's eyes stayed on the arch. "I won't walk away without seeing whether it can be freed." She looked back at Nereth. "You can stay here."

Nereth stared at her for one disbelieving breath.

Then she let go of Seralune's wrist and stepped past her.

"Oh, certainly. I'll wait here and listen to you scream next." She brought the flame forward. "Stay behind me."

"Nereth —"

"Let me look before you touch anything. *Please*."

Nereth went into the next arch. Seralune followed.

---

The cry led them away from the current, up a narrow branch of the aqueduct into a chamber the water had abandoned centuries ago. Roots covered the floor so thickly that stone showed only in small dark gaps between them.

The creature lay near the middle.

From a distance, it looked like a salamander. Closer, the likeness fell apart. Its skin was clear enough that Seralune could see things moving underneath. Roots had replaced one of its back legs and gone into its lower body through an opening that had healed round them. A green sac swelled where its lungs should have been. Every time it breathed, light ran out through the roots under the floor.

The dead behind them answered. "*Help…*"

The creature's own mouth opened, and a thin, childlike cry came out of it.

Pain shook its small body. It tried to drag itself forward, and the roots holding its hindquarters tightened and pulled it back.

Seralune crouched a few paces away. Nereth stayed standing.

"There," Seralune whispered. "It's trapped."

"It's watching your hand," Nereth said. "Not your face."

"It's frightened."

"Frightened things still bite."

Seralune lowered one hand towards the floor.

The roots nearest her opened a path. Not quickly. Not in alarm. They lifted away from the stone and folded aside, as if they recognised the place her palm meant to touch.

Nereth's breathing changed. "Your Highness."

The creature's dark eyes followed her hand.

"I won't hurt you," Seralune told it.

"You think it understands Elvish?"

"I think it understands that."

She moved closer. The creature stopped crying. For one breath, no voice came through the corpses behind them. The whole buried growth seemed to hold itself still.

She could see where the roots went into the animal. Its flesh had grown round them. Pulling it free would tear away half its body.

She couldn't leave it like this.

Her mana stirred under her skin. The growth brightened.

Nereth stepped forward. "Don't."

"I haven't done anything."

"Then why is everything getting brighter?"

Seralune heard it then. Not the creature. The roots. A low vibration moving through them, too deep to be a sound. It came up through the soles of her feet and climbed the bones of her legs.

**HMMMMMMMM.**

The light under the salamander's skin turned violet.

Nereth grabbed Seralune's shoulder.

The creature moved.

It didn't leap at Seralune. It went straight past her outstretched hand and struck Nereth in the face.

Its teeth sank in beneath her left eye.

**TCHK.**

Nereth screamed.

Her flame exploded between them and orange light filled the chamber. The creature tore itself free before the fire could touch it, taking a narrow strip of her flesh with it, and vanished into the roots.

Nereth dropped to one knee. Both hands came up towards her face. One stopped short of the wound, fingers curled against the air. Blood ran between the other's fingers.

Seralune reached for her. "Let me see —"

Nereth struck her hand away. "Move."

The word broke apart in her teeth.

Something ran under the skin of her cheek — a black-purple line pushing out from the bite towards her temple.

Nereth saw Seralune staring. "What?"

A second line split off beneath her eye. It pulsed once. Then again.

Nereth's breath disappeared. Her back arched so hard her shoulder blades almost met. A sound came out of her throat without ever becoming a scream, and she folded round her stomach, one hand digging into the stone, the other clawing at the cloth over her ribs.

Seralune caught her before her forehead hit the roots. "Nereth!"

"Inside."

It came out through a locked jaw.

"What's inside?"

Nereth's fingers dug into her own side. "Biting."

The roots underneath them began to move.

**Krik.**

A pale cord pulled back into the floor.

**Krik-krik.**

Another. The chamber wasn't retreating. It was opening.

Shapes moved inside the walls.

Seralune pulled Nereth's arm over her shoulders and hauled her up. Nereth's knees went. She caught herself before Seralune had to take all of her weight.

"South," she gasped.

"The channel's behind us."

"Then get me back to it."

Nereth forced the first step. Seralune carried the next one.

The first creature came out of the wall as they reached the lower passage. Its body had once belonged to something with four legs. Roots held its chest up in place of bone, and a ring of hollow tubes opened where its face should have been.

Nereth raised one shaking hand, and fire gathered in it.

The black-purple lines on her face clenched.

She screamed, and the flame collapsed.

The creature lunged. Seralune dragged her sideways, and the rooted jaws closed on the edge of her cloak and ripped straight through it. Nereth drove her heel into its body. The growth inside it broke with a wet crack. It fell and folded in on itself — and started getting up again before they'd even passed it.

More movement followed them. Not hurried. Certain.

The roots knew exactly where Nereth was.

They ignored Seralune completely, even while she was carrying her.

---

The pain came in waves.

Seralune learned to recognise each one before it took Nereth. The marks on her face pulsed first. Her left eye lost its focus. Then the muscles down her neck drew tight until her head was pulled towards one shoulder, and whatever came next travelled down through her body. Her steps got shorter. Her breath stopped. Her fingers dug into Seralune's cloak as if something under her skin had started pulling the other way.

Then the wave would break, and the air would tear back into her, and they'd gain a few paces before the next one.

The aqueduct sank lower around them. Flowers opened for Seralune and snapped shut behind Nereth. Pale animals gathered inside the walls, their root-filled bodies all turning together whenever black-purple light moved under her skin.

Seralune tried to count the waves.

She lost the number after nine.

At the next arch, Nereth pulled away from her.

"I can walk by myself." Pride held her up where strength couldn't.

"Not yet."

Nereth managed three steps. The fourth never reached the floor.

She hit the wall shoulder first and flattened both hands against the stone. A narrow shape moved under the skin of her forearm, travelling from wrist to elbow. It didn't follow the veins. It went straight across them.

Nereth stared at it. "No."

Heat came into the passage. Seralune felt it before the flame appeared. Orange fire crawled out between Nereth's fingers and spread across the wall.

The corruption on her face tightened. Her knees bent as something dragged along the inside of her arm, the skin rising behind it in a thin moving ridge.

Nereth screamed then. Not loudly. It came from too deep inside her for volume — it tore through her chest once and left her with no breath for another.

Seralune caught her round the waist.

Nereth turned. Her forearm slammed into Seralune's throat and drove her back into the wall.

Stone hit the back of Seralune's head. The passage blurred.

Nereth held her there. One eye found Seralune. The other had filled with branching darkness, its green split by black-purple veins.

"Nereth —"

"*Don't*."

The pressure on Seralune's throat got worse. Nereth's face twisted as another wave went through her, and anger came up with it — too big for the narrow passage, too sharp to be only pain.

"Cyrandor lost his arm because a *princess* couldn't bear to leave anyone behind."

Seralune stopped reaching for her wrist.

"You couldn't leave me. So he had to stay behind!"

It hurt because it had somewhere true to land.

Cyrandor had told her the choice was hers. She'd chosen Nereth. And he'd stood in front of Thaer because of the time that choice had cost.

Seralune didn't defend herself. Nereth saw that too. The pressure on her throat faltered.

Then something dropped from the ceiling behind Nereth.

Roots hit stone. Seralune saw the ring of hollow tubes where a face should have been.

"Nereth!"

This time the name was a warning.

The creature hit Nereth from behind and hooked two pale limbs over her shoulders. Her arm came off Seralune's throat. She spun with a cry and seized the thing round its narrow body, and fire burst out of both her hands.

**FWHUMM.**

Orange flame opened right through the creature. The roots inside it writhed towards Nereth instead of away, and their tips went into the skin round her wrists.

She tore her hands free. The creature fell burning between them. The flame lasted a heartbeat before the dark swallowed it.

Nereth stumbled over the body and hit the opposite wall. The black-purple marks had reached under her jaw.

Seralune dragged in a breath through her bruised throat. Then she took Nereth's arm again and kept going south.

Neither of them said anything about Cyrandor. The accusation walked along with them.

So did the roots.

---

By the time the aqueduct widened again, the pain had stopped coming in waves. It was simply there.

Nereth couldn't hide where it was anymore. One hand pressed under her ribs, fingers spread, as if she were holding something in. Her ribs barely moved under her palm.

"It's pushing," she said.

"What is?"

Nereth's laugh broke before it could become a sound. "I don't know." She pressed harder. "I only know it wants more room."

Her legs went, and Seralune lowered her against the wall.

The chamber ahead had once divided the water between four descending channels. The central basin was dry. Roots filled all four mouths and crossed the floor in one great pale knot.

Shapes gathered on the far side of them. Not one creature. Many. They made no sound at all until the first one touched the stone.

**Tik.**

A claw.

**Tik. Tik.**

Another.

Nereth tried to get up. Her body wouldn't let her.

Seralune pulled off the cloak and bundled it across her knees. Cyrandor's blood had turned the bottom edge stiff and dark, and the jaws that had caught her in the passage had torn one side open from hem to waist. She found a clean piece near the shoulder and pulled. The cloth resisted. She wrapped both hands round it, and the cut in her palm split open again.

The fabric tore.

**RRRIP.**

Nereth saw the strip. "No."

"The wound's still open."

"That isn't a wound anymore." Nereth's fingers tightened against the floor. "It's where the thing got in."

Seralune's grip on the cloth loosened. "Then tell me what I can do."

Nereth's fingers closed weakly round her wrist. "Don't touch it."

The first creature stepped into the green light.

Seralune looked at the four channels. Every one was blocked. Behind them, pale bodies crowded the passage they'd come down.

Blood was still running down Nereth's neck.

Seralune laid the folded cloth against the bite beneath her left eye.

Cold went into the cut on Seralune's palm the instant the cloth touched Nereth's wound. Not across her skin. Into it.

Her mana moved.

She had spent her whole life unable to give it a shape. Physicians had put crystals in her hands and watched them crack. Tutors had asked her for flame, for water, for wind, for earth — for anything at all the world would recognise.

Nothing had ever answered.

Now something did.

The black-purple veins under Nereth's face opened towards Seralune like roots finding water, and her power poured out of her.

It didn't trickle. It poured.

She tried to pull her hand back. Her fingers stayed pressed to the folded cloth. She tried to move her arm. Nothing. Her thoughts reached every part of her body and found the path between wanting and moving cut clean through.

Nereth's eyes went wide. "Your Highness?"

Seralune couldn't answer. Her mana had never had an edge. For the first time in her life, she could feel it moving — because something else had given it a direction.

Light raced from the wound through Nereth's face. Black-purple branches spread across her throat, into her shoulders, and split down both her arms. Her back lifted off the wall. Her mouth opened round a scream that made no sound.

Every root in the chamber brightened.

The light spread outward from Nereth — through the knot beneath them, into the blocked channels, along the walls, and away. The aqueduct answered from distances Seralune couldn't see.

**HMMMMMMMM.**

The vibration turned to pressure. The pressure turned to hunger. And the creatures stopped coming.

One by one, their bodies turned towards Nereth.

Her fingers curled against the floor. Her nails split. Blood welled under them. The corruption climbed round her ribs in pulsing bands, forcing every breath wider than the last. She jerked against Seralune's hand. Heat surged through the cloth, and Seralune tried again, uselessly, to pull away.

Nereth looked at her.

Pain had stripped every title and duty from her face. She was eighteen years old. Frightened. Furious. Dying underneath a city that had measured her usefulness before she'd even learned to read.

Her mouth opened.

"Seralune."

The name barely existed.

Then Nereth burned.

---

The first flame made no sound.

Violet-white light filled the centre of her body. Orange fire opened round it, too bright to cast shadows and too dense to move the way flame should. The black-purple bands at her throat flared, and wherever the fire pushed back towards her chest, they brightened and drove it out — down her arms, into the roots. Her collar caught. The skin above it blistered. Beneath the branching, her chest kept rising.

Then the aqueduct took a breath.

**WHUMMMMMMM.**

Fire went into every root at once. The knot beneath them turned white. The creatures arched backwards as light split through their clear bodies, and the hidden roots shone out through flesh and stone, showing every inch of the growth inside the walls before it burned.

The force reached Seralune and divided in front of her face.

Flame curved past both her shoulders, over her head, under her frozen hand, and never touched her skin. Her hair lifted in heat that should have turned it to ash. No black-purple marks ran under her skin. Nothing stood between her and the fire at all. It simply went round her.

Nereth disappeared into the white.

And the world broke.

**KRRRRAAAAAAAM.**

The channels split. Collapse raced south from arch to arch, forcing the water into walls of steam. The roots holding the dead burned away, and the bodies fell from the stone with their mouths still open. This time, no voices came out of them.

The floor threw Seralune against the wall. Her hand tore free of Nereth, and her body came back to her too late to be any use for balance. Something heavy hit her thigh. Another piece of stone opened her brow, though the fire passed over both wounds without going into them.

The whole chamber tilted. Beneath it, a deeper pressure was building along the southern channels.

**GRRNNNNNNK.**

The roof tore open to a sky going pale towards morning. Trees fell through the gap before the force flung the whole upper slope outward. Further down the mountain, violet-white columns erupted out of the buried channels, one after another, each one shaking the ground under her and lifting broken stone before it had settled from the last.

Seralune fell back into the place the force had torn her from.

The stone around it had turned black. Beneath her, a perfect circle was still wet, no wider than her outstretched arms. Green moss clung to its floor. A single white flower stood at its edge, untouched, in a chamber where everything beyond it had turned to ash.

She stared at the flower. Everything around it was burning.

Then the ceiling came down.

She rolled towards Nereth. A slab hit the place her head had been, and its broken edge caught her shoulder and slammed her into the floor. Pain opened from her neck to her wrist.

This pain reached her. It proved the rest hadn't been a dream.

"Nereth!"

Steam swallowed the basin. Seralune crawled through it. Blood from her brow ran into one eye. She wiped it away with the back of her wrist and found ash, and shattered root, and stone hot enough to blister her palms.

The torn cloak had caught under her knee. She dragged it free, pulled it over her mouth, and kept searching.

Nobody answered.

A shape lay just past the edge of the untouched circle.

Seralune reached it.

Nereth had fallen on her side. Most of the grey and green cloth across her back had burned away. Her hair was still dark on the right and singed almost to the jaw on the left. Black-purple veins covered that side of her face, branching out from beneath the eye towards her temple and down her throat. The bands that had driven the fire away from her chest were still pulsing under her skin, but the light in them had faded to a thin, uneven flicker.

She didn't move.

Seralune laid two fingers under her jaw.

Nothing.

She moved them and pressed harder.

There — a pulse. Much too fast. But there.

Seralune bowed her head until her forehead rested on Nereth's shoulder.

The relief lasted one breath.

Stone shifted overhead. **Krrrk.** A crack ran across what was left of the wall. **Krrrk.** The whole basin leaned towards the crater.

Seralune lifted Nereth's head. Her left eye opened without finding anything. A tiny ember appeared between two of her fingers, and died.

"Nereth."

Her mouth moved. No sound followed.

Seralune tore another strip from the cloak and bound it over the burned cloth at Nereth's left shoulder and back. There was too little fabric and far too much damage. She tied what she could under Nereth's right arm, then pulled what was left of the cloak round her shoulders.

Nereth started shaking. Not a little. Her teeth chattered. Her whole body drew inward, as if the white fire had left winter behind it.

Seralune took her hands. They were cold.

A single drop of black-purple light moved under the skin of Nereth's wrist, travelled up towards her elbow, and disappeared.

Seralune looked at the ash all around them. Every root had burned. Something inside Nereth was still going.

The wall cracked again.

Seralune put Nereth's arm over her shoulders and stood.

Her legs folded under the weight, and they both hit the floor.

The pain found every hurt she had — the cut in her foot, her bruised thigh, her shoulder. She pressed a palm flat to the stone, found Cyrandor's dried blood on the cloak between them, and forced her knees under her again.

Nereth's cheek fell against her neck.

Seralune stood.

"Come."

Nereth didn't answer.

Seralune tightened her hold. "That wasn't a request."

The words had no authority at all over a body that couldn't obey. But they gave Seralune enough to take the first step.

---

The eruption had built a road out of everything it destroyed.

It climbed up through the broken chambers towards the open sky — a slope of shattered stone, steaming roots, and earth that slid away whenever Seralune trusted it with her weight.

She carried Nereth as far as her strength would let her. Then she dragged her. Then she carried her again, because the stones were tearing through what was left of the cloth round Nereth's legs.

Below them, the crater breathed heat. Water gushed out of broken channels and vanished into cracks before it ever reached the bottom. Steam rose in white sheets. Behind it, parts of the aqueduct kept folding in on themselves.

**Krrr-rrrum.**

The sound followed them up.

Seralune chose every step by testing it before she let Nereth's weight follow. She learned which blackened roots would still hold under the stone, and which would turn to ash in her fingers. She learned that violet fire was still alive inside some of the cracks long after the visible flames had gone.

She learned how heavy an unconscious person becomes when the world wants both of you at the bottom.

Halfway up, Nereth woke. Her breath came in with a small broken sound, and the arm across Seralune's shoulders tightened.

"Don't try to stand," Seralune said. It frightened her how little strength had come back into that arm.

The corrupted eye opened, but it couldn't find her. "Couldn't if I tried."

Her voice barely reached the air.

Seralune shifted her grip round Nereth's waist. The movement drew a sharp breath through Nereth's teeth. She blinked until the ruined eye finally found Seralune's face.

"What…?"

Nereth looked down. The crater answered her before she could finish.

A section of the southern wall collapsed. Stone fell through the white steam and smashed into the ruined aqueduct below.

**GRRRAAAM.**

The mountain shook, and Nereth's fingers opened.

A small flame appeared over her palm. Orange. Its heart flashed white.

They both froze before it vanished.

Nereth stared at her hand.

"Don't call the fire again," Seralune said, closing her hand round Nereth's wrist.

"I didn't call it." Fear had stripped all the dryness out of her.

Seralune looked from Nereth's palm to the corruption beneath her eye. "Then tell me the moment you feel it coming back."

A laugh tried to get out of Nereth. Pain caught it. She folded into Seralune, one hand pressed under her ribs, the veins along her neck pulsing in no rhythm Seralune could follow.

"Inside," Nereth whispered. "It's still…"

Her jaw locked. Seralune held her until the wave passed.

"Still biting?"

Nereth opened her eye.

"Smaller teeth."

That frightened Seralune more than any scream could have.

She pulled Nereth higher against her shoulder, and they climbed.

---

The first morning bell rang before they reached the top.

Its note came from somewhere past the crater, across the mountain and through the torn-open earth. It wasn't the old eastern council bell. It rang higher, and urgently enough that another tower answered before it had faded, and then another.

Natharul was waking up to the wound beneath it.

Seralune looked back once.

The palace rose far above them. Smoke and distance hid the lower walls, but the highest towers stood clear against the paling sky. Lights were moving across the bridges. Far too many. Bells called from one level to the next, carrying the alarm down the mountain.

No riders appeared on the broken slope. No dark figure stood at the lip of the crater with a sword that could cut the distance between them.

Thaer would know. He'd have felt the mountain break. He'd see where the earth had opened, and understand she'd gone underneath it.

She waited for guilt to tell her to turn back.

Nereth's weight shifted against her.

Seralune faced the climb again. A tree had fallen across the top edge of the crater, its roots still half buried on one side, its crown reaching down towards them. The exposed roots were the first holds she'd seen that might take both of them.

Her hurt palm slipped. She banged one knee on the slope.

Nereth made a quiet sound beside her.

"Leave me here."

"No."

"You answered before you'd even thought about it."

"I thought about it under the palace."

"That was before I tried to crush your throat."

Seralune tightened Nereth's arm round her shoulders. "You were being eaten alive."

"I remember," Nereth said.

It wasn't a request for forgiveness. Seralune didn't offer any. They climbed another step.

"Cyrandor's alive," Seralune said.

Nereth looked towards the palace. "He was when we left."

"Thaeroval chose his arm."

"He still took it."

"Yes."

Nereth's eye closed. The bells rang on.

Seralune felt the accusation come back to sit between them. *So he had to stay behind.*

She didn't push it away. She put her next foot higher.

Nereth was quiet for several breaths.

"If this is how you rescue everyone, Your Highness," she murmured at last, "I understand your brother's concern."

It was faint, and so dry, that it broke something in Seralune that had been holding since the palace.

She almost laughed. It hurt too much.

"I'd started to worry the fire had changed your temper."

"No fire's that powerful."

That was very nearly comforting.

"When we reach the harbour, I'm finding you a bed," Seralune said. "You can spend the whole day telling me everything I did wrong."

Nereth's good eye opened halfway.

"Only a day?" she whispered.

It closed again. Seralune kept talking as she pulled them towards the fallen tree, hoping for another answer.

They reached it. Seralune dragged Nereth under the trunk and found solid earth past the crater's edge. Wet grass pressed cold against her knees.

For a while, she couldn't go any further.

Nereth lay against her shoulder, breathing shallowly. The left side of her face had swollen round the bite. Under the skin, black-purple branches kept spreading towards her hairline. They didn't fade as the light came.

Seralune looked south.

The mountain fell away beneath them. Terraces that had once held orchards had gone back to forest, then broken into rocky slopes running down towards the sea. Far below, roofs gathered round a narrow harbour. Boats rested by long wooden piers. Their name didn't come to her.

Beyond them was the ocean.

It filled the whole horizon. She'd seen it before — from the high palace balconies, and once from the southern road when she was small, Thaer riding ahead and turning round every few moments to make sure her pony was still following.

Never like this.

No wall framed it. No window decided how much of it she was allowed to see.

Morning came onto the water one pale line at a time. Against that light, the sea had no border at all — too big to be a prison, too far away to promise safety.

Somewhere beyond it lay Mydea. And somewhere in Mydea, her mother had left a trail that might still remember her.

Seralune pulled the last of the cloak tighter round Nereth and got up once more.

Behind them, Natharul was ringing every bell it had.

Ahead, the ocean took the morning without asking what had broken beneath it.

Against Seralune's shoulder, the darkness under Nereth's closed eye pulsed again.
