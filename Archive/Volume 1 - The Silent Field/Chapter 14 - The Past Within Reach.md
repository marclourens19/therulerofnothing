# The Past Within Reach

The higher-ranking soldier lowered the lamp.

For a moment, its light rested against his knees. He turned towards the king without leaving the machine, and Alaric watched the space between them, waiting for someone to cross it.

No one did.

The king remained behind the drawn swords. One guard had moved nearer to him, but the king shifted away when the man tried to stand between him and the wreck. He kept it in sight without going closer.

Alaric’s fingers pressed Gerolt’s token against his thigh. The edge had begun to hurt through the borrowed cloth, though he could not remember tightening his hand.

“What do you think he found?” he whispered.

Silas did not take his eyes from the field. “I couldn’t really see.”

“He seemed to know where to look, though.”

Silas glanced at him then. Whatever answer he might have given disappeared when green light opened inside the black crystal carried behind the king.

The attendant pulled the silver frame away from his chest. A second line appeared beneath the first. Then both broke into unfamiliar characters that moved across the dark surface without anyone touching them.

He hurried towards the royal escort. A guard stepped into his path, but the king had already seen the light and came forwards before the man could send the attendant back.

Alaric lifted his head a little, careful to remain beneath the thorn. The attendant turned the crystal towards the king. Its green characters shifted into narrow rows and became still.

Nothing else upon the field stopped. A wagon continued between the tents. Soldiers carried something long and dark across the cut wheat. Beside the machine, the higher-ranking soldier waited until the lamp’s weight made him change hands.

The king read the glowing rows once. They moved again. He bent nearer and read whatever replaced them.

Alaric could not distinguish a single mark from where he lay.

The king demanded something of the attendant. The man answered with a quick shake of his head. The king pushed the framed crystal back into his hands and turned towards the landing craft so suddenly that the nearest guard had to step out of his way.

The soldier called after him. This time, Alaric heard the voice, but the warship swallowed every word.

The king stopped. He pointed towards the dead, then swept the same arm across the machines. The soldier began to answer. The king was already walking.

No one had bowed this time. They hurried to catch him.

Alaric watched the king pass the first tent. At its corner, he turned back towards the wreck. His escort continued another pace before closing around him again.

“It looks like he’s leaving,” Redd said.

His voice was scarcely more than breath. Alaric looked across Silas’s shoulder and found him staring at the royal standard, dirt still clinging to his cheek.

Alaric had wanted the king to stay long enough to explain what he had seen. The thought was so foolish that he could not have spoken it. There was no way to ask. There was no way to stand before those guards and say that he had woken among their dead.

Below, the king reached his landing craft’s stair. He climbed without waiting for the soldiers gathered beside it. One followed as far as the lowest step before a guard placed an arm across his path.

The king disappeared inside. The door remained open only long enough for his escort.

---

The first fire began while the stair was folding.

Alaric saw orange spread beneath a low heap beyond the tents. He had taken it for discarded cloth until an elven soldier drew a pole free and an arm fell outwards from the pile.

Another soldier pushed it back. Alaric released the token.

The flame climbed through a sleeve, brightened beneath a breastplate and caught the dry wood laid between the bodies. Smoke rolled low across the ground before finding the air disturbed by the ships.

“They’re burning them,” Alaric whispered.

Silas followed his gaze. His mouth tightened, but he said nothing.

Near the edge of the destruction, two soldiers lifted a body between them. One held beneath the shoulders, the other behind the knees. A strip of pale cloth trailed from the dead man’s wrist and caught upon a broken spear. They stopped long enough to free it before continuing towards the fire.

Alaric had never known the man’s name. Now there would be less of him to recognise.

He searched beyond the soldiers, towards the wrecks. Chains had been laid beside a wagon. Soldiers were lifting them in lengths too heavy for one person to carry. Farther back, another party rolled thick timbers off a cart and arranged them beside the machine the king had inspected.

Alaric watched until he understood. They were preparing to move it.

A second fire opened closer to the torn ground. The pale cloth at the dead man’s wrist disappeared behind smoke, and Alaric found himself trying to remember whether there had been anything written upon it.

He had watched it catch upon the spear, then turned towards the chains.

“Silas, we need to get down there.”

“Not yet.” Silas turned towards him. “Let me see where those soldiers are going.”

“They’re getting ready to take the machines. Look at the chains.”

“I can see them, Alaric. They haven’t moved anything yet.”

“That doesn’t mean we have time.”

Silas reached across him and pressed a low branch away from Alaric’s coat before it could scrape against the cloth.

“We said we’d come to look,” Silas replied. “You could barely get out of bed this afternoon. I can’t take you down there and just hope your leg holds.”

Alaric looked back towards the chains. Darcy had drawn two circles upon a sheet. An orb small enough to carry, behind the thickest armour, near the centre. He could remember the torn place where her quill had pressed too hard.

“I didn’t know they were going to clear it all away.”

“Neither did I. That doesn’t make it safe.”

“But we can’t just lie here while they—”

“Alaric. Keep your voice down.”

The warning struck harder because Silas did not raise his own.

Alaric closed his mouth. Wena lifted her head beneath his arm, and he rested his hand against her neck until she settled again.

The sound of the warship filled the silence between them. Alaric tried once more.

“If they put them on a ship, how am I supposed to find them?”

Silas looked towards the wrecks.

Alaric waited, watching another pair of soldiers lift a chain.

“I don’t know how we’d follow them.”

“That’s what I’m trying to tell you.” Alaric’s voice shook now, though he kept it low. “I don’t know where else to look. Darcy gave me something to find. If it’s in one of those machines, I can’t just watch them take it away.”

“She couldn’t promise it would tell you anything.”

“I know what she said, Silas.”

Silas’s eyes returned to him. Heat rose into Alaric’s face. He had not meant to sound angry at the one person who might help.

“I know it might not tell me who I am,” he continued, more quietly. “But what if she’s right? What if it could help me, and I never even try to find it?”

Below them, a chain dropped against another.

**KLANG.**

Alaric flinched. The soldiers bent to lift it again.

He had asked people for help since the first moment he could remember, sometimes before he had understood what helping him would cost. He could see Silas’s bandaged right hand resting clear of the soil, and the words he had been about to say changed.

“I don’t want you getting killed because of me.”

Silas remained still.

“I just don’t know how to walk away from it.”

Silas looked at him for a while. Alaric wanted to turn back to the field, but made himself remain beneath that scrutiny.

At last, Silas shifted towards the opening in the branches. “All right. Let me have a look.”

Alaric waited while he studied the ground.

The king’s landing craft rose beyond the tents and turned towards the great opening beneath the warship.

Silas watched neither. His attention moved along the lower edge of the devastation, past a wagon, a length of broken metal and the bent limbs of another machine.

“We might be able to reach that one,” he said.

Alaric followed the direction of his gaze.

The wreck lay farther to their right, well away from the seamless body the king had touched. Its middle had fallen between jointed limbs. One extended towards the bank, ending in a broad plate half buried in the earth. Two work lamps stood beyond it. A separate plate lay nearer the bank, with a cut path between it and the wreck.

“What about the soldiers beside it?” Alaric asked.

“I know. There are probably more behind it.” Silas let the branch settle against his sleeve. “But see how the ground dips this side? We can get below the bank and follow it round to that broken plate. I can watch them from there before we try to cross.”

“You’ll help me get it?”

“I’ll try to get you close enough to look inside. That’s all I can promise. If I tell you we have to turn back, you need to listen to me.”

Alaric nodded too quickly. Silas waited until he held still.

“I mean it. If you spot something, tell me. Don’t go running ahead while I’m looking somewhere else.”

“I won’t. I’ll stay with you.”

“And if your foot gets worse, you tell me. Don’t wait until you fall.”

Alaric looked down the slope. Without the stick, the first descent would hurt. He had known it would hurt before he asked.

“All right. I’ll tell you.”

Silas studied him for another breath, then eased himself back from the crest.

---

Redd began sliding down towards the sack.

“Leave the sack, Redd,” Silas said. “I need you to stay here.”

Redd stopped with one hand above the strap. He looked at Alaric, then back at Silas. “You’ll need a hand with him down there. Look at his foot.”

“And I need someone here with Wena.”

“How are you going to help him if he falls? You’ve only got one good hand.”

Silas’s expression hardened. “I know. And I need to be able to help him without worrying that you’re about to draw your sword again.”

Redd pushed himself up upon one elbow. For a moment, Alaric thought he would argue loudly enough to carry beyond the bank.

Instead, Redd looked towards the place where his sword had scraped through the grass.

“I’m not going to do that again, Silas.”

“I heard you. I’m still not willing to risk it.”

Redd rubbed the dirt upon his cheek with the heel of his hand. It spread rather than coming away.

“I know I frightened Alaric.”

“You frightened all of us,” Silas said.

Redd’s hand lowered. Wena shifted against Alaric’s side and turned her head towards him.

“Yes,” Redd said at last. “I know I did.”

He drew his feet farther down the slope and sat with his back against the roots. The sword remained sheathed beside his leg.

“I should’ve left the sword alone,” he continued. “I know that. But he was right there, and everyone just kept getting out of his way—”

His voice stopped.

Alaric remembered the ridge of earth rising beneath Redd’s hand. He could not look at those same fingers without seeing it.

Redd followed his gaze to his hands, then looked at Alaric again.

“I’m sorry, Alaric.”

Alaric wanted to tell him it was all right. Redd had spent two days caring for him. It felt cruel to leave the apology waiting.

But it had not been all right.

“I thought we’d all have to fight,” Alaric admitted. “I didn’t know how we were supposed to stop you.”

Redd’s face changed. Silas looked back towards the field.

“I’m not going to put you through that again,” Redd said. “I promise.”

Alaric nodded. He did not know whether the promise was enough yet, but he had heard the effort inside it.

Above the trees, the enormous warship began to move.

Its light travelled across the branches, turning each leaf pale before passing on. The landing craft had vanished inside the great hull. Below, soldiers returned to the strip where the king had landed and began dragging a wagon into the space he had left.

The hum drew away slowly. Alaric could hear the fires now. He had not realised how loudly they burned.

Silas waited until the last great sweep of armour cleared the nearer trees. Then he looked at Redd again.

“Keep Wena down here with you, and have the sack ready. If you see anyone coming up the bank, take her into the trees. Don’t wait for us.”

Redd’s eyes moved towards Alaric.

“What about you two?”

“We’ll see them coming up the slope. If you have to leave, we’ll know which way you’ve gone.”

Redd looked down at Wena’s rope, still fixed to the low alder. He ran his fingers over the knot without loosening it.

“All right. I’ll keep her here.”

Silas nodded once and moved along the inside of the bank to look for their descent.

Alaric sat back carefully. The movement pulled at his ribs and brought his swollen foot beneath him. He kept the weight upon the other leg until Redd offered an arm. This time, he took it.

Redd helped him settle in the hollow beside the provisions and passed him the water bottle. Alaric drank a little, then handed it back. He could hear someone below calling for another lamp.

His ash stick lay where he had left it.

“Will you keep that here for me?” he asked Redd, nodding towards it.

Redd glanced towards the slope. “Are you sure you can manage without it?”

“I think so, if we take it slowly.”

“Then make him wait if you need to. You’ve only just got back on your feet.”

There was no joke behind the words.

Alaric crouched beside Wena. She pressed her head beneath his chin and tried to rise when his hand moved towards the ground.

“You stay here with Redd, all right, girl?”

Her paws shifted.

Alaric rubbed the rough fur behind her ears. He did not try to explain where he was going. The warmth beneath his hand made it harder to stand and leave her.

Redd settled beside her and placed one hand against her chest. “I’ve got her. You go on.”

Alaric looked at him. Redd met his eyes without reaching for another apology.

“I’ll look after her,” he said more quietly.

Alaric let go.

---

They went along the back of the bank before descending. Silas kept low beneath the thorn, lifting branches with his left hand and waiting for Alaric to pass before releasing them. His right arm stayed close to his body. When the brush thickened, he turned sideways so the dressing would not catch.

The ground dropped between two exposed roots. Alaric sat upon the upper one. He lowered his good foot until it found earth, then brought the other down carefully. The injured ankle turned a little when it took his weight.

He stopped. Silas was below him, one hand lifted. Alaric caught the offered forearm. Silas braced his boots against the slope and held him until he could stand.

Neither spoke. Without the warship overhead, Alaric could hear his own coat moving against the leaves. That sound frightened him more than the great ship had while it remained distant. This was something he might fail to control.

They followed the foot of the bank towards the right. Above them, thorn hid the place where they had been watching. Ahead, pale light reached across a shallow hollow and stopped against the metal plate Silas had pointed out.

Up close, it was taller than Alaric at its highest edge. The broken surface had folded upon itself. Soil filled the lower curve, and a torn branch rested across one side.

Silas stopped in its shadow. Alaric lowered himself beside him, breathing through his mouth until the pressure beneath his ribs eased. He could no longer see Wena or Redd.

A cut path lay beyond the plate. On its far side, the jointed wreck filled the space between two lamps. One of its limbs had folded backwards beneath the middle, lifting that side of the body clear of the ground. The nearer limb lay stretched towards them, its broad end resting beside the path.

Soldiers worked upon the other side. Alaric could see their shoulders rise above the metal whenever they stood. A length of chain passed from one pair of hands to another and disappeared behind the wreck.

Silas watched them without moving. Smoke reached the hollow. Alaric’s first breath tasted of wet wood. The next did not.

Alaric pulled the coat’s collar across his mouth. His stomach tightened, and for several breaths he could do nothing except keep the air moving through his nose.

Silas touched his left elbow. Alaric started shaking his head before the question came, then stopped himself.

“Just a moment,” he whispered.

Silas settled back. Alaric kept the cloth against his mouth. No one told him that the soldiers below were working quickly. No one mentioned the chains. He waited until he could draw a breath without swallowing against it.

“All right. I think I can go on now,” Alaric whispered.

Silas looked at him, making certain, before returning his attention to the path.

A soldier crossed it carrying a bundle of long wooden wedges. He passed the nearer limb and went around the machine. Another followed with a lamp and hooked it onto a projecting part of the far side.

Light entered beneath the wreck, covering ground Alaric had expected them to use. Silas drew back at once.

For a while, they remained behind the plate while the soldiers worked. A hammer struck wood upon the far side. The blows came unevenly, stopping whenever someone called out.

Alaric watched the nearest limb. Its broad end would hide them from the lamp beyond the machine. Between the path and that cover lay only a few paces of trampled stalks.

A few paces.

He looked down at the boot he could not fasten tightly.

Silas shifted beside him.

“Come after me when I move. Keep to this side of the foot.”

Alaric nodded. Silas glanced towards the soldiers once more. Then he crossed the path in a low, quick movement and disappeared behind the metal.

Alaric followed. His first step landed cleanly. The second sank into a rut hidden beneath crushed wheat. Pain travelled up his leg, but the ankle held. He shortened the next step instead of trying to match Silas’s stride.

Silas’s left hand appeared around the edge of the broad foot. When Alaric came within reach, Silas caught his left sleeve and guided him into the darkness behind the metal. Alaric lowered himself with one knee against the earth. He could feel each breath pulling beneath his right shoulder.

Across the path, the broken plate stood empty. Alaric tried not to look at the ground he would have to cross to reach it again.

Silas leaned close enough that Alaric felt the words against his ear. “Stay here a moment.”

Alaric nodded. He looked down and found his wrapped hand resting beside a man’s open fingers. The arm extended from beneath the machine.

Alaric withdrew his hand slowly. Cloth had gathered above the dead wrist, its colour lost beneath dirt. The fingertips had pressed shallow marks into the earth.

He kept his eyes upon them until Silas touched his sleeve and pointed towards the path.

---

The chain came first.

Two soldiers carried its near end between them. Behind them, another pair supported the middle, the remaining links dragging from a wagon.

They turned towards the limb hiding Alaric.

Silas pressed him lower.

Alaric brought his uninjured side against the inward curve of the broad foot, where its edge rose between him and the path. A broken length of the jointed leg lay above them, slanting towards the machine’s middle.

There was room to crouch beneath it, but not to straighten.

Silas settled behind him. His shoulder touched Alaric’s back, then moved away as he found enough room for the sword at his hip.

The soldiers stopped upon the other side of the broad foot.

“Get it under that joint,” someone said. “It’ll slip if you put it around the end.”

A lamp was set upon the outer edge of the foot. Its light reached the bent leg but stopped short of the hollow beneath it.

Alaric drew his head down.

One of the soldiers lowered the chain. Links struck the plate, loud enough to make Alaric’s teeth close upon the inside of his cheek.

**KRRRANK.**

The sound shivered through the metal against his shoulder.

“Hold it there,” a soldier said beyond the plate.

Boots entered the narrow space between the foot and the path. Alaric could see one through the gap beside his knee. Mud had dried across its toe. A loose strip of leather hung from the fastening.

The soldier crouched and reached a hooked pole beneath the joint. Alaric watched the iron point pass through the strip of lamplight beyond his boot. It caught a link upon the ground and dragged it closer.

The chain followed. Silas’s left hand closed around Alaric’s forearm, drawing him backwards. Alaric understood before Silas pulled harder: he needed to move his foot.

Alaric drew the injured foot back, holding it just above the earth. Pain tightened behind the ankle. The chain scraped through the place his boot had occupied and caught against a ridge beneath the broken joint.

The soldier pulled harder. Metal jerked beside Alaric’s leg, and his foot struck the earth.

Pain drove up through the injured ankle, sudden enough that his mouth opened.

No sound came.

Silas’s grip tightened. He held Alaric’s forearm against his own body, steadying him while the first spasm passed.

Alaric could not draw enough air.

The soldier outside shifted nearer. His knee appeared where the boot had been.

Alaric stared at the worn cloth over it. One loose thread moved whenever the soldier’s leg shifted. He could have reached out and touched it.

The thought brought his heartbeat into his head.

**THOOM.**

He waited for the soldier to turn the lamp.

**THOOM.**

The hooked pole pulled again. The trapped link came free.

“There. I’ve got it,” the soldier said.

The chain slid beneath the joint and rose towards the hands waiting above. Its links passed the opening one after another, drawing a trembling line of light across the ground.

The knee withdrew. Alaric kept waiting.

“Wait—don’t pull yet,” the soldier warned. “My hand’s still in there.”

The soldiers paused. Then the chain moved once more, more slowly.

The soldier stood. The lamp lifted from the edge of the foot, and its light followed his boots around the end of the limb.

Darkness returned beneath the joint. Silas did not release Alaric.

On the far side, a fastening struck shut. The soldiers tested it twice, then began carrying the loose end towards the wagon.

“Bring those timbers over here,” the same voice called. “We need to get this side up before we can slide them underneath.”

The answer came from beyond the wreck.

Alaric listened to the boots depart, following the sound as far as the path, then towards the stacked wood.

Only when the nearest voices moved away did Silas loosen his hand.

Alaric’s fingers remained curled around Silas’s sleeve. He made them open.

“Was that your ankle?” Silas breathed.

Alaric nodded.

“Did that make it worse?”

He could not tell yet. The pain filled too much of his leg to separate one part from another.

“I can’t really tell yet,” Alaric admitted.

Silas looked towards the path. “We can still turn back, if you need to.”

Alaric shut his eyes briefly. His first answer would have been a lie. He would have said it was nothing, that he could walk perfectly well, that Silas should not have asked.

Silas waited.

“Just give me a moment,” Alaric whispered. “Please. I’m not ready to go back.”

Silas settled his left shoulder against the metal. Alaric let himself breathe.

The chain now ran beneath the joint beside them. It rested slack upon the earth, but he could see where the last few links turned towards the soldiers preparing to pull it.

One of them could come back to check the chain.

---

Silas checked the far side before helping Alaric rise.

There was no room to stand beneath the broken leg. They backed out a little way, still sheltered by the broad foot, and Silas braced himself while Alaric put weight upon the good leg.

The other boot touched down. Alaric waited until he could bear the pain, then nodded.

They moved along the inside of the fallen limb towards the machine’s middle.

The metal changed as they approached. What had looked smooth from the bank was dented in places, gouged in others. Dirt filled long scratches, but the exposed silver beneath them held the light without a trace of rust.

The limb entered a great rounded joint. Beyond it, the lower edge of the body had split. Alaric stopped.

A flap of outer armour had been driven inwards. It rested against a second curved surface within the wreck, leaving an opening near the point where the two met.

The work lamp on the far side shone through another break above it. Something curved caught that light inside.

Alaric leaned closer before Silas could touch him.

“Wait,” Silas whispered.

“I think there’s something in there.”

“All right. Just let me get a look before you go any closer.”

The firmness in Silas’s whisper made him draw back. He had promised not to move ahead.

Silas lowered himself beside the split armour. Alaric settled upon one knee near his left shoulder, keeping the swollen foot stretched behind him.

Inside the broken outer shell sat a smaller enclosure, supported between thick metal braces. Its front had a narrow opening, scarcely wide enough for two fingers together.

Beyond it, light rested upon a clear curved surface. A dark centre hung within.

Alaric forgot the soldiers moving timber upon the other side. He saw Darcy’s quill darkening the smaller circle. He could remember the scrape when its point tore the paper.

“There—can you see it?” he whispered.

Silas followed his gaze. “Is that the orb she told you about?”

Alaric nodded. His throat had tightened too much for an answer.

Only part of it showed through the opening. The visible curve was smooth, the dark centre solid rather than broken into powder. He could not see the back.

It looked large enough to fill both hands. Alaric looked down at the cloth around his own and the dirt pressed into its folds.

For the first time, he could imagine carrying it.

Silas put his left hand against the enclosure. He felt along the edge of the opening, then reached lower, where the bent outer plate pressed against a projecting fastening.

Nothing moved.

“Can you see any way to get it out?” Alaric asked.

“Give me a moment. I can’t see a way in yet.” Silas shifted closer to the metal, his attention fixed upon the narrow opening and his burned hand held against his chest.

Alaric studied the surface above the opening. There were joins, but no handle. A narrow line followed one side and disappeared beneath the folded armour. Near its lower end, a small round head projected from a bent strip of metal.

Alaric pointed. Silas pressed it, then tried to turn it. The head remained where it was.

Alaric leaned towards the opening again. It was close enough now for him to see the thickness of the clear shell.

No light came from its centre. No movement disturbed it. It was only an object, held inside something neither of them understood.

Someone called upon the far side of the machine. Silas stopped.

Alaric held his breath until the footsteps passed away towards the wagon. A timber struck the ground. Another followed.

Silas waited through the second impact before easing the stolen sword from its sheath with his left hand. He placed its point against the narrow join.

Alaric watched him work it gently into the gap. The blade entered only a little way before meeting metal beneath. Silas tried another angle, then withdrew it.

“Do you think there’s a lock somewhere?” Alaric whispered.

“Could be. Or we might be trying to open the wrong part.” Silas indicated the enclosure with the point. “This whole piece might lift out, but I can’t see what’s holding it at the back.”

“Could we break that edge away?” Alaric pointed beneath the opening.

“I doubt it. Let me see if I can shift it.”

Silas laid the sword beside his knee and pressed his left palm beneath the projecting edge. He set his weight carefully, keeping the injured hand clear, and tried to lift.

The enclosure did not shift. Silas stopped before the effort could turn into noise.

Alaric had been watching the orb while he did it. With each movement, a reflection crossed its shell. White cloth, dark sleeve, a narrow piece of the lamp beyond the wreck. Nothing upon its surface told him whether he had chosen the right machine.

He had not expected that uncertainty to remain once he found it.

“What if it’s cracked at the back?” he asked.

Silas looked towards him.

“We can’t see that side,” Alaric continued. “What if we’ve come all this way and it’s no good?”

“We won’t know until we get it out where we can see it.”

“But can we get it out?”

Silas’s jaw tightened. He looked at the bent fastening, then at the place where the outer armour had folded against the enclosure.

“I don’t know how to open it, Alaric,” Silas said. “I’m trying to work it out.”

Alaric stared at him, then looked back at the slot. He had no better idea.

Outside, the chain scraped across the earth. Both men went still.

Several links rose beyond the broken limb, then settled as a soldier shouted towards the wagon. They were straightening it for the pull.

Alaric looked back through the narrow opening. They had reached it.

He had done what Darcy asked. He had remembered the shape, followed Silas, waited while a soldier knelt close enough to hear him breathe. And now a piece of metal stood between his hands and the thing he had come for.

“Please don’t tell me we have to leave it here.” Alaric’s voice had thinned. He hated how young it sounded.

Silas picked up the sword. “I haven’t given up on it yet.”

“Is there anything I can do?”

Silas studied the point where the bent plate met the housing. His mouth had drawn tight beneath the scar.

“Just give me a little room. I want to try this side.”

Alaric moved back as Silas shifted towards the damaged side.

A hammer struck wood beyond the machine.

Alaric rested his left hand beside the narrow opening. Light caught the bandages and placed their pale reflection upon the orb.

He could see his fingers there.

He could not put them around it.
