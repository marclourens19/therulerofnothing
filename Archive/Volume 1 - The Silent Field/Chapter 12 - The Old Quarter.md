# The Old Quarter

The lane opened ahead of them.

Alaric saw the distance to its far end and knew he could not run it.

There were no corners. No hanging cloth, no doorway deep enough to enter without being seen. Morning lay across the mud in one pale strip, and his ankle had begun to fold even when he placed it carefully.

Silas caught his elbow before he fell.

Behind them, a woman shouted.

Alaric did not need to understand the words. He knew the voice.

He looked towards the lane, then away from it.

People were gathering beneath a broad awning to their left. Baskets rested against ankles. A man pulled a handcart between the waiting workers while someone at the front argued about the number permitted through the gate. Beyond their shoulders, washing crossed the mouths of several narrower paths.

“There,” Alaric said.

Silas followed his gaze. “The well is the other way.”

“She’ll see us.” Alaric pulled against the hand holding him. “All the way down. We can’t——”

His breath failed before the rest could leave him.

Silas looked once behind them.

“Go.”

Alaric entered the gathering sideways, trying to make himself smaller without bending his injured shoulder. A basket scraped his hip. Someone swore when his boot caught theirs, and he whispered an apology that vanished beneath the argument ahead.

Silas’s armour drew eyes. People made room for him before looking at his face.

For a few steps, Alaric could see nothing of the path they had left. A woollen cap blocked it. Then a woman’s shoulder. Then the loose canvas above a loaded cart.

He slowed because the people around him were walking.

Every part of him wanted to run.

The awning ended beside a washing yard. Alaric slipped between a cart shaft and a wall, catching the wood before he could knock it against the woman waiting there. He let it settle and turned towards the strip of daylight beyond the yard.

“Not that way.”

A man stood beside an open gate, holding a bundle of folded sacking beneath one arm. Brown hair stuck out above his ears as though he had slept badly upon both sides of it. A green waistcoat showed through the open front of his patched coat.

He nodded towards the daylight Alaric had chosen.

“The gate at the far end is chained. You’ll be climbing it when she catches you.”

Silas moved closer to Alaric.

The man glanced past them. Whatever he saw removed the ease from his face.

“Quickly. Through here.”

He stepped backwards through the gate and pointed across the yard to a gap beside a lean-to. From where Alaric stood, he could see another lane beyond it.

An actual way through.

Alaric followed.

Silas came behind him, his left hand near the stolen sword.

The man shut the gate without dropping its latch. He led them around the lean-to, past two tubs of soaking linen and into a passage where the roofs almost met. Alaric’s shoulder brushed damp plaster. He kept his hands close to his body so the loose wrappings would not catch.

“Here.” The man stopped beneath a short flight of outside steps. “Against the wall. Don’t go past me.”

Alaric pressed into the shadow beneath them. Through the narrow opening at the passage’s end, he could see the street they had almost entered.

White bandages crossed it.

He stopped breathing.

The elf came fast despite the uneven fall of her boots. Her bound arm remained tight against her body. Her free hand caught the corner of a house, pulling her around it before her injured leg had properly followed.

Her remaining eye searched the lane ahead.

Alaric’s heartbeat struck inside his head.

**THOOM.**

The man with the sacking raised one finger without looking back.

Alaric held still.

A voice called from somewhere behind the elf. She answered sharply and went on. Another shout followed, farther away, but she was already beyond the opening.

The man waited until her footsteps had passed, then moved in the opposite direction.

“She hasn’t waited for the others,” he whispered. “Come on.”

They crossed one empty yard and followed the backs of the houses. Alaric could hear the woman again, louder now, her words striking hard against one another. A wooden barrier rattled. The answer came from the street behind her, too far away to belong to someone at her shoulder.

Silas glanced back but did not stop.

The man took them past a row of roofless privies and around a low wall, where he finally lowered the sacking from beneath his arm. He laid it over the stones and leaned across as though examining the lane beyond.

Alaric braced both forearms against the wall.

His lungs kept trying to take more air than his ribs allowed.

“You were at Marta’s yesterday,” the man said quietly.

Alaric lifted his head.

“With the dog. I was collecting empty sacks from the baker opposite.” His gaze moved to Silas, then back. “She wasn’t pleased to see all of you.”

“Where is she?” Alaric asked.

“Came out with her cart before the bell. Headed past the kilns.”

Silas kept his eyes upon the lane. “Did anyone follow her?”

“Not that I saw.”

“And us?”

The man watched the opening beyond the wall for several breaths before answering.

“No one yet. But I’d rather not hold a meeting here.” He gathered his sacks again. “I’m Redd.”

“Alaric.”

Redd nodded and glanced down at the foot Alaric was keeping clear of the ground. “Can you keep walking, Alaric?”

He wanted to say yes without having to test it.

He pushed away from the wall. Pain climbed from his foot to his knee, but the leg held.

“Yes.”

“Good. Stay beside me.”

Redd shortened his stride to match Alaric’s without being asked.

---

They reached the kilns without seeing another soldier. Redd stopped at the last turning to watch the path behind them. Silas waited beside him before beckoning Alaric onwards.

The broken well stood beyond the last kiln wall, where the ground began to rise away from Kelmend.

Its wooden frame had fallen inwards. Someone had covered the opening with crossed planks and weighted them with stones. A provision cart waited upon the far side, partly concealed by a tangle of elder branches.

Marta stood beside it. Alaric saw her hand leave the cart’s handle as something moved beneath the canvas.

Wena’s head appeared first. She pushed through the hanging folds and dropped from the low bed of the cart before Marta could catch her. Her paws struck the ground, and she came towards Alaric with her ears flattened and her whole body swaying with her tail.

He tried to crouch, but his ankle would not allow it.

Wena reached him before he could decide what to do instead. She pressed against his legs, whined and pushed her nose beneath his hanging hand.

“I’m here,” he whispered.

She smelled of straw and the dried herbs Marta kept among her stores. Alaric lowered his hand into the thick fur behind her ears. Wena pushed harder, almost sending him backwards, and he caught himself against Silas’s arm.

“I’m here, girl. Wait.”

She did not wait. Her nose travelled over his clothes, stopped at the blood upon his stomach and returned to his hand.

Marta approached them, her eyes passing over Alaric, Silas and Redd before returning to the path behind them.

“Where is Darcy?”

Alaric’s fingers stopped moving through Wena’s fur. “She wouldn’t come.”

Marta waited without moving towards either of them.

“The captain said he’d burn the quarter if she left.” Alaric had to swallow before he could continue. “We heard him. She knew he meant it.”

Marta looked towards the roofs beyond the kilns.

For a while, she said nothing. Her thumb pressed hard into the side of her forefinger, whitening the skin beneath the nail.

“Did she know I was waiting?”

“Yes. I told her.”

Marta closed her eyes.

“I thought she might refuse,” she said. The words came quietly, almost lost beneath Wena’s breathing. “I kept thinking that if she knew there was somewhere to go…”

She did not finish.

Alaric looked down. Wena had settled against his leg as though she intended to remain there regardless of what he did next.

“I’m sorry.”

Marta opened her eyes and looked at him properly. Her gaze moved over the torn shirt, the sagging wrappings and the way he held himself above his injured foot.

“You reached her,” Marta said. “Thank you for trying.”

Silas shifted beside him. Marta noticed the hand he kept against his chest.

“What have you done to that?”

“A grate blocked the way out. I held the joint while I heated it.” Silas kept his hand against his chest, unable to close the fingers.

Marta looked at the blisters. Her jaw tightened, but she did not try to examine them in the open.

Redd cleared his throat softly.

“My place is close. There’s water, and they can sit down.” He glanced towards the kilns. “I brought them clear, but I wouldn’t keep standing here.”

Marta looked at him. “You’re still in the old quarter?”

“Same house. The roof’s better.”

“How much better?”

“You can stand beneath all of it now.” Redd’s smile faded when Alaric shifted his weight. “It’s dry. I’ll take you there.”

Silas looked back along the route they had followed.

“Show me the approach before we go inside.”

Redd nodded and set his folded sacks upon the cart. He took its handles from Marta while she moved beside Alaric.

“Your left arm,” she said.

“I can walk.”

“Then walk with someone holding you.”

Alaric let her take his weight.

---

The houses grew farther apart.

At first, Alaric thought the spaces between them were gardens. Then he recognised thresholds beneath the weeds and a hearth standing by itself where the walls around it had fallen. A row of doorways opened onto rooms filled with nettles. Beyond them, an apple tree had grown through a roof and spread its branches above the chimney.

People had lived here long enough to plant trees. They had been gone long enough for the trees to enter their houses.

Redd followed a path worn through the grass. It turned away from the open road, keeping the remaining walls between them and the occupied quarter. The cart jolted over a buried stone. He looked back at Alaric, then chose a smoother strip along the next garden’s edge.

“Most of them moved closer to the wall years ago,” Redd said. “There’s no work out here now.”

“You stayed?” Alaric asked.

“I arrived after they left. Fewer arguments about where I slept.”

His cottage stood near the end of the row.

It was small, with a low roof patched in two colours of tile. The front window had one proper shutter and another made from boards that did not quite match. Someone had swept the threshold. A pair of boots dried upside down upon sticks beside the door.

Redd left the cart behind a standing garden wall. He showed Silas the narrow rear yard and the gap opening from it into the next abandoned plot. Silas checked both directions before joining them at the door. Redd lifted the latch.

“Mind the step.”

Alaric looked down.

“The one inside,” Redd added. “I keep meaning to do something about it.”

There was a second, smaller rise just beyond the door. Marta guided Alaric over it and into a room that smelled of cold ash, dried mint and wool left too near a fire.

A table occupied more space than the room could comfortably spare. Two chairs stood beside it, one with a folded scrap of leather beneath a short leg. A narrow bed fitted beneath the side window. Beyond an open inner doorway, shelves and a covered water barrel crowded a small kitchen.

Nothing matched. Everything had been put somewhere deliberately.

“The bed,” Marta told Alaric.

Redd moved aside when Alaric looked at him. “Go on. I’ve had it all night.”

Alaric sat carefully upon its edge. The mattress gave beneath him, and the relief of no longer standing reached his body so abruptly that he had to grip the blanket.

Wena came between his knees. She turned twice, found that neither direction provided enough room and lay down with her head upon his boot.

Redd watched her settle.

“Right. That’s most of the floor taken.”

“She can move,” Alaric said.

“No need. I’ve been stepping over that loose board for three years. At least she’s pleasant to look at.”

Redd went back for the supplies. Marta followed, leaving Silas beside the front window with the shutter open no wider than two fingers.

Alaric let his gaze travel around the room. A sword rested upon wooden pegs above a chest. The scabbard had been repaired near its mouth. Its leather looked darker and better cared for than the belt hanging beneath it, and the grip had been wrapped so neatly that Alaric could not see where the strip ended.

He looked away when Redd returned.

Redd left his folded sacks beside the hearth and placed a bundle upon the table. He noticed where Alaric’s gaze had been and followed it.

“My father’s.”

Alaric glanced back at the sword.

“Do you use it?”

“Enough to know I’m better at running.” Redd shifted the bundle to make space for Marta’s basket. “You’ll have noticed that was the part I offered.”

“A useful preference,” Silas said from the window.

Redd smiled as he moved the short-legged chair away from the bed. The folded leather slipped out from beneath it.

“Mind that piece,” he said quickly as Marta stooped to pick it up.

Marta caught the chair before it tilted and looked at him.

“You had time to mend a roof.”

“The roof was wetter.”

She put the leather back beneath the leg herself.

---

Marta opened her basket upon the table. Clean cloth, a stoppered jar and a small roll of dressings emerged from beneath the food. Redd brought a basin and water, then set a kettle upon the hearth and began laying a small fire.

Redd struck the flint, swore quietly when the first spark failed and tried again. Alaric watched the dry fibre catch. Redd cupped it until the flame steadied and lowered it beneath the twigs.

Marta drew the sound chair close to the bed. “Let me see your hands.”

Alaric placed them upon his knees. The outer strips Silas had tied around them were scarcely distinguishable from the cloth beneath. One end hung loose from his right palm. Dark water had soaked into the folds, carrying grit wherever the bandages crossed.

Marta began at his left wrist. He tried to watch, but when the first strip lifted, he looked towards the window instead.

“Tell me if I pull too hard,” she said.

“It doesn’t matter.”

Her fingers stopped. “It matters to the skin underneath. Look at me, Alaric.”

He did.

“I can loosen it with water,” she told him. “You do not have to sit there and let me tear it off.”

He nodded, ashamed without knowing what he had done to deserve the feeling.

Marta wetted the cloth and waited.

The room continued around him. A cupboard opened. Silas adjusted the shutter. Wena gave a long breath against his leg.

When Marta tried again, the cloth came free more easily. Beneath it, his hand looked smaller than he remembered.

She turned it towards the light. The cuts had opened again, but much of the blood lay beyond them, dark between his fingers and beneath his nails.

“It wasn’t all mine,” Alaric said.

Marta’s grip remained steady. “All right. You don’t have to tell me now.”

Alaric looked towards Silas. The man was still watching the approach, his injured hand held clear of the window frame. He did not turn or explain.

Marta changed the water before taking Alaric’s right hand. He kept his eyes upon Wena while she worked.

The memory returned anyway: another hand closing around his own, trying to hold something in place. Alaric’s fingers curled before he could stop them.

Marta loosened her hold immediately. “A moment?”

He nodded. She waited with the wet cloth resting in the basin. Behind her, Redd returned to the hearth and adjusted the kettle over the fire.

After a while, Alaric opened his hand.

Marta cleaned what remained and bound both palms in fresh cloth. Then she helped him remove the ruined grey tunic. The tear down its back made it easier to lift away from his shoulder. Beneath it, the linen shirt she had given him was wet through, its front stained where the guard’s blood had passed between the layers.

Redd had set a folded shirt and trousers upon the chest beneath the sword.

“Those are clean,” he said when Marta looked over. “They’re the ones I wear when I’m trying to be paid properly.”

Marta inspected the shirt and brought it to the bed. “Turn around, both of you.”

Redd obediently faced the kitchen. Silas remained at the shutter.

Changing took longer than Alaric wanted anyone to notice. Marta helped him lift the wet linen past his right shoulder and checked the dressing along his ribs. Water from the drain had reached it too. He breathed through his teeth while she replaced it, then let her guide his arms into Redd’s shirt.

Marta coaxed Wena out from between his knees and bent to his boots. Alaric reached down to help.

“Leave it,” she said. “You have just let me wrap those hands.”

She removed the easier boot first. The other released his swollen ankle reluctantly. Even the pressure of air against it seemed to hurt. Redd fetched a spare blanket from the chest without turning towards the bed and passed it back. Marta folded it beneath Alaric’s foot, then removed the dirty layers of cloth around his ankle.

“It’s worse,” Alaric said.

“You’ve been running on it,” Marta said, looking up at him.

“Will I be able to walk?”

“Not on this yet.” Marta returned her attention to the swelling. “Rest it. We’ll see how it is when you wake.”

She wrapped the ankle in fresh cloth, pausing when he needed her to.

Alaric let his head rest against the wall.

She helped him out of the patched outer trousers and the damp pair underneath, holding the bedcover across his lap while he changed. Something struck the bed frame as she folded the plain trousers aside.

Gerolt’s token.

Alaric reached for the pocket before she could put the trousers down.

Marta saw what he was doing and retrieved it for him. She wiped it dry and placed it upon the bedcover beside his hand.

The split shield had gathered a dark line of filth along its centre.

He rubbed it once with his thumb, then stopped. Fresh white cloth covered the thumb now.

Marta opened the bundle Redd had carried in with the supplies. Alaric’s black garments lay folded beneath the charcoal messenger’s coat.

“These go with you,” she said. “Keep them covered when you’re outside. No one needs another look at that collar.”

She wrapped the bundle again and placed it at the foot of the bed. His wet clothes went into an empty pail Redd fetched from the kitchen. Alaric slid the token into the pocket of his borrowed trousers before lowering his foot.

Marta caught his calf. “Keep it up.”

He rested his foot upon the folded blanket again while she checked the new wrapping.

Marta stood and turned towards the window. “Now you, Silas.”

“I’ll manage.”

Marta looked at the hand he could not close.

“Then manage those straps. Show me.”

Silas’s jaw shifted. He did not attempt it.

Redd took his place at the window. Silas worked the remaining glove off his left hand with his teeth and laid it upon the table. He unbuckled the sword belt with that hand and set it beside the hearth before sitting in the chair Marta had vacated.

Only then did he let her unfasten the armour.

The bent breastplate caught beneath his arm. He dragged in a breath when she lifted it clear.

“Stay still,” she snapped. “I am not doing this twice.”

She set the plates beside the hearth and covered them with one of Redd’s sacks, leaving the sheathed sword and its belt uncovered. Marta brought Silas’s right hand over the basin.

Alaric looked at the burned palm. Blisters rose along the places that had held the iron. One had broken, exposing a raw strip beneath his fingers. Silas kept looking at the window while Marta cleaned around it.

“He couldn’t move the grate any other way,” Alaric said.

“I heard him,” Marta said without looking up. “He can answer for himself. Keep that foot still.”

Silas glanced towards him and gave the smallest shake of his head. Alaric fell quiet.

Marta worked with the same care she had used upon Alaric’s palms. When Silas flinched, she loosened the cloth. When he tried to help with his other hand, she pushed it aside.

“No glove over this,” Marta said, securing the dressing. “If it sticks to the burn, I won’t be here to take it off for you.”

“I’ll leave it alone.” Silas drew his hand back carefully. His face had grown grey beneath the stubble.

Redd glanced back from the shutter.

“I’ve a coat on the hook. No badge on it, which ought to be an improvement.”

Silas looked at the patched brown wool. “You’ll want it back.”

“Only if you improve it. I’ve spent three winters meaning to mend those elbows.”

The smile came before Alaric could decide whether he was allowed it. Redd caught it and smiled back before returning his attention to the lane. Marta helped Silas guide his bandaged hand through the loose sleeve.

---

The kettle was murmuring when Marta finished putting away the dressings. Sunlight had reached the upper edge of the side window.

Alaric watched it travel across a crack in the plaster. Nothing followed it into the room. No order came through the door. Redd lifted the kettle from the hearth with a folded cloth and filled three cups, then found a fourth behind a row of jars.

He examined its broken handle and kept that one for himself.

Marta divided bread and cheese from the provisions and placed Alaric’s portion upon a small board beside him. Silas got up from the chair beside the bed, and she sat down for a moment herself.

Silas carried the short-legged chair to the window, nudging the scrap of leather beneath it with his boot. His borrowed coat remained open, leaving room for his bandaged hand. He set down his cup and brought the sheathed sword over from the hearth, placing it within reach of his left hand.

Redd sat upon the chest with his cup and a piece of bread. Wena lifted her head.

“Oh, you’ve noticed this.” Redd looked from her to the bread in his hand. “You slept through everything useful.”

“Wena,” Alaric murmured as her tail struck the bed frame. She looked at him briefly, then returned her attention to Redd.

“Does she answer to that?” Redd asked.

“Usually.”

“There’s another fellow near the west gate has a dog like her. He tells everyone it’s trained. He shouts ‘sit’ after it sits and ‘come’ after it reaches him. I’ve watched them for half an hour. Never been wrong once.”

Alaric looked down at Wena. She had edged one paw towards the chest without standing.

“Stay,” he said.

Redd grinned into his cup. The laugh hurt Alaric’s ribs, but it escaped anyway, little more than air. He pressed one hand against his side.

Marta looked up sharply.

“I’m all right,” Alaric told her.

This time, it was true enough.

Redd waited until Alaric lowered his hand, then set his cup beside him and broke a small piece from his bread. He glanced towards Marta.

“A little,” she said. “Then leave her to rest.”

Wena took the bread with surprising care. Redd held out his empty hand while keeping the rest of his portion out of reach. She checked beside him as well before settling back beside Alaric’s bed.

For several minutes, they ate without speaking.

Alaric could hear Silas swallow. A small insect worried at the closed side window, disappearing into the shadow of the frame and returning to the same patch of light. The kettle clicked softly as it cooled.

He finished the bread and discovered he had been listening for the sound of a key.

There was no lock in Redd’s door. A wooden bar leaned beside the frame, its brackets fixed upon their side.

Marta reached into the basket for another cloth-wrapped portion. She opened it halfway, exposing bread and a small packet of dried apple, then stopped.

Her thumb remained beneath the knot.

Alaric watched her fold the cloth back over it.

“For Darcy?”

Marta nodded without looking up.

“She used to take the apple out of anything I baked. Said the rest spoiled it.” Her thumb smoothed the fold unnecessarily. “I thought she could have these on the road.”

She put the portion back into the basket.

Redd stopped eating. Silas looked through the shutter.

Alaric remembered Darcy’s hands straightening the paper upon her desk. He had been angry that she would not leave. Now Marta sat with food she would not get to give her, and he could not find a way to tell her about the cell that would hurt less than what she already knew.

“She helped me,” Alaric said instead. “After she refused. She told me where I could look.”

Marta lifted her eyes. “For what happened to you?”

Alaric nodded. His fingers found the edge of the token through the borrowed trousers.

“There’s something inside the machines. An orb. She called it a war core. If I find one that isn’t broken, her father might be able to tell where it came from.”

“Helmi,” Marta said quietly.

“In Favale,” Alaric confirmed.

Marta leaned back in the chair, her gaze resting upon the portion she had returned to the basket.

“Did she tell you how to find him?”

“Yes.” Alaric looked towards Silas. “I need to go back to the farm.”

“You need to lie down,” Silas said, turning from the window.

“I know. But after.”

“We came through the trees because the road was being watched. You think they’ll have left the farm unguarded?”

“No.”

Alaric looked down at the swelling above his ankle bandage. He would still have to walk there.

“Darcy said an unbroken one. I don’t know whether there is one.” He raised his eyes. “But someone can read it if I find it. Until now, I didn’t even know what to look for.”

Silas’s gaze moved to Marta, but she was watching Alaric.

“Not before dark,” Marta said at last. “You rest until then. That foot stays up, and you leave the wrappings alone.”

“I’ll rest.”

“And you do not go there by yourself.”

Alaric had not meant to look at Silas so quickly. He tried to turn back, but the man had already seen it.

Marta set her cup upon the table. “Silas. I need you to take him.”

He looked down at his bandaged hand. “If they’ve put a company on that field, I can’t get him through it.”

“Then find out what is there before you take him into it. I’m asking you to help him reach the farm and come away alive. Not fight everyone standing upon it.”

“And if there is no way in?”

“Then there is no way in.” Marta’s voice hardened. “You tell him that, and you find somewhere to wait. You do not decide the risk is smaller because he cannot judge it himself.”

Silas held her gaze. His jaw shifted, and he looked towards Alaric’s ankle before answering.

“Then we wait. I won’t take him in blind.” He turned towards Alaric.

“We travel when the light is gone. I’ll look at the roads before we get near the farm.” He paused. “If I tell you we cannot approach, you listen.”

“I will.”

“And you don’t go looking while I’m asleep.”

Alaric glanced down at the foot lying upon Redd’s blanket. “I don’t think you’d have trouble catching me.”

Silas’s mouth moved faintly. “I’d prefer not to find out.”

Across the room, Redd brushed the crumbs from his palms.

“What happened on this farm?”

Alaric looked at him. Redd had stopped smiling, but the question held curiosity rather than suspicion.

“A battle,” Alaric said. “Or what was left of one. There were bodies everywhere.”

“Near here?”

“East,” Marta answered. “My uncle’s land.”

Redd looked towards the shutter. “I’ve heard nothing about that.”

“They don’t want you to,” Silas said.

“How many?” Redd asked Alaric.

The room remained small and warm around him. He tried to keep his answer inside it.

“Thousands. Humans, elves. Others too.”

Redd’s gaze returned from the window. “Elves?”

Alaric nodded.

Redd looked towards the sword above his shoulder. Only for a moment. When he faced Alaric again, the ease had left his expression.

“And now they’re hunting you.”

“Yes.”

Redd rubbed his palms against his knees and stood. “Let me come with you.”

Silas watched him. “Why?”

“Because I want to see it.” Redd looked at Alaric rather than the sword this time. “All that happening this close, and they think they can keep everyone from knowing. I’ve spent my whole morning moving out of their way. I’d like to know what they’re so desperate to keep us from.”

“There may be soldiers everywhere you want to look,” Silas said.

“I know how to keep out of sight,” Redd said.

Silas lifted his bandaged hand. “So do I.”

Redd’s mouth tightened, and some of his eagerness eased.

“I know,” he said. “I’m not saying it’s safe.”

Marta looked at him over the table. “You have a home here, Redd.”

“It’ll still be here when I get back.”

“Getting back is what concerns me.”

He looked around the room, taking in the patched shutter, the short chair and the remaining cups.

“It concerns me too,” Redd said. “But I’d still like to go. I can carry the supplies, at least. Neither of them should be doing that.”

Alaric had been waiting for someone to explain what Redd expected in return. Nothing followed.

“You don’t have to,” he said.

Redd looked at him. “I know. That’s why I’m asking.”

Redd remained beside his chest, waiting. Alaric had not expected anyone to volunteer after seeing the state they were in.

“I’d like you to come,” Alaric said. “I just don’t want you thinking we know how to do this.”

Redd glanced at Silas. “I hadn’t got that impression.”

“You stay with us,” Silas told him. “If something interests you, you tell me before walking towards it.”

Redd nodded. “Fair enough.”

Then he collected the cups and carried them into the kitchen, leaving his father’s sword upon the pegs.

---

Marta left the remaining provisions upon Redd’s table.

Darcy’s portion stayed wrapped. She placed it among the others, then divided the clean cloth and showed Silas which dressings were for his hand. He listened without interrupting.

“You need sleep as well,” she told him. “Take turns. Neither of you is watching anything properly after falling asleep in a chair.”

Redd dried his hands upon a cloth. “I’ll take over when he wakes me. I know what belongs on the lane.”

Silas nodded without leaving his place at the window.

Marta moved Alaric’s clothing bundle from the foot of the bed to the chest, leaving space for him to stretch out. Wena rose reluctantly while Redd pulled the bedcover straight. He repositioned the folded blanket beneath Alaric’s ankle as Alaric lay down, then drew the cover over him.

Pain settled beneath his right shoulder. Alaric adjusted once, then stopped fighting the mattress.

Marta stood beside him with the empty basket upon one arm.

“Will they let you back in?” he asked.

“I left through the workers’ postern with a provision cart. I will return through it with one.”

“They might ask where you’ve been.”

“People have been asking me questions all morning.” Her expression softened a little. “Let me deal with mine.”

Alaric looked towards the covered bundle upon the chest.

“Thank you. For bringing those. And Wena.”

Marta followed his gaze, then looked down at the dog waiting beside the bed.

“Take better care of each other on this road.”

“I’ll try.”

She nodded. It seemed to be the answer she had wanted.

Redd accompanied her to the cart. Alaric heard its wheels begin to move, catch briefly against something and continue. Marta said something too quietly for him to distinguish. Redd answered, and her steps went on without his. As the sound faded, Alaric’s eyes slipped closed.

Redd returned a little later and lowered the wooden bar into its brackets.

**Tok.**

Alaric opened his eyes.

Redd remained inside. Silas remained at the window. The sound had not placed anyone beyond reach.

Wena laid her chin upon the edge of the mattress.

Alaric lowered his hand beside her. Her nose touched the fresh cloth, warm and dry. He left his fingers there until she settled upon the floor, close enough that he could feel her breathing against them.

“Wake me before you go,” he murmured.

Silas turned from the shutter.

“We’re not leaving you here, Alaric.”

He had meant before they left for the farm. He thought he had.

He nodded anyway.

Redd began clearing the table. The basin emptied in the rear yard. Cups touched the kitchen shelf. Silas shifted in his chair, and its short leg knocked softly against the floor.

“You’ve moved it off the leather,” Redd said.

“Fix the bloody leg,” Silas muttered.

“I will. You can hold it steady when that hand works.”

Silas gave an answer too low for Alaric to catch. Redd laughed softly.

Alaric could have asked what he had said. He could have lifted his head and checked the window again.

Instead, he let his eyes close.

For a while, he followed the voices moving through Redd’s cottage. When he began to lose the words, he let them go.
