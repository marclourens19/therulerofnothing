# Liluth

The blade came free without sound.

Alaric could no longer see the people moving through Foramen. He heard them instead: a door closing near the cistern, bare feet crossing sand, the scrape of a metal cover being dragged over the last cooking pit.

Five horses advanced along the western street.

Their tack clicked softly between the ruined buildings. No rider carried a lamp. Moonlight found the pale edges of their armour whenever they passed beneath a gap in the old channels, then lost them again.

Silas reached Alaric before the riders reached the next arch.

“Keep that point down,” he said. “Half the people around you can’t see where you’ve put it.”

Alaric lowered the sword.

Redd came from the other side with Wena pulling against him. “She isn’t going inside.”

Wena barked towards the riders.

“Make her,” Silas said.

“You’re welcome to explain it.”

Orieth returned without her daughter. She caught the rope close to Wena’s collar and crouched beside her.

“The little one is frightened,” she told the dog. “You can frighten everyone else after she stops.”

Wena continued straining towards the western street.

Orieth pulled once. “Come.”

The dog resisted until another horse stepped upon loose stone. At the sound, she turned and allowed Orieth to lead her towards the council building.

Redd followed them to the doorway.

Orieth left Wena inside and returned to the threshold before the riders stopped.

“Stay there,” Silas told him.

“If you need me—” Redd began.

“I’ll call. Watch the other end of the street.”

Kurdag had reached the centre of the western street. He stood alone with his staff resting against one shoulder. The riders slowed before him.

The horse at their centre stopped first.

Alaric recognised the woman upon it when she turned her uninjured side towards the moon.

A dark covering crossed the place where Gerolt’s sword had destroyed her left eye. The scar beneath it pulled from her brow, across the bridge of her nose and into her cheek. Pale hair hid part of the damage before disappearing beneath the raised collar of her armour.

Her left arm remained close to her body. Metal protected it from shoulder to wrist, newer and less marked than the armour around it.

The hand hung stiffly beside her thigh, never joining the other upon the reins. Alaric saw Gerolt’s blade enter the bracer again. Heard the bone break beneath it.

The woman’s remaining eye found Kurdag.

“Move away from the road,” she said.

Kurdag shifted the base of his staff from stone to sand. “The road ends behind me.”

“We didn’t come for your settlement.”

“Then you can leave it.”

The two riders beside her separated. One remained near the broken arch. The other guided his horse towards the row of brown-walled homes north of the street.

Silas watched them without raising his sword. “They’re spreading.”

Alaric saw it now. The riders were not surrounding Kurdag. They were placing distance between themselves.

The woman spoke across the dark.

“A human male entered these ruins today. Young. Dark hair. He travels with an older human, another man and a farm dog.”

Redd moved away from the council doorway.

Silas put one arm across his path. “Not yet.”

“You were the one who wanted me inside.”

“I still do.”

Kurdag did not turn towards them. “What has he done?”

“He survived a site under Natharul investigation and fled lawful recovery.”

“That isn’t a crime.”

“It isn’t your concern.”

“You brought it into my street.” Kurdag pointed his staff towards the outer arch. “That made it my concern.”

The rider to the north stopped beside a roof covered in dry reeds. His horse turned sideways. One hand disappeared beneath his cloak.

“The survivor is required alive,” the woman said. “Give him to us, and no one here needs to make themselves his accomplice.”

No promise followed.

The woman noticed Orieth in the council doorway.

“You. Come away from them.”

Orieth stayed where she was. “No.”

“You would put yourself between humans and Natharul?”

“I’m putting myself between you and my daughter.”

“Then you can be judged with the rest.”

Alaric looked at the roofs around her. One flame would cross three homes before reaching the old stone hall. Beyond those walls lay the garden, the cistern and every store Foramen needed to remain alive.

The rider near the reeds withdrew his hand.

A faint orange glow occupied his palm.

Silas saw it at the same time. “Kurdag.”

The beastman’s ears angled towards him.

Alaric stepped past Silas’s arm.

“I’m here.”

Every mounted figure turned.

The orange light disappeared inside the rider’s fist.

Silas caught the back of Alaric’s coat and pulled him short of the open street. “What are you doing?”

“If they search, they’ll burn the roofs.”

“Walking into the road won’t prevent that.”

“She’ll follow me.” Alaric kept his voice low. “I only need to keep her away from the houses.”

Silas looked towards the covering over the woman’s left eye.

“And when she reaches you?”

“Gerolt left her a side she can’t see.”

“That’s not enough.”

“It’s what I have.”

Silas did not release him immediately. The woman urged her horse forwards.

“If the ground moves,” Silas said, “don’t watch it. Watch her.”

His grip opened.

Alaric entered the street.

The woman stopped ten paces beyond Kurdag. Her horse pulled against the reins, unsettled by the scent of so many people hidden nearby.

She studied Alaric from his boots to the sword in his hands.

“That isn’t his blade.”

Alaric did not need to ask whom she meant.

“No.”

“Where is the farmer?”

Gerolt stood within the burning doorway for an instant, sword dragging behind him while Alaric ran through the wheat.

“He’s dead.”

The horse sidestepped. She corrected it with her right hand.

“Did you see him die?”

“I saw him stay behind.”

“After he put a sword through my partner’s throat.” Her voice remained controlled, but the layers within it no longer moved together. “He attacked before a question had been answered.”

Alaric remembered the pale man framed inside Gerolt’s ruined doorway. The sword beneath his jaw. The wet clicking that followed.

“You tore his door off its hinges,” Alaric said.

“We gave him the opportunity to answer.”

“You threatened everyone in the village when he didn’t.”

The woman leaned forwards slightly. Moonlight caught the gold lines upon her armour.

“You called his name.”

Alaric’s stomach tightened.

“You warned him,” she continued. “My partner would still be alive if you had remained quiet.”

“Gerolt would’ve been dead.”

“He is dead.”

Alaric adjusted his hands around the sword. “Then killing everyone here won’t change what happened.”

“Come with us, and no one else needs to be involved.”

Behind her, the Fire rider opened his hand again.

This time the flame climbed between his fingers.

Alaric faced him. “He’s going to burn the roof.”

The rider’s arm drew back.

Kurdag struck his staff against the street.

Earth rose beneath the horse’s front legs.

The animal screamed. Its chest struck the sudden ridge, throwing its rider across the sand as the flame left his hand.

**WHUMM.**

Fire rolled along the roof.

Water struck it in six narrow streams from separate windows. None reached far enough alone. The streams crossed, spread and darkened the reeds while wind pulled the smoke away from the rooms beneath.

The roof continued burning at its western edge.

Foramen answered from every side.

Spears emerged between walls. A horn sounded once near the cistern. People ran clay jars from the covered channel while others dragged soaked cloth across the nearest doors.

The riders moved.

Wind crossed the street before Alaric saw the elf who shaped it. The pressure caught Kurdag across the shoulder and tore the hood from his head. A second stroke followed low, scattering sand in a line towards his legs.

Kurdag drove his staff into the ground. Stone folded upwards between him and the attack. The wind split against it and struck both sides of the street hard enough to burst shutters from their frames.

Silas ran past Alaric.

Fire wrapped his right hand and travelled along the flat of his sword. He did not try to meet the next pressure stroke. He sent a short flare towards the rider’s face, forcing the elf to abandon the spell before it reached the people carrying water.

The Wind rider’s horse reared from the flame. The elf abandoned the saddle before it could throw him and landed hard upon one leg, but his sword was ready when Silas reached him.

The woman upon the centre horse pointed towards Alaric.

“Take him.”

Water lifted from two of the clay jars.

It crossed the street in a twisting length and closed around Alaric’s sword arm. Cold locked from his wrist to his elbow. The pull wrenched him sideways.

He planted his feet as Silas had shown him.

The water dragged both boots through the sand.

Redd struck it with the flat of his father’s sword. The blade passed through without resistance. Water closed around the metal and pulled him forwards as well.

“I dislike magic,” Redd said through his teeth.

The rider shaping it turned one hand.

The water climbed towards Alaric’s throat.

Steel rang beneath the old channel. Silas forced the Wind rider back onto the leg injured in his fall and sent a narrow flare from his sword while the elf recovered.

Heat passed close enough to tighten the skin along Alaric’s neck. The flame touched the length of water stretched between him and the rider. Steam burst from the severed line, and the binding fell from him.

“Move!” Silas shouted.

Alaric moved.

He ran towards the open space beside the old cooking pits. The mounted woman followed as he had hoped.

Her horse covered half the distance before he had taken four strides.

The earth ahead of Alaric split.

He did not look down.

He watched her.

The woman’s shoulder shifted before the stone rose. Alaric threw himself left. A narrow point broke from the street where his chest had been and caught the side of his coat instead. Cloth tore. The force turned him and sent him onto one knee.

Her horse bore down upon him.

Kurdag swept his staff sideways.

The ground beneath the animal softened. Both front hooves sank to the joint. The horse pitched forwards, and the woman left the saddle before its head struck the street.

She landed upon her feet.

Kurdag had already turned elsewhere. The other Earth rider drove a rolling break towards the cistern, splitting the fitted stones in a line. Kurdag planted his staff across it. The fracture stopped close enough to shake water over the channel’s rim.

He could hold that line or hold the ground beneath the woman.

Not both.

She drew her sword.

Alaric rose and brought his own point between them.

The first impact nearly removed it from his hands.

He had seen her move. He had raised his guard. Neither mattered. Her blade struck his, slid down its length and stopped against the guard close enough for the edge to touch his knuckles.

She turned her wrist.

Alaric’s sword went aside.

Her shoulder drove into his chest. He struck the ground upon his back, still holding the grip only because both hands had closed around it.

The woman stepped over him.

“Thousands died around you,” she said. “You stood up and walked away.”

Alaric rolled as her sword entered the sand beside his ribs.

He cut at her leg without reaching it. She pulled the blade free and kicked his shoulder, sending him across the street.

There was no exchange to win. No movement slow enough to study.

Only distance.

Alaric found his feet and ran farther from the houses.

Behind him, wood cracked.

He looked back despite Silas’s warning.

The wall beside the council building had shifted away from its supports. A dark opening widened beneath the roof while people crowded through the passage below it.

Orieth stood beneath the failing edge, pushing two children towards the inner room.

Beyond them, Wena stood across the inner doorway with Orieth’s daughter behind her. Every bark struck the walls hard enough to reach the street.

Redd reached her.

“The west brace!” she shouted.

“Which one is that?”

“The wall falling on us!”

Redd dropped his sword and pressed one palm towards the ground.

Earth rose beneath the wall.

It did not form the small support Alaric had seen him attempt beside the road. A broad shelf broke through the floor, lifting the entire lower course of brown blocks. The roof lurched upwards. Dust poured across Redd’s head and shoulders.

He stared at what he had done.

Orieth caught him by the coat. “Keep it there!”

Redd braced both hands. His elbows shook, but the stone held while the remaining residents cleared the passage.

The woman’s sword passed through the space Alaric’s head had occupied.

Someone called her name from the western arch.

“Liluth!”

The Fire rider had regained his feet. Three Foramen residents held spears between him and the burning house. Blood crossed one side of his forehead. Flame gathered around both hands.

“They have children in there,” the Water rider shouted.

Liluth did not turn.

“Then they should have surrendered him.”

The Fire rider released both hands.

Kurdag twisted the staff where it lay across the fracture beside the cistern.

Stone closed around the elf’s ankle. His flame climbed higher as he fought to pull free. A woman leaned from the wet roof and emptied a clay jar over his head. Steam covered him. The three spears advanced through it.

One entered beneath his arm.

The fire went out.

Liluth. The name belonged to the woman standing over him.

Liluth attacked again.

He met the blade because there was nowhere left to move. Steel struck. His wrists gave before the rest of his body understood the pressure. His sword fell across his shoulder, and her edge opened the sleeve above his elbow.

The cut burned without depth.

Liluth reversed her weapon.

Alaric stepped backwards. His heel caught the rim of an extinguished cooking pit, and he went down beside it.

Near the western arch, Silas broke from the Wind rider by throwing fire across the narrow street. The elf’s next pressure stroke opened the flames, but Silas had already turned towards Liluth.

She raised one hand.

The covering over her left eye faced Silas.

He closed from that side before she turned.

His sword struck the newer armour protecting her damaged arm. Fire flashed along the point of contact. The metal blackened but did not open. Liluth turned too late, catching his second cut against the lower edge of her bracer.

Her arm buckled.

Silas saw it. “Alaric, get up.”

Liluth drove her boot into the ground.

A slab of stone rose beneath Silas and threw him across the cooking pit. He twisted before landing, shoulder striking first, sword still in his grasp.

Liluth faced Alaric. When she spoke, the layers within her voice no longer moved together.

“He died because you spoke.”

She lifted her sword with both hands. The damaged arm trembled beneath its armour.

“You should have died with him.”

Alaric pushed himself upright.

“I didn’t.”

Liluth came forwards.

Kurdag pulled his staff free of the fracture beside the cistern.

The Earth rider lunged after him. Kurdag caught the attacking spear beneath one metal band, turned it away and drove the heel of his hand into the elf’s breastplate. Armour folded. The rider left the ground and struck the broken channel behind him.

Kurdag did not watch him fall.

He extended the staff towards Liluth.

The street moved beneath her.

Not enough to break it. One fitted stone sank while the next rose beneath the outside of her boot.

Liluth’s weight shifted towards her damaged side.

She threw her left arm out to remain standing.

The blackened bracer separated from the plate beneath her shoulder. A narrow space opened under her arm, unprotected except for the dark cloth drawn across her ribs.

Silas reached her from the right.

He struck once at her sword rather than her body. The blades met. Liluth turned towards him, good eye following the greater threat.

Her missing eye faced Alaric.

Her boot scraped for purchase. Silas held her sword aside, but the blades were already turning back towards him.

Alaric placed his left foot forwards.

He chose what the next movement was meant to do.

Then he completed it.

The point entered beneath Liluth’s raised arm.

Cloth resisted first. Something harder followed. Alaric drove with both hands and felt the blade force its way between ribs.

Liluth’s body turned around the steel.

Her sword fell from Silas’s blade. The point struck stone.

Alaric continued until his guard reached her armour.

The movement ended.

For an instant, Liluth remained upright between him and Silas.

Her good eye found Alaric.

No word came.

The fingers of her damaged hand opened once beside his shoulder. Her weight travelled down the blade, pulling his arms with it.

Alaric withdrew the sword.

Blood followed.

Liluth dropped onto one knee. Her undamaged hand pressed against the wound, then slipped. The earth beneath her palm trembled without rising.

She fell upon her left side.

Alaric knew what he had done before she stopped moving.

He had known before the sword entered her.

A pressure stroke split the rim of the cooking pit beside him.

“Alaric!” Silas shouted. “Move!”

The battle had not ended with her.

Alaric ran.

Silas met the Wind rider near the western arch. Their blades struck inside a whirl of sand, the elf favouring the leg that had taken his fall.

The rider looked towards Liluth’s fallen body.

Silas did not.

The elf dragged the air back for another cutting stroke. Silas stepped inside it before it formed, caught the rider’s weapon against his guard and drove fire-wrapped steel beneath the chin.

The wind collapsed outwards.

Alaric covered his face as sand struck him.

Near the cistern, Kurdag lowered the Earth rider from the broken channel. The armour remained folded inward. The body inside it did not move.

The Water rider stood alone beside her horse.

A ring of shallow water turned around her wrists. Four Foramen spears pointed towards her from different streets. Orieth stood behind the nearest one with dust across her cropped hair.

“Drop it,” she said.

The rider looked towards Liluth.

Her water fell to the road.

She removed her sword with two fingers around the grip and let it drop beside it.

“I surrender.”

No one lowered a spear.

---

The fire surrendered last.

It had entered the reeds above two homes and crossed into one of the old wooden supports before enough water reached it. Smoke remained beneath the open roofs after the flames were gone, catching in every throat and turning the covered lamps into pale circles.

Two of the riders’ horses had fled into the dead streets. Three remained in Foramen, shivering while residents cut away damaged tack. Liluth’s horse stood after Kurdag released its legs from the softened road, blood darkening one knee.

Alaric left his sword upon the council table and joined the line carrying jars. He kept going until someone took the empty one from him and told him the cistern could not spare more.

Only then did he see the musician.

The man lay beside the passage to the inner rooms. Wind had opened his coat and the body beneath it in one diagonal line. One arm remained around a child who was no longer there.

The three-stringed instrument rested beneath the broken channel. Its wooden face had split near the lower edge. Two strings remained drawn across it. The third curled against the sand.

Alaric stopped close enough to recognise the dark stain beneath the man’s side.

He had not known his name.

Someone called for another bandage near the burned house. Alaric picked up the instrument and carried it to the council building.

Wena waited inside the doorway.

Orieth’s daughter sat against her chest with both arms buried in the fur around her neck. Wena watched every person who entered. When Alaric crossed the threshold, her tail struck the floor once.

He placed the instrument upon the low table.

Redd stood beside the lifted wall while two residents wedged timber beneath the shelf of earth he had raised. Dust had turned his hair grey. He kept looking down at his hands whenever he thought no one was watching.

Orieth returned from the street with blood upon one sleeve that did not appear to be hers.

“You can let it go now,” she told him.

Redd lowered his palms.

The broad shelf remained.

He looked at Orieth. “Was it supposed to do that?”

“You’re asking me?”

“You sounded confident.”

“I was hoping you knew.” She went to her daughter and checked the child’s face and arms. “At least it’s still up.”

Redd retrieved his father’s sword from the floor. He wiped dust from the hilt and said nothing more about the earth.

Silas found Alaric beside the table.

His coat had been cut across one shoulder. The skin beneath remained unbroken. Blood darkened the length of his sword, though none appeared upon him.

“Your arm,” he said.

Alaric looked at the opening in his sleeve. The cut beneath it had already stopped bleeding.

“It’s shallow.”

“Let someone with bandages decide that.”

“Are you hurt?”

Silas moved his shoulder once. “No.”

Alaric looked through the doorway.

Liluth remained where she had fallen. Someone had covered her face, leaving the rest of her beneath a length of brown cloth. Alaric picked up his sword from the table. A dark line ran almost to its guard.

He held it away from Wena and the child.

“I meant to kill her,” he said.

Silas followed his attention into the street.

“I know.”

“At the fort, I didn’t know until the knife was already inside him.”

“This wasn’t the fort.”

Alaric waited for something more.

Silas took a folded cloth from the medicine supplies and held it out.

“Clean the blade before it dries.”

Alaric accepted the cloth.

He cleaned the sword himself.

---

The surviving rider sat beneath the old arch with her ankles bound and both hands secured in front of her. Someone had removed the fitted armour from her forearms. Without it, she appeared younger than Alaric had first thought.

Six people stood around her. More listened from the nearby roofs and doorways.

Kurdag remained outside the circle. A split crossed the wood of his staff near its lower band. He had wrapped one hand around it to keep the damage closed.

The frightened man who had met Alaric with a spear now held the rider’s sword.

“Ask her again,” he said.

Orieth was crouched before the prisoner. “Did Liluth send word before you entered the city?”

“I don’t know.”

“You rode with her from Kelmend.”

“She left us twice after dark. She said she was checking the trail.” The rider glanced at the sword in the man’s hand. “She could have sent a bird. She could have done nothing. She didn’t tell us.”

The man with the sword looked towards Kurdag. “She’s lying.”

“I’m not.”

“You expect us to trust that?”

“No,” the rider said. “I don’t expect you to.”

Silas stood beside Alaric at the edge of the listeners.

“What were your orders?” he asked.

The rider turned towards his voice. “Recover the survivor and return him to Kelmend alive.”

“Whose orders?”

“The field command.”

“Did field command send you into the Expanse?”

“Liluth said the trail would be lost if we waited.”

“That isn’t what I asked.”

“No,” the rider said.

The man holding her sword stepped closer. “Then no one knows you found us.”

“I didn’t say that.”

“You said she went alone.”

“I said I don’t know what she did.” The answer came louder than her others, then dropped when the nearest spear moved. “She told us almost nothing. We were meant to follow, take the human and return before command sent a larger party.”

“A larger party will come looking for you,” someone said from a roof.

“Perhaps.”

“That means yes.”

“It means I cannot promise you otherwise.”

The man raised the rider’s sword. “Then we kill her and bury all five beyond the dead streets.”

Several residents answered at once.

One agreed. Another demanded he lower the weapon. A woman near the burned house asked what killing the prisoner would change if a message had already left. Someone else said releasing her would make the message certain.

The rider pulled against the rope when the sword moved nearer.

Orieth stood between them. “Put that down.”

“Your daughter was beneath that roof.”

“I know where my daughter was.”

“Then move.”

“No.”

Kurdag entered the circle.

He took the sword from the man before the grip could become a struggle. The man released it reluctantly and did not step away from the prisoner.

“We decide together,” Kurdag said.

“While we argue, more riders could be coming.”

“Then we prepare for them.” Kurdag gave the sword to a woman behind him. “Move the western watches beyond the dead arch. Wake everyone who knows the northern paths. Count the water and see what the fire cost us.”

The man faced the prisoner. “And her?”

“She stays bound until the council finishes.”

“For how long?”

Kurdag looked around at the people waiting for him to decide alone.

“I don’t know.”

The spears around the prisoner remained raised.

Alaric stepped away before the argument began again.

Kurdag followed him to the council building.

“They came for me,” Alaric said.

“They chose what they did after they arrived.”

“If another group comes, they’ll be looking for the same person.”

Kurdag examined him. “What are you asking?”

“Which way avoids the centre?”

Silas had followed them. “It adds at least two days.”

“Then it adds two days.”

“You wanted the quicker way when we arrived.”

“That was before this.” Alaric looked towards the people carrying burned reeds from the nearest roof. “I don’t want to lose anyone else. If it takes longer, it takes longer.”

Redd approached with his travelling sack already over one shoulder. “I appreciate being consulted after the decision.”

Alaric turned towards him. “Do you want to stay?”

Redd glanced at Orieth’s daughter inside the council building, then towards the prisoner beneath the arch.

“No,” he said. “But I don’t enjoy leaving people to repair a wall I helped lift out of the ground.”

“It would have crushed them if you hadn’t,” Alaric said.

“It can be useful and inconvenient at the same time.”

Silas took the travelling sack from him and tightened the loose shoulder strap. “Pack the water. Complain while you do it.”

“That was the plan.”

Redd went to the covered cistern.

Kurdag led Alaric and Silas inside. He cleared a space upon the low table without moving the broken instrument and drew three lines through the dust.

“The direct route runs here,” he said, indicating the deepest mark. “It will bring you within half a day of the central crater. Don’t use it.”

His claw traced a wider path west.

“Follow the buildings whose windows have melted closed. When you reach two stone figures facing away from one another, turn south. The ground between them looks level. It isn’t. Keep to the darker sand.”

Silas studied the route. “Water?”

“A buried channel crosses the southern line. You’ll hear it before you see the opening. Fill everything there. After that, you have three dry days if the paths remain where they were.”

“And if they don’t?” Alaric asked.

Kurdag tapped the western line. “Return to the figures. Do not follow your own tracks if they begin curving towards you.”

Redd returned in time to hear the warning.

“I was worried the safer road might become dull.”

“You will be disappointed,” Kurdag said.

He wiped away the direct route and left the western line in the dust.

They gathered the blankets, food and water they had not unpacked. Alaric checked the satchel beneath his cloak. The wrapped orb remained cold and still inside it. Redd fastened Wena’s rope while she pushed her nose against Orieth’s daughter through the doorway.

Orieth came to collect the child.

She checked the knot Redd had tied and pulled the loose end through a second time.

“That would’ve opened before the outer arch,” she said.

Redd inspected the corrected knot. “I was leaving room for improvement.”

“You found it.”

Redd accepted the end of the rope from her.

“If the wall shifts again—”

“We know where to brace it now.”

He nodded.

She lifted her daughter. The girl kept one hand in Wena’s fur until distance drew it free.

Orieth carried her daughter towards the injured without saying goodbye.

Kurdag waited at the beginning of the western street. Behind him, Foramen remained awake. Lookouts left through the northern ruins. Wet reeds came down from the burned roofs. The prisoner had been moved inside the council building, and the disagreement followed her through the door.

Kurdag indicated the road with his staff.

“The first figures are two days from here if you don’t lose the dark sand.”

“We won’t,” Alaric said.

“You might.” Kurdag looked at Silas. “He knows enough to check.”

“We’ll check,” Silas said.

Alaric rested one hand against Wena’s back as they passed Kurdag.

“I’m sorry about the musician.”

“So am I,” Kurdag said.

He turned towards his people.

They left before the sky changed.

The outer streets received them one by one. Silas went first. Redd followed with Wena, and Alaric took the rear until the last repaired wall of Foramen disappeared behind the ancient stone.

Through the first dead street, only their steps travelled through the ruins.

Then a string sounded behind them.

The note was thin and uncertain.

A second followed.

Alaric recognised the beginning of the melody from the fire.

He listened for the third.

It never came.
