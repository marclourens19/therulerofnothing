# Chapter 20 — Story Design

_Status: architecture approved through the Chapter 20 question-and-answer process on 2 September 2026. Final title remains open until the prose has been drafted and reviewed._

## Chapter Purpose

Remain entirely in Alaric's close-third viewpoint and resume on the exact instant Chapter 19 ends: Foramen has extinguished every light, five mounted figures have entered the western street and Alaric has drawn Silas's spare sword.

This chapter closes Liluth's pursuit rather than extending another chase across the Great Expanse. Its central movement is an exposed confrontation, not another infiltration, confined escape or prolonged attempt to hide. The danger comes openly into Foramen and forces its residents, Alaric and his companions to decide what they will defend and what violence they are prepared to use.

The chapter must give Alaric material agency. He does not merely object to cruelty, surrender himself or follow another person's instructions. He forms a narrow, imperfect plan: draw Liluth away from the occupied houses, keep her attention fixed upon him and use the physical limitations Gerolt left her if an opening appears. He cannot predict the battle, command Foramen or defeat Liluth through superior skill.

Alaric intentionally kills Liluth after Kurdag, Silas and Liluth's own fixation create the only opening he is capable of using. The victory belongs to collective action, but recognising the opening and choosing to strike belong to Alaric.

The aftermath tests Foramen rather than presenting it as morally perfect. One rider survives without knowing whether Liluth reported the settlement. The community cannot abandon its cistern, crops, children, stores and years of labour, yet releasing, killing or indefinitely imprisoning the captive each violates something its people value. The council begins that dispute without resolving it in this chapter.

The chapter ends before dawn as Alaric, Silas, Redd and Wena leave by the slower route Kurdag recommends. Behind them, someone tries to resume the musician's unfinished melody from Chapter 19. The missing note never comes.

## Structural Difference From Recent Chapters

### Plain scene skeleton

**blackout → open demand → negotiation under visible threat → Alaric identifies an immediate attack → Alaric redirects Liluth → public battle across a defended settlement → collective opening → deliberate killing → unresolved communal judgment → chosen departure**

This must not become:

**hide → sneak → nearly discovered → quiet killing → escape through narrow route**

Specific safeguards:

- Alaric reveals himself deliberately; discovery is not an accident.
- The opposing sides speak face to face.
- Combat occurs across open streets, roofs and occupied structures.
- No drain, tunnel, crawlspace, hidden door or secret passage solves the chapter.
- No rescuer arrives from outside Foramen.
- The group does not escape while Foramen fights in their place.
- Liluth does not survive to restart the same pursuit.
- The principal consequence is not another severe injury to Alaric or his companions.
- The chapter includes no new memory, core activation or mysterious headache.

## Viewpoint, Time and Starting State

### Viewpoint

- Entire chapter: Alaric close third.
- Do not enter Liluth, Kurdag, Silas, Redd, Orieth or the surviving rider's private thoughts.
- Liluth's grief and loss of control must be visible in what she says, notices, demands and chooses.
- Alaric does not know any rider's name until it is spoken within his hearing.

### Time

- Begin immediately after the final line of Chapter 19.
- The confrontation occurs during the same night.
- The battle is short, violent and difficult to control. It must not occupy hours.
- Immediate treatment, recovery of bodies, the first council disagreement and preparations for departure occupy the remainder of the night.
- End before dawn as Alaric's group leaves Foramen.
- Do not add a complete extra day of rest, burial ceremonies or farewell scenes.

### Physical starting position

- Alaric stands near the council building with Silas's worn spare sword drawn.
- Silas is nearer the western street and has already drawn his own weapon.
- Redd stands near Wena with his father's sword.
- Wena is agitated by Liluth's familiar scent.
- Orieth has taken her young daughter towards the inner rooms.
- Kurdag is walking west with his staff.
- Five mounted figures have entered the far end of the street.
- All public fires and window lights have been extinguished.
- Foramen's residents are moving according to an established defensive practice rather than panicking without purpose.
- The ancient war core remains wrapped, hidden and dormant with the group's belongings.

## Established Opposition

### Five riders total

Chapter 19 explicitly reports five riders. Liluth is one of those five.

1. **Liluth:** High Earth『Affinity』; commander of the small pursuit.
2. **Wind rider:** High Wind『Affinity』; reconnaissance, rapid movement and cutting pressure.
3. **Fire rider:** Common Fire『Affinity』; threat to Foramen's open-roof homes, stored food and dry structures.
4. **Water rider:** Common Water『Affinity』; restraint, disruption and battlefield support. This rider survives.
5. **Earth rider:** Common Earth『Affinity』; protection, mounted stability and breaking local defences.

Do not pause the action to state classifications. The abilities must be understood through use. The ranks belong to the design and bible unless a character has a credible reason to name them.

### Liluth's authority

Liluth possesses legitimate orders connected to finding and capturing the impossible survivor from the battlefield outside Kelmend. She does not invent the wider Natharul interest in Alaric.

She exceeds that authority by:

- taking only five riders into the Great Expanse;
- concealing the personal revenge driving the pursuit;
- refusing to withdraw after discovering an inhabited settlement;
- treating resistance as permission to punish residents;
- eventually abandoning capture in favour of killing Alaric.

The accompanying riders know they are tracking a battlefield survivor wanted for questioning. They do not know everything Liluth learned, what Eldrin meant to her or how far she is prepared to depart from orders.

Liluth has not told them whether she reported the route or Foramen before entering. The surviving rider is genuinely ignorant of this, not secretly lying.

### Why only five

- Speed and secrecy mattered more to Liluth than formal support.
- She expected Alaric, Silas, Redd and Wena—not a settlement of roughly two hundred people.
- She did not want command examining the private motive beneath her pursuit.
- Her riders believe this is an authorised recovery mission conducted by a scout commander, not a private execution.

### Liluth's physical condition

- Gerolt permanently destroyed her left eye.
- Her cheek remains deeply scarred from brow to mouth.
- The arm broken during the farm confrontation has received treatment but remains weaker and painful.
- Armour conceals much of the damaged arm, allowing her to present strength until sustained combat exposes the limitation.
- These injuries constrain her without making her helpless.
- She remains far more experienced than Alaric and would defeat him immediately in an ordinary duel.

### Eldrin

- Eldrin's name remains reader-only. Alaric must never hear or learn it.
- The exact cultural or romantic definition of Liluth and Eldrin's bond remains unresolved.
- Liluth may refer to him as **him**, **my partner** or **the man your farmer butchered**, but never name him within Alaric's hearing.
- Do not give Liluth a dying explanation of their relationship.
- Their bond should be evident through the disproportionate control Gerolt's death and Alaric's survival possess over her conduct.

## Foramen's Defence

Foramen has survived inside the Great Expanse because it has plans for discovery. Its residents are not trained as a conventional army, and the settlement is not secretly invincible.

### Defensive method

- Extinguishing the lights removes distant targets and protects night-adjusted residents who know the streets.
- Weak Wind users redirect smoke and spoken warnings through prepared roof channels.
- Several Faint Earth users brace the same wall or street together rather than producing one spectacular spell.
- Weak Water users protect stored grain and dampen roofs against Fire.
- Lookouts communicate through whistles already established in Chapter 19.
- Residents guide children and noncombatants into the most stable inner structures.
- Tools, hunting spears and repaired weapons provide physical defence.
- The community's strength comes from coordination, preparation and knowledge of its home—not a sudden increase in individual power.

### Kurdag's limitation

Kurdag is an Exalted Earth user and the strongest person in Foramen, but he cannot simply destroy all five riders.

- Ancient foundations beneath Foramen are cracked, hollow and altered by the vanished city.
- Unrestricted Earth magic could collapse homes, rupture the cistern or bury the people he is protecting.
- The riders spread pressure across roofs and civilians, forcing Kurdag to defend several places rather than concentrate everything upon Liluth.
- Until the first attack, Kurdag attempts negotiation rather than make Foramen responsible for beginning the violence.
- Kurdag uses immense control rather than indiscriminate scale: lifting protective ridges, breaking a horse's footing, bracing structures and changing the ground beneath specific attackers.

His restraint is competence, not weakness.

### Orieth and Redd

- Orieth helps direct residents because she is a council member, mother and survivor—not because the chapter needs her to reform Redd.
- Liluth recognises Orieth as an elf living among escaped human Faints and treats her as a traitor or degraded subject.
- Orieth refuses through action rather than delivering a speech about belonging.
- Redd and Orieth defend the same residents when the attack begins.
- Neither apologises, forgives nor announces a changed opinion.
- Redd's instinctive Earth response surfaces under pressure, but no one identifies its true Exalted ceiling.
- His magic may hold a failing wall or lift a ridge farther than he intended. The effect surprises him, then the battle forces him to continue before anyone can examine it.

### Wena and the child

- Wena does not charge Liluth or create Alaric's killing opportunity.
- Orieth's daughter remains frightened by the fighting.
- Wena stays with or returns to the child, placing herself between the inner room and the street.
- Her contribution is protective stillness, warning and presence—not another improbable rescue.

## Scene Architecture

## Scene 1 — The Dark Holds

### Immediate want

Alaric wants to understand whether the riders have found him without giving away the position of Foramen's residents.

### Pressure

Wena already recognises Liluth's scent. The riders have heard barking and entered the inhabited street. Foramen's darkness can delay identification but cannot make two hundred people disappear.

### Development

- Continue from Alaric drawing the sword.
- Silas reaches him or speaks without crossing the whole square.
- His first instruction is practical: Alaric remains behind cover while they learn what the riders want.
- Alaric recognises the wounded woman's outline or voice before he knows her name.
- Kurdag places himself openly in the western street with his staff, preventing the riders from moving deeper without passing him.
- Foramen's defenders remain present but difficult to count in the dark.
- No lengthy whispered planning occurs. The settlement is already acting.

### Turn

Liluth calls for the human survivor and describes enough of Alaric and Wena that concealment is no longer a lasting solution.

---

## Scene 2 — The Demand

### Immediate want

Kurdag wants the riders to leave without combat. Liluth wants Alaric surrendered under Natharul authority.

### Liluth's presentation

Liluth begins controlled, formal and recognisably military. She speaks as though the outcome is administrative rather than personal.

She may establish:

- the survivor is required for questioning;
- sheltering him after the demand makes residents participants;
- Foramen has one opportunity to avoid being treated as obstructing Natharul.

She must not promise safety. A suitable underlying construction is:

> Give me the survivor, and no one here needs to make themselves his accomplice.

This wording preserves punishment as an available consequence without requiring a transparent lie.

### Kurdag's response

- He asks what crime Alaric committed.
- Liluth provides institutional suspicion, not proof.
- Kurdag does not surrender someone merely because a powerful nation demands him.
- He does not answer in polished thematic maxims.
- He may ask Alaric whether he wishes to answer for himself, leaving the choice without placing responsibility for Foramen's survival entirely upon him.

### Alaric's narrow plan

Alaric sees that hiding forces the riders to search occupied homes. He also sees at least one rider beginning to angle towards the open roofs rather than watching Kurdag.

He decides to step into view before Silas can make the decision for him.

His purpose is not to surrender or die. It is to:

1. make Liluth focus upon him;
2. draw her farther from the occupied structures;
3. keep her damaged left side relevant if she closes the distance;
4. give Kurdag and Silas fewer civilians between themselves and the threat.

Alaric cannot foresee the complete fight.

### Emotional conflict

- Liluth recognises him immediately.
- She recognises the sword and may realise it is not Gerolt's.
- Her control begins to strain when Alaric confirms Gerolt died.
- Alaric does not apologise for being alive.
- She may call Gerolt **your farmer**, exposing how completely she has attached Alaric to the man who killed her partner.
- Alaric understands that Gerolt's violence had a survivor standing on the other side of it. He does not therefore accept responsibility for Liluth's choices.

### Turn

Alaric notices the Fire rider has moved into position to attack a roof, food store or defensive gathering while Liluth holds everyone in conversation. He warns Kurdag or calls the rider out.

Liluth does not order the Fire rider to stand down quickly enough.

The attack begins.

---

## Scene 3 — Foramen Defends Itself

### Immediate want

Alaric must keep Liluth away from the residents while surviving a fighter he cannot match.

### Required battle character

- Open, confused and communal rather than a sequence of isolated duels.
- Short enough that every action retains consequence.
- Geography remains clear through major anchors: western street, council building, roofs, cistern route and inner rooms.
- Do not count steps, doors or exact distances repeatedly.
- Magic affects structures and choices; it does not become decorative spectacle.

### Opening exchanges

- The Fire rider sends flame towards dry roofing or stored provisions.
- Foramen's Water users cannot overpower the fire individually. Together, they wet the intended surface while Wind users bend smoke and heat away from the inner rooms.
- Kurdag raises a controlled ridge that breaks the Fire rider's line without rupturing the street.
- The High Wind rider attacks Kurdag or the defenders sustaining the roof protection.
- Silas intercepts the strongest mobile threat and is permitted to look experienced without being injured to prove the danger.
- The Common Earth rider attempts to stabilise mounted footing and break Kurdag's smaller barriers.
- The Water rider uses restraint against residents or Alaric and later survives the battle.

### The musician's death

- The unnamed musician from Chapter 19 is helping move children, carry water or sustain the settlement's defence.
- The musician does not leap in front of an attack meant for Alaric.
- A rider's attack kills the musician amid the wider assault.
- The death is immediate and observed only as much as Alaric's position allows.
- Do not pause the battle for a biography, final words or prolonged grief.
- The instrument survives or is recovered damaged, allowing the unfinished melody to return during the ending.

### Orieth and Redd

- Falling stone or a damaged wall threatens residents moving towards shelter.
- Orieth reacts first through knowledge of the structure or people.
- Redd attempts the small Earth movement he believes he can manage.
- More stone answers than expected. The wall holds or a protective rise forms.
- Redd has no time to celebrate or explain.
- Orieth uses the opening he created and continues moving people.
- Their cooperation is practical and imperfect.

### Wena

- Wena remains near Orieth's daughter inside the protected structure.
- She warns when debris, flame or an attacker nears the entrance.
- She does not cross the battlefield to save Alaric.

### Turn

Foramen's defence prevents the first attempt to destroy its homes, but the divided threats stop Kurdag from concentrating on Liluth. Liluth abandons formal capture and moves after Alaric personally.

---

## Scene 4 — Liluth Breaks From the Mission

### Immediate want

Liluth wants to end Alaric once resistance and Gerolt's death strip away the restraint of her orders. Alaric wants to keep her moving towards ground Kurdag can affect without placing civilians behind him.

### How her control breaks

The shift must be gradual and visible:

1. She begins with official demands.
2. Gerolt's death makes her language personal.
3. Resistance turns her attention from recovery towards punishment.
4. Alaric's continued survival becomes intolerable to her.
5. She uses Earth with increasing force despite Foramen's fragile foundations.

Do not convert her into a screaming caricature. Grief may make her colder, more exact or more willing to accept collateral death before it finally breaks her discipline.

### Alaric cannot duel her

- He does not trade several competent sword combinations with Liluth.
- His limited training lets him hold the weapon correctly, move without crossing his feet, obey one warning and commit to one strike.
- Liluth defeats his guard or forces him backwards almost immediately.
- He survives by moving towards the chosen open ground, by Liluth's fixation and through intervention from others.
- His improving health does not produce hidden combat knowledge.

### Collective creation of the opening

The opening must have an explicit causal chain:

- **Alaric** draws Liluth onto a section of street with fewer people behind him.
- **Silas**, after dealing with or escaping the Wind rider, intercepts one killing attack and forces Liluth to turn through her damaged side. He does not defeat her for Alaric.
- **Kurdag** changes the earth beneath Liluth with controlled precision. The shift breaks her footing or drops one leg without collapsing the surrounding homes.
- **Liluth** refuses to withdraw, braces through her weakened arm or raises it to cast despite the pain, opening the gap beneath its armour.
- Her missing left eye prevents her from seeing Alaric's movement soon enough once her body turns.
- **Alaric** recognises what has been created.

The prose must not claim that fate, hidden power or instinct guided him. Gerolt caused the injuries. Silas and Kurdag create the moment. Alaric chooses to use it.

### The remembered lesson

Alaric recalls only the practical principle Silas has actually taught him: hesitation inside a committed strike is more dangerous than choosing it completely.

Do not pause for a full training flashback or repeat a previous lesson word for word unless the exact words are necessary.

### Turn

Alaric decides before moving that the strike is intended to kill.

---

## Scene 5 — One Strike

### Execution

- Alaric moves from Liluth's damaged left side.
- He uses both hands and commits his weight as trained.
- The blade enters beneath the armour of her weakened arm and passes into her torso.
- This is the only meaningful strike Alaric lands against her.
- Do not make it elegant.
- Do not make it effortless.
- Do not let him display technique he has not earned.
- The strike succeeds because the opportunity is unusually complete and because he does not stop once he chooses it.

### Liluth's death

- She receives no explanatory final speech.
- She does not forgive Alaric, recognise his goodness or reveal hidden lore.
- Eldrin remains unnamed.
- Her expression and one unfinished physical action may preserve the fact that she remained fixed upon Alaric or Gerolt until the end.
- She dies without the narrative declaring her merely a monster or absolving the torture and collective punishment committed during her pursuit.

### Alaric's response

- He understands immediately that this killing was a choice, unlike the desperate thrust into Sirden.
- He may experience physical shock, revulsion and relief together.
- He does not collapse, lose consciousness, trigger a memory or discover power.
- Do not repeat the complete Chapter 8 reaction.
- He need not attempt impossible treatment merely to prove compassion.
- Silas does not deliver another lecture about killing.
- The continuing battle requires Alaric to move, warn someone, withdraw the sword or help end the remaining threat before he can process what he has done.

### Turn

Liluth's death breaks the riders' cohesion. The surviving Water rider yields once the others are dead or incapable of continuing.

---

## Scene 6 — The Last Rider

### Battle resolution

By the end of the fight:

- Liluth is dead by Alaric's hand.
- The Wind rider is killed by Silas during a credible exchange.
- The Fire rider dies during the settlement's defence after continuing to attack occupied structures.
- The Earth rider is killed or fatally wounded by Kurdag while Kurdag prevents a larger collapse.
- The Water rider survives, disarmed and restrained.
- Silas receives no serious wound.
- Kurdag, Orieth, Redd, Wena and Orieth's daughter survive.
- Foramen has several wounded residents and one confirmed death: the musician.

Do not inventory every corpse in the prose. Reveal the outcome through the immediate conditions after the fighting stops.

### What the survivor knows

The rider can credibly confirm only:

- Liluth held authority to locate and capture the battlefield survivor;
- the five-rider pursuit was presented as part of that mission;
- Liluth shared little about the source of her certainty;
- the riders did not receive an explanation of Foramen before entering it;
- the survivor does not know whether Liluth sent a bird, signal or prior report.

The rider cannot explain:

- Alaric's nature;
- the ancient battlefield;
- the war core;
- Eldrin and Liluth's complete relationship;
- the higher political hunt;
- whether Natharul already knows Foramen's location.

Their ignorance is genuine. Interrogation cannot manufacture certainty.

---

## Scene 7 — Foramen's Uncomfortable Choice

### Immediate want

Foramen's residents want assurance that another Natharul force will not arrive. No one can provide it.

### Material aftermath

- Fires are controlled rather than allowed to consume the settlement.
- Injured residents receive treatment through limited supplies and combined weak magic.
- Bodies are moved without extensive funeral ritual during the remaining night.
- Damage to roofs, walls, stored food and water is assessed concretely.
- The cistern and core food stores survive; otherwise Foramen would face an immediate forced migration.
- The musician's instrument is recovered damaged or blood-marked.

### Council disagreement

The captive rider creates an actual conflict among people who share Foramen's larger ideals.

Positions may include:

- killing the rider because releasing them may condemn two hundred people;
- permanent imprisonment because freedom cannot require collective suicide;
- refusing execution because Foramen cannot answer ownership with a helpless prisoner's death;
- waiting until they know whether a report was sent, despite having no reliable way to learn it;
- moving lookouts and concealing the western approach while keeping the settlement itself in place.

The fearful man from Chapter 19 may argue openly for execution. His fear is rational even if others reject his conclusion.

Kurdag does not instantly provide the morally correct answer. Orieth does not speak for every escaped Faint or every elf. The council remains divided.

### Alaric's boundary

- Alaric may be asked what happened or what Liluth wanted.
- He tells the relevant truth without assuming authority over the prisoner.
- He does not decide what Foramen must do.
- He understands that every available choice now carries danger because the riders followed him there.
- He does not give a speech about freedom, protection or responsibility.

### Setup ledger

The prisoner's fate becomes a deliberate continuing thread:

- **State at Chapter 20 end:** planted and unresolved.
- **Known:** Foramen cannot know whether its location was reported.
- **Required later development:** news, a traveller, Natharul movement, a returned character or a later visit must reveal what Foramen decided and whether that decision carried consequences.
- **Prohibition:** do not forget the prisoner after using the dilemma to complicate Foramen.

## Scene 8 — Choosing the Road

### Immediate want

Alaric wants to prevent his presence from drawing a larger force into Foramen while continuing towards Favale with the war core.

### Alaric's decision

Alaric chooses to leave before dawn. This is not a punishment imposed by Foramen and not a theatrical offer to disappear forever.

His reasoning may be visible through practical action:

- checking the core remains secure;
- packing without waiting to be expelled;
- asking Kurdag for the route rather than whether he is still welcome;
- accepting that the fastest road is no longer automatically the right road.

Silas may understand before Alaric explains. Redd may object to leaving injured people behind or to departing before the prisoner's fate is known, but the conversation must remain brief and responsive.

### Kurdag's route

Kurdag gives Alaric practical information rather than another thematic lesson:

- a slower line around the most unstable approach to the central crater;
- landmarks that remain reliable despite warped geography;
- where water can be found;
- where fires or altered creatures have recently been seen;
- a warning that the route adds time but avoids the place that worsened Alaric's pain.

Alaric chooses the slower, safer route. This shows development from the person who repeatedly pushed forward until pain or danger made the choice for him.

Kurdag remains in Foramen. Protecting its people, judging the prisoner and repairing the damage are his responsibilities.

### Farewell restraint

- Do not repeat Marta's warm departure or another exchange of profound parting lines.
- Kurdag's care appears through information accurate enough to keep them alive.
- Orieth may return or adjust one practical item belonging to Redd without forgiving him or discussing their conflict.
- No new weapon, magical gift or companion joins the group.
- Foramen does not celebrate Alaric for killing Liluth.

## Ending Image — The Missing Note

As Alaric, Silas, Redd and Wena reach the outer ruins before dawn, the settlement begins recovering its ordinary sounds.

Someone—possibly one of the children who argued over the tune in Chapter 19—attempts to play the dead musician's three-stringed instrument.

The first notes travel after the departing group.

Alaric recognises the unfinished melody and waits for the note the musician never found the previous evening.

It does not come.

The ending must remain quiet. Do not add:

- another rider on the horizon;
- a message from Natharul;
- activation of the core;
- a memory fragment;
- Seralune sensing the death;
- a final speech explaining what the missing note means.

The reader already understands that Foramen survived and was changed.

## Character Movement

### Alaric

**Beginning:** draws a sword because danger has reached a community that sheltered him.

**Decision:** refuses passive concealment, forms a limited plan and deliberately uses an opening to kill Liluth.

**Cost:** understands that protecting people can require chosen violence and that survival does not erase responsibility for where pursuit leads.

**Development:** accepts a slower route because the safety of his companions matters more than speed or his desperation for answers.

**End:** leaves Foramen without being expelled and without pretending his departure repairs what happened.

### Silas

- Objects to Alaric acting alone but recognises the narrow intention behind the movement.
- Demonstrates real battlefield competence without becoming invulnerable or receiving another major injury.
- Helps create Alaric's opening rather than taking the final kill.
- Does not explain Alaric's emotions or repeat earlier lessons about violence.
- Supports the chosen departure because remaining invites larger pursuit.

### Redd

- Wants to fight the Natharul riders directly because they embody the force that destroyed his childhood.
- Protects residents beside Orieth instead of chasing revenge across the street.
- Produces an unexpectedly strong but uncontrolled Earth response.
- Does not suddenly trust all elves or apologise for his hatred.
- Leaves with the group because he chooses them, not because Foramen rejects him.

### Kurdag

- Protects Foramen through restraint, preparation and precise strength.
- Leaves Alaric's decision to reveal himself with Alaric.
- Contributes directly to Liluth's defeat without solving the entire battle.
- Faces a prisoner dilemma with no clean answer.
- Remains with the community whose survival depends upon him.

### Orieth

- Acts as a council member and mother under attack.
- Rejects Liluth's racial claim upon her through conduct.
- Works beside Redd without becoming responsible for curing his prejudice.
- Participates in the unresolved debate after the battle.

### Liluth

**Beginning:** controlled scout commander invoking legitimate authority.

**Pressure:** Gerolt's death, Alaric's survival and Foramen's refusal make the personal motive harder to conceal.

**Choice:** abandons recovery, accepts harm to residents and pursues Alaric beyond her mission.

**End:** dies because grief and entitlement narrow her judgment until she exposes the injuries Gerolt left her.

Her history explains her. It does not excuse her.

## Dialogue Rules for This Chapter

- Follow revised Chapters 14–19 and [[Chapter Craft Writing Rules]].
- Liluth begins formal and controlled. Her language becomes personal by degrees.
- Do not give Liluth a villain monologue or dying confession.
- Kurdag speaks concretely about the residents, structures and choices before him. Do not write strings of wisdom quotations.
- Silas uses short instructions during violence and ordinary speech afterwards.
- Redd's humour may surface once under fear but cannot turn the attack into comedy.
- Orieth does not debate racism while defending her child and neighbours.
- Alaric does not deliver a speech announcing that he will protect Foramen.
- Every exchange must respond to the line or action before it.
- Urgent dialogue remains brief because urgent people do not pause for lore.
- Ordinary tags are preferable to emotional stage directions attached to every sentence.
- Keep every speaker unmistakable during the council disagreement.
- Apply the interchangeability test: Liluth, Kurdag, Silas, Redd and Alaric must not sound like one author speaking through different mouths.

## Power and Combat Guardrails

- Liluth is High Earth, not Exalted and not a Ruler.
- Kurdag is Exalted Earth but is protecting fragile structures and dispersed residents.
- Redd contains latent Exalted Earth potential but cannot consciously access or control it.
- Alaric displays no magical power.
- The dormant core does not react.
- No soul connection, memory fragment or unexplained healing occurs.
- Alaric does not sense Affinities or rank opponents accurately.
- Alaric does not outfight Liluth.
- One week of intermittent instruction gives him stance, commitment and limited movement—not experience.
- Silas remains a skilled mercenary whose Fire『Affinity』 and combat judgment are useful without making him stronger than Kurdag.
- Weak residents contribute through combined effort and preparation, not sudden rank advancement.
- Magic must respect the dry settlement, open roofs, unstable foundations and limited water.
- Do not use **THOOM** unless Alaric physically experiences his established heartbeat motif and the moment truly requires it. The Liluth kill does not require the sound merely because it is important.
- Sound effects must have physical sources and must not substitute for choreography.

- Alaric carries Silas's worn spare sword.
- Redd carries his father's sword.
