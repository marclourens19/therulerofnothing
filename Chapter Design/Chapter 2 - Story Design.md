The best development is one you may not have realised you created:

- Chapter 1 ends with Alaric choosing silence.
- Chapter 2 forces him to break that silence to warn Gerolt.
- His warning saves Gerolt from the earth attack.
- But the surviving scout hears him and learns that Gerolt was hiding someone.

That is excellent cause and effect. Alaric makes a compassionate choice, saves someone, and immediately pays for it. That perfectly supports your central theme.

A few things still need refining.

### Gerolt’s wound

Make the earth strike pierce beneath his ribs or tear through his side, rather than simply through his abdomen. That better supports him coughing blood while remaining mobile for a few minutes.

The injury must also affect the fight. Gerolt should only wound the female because Alaric’s warning gives him a fraction of a second—not because he effortlessly overpowers her.

### The surviving scout

Keep her alive. Do not add a third hidden elf.

She retreats because:

- Her companion is dead.
- Gerolt has wounded her.
- She heard Alaric shout.
- She knows reinforcements are close.

When she reports another person inside, the reader understands that Alaric’s choice exposed him.

### Gerolt’s exposition

Keep it extremely short:

> “Natharul scout.”
> 
> “Why are they here?”
> 
> “Doesn’t matter. She got away.”

He should not calmly explain elven politics while bleeding and listening to approaching horns.

### Give Alaric proof for Kelmend

Gerolt should give him something the innkeeper will recognise: a ring, military token, broken medallion or sealed message. Otherwise, an unknown young man arriving with no name and claiming Gerolt sent him may not be believed.

### The name reveal

The idea is excellent, but this line is too direct:

> “I REMEMBER MY NAME, ALARIC! DON’T LEAVE ME!”

It makes Alaric sound much younger and tells the emotion instead of allowing it to land. The stronger version is quieter:

Another horn answered from beyond the wheat.

“Take Wena,” Gerolt said. Blood shone between his teeth. “West across the river. Kelmend. Find the innkeeper and show him this.”

Gerolt gave a broken, breathless laugh.

“Stubborn little bastard. All that emptiness, and still no room for sense.”

The pain returned.

White split the darkness behind the boy’s eyes.

A voice reached through it, frayed almost beyond recognition.

“——Ala…”

He seized his head.

“——Alaric.”

The light vanished.

The boy stared at Gerolt.

“My name,” he whispered. “My name is Alaric.”

For the first time, Gerolt looked at him as though he were seeing a person rather than a mystery.

“Alaric,” he repeated.

Another volley struck the cabin.

Gerolt pushed the token into his hand.

“Then run, Alaric.”

That is the emotional heart of the chapter. Gerolt becomes the first person in this world to say Alaric’s name, immediately before sending him away.

One warning: don’t make “quick and relentless” mean emotionally weightless. The fight should be brutally fast, but the farewell deserves room to breathe. A few seconds can feel enormous when Alaric realises he must abandon the first person who helped him.

My preferred title is **The Price of a Voice**. It refers simultaneously to:

- The visitors demanding an answer.
- Alaric breaking his silence to save Gerolt.
- The scout discovering his presence.
- The mysterious voice returning his name.
