# Reply to the Volume 1 Working Design

10 October 2026. My answer to *The Ruler of Nothing — Volume 1: Complete Working Design*, kept word for word in `Volume 1 Complete Working Design (10 October).md`. I checked it against `Decisions.md`, the Volume 1 and Volume 2 Pictures, the structural map and Chapters 1–10.

## In short

It's the best planning document the project has. It builds in the fixes the volume review asked for: Alaric chooses an investigation in Chapter 11, Empty has consequences, Redd and Freya arrive by position 13, and the group is the centre of his story. It's strict about cause and effect: a traceable chase, a real reason for Darcy to refuse, and a reason to choose the Expanse. It also holds back the big answers without making people stonewall. The section on the three losses (Gerolt, Foramen, Favale) and the checklist of what must be on the page before the tear are particularly good. I'd adopt it as the working map.

Six things need settling or fixing first. The first matters most.

## 1. The first encounter conflicts with three recorded plans

The design lists this as settled: "Alaric and Seralune first knowingly encounter one another briefly at the Volume 1 climax. Their relationship develops in Volume 2." Its last paragraph says this supersedes the structural map's later meeting. But the later meeting isn't only in the structural map. It's in canon:

- **The series timeline** (approved 26 September): "Alaric and Seralune meet at the end of Volume 3."
- **The Volume 2 Picture:** both groups head for Kozmagar; "The reader knows they're converging; the characters don't. They meet at the end of Volume 3." In Volume 2 she's in the Church's custody and escapes with Nereth, while he rescues Darcy.
- **Volume 3's climax:** "Alaric thinks Seralune is one of the assassins, because she's an elf." If he has already seen her at the centre of Favale and tied her to the power he felt, he won't take her for an assassin. He'll know her as the woman from Favale. That's arguably a stronger motive, but it's a different one.
- **Freya:** "Alaric and Freya become mutual before Seralune arrives, late in Volume 2." If their relationship "develops in Volume 2", Seralune is in his life during Freya's volume.

So which is it?

- **(a) A brief first sighting at Favale; their real meeting stays at the end of Volume 3.** *My recommendation.* The sighting gives him a face for what he felt, and gives Volume 3's attack a sharper motive. "Their relationship develops in Volume 2" then means cause and effect at a distance: he hears what the world says about the witch of Favale, and she hears of what was seen at the centre. It needs no shared scenes, and Volumes 2–3 and Freya's arc stay as planned.
- **(b) They meet at Favale and their relationship develops on the page in Volume 2.** Then the Volume 2 and Volume 3 Pictures, Volume 3's climax and Freya's timing all need redesigning.

## 2. Seralune needs to meet "the princess history condemns" before the tear

Canon gives her this last choice in Volume 1: "She accepts responsibility for who she was, even though that isn't her true self and she didn't actually do those things." The design has her accept responsibility for the deaths at Favale only. Those can be the same ending only if she learns during the volume what history says the princess was.

The design already has the source on Alaric's side: at Favale, the core is tied to "the ancient war against the princess history condemns" (position 50). Nothing in her thread brings her to it. The structural map had it ("Seralune encounters the public version of herself before anybody recognises the living woman"), and this design dropped it.

**Recommendation:** her inquiry at Favale (position 51) is where she meets that history. She reacts with disbelief and anger first, as the map said, so that accepting it after the tear grows from evidence and loss rather than meekness. Then she takes on both: the deaths at Favale, and the name history gave her.

It also gives the reserved Inrandeel-to-Favale lead one candidate. Her Volume 1 question is why she was sealed. A lead towards wherever the history of that war is kept answers her own question, not just her mother's whereabouts. That's different from the rejected dispersed-community records. The bridge stays yours to choose.

## 3. Gilmot answers the design's own question about Darcy

The design asks the fort's design to give Darcy's dependants "a concrete connection to her and the institution a credible way to retaliate". It also insists that escape and an authorised transfer must have different consequences. Recorded canon already has this, and the design doesn't mention it:

- `Decisions.md` and the Volume 2 Picture both have Gilmot's threat against Kelmend's Faint quarter. In Volume 2 it never comes true, because the war draws the soldiers off.
- The old chapters made it concrete: an order to burn the quarter if Darcy is lost. It's raw material, not canon, but it's exactly the mechanism the design asks for. Escape triggers it; an authorised transfer doesn't.
- The Faint quarter is Redd and Freya's home. So meeting them there (position 13) and her refusal (16) become one chain: she refuses for the sake of the people Alaric has just met. The outline of 29 September planned this ("Darcy's choice is about their lives before they're ever Alaric's friends").

**Recommendation:** use it.

## 4. Natharul's search for its princess is missing

The design asks for a traceable chain for Liluth, but not for Thaer, who is in Favale by position 53. And Seralune's road between Inrandeel and Favale (positions 34, 37 and 43) has no pressure on it while its purpose is reserved. The page has already set up a search:

- She escaped the palace and opened the side of a mountain, and a port watched it happen.
- Thaer told her on the stair: "While I'm here, he has to get past me before he sends anyone after you."
- Natharul's scouts are all over Mydea, all the time, never seen.

The structural map had it too ("searches for Seralune tighten the city").

Using Natharul's search would:

- put real pressure on her road chapters;
- give Thaer a traceable way to Favale;
- answer the design's open question about Liluth. Once the princess is reported in Mydea, Natharul's people can be pulled off the hunt for a boy and a dog to hunt for her. That's "changed orders" with a cause, and it ties the threads together by cause and effect, as the series rule asks ("never through near-misses").

Who searches, and how, is your call.

## 5. The reserved bridges are needed earlier than the design says

- **The tear's trigger.** If the core is involved at all, its danger has to be planted in the chapters that handle it: recovering it (22), the examination at Foramen (38) and the answers at Favale (50). So the trigger needs deciding before position 22 is designed, not before 56. Even if the core plays no part, those chapters must avoid promising one.
- **Seralune's Favale lead.** If it comes from Inrandeel's escapees, it has to be seeded in positions 26–29. Decide it before 26.

## 6. Length, and the double's long absence

- **Length.** Sixty-two positions at the chapters' current length (about 4,500 words) is about 280,000 words. That's fine for a serial, but the Kelmend block repeats the review's pace problem. Alaric reaches Kelmend in Chapter 8 and leaves for the field at position 19, which is nine of his chapters in one town, and the return to the field lands at chapter 21 of the book. The design allows merging. I'd merge 13–17 now, into three chapters: the Faint quarter; reconnaissance and entry; Darcy and the escape.
- **The double.** After Chapter 9 it doesn't appear until position 32, twenty-three chapters later. The design wants it sparing, and it's right. But Gerolt's field (21) is the natural second appearance: the place he woke, in the clothes he woke in, at the moment he goes looking for his past on purpose. One appearance there won't make it a way of travelling.

## Smaller points

- **Thaer and Alaric.** §15 has Thaer spare Alaric "at the encounter", but §11 cuts Thaer off from the centre, which is where the encounter happens. Decide where the two of them cross: on the way out of the centre, in the aftermath, or in a later volume.
- **The POV split.** The table gives about 64/36 for Alaric, close to the outline's 60/40, so no change is needed.
- Everything else I checked agrees with canon: no evidence of erasure, the three hazards kept apart, Wena surviving, Freya's Water left alone, Nereth's bite lasting.
